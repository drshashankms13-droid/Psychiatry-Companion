## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Soon after the first antidepressants were introduced, it became clear that a significant proportion of patients fail to respond. This led to the concept of **treatment-resistant depression (TRD)** [1, p44].

### Definitions and staging
- **Current common definition:** failure of **two adequate antidepressants**, given at adequate dose for adequate duration, to produce a **response** (usually ≥50% reduction in depressive symptoms) [1, p44].
- **Thase and Rush staging (5 stages)** [1, p44]:
  - Stage I is failure of one adequate antidepressant trial.
  - Each later stage adds failed trials of antidepressants from other classes.
  - Stage V is failure to respond to ECT.
- **Maudsley Staging Method (MSM):** TRD as a multidimensional construct along **three axes**: treatment failures, duration of symptoms and severity of symptoms [1, p44]. TRD is a graded spectrum of severity, and outcome is closely linked to grade [2, p348].
- **"Difficult-to-treat depression":** a proposed alternative term [2, p348].
  - "TRD" implies treatments normally work, so non-response looks abnormal.
  - "TRD" pushes clinicians towards more drugs in more complex regimens, instead of setting realistic expectations of recovery.
- **Hidden bipolarity:** a significant minority of apparently resistant unipolar depression may be **bipolar depression**, which often does not respond to standard antidepressants [2, p348].

### Step 1 — Evaluate: is it really TRD? [1, p44]
- **Confirm MDD** through a detailed history of current and past episodes.
- **Identify misdiagnosis or comorbidity:**
  - bipolar disorder, especially type II;
  - anxiety disorders, substance use, PTSD, ADHD.
- **Perpetuating factors:** temperament and personality, psychosocial and other stressors.
- **Medical mimics and contributors:**
  - hypothyroidism;
  - obstructive sleep apnoea;
  - early Parkinson's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders;
  - anaemia.
- **Examination:** general physical and neurological.
- **Investigations:**
  - haemogram, LFT, RFT, fasting blood sugar, electrolytes;
  - B12, folate and vitamin D3 if malnutrition is suspected.
- **Past treatments:** scrutinise previous records and document **adherence and response** to each trial.

### Step 2 — Optimise [1, pp44–45]
- Increase the current antidepressant to the **upper limit of the standard dose range**, or as tolerated, while monitoring adverse effects.
- At target dose, continue for **6–12 weeks**, assessing response and adherence. An additional **one-fifth** of earlier non-responders respond in weeks 5–8.
- **Exception:** in severe depression with little or no benefit so far, move to another strategy sooner.

### Step 3 — Augment, switch or combine [1, pp45–47]

**A. Augmentation** (adding a psychotropic to enhance the antidepressant's effect). Evidence is largely limited to three classes:

| Agent | Evidence and issues |
| --- | --- |
| **Atypical antipsychotics** | Four are **US FDA-approved** for TRD augmentation: aripiprazole, quetiapine XR, olanzapine–fluoxetine combination, brexpiprazole. Supported by several meta-analyses. Risks: metabolic syndrome, sedation, EPS [1] |
| **Lithium** | A meta-analysis of 9 RCTs found it more effective than placebo for augmenting TCAs and SSRIs, but many trials were old and short (2–42 days). Underused, owing to level monitoring, adverse effects and long-term renal effects [1] |
| **Thyroid hormones** | Conflicting results. A 2015 network meta-analysis: OR 1.84 (95% CI 1.06–3.56) by network methods, but non-significant by classic meta-analysis (OR 1.55, 0.55–4.36). Another meta-analysis of 6 RCTs: OR 1.56 (0.50–4.84). **Weak and conflicting evidence** [1] |

**B. Switching** [1, pp46–47]
- **When to switch:**
  - **No response** (<25% improvement) at 2–3 weeks is unlikely to change with more time on the same dose, so increase the dose or switch.
  - **Partial response** (26–49%) at 2–3 weeks is less studied; give a full trial and assess at about 6 weeks before switching.
- **How to switch:**
  - **Cross-titration** suits SSRIs and SNRIs, avoiding withdrawal and allowing a rapid transition.
  - **Never cross-titrate with TCAs or MAOIs**, because of serotonin syndrome risk; use a **washout** period instead.
- **What to switch to:**
  - **STAR*D** found no difference in remission between sertraline, venlafaxine and bupropion after failed citalopram; other studies agree.
  - Against this, **ARGOS** found a modest advantage for venlafaxine after a failed SSRI.
  - A **2008 meta-analysis (Papakostas)** found a statistically significant but clinically marginal advantage (**NNT 22**) for switching to a non-SSRI.
  - **Bottom line:** not enough evidence to prefer between-class over within-class switching.

**C. Combining antidepressants** [1, p47]
- **CO-MED (MDD, not TRD):** escitalopram + placebo vs escitalopram + bupropion vs venlafaxine + mirtazapine. Remission was no different; adverse effects were higher with venlafaxine + mirtazapine.
- **MIR:** adding mirtazapine to the existing antidepressant gave no significant gain in remission at 12 weeks.
- **Meta-analysis:** a small benefit overall (SMD 0.16, 0.02–0.29), but non-significant in double-blind RCTs of TRD (SMD 0.13, −0.18 to 0.44).
- **Conclusion:** little evidence that combination is superior in TRD.

### Step 4 — ECT [1, p47]
- **Gold standard for MDD:** remission rates up to **60–90%**.
- **In TRD:** remission is lower but still substantial (about **50%**), and still higher than any other modality.
- **Royal College of Psychiatrists (2017):** ECT is **first line** in TRD that responded to ECT before, and **second line** in TRD generally.

### Step 5 — Newer modalities [1, pp47–49]

| Modality | Source summary |
| --- | --- |
| **Ketamine** | A meta-analysis of 21 RCTs (IV, intranasal, oral) gave an effect size of **0.529**. The effect is transient (days to a week), so it is reserved for **severe TRD with high risk of self-harm**. IV 0.5 mg/kg over 40 minutes with monitoring of pulse, BP, ECG, respiratory rate and SpO2. Given twice or thrice weekly; **maintenance not currently recommended** — taper and stop |
| **rTMS** | Some efficacy in TRD (RCTs and meta-analyses). FDA-approved first line for MDD with ≥1 failed antidepressant. Inferior to ECT, and ECT non-responders are unlikely to respond. No anaesthesia and no cognitive impairment |
| **tDCS** | Level A evidence for anodal left DLPFC tDCS in MDD (13 studies, effect size −0.36). Equivocal in TRD; **not recommended for TRD** |
| **Deep brain stimulation** | Several targets studied (SCG, NAc, VC/VS, ITP, vALIC, MFB, LHb). Only VC/VS and SCG have had multicentre sham-controlled RCTs, with **inconclusive** results. **Strictly experimental** |
| **Psychedelics** | 5-HT2A agonists. Psilocybin (10 mg, then 25 mg a week later, with psychological support) reduced symptoms at 1 week, 3 months and 6 months in open studies (n=12, then 20). Ayahuasca (single dose, RCT, n=29) was effective at days 1, 2 and 7 |

### Maudsley's tiers of pharmacological options [2, pp348–354]
- **Commonly used, well supported:**
  - add aripiprazole 2–20 mg/day (low doses of 2–10 mg may work; akathisia is common at ≥10 mg);
  - add lithium, aiming for 0.4–0.8 mmol/L and rising up to 1.0;
  - olanzapine 6.25–12.5 mg + fluoxetine 25–50 mg;
  - add quetiapine 150 or 300 mg to an SSRI or SNRI;
  - SSRI + bupropion up to 400 mg;
  - SSRI or venlafaxine + mianserin 30 mg or mirtazapine 30–45 mg (one RCT showed no advantage for mirtazapine).
- **Less commonly used, well supported:**
  - ketamine or esketamine;
  - lamotrigine (100–400 mg; probably the best-tolerated augmentation);
  - ECT;
  - tri-iodothyronine 20–50 mcg (some negative studies).
- **Other reported treatments, limited support, for exceptional circumstances only:**
  - pramipexole, risperidone add-on, dextromethorphan–quinidine, nortriptyline ± lithium, MAOIs, high-dose venlafaxine;
  - psilocybin, nitrous oxide, minocycline, pindolol, and others.

**Conclusion** [1, pp49–50]:
- TRD means failure of two adequate trials. It needs careful diagnostic re-evaluation and a detailed medication history.
- **Optimise first.** Combination therapy has proved ineffective; there is not enough evidence to choose between switching and augmentation.
- **ECT** remains the gold standard for severe depression needing rapid response and for pharmacological non-response.
- **rTMS** is the only non-invasive stimulation technique with some efficacy in TRD.
- **Ketamine** has been widely adopted for rapid effect.

## In depth

### Background
- Definitions vary between groups, but the premise is the same [1, p44].
- **Maudsley's critique of STAR*D** [2, p348]:
  - a pragmatic effectiveness programme with remission as the outcome, now "somewhat discredited";
  - high dropout, no placebo group, open treatment and assessments;
  - a secondary measure used as the main outcome, and payments to subjects;
  - 93% of 1,518 remitters had relapsed or dropped out by 12 months;
  - on reanalysis, overall remission was **35%, not the claimed 67%**.

### Classification and criteria
See the definitions and staging section above. Both sources agree on "two adequate trials" as the working definition. The MSM adds duration and severity [1, p44; 2, p348].

### Assessment in detail
- **Primer checklist** [1, p44]: diagnosis, comorbidity, personality and stressors, medical mimics, physical and neurological examination, laboratory tests, review of past records and adherence.
- **Maudsley adds** [2, p348]: actively look for bipolar depression masquerading as resistant unipolar depression.

### Management details — practical cautions
- **Lithium** is potentially toxic, needs level monitoring and usually specialist referral [2, p348].
- **Quetiapine:** dry mouth, sedation, constipation; weight gain longer term [2, p349].
- **Mianserin** carries a risk of blood dyscrasia [2, p349].
- **SSRI + mirtazapine:** theoretical serotonin syndrome risk, so inform the patient [2, p349].
- **T3** needs clinical and biochemical thyroid monitoring [2, p351].
- **Lamotrigine** needs slow titration because of the rash risk [2, p351].
- **IV ketamine** needs a hospital setting and a pre-treatment ECG; repeated treatment is needed to maintain the effect [2, p351].
- **Prescribers** should read the primary literature before using these strategies [2, p353].

### Critical appraisal (from the sources)
- **Switch vs augment:** there is no adequate evidence to choose between them [1, p50].
- **Lithium evidence** rests on older, short trials [1, p45].
- **Thyroid evidence** changes with the analytic method [1, p46].
- **STAR*D's** influence on TRD algorithms is undermined by its methodological problems [2, p348].
- **The "TRD" label** may drive polypharmacy; "difficult-to-treat depression" reframes the goal [2, p348].
- **Ketamine maintenance — the sources disagree:**
  - the Primer says maintenance is not recommended [1, p48];
  - Maudsley gives weekly, fortnightly and monthly maintenance schedules [2, p358].
  - Both should be presented in an exam answer.
