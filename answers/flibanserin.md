> **Source discipline:** this answer uses only *Maudsley Prescribing Guidelines* 15e, "Antidepressants and sexual dysfunction" (table and text) [1]. The source covers flibanserin in **two entries only**, so this answer is necessarily brief.

## Exam answer

**What it is [1].**
- **Mechanism:** a **5-HT1A agonist, 5-HT2A antagonist and dopamine antagonist**.
- **Indication:** licensed **in the USA** (FDA-approved) for **lack or loss of sexual desire in premenopausal women**, that is, **hypoactive sexual desire disorder**.
- Bremelanotide is the other FDA-approved drug for this indication.

**Safety [1].**
- It **appears to be safe in women taking antidepressants** (from a randomised placebo-controlled study by Clayton et al., 2018).
- **Adverse effects:** **hypotension, syncope**, sedation, dizziness, nausea, dry mouth.

**Antidepressant-induced sexual dysfunction [1].**
- There are **no data** supporting its use for antidepressant-induced sexual dysfunction, even though it is safe alongside antidepressants.
- The source lists other options for SSRI-induced dysfunction: **switching**, bupropion, buspirone, cyproheptadine, PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil), and adjunctive aripiprazole (improves desire in women).

## In depth

### Where it sits among treatments for sexual dysfunction [1]
| Agent | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Flibanserin | 5-HT1A agonist, 5-HT2A antagonist, D antagonist | Low desire in premenopausal women (USA) |
| Bupropion | NA and DA reuptake inhibitor | SSRI-induced sexual dysfunction |
| Buspirone | 5-HT1A partial agonist | SSRI-induced low libido or anorgasmia |
| Cyproheptadine | 5-HT2 antagonist | SSRI-induced dysfunction (may reverse the antidepressant effect) |
| Sildenafil and other PDE-5 inhibitors | PDE-5 inhibition | Erectile dysfunction |
