## Exam answer

### Definition

Digital phenotyping is moment-by-moment quantification of behaviour and context using personal digital devices. **Active data** include surveys and ecological momentary assessments; **passive data** include movement, sleep–wake patterns, phone use, location-derived mobility, and voice features. It should complement, not replace, clinical assessment.

### Applications

- **Depression:** Mobility, sleep timing, social communication, and self-reported mood may track symptoms in some people.
- **Bipolar disorder:** Activity, sleep, communication, and circadian regularity may help identify individual early-warning patterns.
- **Psychosis:** Mobility, social activity, sleep, and speech-related signals are being studied for relapse/functional change.
- **Suicide prevention:** Digital signals may support monitoring, but no single marker reliably predicts an individual attempt.
- **Treatment monitoring:** Frequent brief measures can reveal fluctuation between visits.

### Advantages

Longitudinal, ecologically valid data; less recall bias; possible detection of within-person change; and more tailored follow-up. It may help where clinic visits are infrequent if access is equitable.

### Limitations

Small samples, short follow-up, inconsistent methods, poor replication, missing data, device changes, and limited external validation are common. Population models may not generalize to an individual. Phone behaviour varies by occupation, culture, disability, income, and preference. Monitoring can cause anxiety, surveillance concerns, or false alarms.

### Ethics/implementation

Obtain specific informed consent; explain data collected, analysis, access, sharing, and withdrawal. Minimize collection, secure storage, define retention, and ensure refusal does not compromise care. Assess algorithmic bias and require human clinical review. Do not use unvalidated algorithms for high-stakes decisions. In India, consider language diversity, shared devices, connectivity, and digital exclusion.

### Conclusion

Digital phenotyping is promising for tracking within-person change but is not a stand-alone diagnostic tool. Utility requires validation, interpretability, privacy, equity, and accountable clinical integration.
