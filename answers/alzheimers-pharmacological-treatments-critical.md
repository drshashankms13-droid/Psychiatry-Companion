## Exam answer

**Introduction.** **Alzheimer's disease (AD)** is the commonest cause of dementia (about 60–70%). It is a progressive neurodegenerative disease defined biologically by **amyloid-β plaques and tau neurofibrillary tangles** (A/T/N framework), with neuronal loss. The clinical stages are preclinical, MCI due to AD, and mild, moderate and severe dementia [1].

"Altering the course" means **disease modification**: slowing, halting or reversing the underlying pathology and clinical decline, not just improving symptoms.

**Position.** The statement is **largely false for most of AD's history and still only modestly true today**:
- **Symptomatic drugs** (cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine) give small, temporary benefits **without altering the course** [1].
- **Anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies** (lecanemab, donanemab) are the **first drugs to slow decline**, by about **25–35% over 18 months**, in **early AD with confirmed amyloid**. That is real disease modification, but small, costly and risky [1].

### Drug classes

| Class | Drugs | Mechanism | Effect on course |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cholinesterase inhibitors (ChEIs)** | **Donepezil** 5→10 mg (all stages; half-life about 70 h); **rivastigmine** 1.5→6 mg twice daily or patch 4.6→9.5→13.3 mg/24 h (also PDD); **galantamine** 4→12 mg twice daily (also nicotinic modulation); tacrine (hepatotoxic, withdrawn) | ↑ synaptic acetylcholine (cholinergic hypothesis) | **Symptomatic:** ADAS-Cog improves about 2.5–4 points vs placebo; small ADL and global benefit; benefit may last 1–2 years. **No disease modification** [1] |
| **NMDA antagonist** | **Memantine** 5→20 mg (moderate–severe AD) | Uncompetitive, voltage-dependent NMDA blockade; reduces excitotoxicity | Small symptomatic benefit; additive with donepezil in moderate–severe AD (Tariot 2004) [1] |
| **Anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies** | **Aducanumab** (accelerated approval 2021; controversial; discontinued 2024); **lecanemab** (Aβ protofibrils; full FDA approval 2023); **donanemab** (N3pG-Aβ plaque; FDA 2024; **launched in India 2026**) | Remove amyloid (plaque clearance on PET) | **CLARITY-AD:** CDR-SB decline slowed **27%** at 18 months (difference 0.45 points). **TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2:** iADRS decline slowed **22–35%** (greatest in low/medium tau) [1, 3] |
| Others | Brexpiprazole (FDA, agitation in AD, 2023); suvorexant (insomnia in AD); vitamin E, statins, NSAIDs, oestrogen, gingko (no benefit); tau-directed agents, anti-inflammatories (investigational) | Symptomatic or experimental | No course modification |

### Arguments FOR the statement
- **Anti-amyloid antibodies show biological and clinical disease modification:** large amyloid clearance, slowing on biomarkers (p-tau, NfL), and statistically significant slowing of clinical decline in phase 3 RCTs. This is the first such proof in AD history [1, 3].
- **Delayed-start analyses** and long-term extensions suggest the benefit **widens over time** (donanemab at 3 years, lecanemab open-label extension).
- **ChEIs and memantine** improve cognition, function and behaviour modestly, may **delay nursing-home placement** in observational data, and improve carer burden.
- Earlier diagnosis (biomarkers, blood tests such as plasma p-tau217) makes earlier, more effective treatment feasible.

### Arguments AGAINST
- **Effect size is small:** a 0.45-point CDR-SB difference (on an 18-point scale) is **below many estimates of the minimal clinically important difference** (about 1–2 points). Patients and carers may not notice it [1].
- **Only for a narrow population:** MCI or mild dementia with **amyloid confirmed by PET or CSF**. Most patients (moderate to severe, mixed or vascular dementia, no biomarker access) are ineligible.
- **Safety: ARIA** (oedema/effusion and microhaemorrhage) on MRI in **20–40%**, symptomatic in **3–10%**. Deaths have occurred. **APOE ε4 homozygotes** are at highest risk. Anticoagulants are contraindicated [1].
- **Burden:** IV infusions (fortnightly or monthly), serial MRIs, specialist monitoring.
- **Cost:** about **US$26,500/year** for lecanemab in the US [1]. In India, donanemab is priced at about ₹92,000 per 350 mg vial, with several vials per monthly dose, plus PET and MRI costs. It is unaffordable for most people.
- **Mortality and long-term outcomes** (institutionalisation, survival) have not been shown.
- **Aducanumab saga:** discordant trials (EMERGE positive, ENGAGE negative), accelerated approval, regulatory controversy, and withdrawal undermine confidence [1].
- **Amyloid hypothesis limitations:** many anti-amyloid trials failed (solanezumab, bapineuzumab, BACE inhibitors). Clearing amyloid helps only modestly, so tau, inflammation and vascular factors matter.
- **Symptomatic drugs:** Cochrane and NICE confirm benefit is modest. **AD2000** (Courtney 2004) found no effect on institutionalisation or disability progression with donepezil.

### Practical management today
1. **Diagnose early**, with biomarkers where available. Treat reversible contributors (B12, thyroid, depression, hearing loss, medications).
2. **Mild–moderate AD:** a ChEI. **Moderate–severe:** memantine, or ChEI + memantine.
3. **Early AD with amyloid confirmed, APOE genotyped, no contraindications, and MRI monitoring available:** consider an anti-amyloid antibody after shared decision-making.
4. **Non-pharmacological care:** cognitive stimulation therapy (benefit comparable to ChEIs), exercise, carer support, BPSD management, legal and financial planning [1].
5. **Vascular risk control** and multidomain prevention (FINGER).

**Conclusion.** For three decades, AD drugs were **symptomatic, not disease-modifying**: they did not alter the course. The anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab and donanemab) mark a **genuine but modest turning point**, slowing decline by about a quarter to a third in carefully selected early-stage patients, at significant risk, cost and monitoring burden. So drugs have **begun** to alter the course of AD, but not yet **significantly** for most patients. Prevention, early diagnosis and combination approaches targeting tau, inflammation and vascular pathology remain the future.

## In depth

### Background
- **1906:** Alois Alzheimer described "Auguste D".
- **1970s–80s:** the cholinergic hypothesis (Davies, Perry, Whitehouse: loss of nucleus basalis neurons).
- **Drug approvals:**
  - **1993:** tacrine (FDA).
  - **1996–2001:** donepezil (1996), rivastigmine (2000), galantamine (2001).
  - **2003:** memantine.
- **1992:** the amyloid cascade hypothesis (Hardy & Higgins).
- **2002–2020:** a long series of failed anti-amyloid drugs (AN1792 vaccine with meningoencephalitis; bapineuzumab; solanezumab; BACE inhibitors).
- **2021:** aducanumab accelerated approval → **2023** lecanemab full approval → **2024** donanemab approval; aducanumab discontinued [1].
- **2024:** the **Alzheimer's Association revised criteria** proposed a biological (biomarker-based) definition of AD (controversial).

### Epidemiology
- **Global:** about 57 million people with dementia (2021), 60–70% AD.
- **India (LASI-DAD 2023):** dementia prevalence about 7.4% in people over 60 (about 8.8 million). VaD makes up a higher proportion in India (up to about 30%) [1].
- **Implication:** an even smaller proportion are eligible for antibodies because of mixed pathology and diagnosis at later stages.

### Classification and criteria
- **NIA-AA 2011/2018** research framework (A/T/N); **IWG** criteria; **DSM-5-TR** major or mild NCD due to AD; **ICD-11** 6D80.
- **Stages for antibody eligibility:** MCI due to AD or mild AD dementia (MMSE about 22–30 in trials; CDR 0.5–1).

### Mechanism in detail
| Target | Rationale | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acetylcholine (ChEIs) | Cholinergic deficit correlates with cognition | Symptomatic |
| Glutamate (memantine) | Excitotoxicity | Symptomatic |
| Aβ protofibrils (lecanemab) | Soluble toxic species | Approved; modest |
| Aβ plaque, N3pG (donanemab) | Plaque clearance; stop dosing once cleared | Approved; modest |
| Tau (antisense, antibodies) | Tau correlates better with symptoms | Phase 2–3 |
| Neuroinflammation (TREM2, microglia) | Genetic risk (TREM2) | Investigational |
| GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide) | Metabolic and inflammatory | EVOKE trials negative (2025) |
| Synaptic / neurotrophic | — | Investigational |

### Clinical features in detail: what "27% slowing" means
- CDR-SB placebo decline about 1.66 vs lecanemab about 1.21 over 18 months.
- This equates to a **delay of about 5–7 months** of progression over 18 months, if the effect is sustained.
- **Patient-relevant outcomes** (ADCS-MCI-ADL, quality of life) showed small benefits.

### Assessment in detail: eligibility work-up for antibodies
- Clinical diagnosis of MCI or mild dementia.
- **Amyloid confirmation:** amyloid PET or CSF Aβ42/40, p-tau. Blood biomarkers are emerging.
- **APOE genotyping** (ARIA risk counselling).
- **Baseline MRI:** exclude more than 4 microbleeds, superficial siderosis, or extensive white matter disease.
- **Medication review:** no anticoagulants.
- **MRI monitoring** before the 2nd, 3rd, 4th and 7th infusions (varies by drug).

### Differential: what else to treat
Depression ("pseudodementia"), delirium, hypothyroidism, B12 deficiency, NPH, subdural haematoma, alcohol, medication-induced cognitive impairment, vascular dementia, DLB (ChEIs particularly useful; avoid antipsychotics), FTD (ChEIs not helpful).

### Management details
- **ChEI side effects:**
  - Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, anorexia, weight loss.
  - **Bradycardia, heart block, syncope**: check ECG and avoid in sick sinus syndrome.
  - Vivid dreams (give donepezil in the morning) [1].
- **Memantine:** dizziness, headache, confusion. Reduce the dose in renal impairment [1].
- **Switching:** if one ChEI is not tolerated, try another. Stop if there is no benefit or the patient is at end-stage, with careful review (discontinuation may cause decline).
- **Antibody ARIA management:** suspend dosing for symptomatic or moderate–severe ARIA; consider steroids for severe ARIA-E; stop permanently for ARIA-H with macrohaemorrhage.
- **BPSD:** non-drug first; antipsychotics carry stroke and mortality risk; citalopram for agitation (CitAD); brexpiprazole (FDA).

### Critical appraisal
- **Statistical vs clinical significance:** the central debate. Small effects across large trials, with possible functional unblinding from ARIA on MRI.
- **Generalisability:** trial participants were mostly white, educated, healthy and early-stage. Indian patients are under-represented, and vascular comorbidity is common in India [1].
- **Health economics:** ICER (US) and NICE (2024 draft) judged lecanemab and donanemab not cost-effective at current prices. **NICE did not recommend them for NHS use.** EMA initially refused lecanemab and then approved it for APOE4 non-homozygotes (2024–25).
- **Equity:** PET, MRI and infusion infrastructure is concentrated in urban private hospitals.
- **Hope vs hype:** antibodies prove the amyloid pathway is causally involved and open the door to combination and earlier (preclinical) prevention trials (AHEAD 3-45, TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3).

### Indian context
- **Availability:**
  - Donanemab (**Lormalzi**) was approved by CDSCO and launched by Lilly in India in **2026**, priced at about ₹92,000 per 350 mg vial [4].
  - Lecanemab's Indian availability should be checked.
  - Amyloid PET is available in only a few centres; blood biomarkers are emerging.
- **Standard care in India** relies on ChEIs (donepezil widely available and cheap) and memantine, BPSD management, and carer support (ARDSI). NPHCE geriatric clinics and DMHP are relevant.
- **Research:** LASI-DAD, the Srinivaspura Aging Neuro Senescence and COGnition (SANSCOG) study (CBR, IISc) and others are building Indian biomarker data.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** lecanemab and donanemab approvals [1].
- **Emerging:**
  - Subcutaneous lecanemab maintenance (FDA 2025).
  - Modified donanemab titration to reduce ARIA.
  - **Blood-based biomarkers** (p-tau217; FDA-cleared Lumipulse plasma test 2025).
  - Tau-targeting antisense (BIIB080).
  - Preclinical AD prevention trials.
  - Combination therapy.
- **Negative:** semaglutide (EVOKE, 2025) did not slow AD progression.
