## Exam answer

**Definition [1].**
- **Pain** (IASP, Merskey 2007): "an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with actual or potential tissue damage, or described in terms of such damage". It can therefore exist **without tissue damage** and is inherently subjective.
- **Persistent (chronic) pain** in the epidemiological study cited is constant or frequent pain **lasting at least 3 months** (Kennedy 2014).
- **Neuropathic pain** arises from a lesion or disease of the somatosensory system (Table 34–1).

### Epidemiology [1]
- Pain is the **commonest reason people see doctors**.
- About **19% of US adults** have persistent pain. It is commoner in **women**, people aged **60–69**, people with fair or poor self-rated health, the **overweight or obese**, and those hospitalised in the past year.
- Chronic pain patients use about **twice** the health-care resources. US costs in 2010 were **$560–635 billion**.

### Psychiatric comorbidity: the core of the answer [1]
| Domain | Key facts |
| --- | --- |
| **Depression** (bidirectional) | About **60%** of depressed patients report pain at diagnosis. In the WHO 14-country study, **69%** of depressed patients presented with **only somatic symptoms**, mostly pain. Depression gives about a **4-fold** rise in pain conditions. **One-third to over half** of pain-clinic patients have **current MDD**. **Depression predicts disability better than pain intensity or duration does**. Baseline depression predicts future pain better than baseline pain ratings. Depression with pain is **more treatment-resistant**, so treat it aggressively rather than "understanding" it away. |
| **Suicide** | Chronic pain patients die by suicide at **2–3 times** the general rate. Of patients on prescribed opioids, **36.5%** had suicidal ideation in the past year and **16.4%** a lifetime attempt. **Depression is the strongest predictor**. **Catastrophizing predicts ideation independently** of depression and pain severity. Risk is higher with headache or multiple pains. |
| **Anxiety and PTSD** | Anxiety **symptoms in almost half**; **up to 30%** have an anxiety disorder or PTSD. After severe accidents, **PTSD predicted later chronic pain** better than accident variables. In panic disorder, about two-thirds have current pain. Comorbid anxiety means worse outcomes. |
| **Fear-avoidance and catastrophizing** | Fear of pain, movement or reinjury leads to avoidance, then disuse (weight gain, muscle atrophy), then disability. This is the **fear-avoidance model** (Leeuw 2007) of the move from acute to chronic low back pain. Fear-avoidance is among the **strongest predictors of not returning to work**. **Catastrophizing** (amplifying threat) predicts disability from new low back pain and chronic pain after knee arthroplasty. |
| **Substance use** | Addiction in **3–48%** of chronic pain patients. Criteria: **loss of control**, **preoccupation despite adequate analgesia**, **adverse consequences**. **Aberrant medication behaviours in about 50%** of those on long-term opioids. Risk of a new SUD is highest in the **first 5 years** of pain, especially with past SUD or psychiatric illness. |
| **Pseudoaddiction** | Drug-seeking driven by **undertreated pain**, which settles once analgesia is adequate. ⚠️ The source adds that **there is no empirical evidence that pseudoaddiction is a separate entity**, so keep a high index of suspicion. |
| **Somatic symptom disorder** | The history runs DSM-II psychophysiological → DSM-III psychogenic pain → DSM-III-R somatoform pain → DSM-IV pain disorder → **DSM-5 SSD** (which no longer distinguishes explained from unexplained symptoms). DSM-IV pain disorder: lifetime **12%**, 6-month **5%**, F:M **2:1**. **Grey-matter loss** in prefrontal, cingulate and insular cortex argues against "purely psychogenic" pain; the label carries **mind–body dualism**. |
| **Sleep** | Reciprocal, but **sleep problems may influence pain more than pain influences sleep** (Finan 2013). |

### Assessment (as drawn from the chapter) [1]
- Pain rating scales, interpreted in light of mental disorders, distress, personality and **meaning attributed** to symptoms. There are special tools for older and cognitively impaired people.
- **Screen for:** depression and suicidality; anxiety and PTSD; catastrophizing and fear-avoidance; sleep; substance use.
- **Opioid risk:**
  - **before starting:** SOAPP, ORT, DIRE;
  - **during treatment:** **PADT** (analgesia, activity, adverse events, aberrant behaviour);
  - **for misuse:** **COMM**;
  - all are useful but have significant limitations.
- **Beliefs and coping:**
  - pain = injury, so avoid activity;
  - solicitous family responses;
  - self-efficacy, locus of control;
  - **acceptance**;
  - compensation and litigation, and the **economic and social rewards** of disability (associated with more disability and depression in back pain).

### Management [1]
**Principle:** the goal is **function and return to work**, not just pain scores.

**1. Treat psychiatric comorbidity first-class.**
- Treating depression often **reduces pain** independently of analgesia.

**2. Antidepressants** (analgesia is **independent of the antidepressant effect**; it works by boosting descending inhibition through 5-HT and NE reuptake blockade).
- **TCAs:**
  - the **most effective drugs for neuropathic pain**, and effective in headache;
  - effective in PHN, diabetic neuropathy, central post-stroke pain and post-mastectomy pain;
  - **not** in spinal cord injury, phantom limb or HIV neuropathy;
  - **nortriptyline is better tolerated than amitriptyline**;
  - analgesia comes at **lower doses and sooner**, though PHN and diabetic neuropathy have been treated at **100–250 mg/day**, and older patients are often under-dosed (average 23 mg/day);
  - titrate guided by serum levels.
- **SNRIs:**
  - **duloxetine:** diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia, osteoarthritis; recommended by Cochrane for neuropathic pain;
  - **venlafaxine:** post-breast-cancer neuropathic pain, migraine prophylaxis;
  - milnacipran: fibromyalgia;
  - SNRIs cannot be monitored by serum levels.
- **SSRIs:** inconsistent; **not first-line**, but worth considering when depression coexists.
- **Mirtazapine** helps chronic tension-type headache; bupropion and trazodone have limited data.

**3. Anticonvulsants.**
- NNT about **2–4** in neuropathic pain; better adherence than TCAs.
- **Carbamazepine** is the best studied, and first-line for **trigeminal neuralgia (NNT 1.8)**.
- **Gabapentin and pregabalin** (diabetic neuropathy, PHN, fibromyalgia, phantom limb, SCI pain) are **renally excreted**, so reduce the dose in renal impairment.
- Lamotrigine (HIV neuropathy, post-stroke pain); topiramate (migraine; causes cognitive impairment).

**4. Opioids.**
- **For:** moderate to severe pain lasting **more than 3 months** that impairs function.
- **Documentation:** record improvement in pain and function, without adverse effects or aberrant behaviour (the PADT covers analgesia, activities of daily living, adverse events and aberrant drug-related behaviours).
- **Stop:** **taper and stop** if risks outweigh benefits.
- **Specific risks:**
  - harm is dose-dependent; tolerance and opioid-induced hyperalgesia;
  - **methadone:** half-life **15–120 hours**, but **analgesia lasts only about 6 hours** (8–12 with repeated dosing); **QTc** prolongation;
  - **pethidine:** seizures and delirium.
- **Opioid-maintained patients** may need **up to 50% higher** doses for acute pain. **Undertreatment is commoner than diversion.**

**5. Avoid or limit.**
- **Benzodiazepines:** with opioids, **fatal overdose risk rises**. In chronic pain they are linked to less activity, more depression, more health-care use and more disability days.
- **Antipsychotics:** mixed add-on evidence; metabolic risk.

**6. Psychological therapy.**
- **Operant behavioural model** (Fordyce): reinforce healthy behaviour and extinguish pain behaviour.
- **CBT:** relaxation, imagery, biofeedback, hypnosis, cognitive restructuring, coping self-statements, aimed at **self-management**.
  - **Cochrane:** small effects on pain and disability; **moderate** effects on mood and catastrophizing; benefit lasts up to 6 months;
  - online and in-person are equally effective.
- **Acceptance** (activity engagement plus willingness to have pain) predicts adjustment independently of catastrophizing.

**7. Interdisciplinary rehabilitation.**
- Improves function even in severe disability.
- **Cochrane (chronic low back pain):** better than usual care for pain and disability.
- Helps **return to work** and lowers costs.

**Placebos:** using a placebo to "prove" pain is not real is **unethical and misleading**. A placebo response neither shows pain is psychogenic nor rules out benefit from active treatment.

**Conclusion.** Chronic pain is a **biopsychosocial** condition. [Certain, per the source] **Depression, not pain intensity, best predicts disability and suicide**, and catastrophizing and fear-avoidance drive chronicity. The psychiatrist's role is to:
- diagnose and treat depression, anxiety, PTSD and substance use;
- use **TCAs, SNRIs and gabapentinoids** for their analgesic effect;
- keep opioid therapy monitored and functional, and avoid benzodiazepine co-prescription;
- deliver **CBT within interdisciplinary rehabilitation** aimed at function and return to work.

## In depth

### Pain terms (Table 34–1) [1]
- **Allodynia:** pain from a non-painful stimulus.
- **Hyperalgesia:** exaggerated response to a painful stimulus.
- **Sensitization:** lowered threshold and prolonged response.
- **Deafferentation pain:** loss of sensory input.
- **Dysaesthesia:** unpleasant abnormal sensation. **Paraesthesia:** abnormal sensation that is not unpleasant.
- **Nociception:** detection through Aδ and C fibres.

### Acute pain in consultation [1]
- Treat early to reduce later analgesic need.
- The **absence of tachycardia, hypertension or sweating does not exclude pain**.
- Treat **anxiety and insomnia separately**; both amplify pain and analgesic requests.
- Consultation is typically requested when analgesic needs exceed expectations or there is a substance use history.

### Selected syndromes [1]
| Syndrome | Psychiatric and treatment points |
| --- | --- |
| **Post-herpetic neuralgia** | Pain at least 3 months after zoster. About 10% of zoster cases, but more than half of those over 65. Risk factors include **psychological distress**. About 25% still have pain at 1 year. First-line: anticonvulsants, TCAs, topical lidocaine. |
| **Diabetic neuropathy** | Up to 90% of diabetics. A **stronger predictor of depression than other diabetic complications**. Comorbid depression or anxiety means more medication and health-care use. TCAs, SNRIs, anticonvulsants, lidocaine, plus CBT and TENS. |
| **Central post-stroke and SCI pain** | Post-stroke 8%; SCI **60–70%**. Allodynia in more than 50% of post-stroke pain. Hard to treat: antidepressants, anticonvulsants, opioids; motor cortex stimulation, DBS or rTMS if refractory. |
| **Migraine** | Lifetime 18% of women, 6% of men. **Anxiety is more strongly linked to migraine risk than depression**, and the physical symptoms of depression more than the emotional ones. Prophylaxis: propranolol, metoprolol, flunarizine, valproate, topiramate, botulinum toxin; plus CBT and biofeedback. |
| **Chronic daily headache** | About 5% of the population. Includes transformed migraine, **medication-overuse headache**, **chronic tension-type headache**, new daily persistent headache and hemicrania continua. Overlaps with depression and anxiety. Amitriptyline, gabapentin, tizanidine, mirtazapine, topiramate, memantine, botulinum toxin. **Medication plus CBT beats either alone**. |
| **Fibromyalgia** | Widespread pain with poor sleep, cognitive difficulty, depression and fatigue. Cyclobenzaprine, milnacipran, gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, tramadol; education, physiotherapy, exercise. |
| **Phantom limb** | 40–80% within a year. TCAs, gabapentin and carbamazepine are "first-line" **without controlled trials**. Morphine, calcitonin and ketamine help short-term. |
| **CRPS** | About 7% after limb fracture or surgery. Mood **46%**, anxiety 27%, SUD 14%, generally considered **consequences, not causes**. Suicidal ideation is linked to pain severity, depression and low function. Multidisciplinary, function-focused care. |
| **Trigeminal neuralgia** | Insomnia, weight loss, withdrawal, depression, suicide if uncontrolled. Carbamazepine first-line. |
| **Temporomandibular disorder** | Linked to stress. No evidence comparing occlusal splints with psychological treatment. |
| **Low back pain** | Lifetime risk over 80%. **Poor function beyond 1 month is the strongest predictor of chronicity**. Depression gives a nearly 4-fold rise in seeking treatment for new low back pain. Short-term NSAIDs, physiotherapy, exercise, behavioural therapy. Opioids only short-term for severe flares. Antidepressants and anticonvulsants are uncertain. |

### Pharmacology details [1]
- **Opioids:**
  - morphine has poor oral bioavailability (22–48%); oxycodone is more than 60% bioavailable, releases less histamine and causes fewer hallucinations;
  - **fentanyl** is the most potent, with the highest overdose risk; transdermal effect lasts up to 72 hours;
  - **tramadol** weakly binds opioid receptors and weakly inhibits 5-HT and NE reuptake;
  - **constipation** is the commonest long-term side effect.
- **Topical agents:** lidocaine (PHN; weak evidence); high-dose capsaicin for musculoskeletal and neuropathic pain.
