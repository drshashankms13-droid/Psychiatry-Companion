> **Source discipline:** this answer uses only *Kaplan & Sadock's Comprehensive Textbook of Psychiatry*, 10th ed., §27.15 "Management of chronic pain" (Hobelmann, Clark) [1].

## Exam answer

**Definition [1].**
- **IASP:** pain is "an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with actual or potential tissue damage, or described in terms of such damage".
- Pain can therefore occur without tissue damage. It is subjective, and the patient's report should be accepted as valid unless there is evidence otherwise.
- **Chronic pain:** pain that persists daily for **a month beyond the expected healing time**. It serves no survival purpose and is harder to manage than acute pain.

**Classification [1]:**

| Type | Subtype | Quality | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nociceptive | Somatic (well localised) | Throbbing, aching, pressure | Arthritis, fibromyalgia, myofascial pain |
| Nociceptive | Visceral (poorly localised) | | IBS, endometriosis, interstitial cystitis |
| Neuropathic (lesion or disease of the somatosensory system) | Peripheral | Shooting, stabbing, electric | Peripheral neuropathy, PHN, trigeminal neuralgia, CRPS, phantom limb |
| Neuropathic | Central | | Post-stroke pain, SCI, MS, Parkinson disease |

- Over time, all chronic pain produces **central sensitisation**: changes in CNS function and structure that are hard to reverse.

**Epidemiology [1]:**
- Pain is the **commonest reason** patients see a physician. About 100 million US adults reported chronic pain in 2008.
- Commonest conditions: back pain, then headache, arthralgia, neck pain.
- Patients use about **twice** the health-care resources of the general population and lose 8 work days every 6 months. US costs ($560–635 billion in 2010) exceeded those of heart disease, diabetes or cancer.
- Women report more severe, frequent and longer-lasting pain. Prevalence is higher with low income, low education and unemployment.
- **Most reliable risk factors:** a previous episode of pain, and greater intensity of acute pain.

**Neurobiology relevant to psychiatry [1]:**
- **Peripheral sensitisation:** substance P and neurokinin A cause neurogenic inflammation and lower nociceptor thresholds.
- **Central sensitisation (dorsal horn):** windup, facilitation, lower thresholds, receptive-field expansion and long-term potentiation. The main mechanism is NMDA activation by glutamate.
- **Supraspinal:**
  - lateral (ventroposterior) thalamus carries the sensory–discriminative aspect;
  - medial thalamus and **cingulate cortex** carry the **affective–motivational** aspect.
- **Descending inhibition** (serotonin and noradrenaline) is the basis of antidepressant analgesia.
- **Imaging:** chronic pain reduces **grey matter** in pain-processing areas. This is a result of the pain and **reverses with successful treatment**.

### Psychiatric implications / comorbidity [1]
- **Up to two-thirds** of chronic pain patients have comorbid psychopathology. In order of frequency: **depression**, then anxiety disorders, somatoform disorders and substance use disorders.
- Comorbidity predicts a **poorer prognosis** and should be treated concurrently. **Personality vulnerabilities** can be a barrier to treatment.

**1. Depression:**
- The relationship is **bidirectional**:
  - about **60%** of depressed patients complain of pain;
  - depression carries about a **fourfold** higher prevalence of chronic pain;
  - baseline depression predicts **future pain and disability** better than initial pain ratings.
- **Up to 50%** of pain-clinic attenders have MDD. In chronic low back pain (LBP), the rate is more than **3 times** that of the general population.
- Depression adds:
  - more severe and persistent pain, more interference and more pain behaviour;
  - early retirement and daily analgesic or sedative use;
  - **nearly double** the health-care costs.
- Pain makes depression **more treatment-resistant**. Depression is not "expected" and should be treated **aggressively**. Treating it reduces pain and improves function, **independently** of pain treatment, because the neurobiology overlaps.

**2. Suicidality:**
- Suicidal ideation, attempts and completions are **2–3 times** higher in migraine, chronic abdominal pain and orthopaedic pain.
- Low self-efficacy leads to hopelessness.
- **Depression is the strongest and most consistent predictor**. **Catastrophising** predicts suicidality independently of pain severity and depression.

**3. Anxiety, fear and PTSD:**
- Anxiety symptoms occur in **almost half** of patients, and **up to 30%** meet criteria for an anxiety disorder (GAD, panic, agoraphobia, PTSD).
- **PTSD** is associated with more pain, distress, interference and disability.
- Pain occurs in **up to two-thirds** of patients with panic disorder.
- **Fear-avoidance model:** fear of pain, movement or reinjury leads to avoidance, disuse (weight gain, muscle atrophy) and disability.
  - It is reinforced by low self-efficacy and catastrophising, and is one of the strongest predictors of **failure to return to work**.
  - **Operant reinforcement** occurs when avoidance brings short-term gains.
- **Catastrophising:** rumination, magnification, pessimism and helplessness. It predicts poor adjustment **better than disease status**.

**4. Somatic symptom disorder / somatoform pain disorder:**
- DSM-5 SSD **abolished the medically explained vs unexplained distinction**, so more patients will now qualify.
- In somatoform pain disorder, pain is amplified by psychological factors. "Psychogenic pain without a real cause" is a **misconception**: grey-matter changes are demonstrable, and psychological and physical factors interrelate rather than being "either/or".
- Full SPD is uncommon in chronic pain; **multisomatoform (subsyndromal)** presentations are commoner.
- These patients:
  - believe doctors think the pain is imaginary;
  - catastrophise and fear a mysterious disease;
  - risk excess tests, inappropriate drugs and unnecessary surgery.

**5. Substance use disorder:**
- Severe SUD occurs in **7–20%** of patients with chronic pain. The US has 5% of the world's population but consumes **80%** of its opioids.
- **Aberrant medication-taking** occurs in about **50%** of patients on opioids, but only about **10%** of them have SUD.
- **Pseudoaddiction:** drug-seeking caused by undertreated pain. It **abates once pain is controlled**; addiction does not.
- Risk is highest in the **first 5 years** of treatment, and with a past SUD or psychiatric comorbidity.
- Synthesis: about **50%** show aberrant behaviour, **20%** abuse and **10%** become addicted.
- **Screening tools:** ORT, SOAPP, COMM (self-report, with limitations).
- **Safeguards:** opioid consent form, random toxicology, frequent follow-up, stepped-care integration.
- Conversely, patients with SUD often have pain that is **stigmatised and undertreated**.

### Psychiatric evaluation [1]
- **History:** quality, site, temporal pattern, aggravating and relieving factors, **functional interference**, and response to prior treatment.
- **Psychiatric history:** prior diagnoses, work history, legal history, substance use, **suicidality**.
- **Rating:** 0–10 numerical scale or **VAS** (10 cm), using the same scale over time; facial expression scales for children or cognitively impaired patients.
- Also assess function, emotional state, satisfaction and adverse effects, and distinguish neuropathic from nociceptive pain.
- Chronic pain usually lacks autonomic signs.
- **Imaging correlates poorly** with pain. More than 50% of asymptomatic people have spinal abnormalities, and early imaging in back pain does not improve outcome.

### Management [1]
**Aims:** find a cause, relieve suffering and **restore function**, using an **interdisciplinary biopsychosocial** approach.

**Pharmacological:**
- **Antidepressants:** analgesia is **independent of mood effect**, through 5-HT/NA descending inhibition, opioid, ion-channel and NMDA mechanisms.
  - **TCAs** are the **most effective agents for neuropathic pain**, and also help headache, central pain, PHN and post-mastectomy pain. They do **not** help SCI, phantom or HIV neuropathy pain. Nortriptyline is better tolerated than amitriptyline. Use low doses titrated to effect; serum levels can be monitored.
  - **SNRIs:**
    - duloxetine for fibromyalgia and diabetic neuropathy;
    - venlafaxine for neuropathic pain, atypical facial pain and migraine prevention;
    - milnacipran for fibromyalgia.
  - **SSRIs:** inconsistent evidence. Some benefit in fibromyalgia, IBS pain and diabetic neuropathy; **not first-line**.
  - **Others:** mirtazapine (tension headache), bupropion (neuropathic pain), trazodone (limited).
- **Anticonvulsants:**
  - carbamazepine is first-line for **trigeminal neuralgia**;
  - gabapentin and pregabalin for diabetic neuropathy, PHN, fibromyalgia, phantom and SCI pain;
  - lamotrigine for HIV neuropathy and post-stroke pain;
  - topiramate for migraine and LBP.
- **Antipsychotics:** increasing evidence across several pain types, offset by metabolic risk.
- **Benzodiazepines:** **not supported**. They are linked to less activity, more disability days and depression, and are dangerous with opioids (implicated in opioid deaths).
- **Simple analgesics and opioids:**
  - opioids are the cornerstone for cancer pain, but long-term benefit in non-malignant pain is unproven;
  - use them with a **function-based plan and exit strategy**, informed consent and monitoring.

**Psychological:**
- **Operant behavioural model (Fordyce):** pain behaviours are reinforced socially, including by doctors. The aim is to reinforce healthy behaviour.
- **CBT:** relaxation, biofeedback, imagery, cognitive restructuring, coping skills, motivational interviewing.
  - It reduces pain, pain behaviour, distress and depression, with benefits lasting **more than 6 months**.
  - A change in **perceived control** predicts response.
- **Third-wave therapies:** ACT, behavioural activation, DBT, MBCT, metacognitive therapy.
- **Target cognitions:**
  - raise self-efficacy and internal locus of control;
  - build **acceptance** of pain (linked to less pain, anxiety, depression and disability, and better work status);
  - replace passive or maladaptive coping.
- **Placebo:** a placebo response does **not** prove pain is psychogenic. Using a placebo to test whether pain is "real" is **dishonest and counterproductive**.

**Multidisciplinary rehabilitation:**
- Interdisciplinary pain rehabilitation programmes reduce pain, improve function and QoL, and **return patients to work**.
- Early failure of single-modality treatment harms later outcomes.

**Invasive treatments:** nerve blocks, spinal cord stimulation and surgery, for carefully selected patients. A **psychosomatic assessment** should precede invasive treatment in CRPS.

**Conclusion [1].**
- The split between pain from tissue (nociception) and pain from mind (suffering) is outdated.
- Psychiatric comorbidity is common, worsens outcome and is often neglected. A **psychiatric evaluation belongs in every comprehensive pain assessment**.
- Care should sit in a medical setting, so that patients feel the somatic side is not neglected.

## In depth

### Terms [1]
- **Allodynia:** pain from a normally non-painful stimulus.
- **Hyperalgesia:** an exaggerated response to a painful stimulus.
- **Hyperpathia:** an exaggerated response to any stimulus.
- **Dysaesthesia:** abnormal pain, spontaneous or evoked.
- **Paraesthesia:** an abnormal non-painful sensation.
- **Deafferentation pain:** pain from loss of sensory input.
- **Sensitisation:** a lower threshold and enhanced response.
- **Nociception:** detection of tissue damage, carried by Aδ and C fibres.

### Specific syndromes with psychiatric relevance [1]
- **Low back pain:**
  - depressed mood and somatisation predict the move to chronicity;
  - the **strongest predictor** is poor function 1 month after seeking care;
  - depression nearly **quadruples** treatment-seeking for new LBP;
  - economic and social rewards are linked to more disability.
- **Chronic daily headache:**
  - affects about 5% of people and is the commonest cause of lost US productivity;
  - linked to depression and anxiety, and to analgesic overuse with rebound headache;
  - **medication plus CBT is better than either alone**.
- **Migraine:**
  - lifetime prevalence 18% in women, 6% in men;
  - prophylaxis: β-blockers, flunarizine, valproate, topiramate, **CBT**.
- **Fibromyalgia:**
  - affects 2–8% of people;
  - central sensitisation with fatigue, poor sleep, cognitive difficulty and depression;
  - treated with muscle relaxants, membrane stabilisers, SNRIs and tramadol.
- **CRPS:**
  - mood, anxiety and substance disorders are common but are a **consequence, not a cause**;
  - graded motor imagery and graded exposure help;
  - assess psychosomatically before invasive treatment.
- **Phantom limb pain:** occurs in 50–80% of amputees; CBT and biofeedback have been tried.
- **Trigeminal neuralgia:** uncontrolled attacks raise the risk of anxiety, depression, insomnia, weight loss and withdrawal.
- **TMD:** linked to stress and responsive to behavioural treatment.
- **Burning mouth syndrome:**
  - mainly affects middle-aged and postmenopausal women;
  - linked to anxiety, depression, trauma and somatisation;
  - treated with TCAs, anticonvulsants and habit-awareness counselling.
- **Parkinson disease:** most patients have pain. It is often relieved by levodopa, which implies a dopaminergic role in pain modulation.
- **Central post-stroke pain / SCI pain:** under-reported, and overshadowed by the neurological deficits.

### Opioid pharmacology points [1]
- **Methadone:**
  - long and variable half-life (15–120 hours), so the risk of delayed overdose is higher;
  - QTc prolongation above 100 mg/day;
  - analgesia lasts only about 6 hours, although once-daily dosing prevents withdrawal.
- **Tramadol and tapentadol:** weak opioid agonists plus 5-HT/NA reuptake inhibition. They interact with SSRIs/SNRIs and warfarin.
- **Buprenorphine:** a partial μ agonist. It can precipitate withdrawal in patients on full agonists.
- **Opioid rotation:** cross-tolerance is incomplete, so the equianalgesic dose may fall by up to 50%.
