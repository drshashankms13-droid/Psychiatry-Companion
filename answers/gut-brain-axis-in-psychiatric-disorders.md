## Exam answer

### Introduction

The gut–brain axis is a bidirectional communication network linking the gastrointestinal tract and central nervous system through neural, endocrine, immune and metabolic pathways. The gut microbiota is an important modulator of this system.

### Components and pathways

-   **Neural:** vagus nerve, enteric nervous system and autonomic
    pathways.
-   **Endocrine:** HPA axis and gut-derived hormones.
-   **Immune:** cytokines, immune cells and intestinal barrier
    integrity.
-   **Metabolic:** microbial products such as short-chain fatty acids,
    bile-acid derivatives and tryptophan metabolites.
-   **Neuroactive signalling:** modulation of pathways involving
    serotonin, GABA and other neurotransmitter systems.

### Psychiatric relevance

Research has identified associations between gut–brain signalling and depression, anxiety, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and autism spectrum disorder. Potential mechanisms include inflammation, stress reactivity, altered intestinal permeability and changes in microbial metabolites. These associations remain under investigation and do not establish that microbiome abnormalities cause these disorders.

### Therapeutic implications

Dietary improvement, prebiotics, probiotics, synbiotics and microbiota-based interventions are being studied. FMT remains investigational for psychiatric indications and should not be presented as routine treatment.

### Limitations

Findings vary by population, diet, medication, geography and methodology. Causality and clinically useful microbial signatures remain uncertain.

### Conclusion

The gut–brain axis provides a promising framework for understanding interactions between bodily systems and mental health. At present, it is best regarded as an emerging research and potential adjunctive therapeutic domain.
