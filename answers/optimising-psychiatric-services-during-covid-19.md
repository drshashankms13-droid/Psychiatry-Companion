## Exam answer

**Introduction: why services needed re-design [1, 3].**
- **Lockdown** and **closure of in-person services** caused relapse and exacerbation in previously stable patients [1].
- People with psychiatric illness had **higher COVID mortality**, and more worry, anger, impulsivity and **intense suicidal ideation** during acute infection [1].
- **Survivors:** 34% (ward) and **46% (ICU)** received a neurological or psychiatric diagnosis within 6 months. Post-COVID fatigue, stigma and cognitive deficits added to the burden [1].
- **Healthcare workers** developed burnout, depression, anxiety, sleep and substance-use problems [1].
- **General population:** fear of infection, grief, depression, and social and economic chaos [3].

### Strategies to optimise services
**1. Telepsychiatry as the backbone [2]:**
- MoHFW issued the **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines on 25 March 2020**. NIMHANS, IPS and TSI followed with operational telepsychiatry guidelines. "Post-Covid, telemedicine is going to stay."
- **Advantages:** overcomes distance, travel cost and the concentration of specialists in cities. Usable with a smartphone; reaches far-flung areas; **narrows the treatment gap**. Psychotherapy can also be delivered online.
- **Safe prescribing by list:**
  - **List A** (relatively safe, low abuse potential: e.g. escitalopram, fluoxetine, imipramine, lithium, valproate, carbamazepine, haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine, trihexyphenidyl, clonazepam, clobazam). Prescribable at a **first consultation by video**; refills by any mode.
  - **List B** ("add-on", e.g. sertraline, clozapine, aripiprazole, quetiapine, anti-craving drugs, anti-dementia drugs): **follow-up only**, within 6 months of an in-person visit.
  - **List C**: **prohibited online** (Schedule X; NDPS drugs such as lorazepam and zolpidem).
  - Injectables only via a collaborating RMP or health worker.
- **Practice rules:**
  - verify identity and relationship;
  - assess **capacity** (MHCA s4);
  - follow any **advance directive** and nominated representative;
  - get explicit consent;
  - switch to in-person care for emergencies or suspected coercion.
- **Dementia:** if moderate–severe dementia and loss of capacity are documented, family follow-up may proceed without an authorisation letter.

**2. Consultation-liaison in COVID wards [1]:**
- **Flexible models:** MHPs in the ward round the clock, for fixed hours, on call in person, or **teleconsultation only** (audio or video). Discuss the plan with the primary team.
- **Screening and surveillance:** nursing and medical staff in COVID wards can screen and report to MHPs. Use **PHQ-9 / PHQ-2 / PHQ-4** and **GAD-7**; screen for **delirium**, sleep, suicidality and substance withdrawal.
- **Special populations:** children, elderly, peripartum women.
- **Advocacy:** people with mental disorders were **discriminated against for admission to COVID wards**. MHPs should facilitate their admission.
- **Liaison with families:** families often cannot stay, so keep in touch by **voice or video calls**, update them regularly, and use them for monitoring.
- **Monitoring:** stricter follow-up by teleconsultation, video or in person during admission or isolation.
- **Prescribing in COVID:**
  - **start low, go slow**;
  - consider oxygen needs, renal and hepatic function, coagulopathy and stroke;
  - watch **drug interactions** (e.g. QTc prolongation with COVID medicines plus antipsychotics) and hyponatraemia;
  - watch **respiratory depression with benzodiazepines**.
- **Breaking bad news** to families with compassion (SPIKES, ABCDE models).

**3. Post-COVID care [1]:**
- Follow up survivors for mood, anxiety, cognition and fatigue.
- Address **anticipated stigma** before they return to society.

**4. Healthcare-worker mental health [1]:**
- Awareness programmes; screening for **burnout**, stress and psychiatric morbidity; treatment per existing guidelines.

**5. Public mental health [1, 3]:**
- **Anti-stigma media campaigns** for COVID survivors [1].
- **National digital scale-up:** **Tele-MANAS**, announced in the **Union Budget 2022** after the pandemic's mental-health toll, provides **24×7 tele-mental-health counselling** in every state, linked to in-person care [3]:
  - nodal agencies: NIMHANS (service delivery), IIIT-B (IT);
  - integrated with eSanjeevani, Ayushman Bharat HWCs and ABDM.

**6. Adapting treatment protocols to reduce contact [4]:**
- **Clozapine:** blood monitoring was re-evaluated, with a proposal to move from **monthly to 3-monthly** testing after the first year in patients without neutropenia. Implementation showed **no rise in severe neutropenia**.
- **Olanzapine LAI:** **shorter post-injection observation periods** were used.
- **Opioid substitution:** **unsupervised (take-home) dosing** during the pandemic was a "positive experience" that changed practice.

### Challenges [1, 2]
- Digital divide and poor connectivity (audio-only consultations).
- Privacy and consent in home settings.
- **Prescribing limits** under the lists.
- Need for in-person care in emergencies.
- **Discrimination** against people with mental illness in COVID facilities.

**Conclusion.** Services were optimised by:
1. making **telepsychiatry mainstream** under clear prescribing and consent rules;
2. building **flexible CL models** in COVID wards with screening, family liaison and safe prescribing;
3. protecting **continuity of care** (reduced monitoring burdens, take-home OST);
4. looking after **healthcare workers** and fighting **stigma**;
5. scaling up nationally through **Tele-MANAS**.

## In depth

### Assessment checklist for a COVID-positive patient (CPG) [1]
| Domain | Content |
|---|---|
| History | Pre-existing disorders; current symptoms; past and current psychotropics (dose, adherence); substance use and withdrawal; past delirium; sleep; coping; frustration tolerance; personality; lifetime suicidal behaviour |
| Current | PHQ, GAD-7; cognition; delirium screen; sleep; suicidal ideation or plans; full MSE |
