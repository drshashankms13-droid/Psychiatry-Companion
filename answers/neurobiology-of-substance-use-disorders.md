## Exam answer

### Overview

SUD results from interactions between drug pharmacology, learning, stress, executive control, genetics, development, and environment. A useful model describes three overlapping stages: **binge/intoxication, withdrawal/negative affect, and preoccupation/anticipation**.

### Core circuits

1. **Reward:** VTA–nucleus accumbens pathways support reinforcement learning. Many drugs increase dopamine signalling directly or indirectly. Dopamine contributes to learning, motivation, salience, and prediction error, and not simply pleasure.
2. **Extended amygdala/stress:** CRF and noradrenergic systems contribute to withdrawal-related anxiety, irritability, and dysphoria. Use may recur through negative reinforcement (relief of distress).
3. **Prefrontal cortex:** Supports planning, inhibition, valuation, and flexible decisions. Stress, cue exposure, and repeated use can weaken control over substance-seeking responses.
4. **Hippocampus/amygdala:** Encode contextual and emotional memories; places, people, moods, and paraphernalia can trigger craving.
5. **Dorsal striatum:** Repeated behaviour may shift from goal-directed action toward habitual responding.
6. **Insula:** Integrates bodily states and subjective urges and may contribute to craving and withdrawal awareness.

### Neuroadaptation

Repeated exposure produces tolerance, withdrawal, altered receptor signalling, synaptic plasticity, and changes in reward/stress function. Mechanisms differ by substance: opioids act at opioid receptors; alcohol affects GABA, glutamate, and other systems; stimulants alter monoamines; nicotine activates nicotinic receptors; cannabinoids act through endocannabinoid systems.

### Vulnerability/development

Genetic variation is neither necessary nor sufficient. Early adversity, chronic stress, psychiatric illness, impulsivity, adolescent development, availability, and social context influence risk. Neurobiology is bidirectional: prior traits shape exposure, and exposure changes brain function.

### Clinical implications

Combine medication, psychosocial treatment, and environmental change. Cue management, coping skills, contingency management, motivational interventions, and relapse prevention address learning; medicines target withdrawal, craving, or reinforcement for particular substances. Neuroplasticity also supports recovery.

### Conclusion

SUD involves reward, stress, memory, habit, and executive-control systems. Neurobiology explains important aspects of craving and relapse but must be integrated with psychological and social determinants.
