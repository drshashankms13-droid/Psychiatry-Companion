**Vignette:** a 33-year-old woman with:
- 16 years of generalised tonic–clonic seizures (GTCS), preceded by an **aura of intense fear and déjà vu**;
- for the past 8 years, **continuous** persecutory and referential delusions and auditory hallucinations;
- **poor adherence** to treatment for both conditions.

## Exam answer

### 1. Most likely diagnosis [1]
- **Epilepsy:** **temporal lobe epilepsy (probably mesial)** with **focal seizures evolving to secondarily generalised tonic–clonic seizures**.
  - **Fear** (ictal fear; mesial temporal/amygdala) and **déjà vu** (dysmnestic) are classic TLE auras.
  - An aura before a GTCS means the GTCS is **secondarily generalised**, not primary generalised.
- **Psychosis:** **interictal schizophrenia-like psychosis of epilepsy** (psychotic disorder due to epilepsy).
  - **Continuous** for 8 years: not tied to individual seizures.
  - **Onset about 8 years after epilepsy onset**: interictal psychosis typically begins 10–15 years after epilepsy onset (mean 14 in Slater). The timing fits.
  - Phenomenology indistinguishable from schizophrenia (persecutory and referential delusions, auditory hallucinations).
- **Complicating factor:** **non-adherence**. Poor seizure control worsens both disorders; seizure severity is the main risk factor for psychosis.

### 2. Differential diagnosis of the psychosis [1, 2]

| Possibility | For / against in this case |
| --- | --- |
| **Interictal schizophrenia-like psychosis** | **Most likely**: chronic, continuous, years after onset |
| Primary schizophrenia co-occurring by chance | Possible (up to half of comorbid cases may be coincidental); management is similar |
| **Postictal psychosis** | Against: PIP is brief (days), follows clusters after a lucid interval, and is pleomorphic or affective. But **14–20% of PIP becomes chronic**, so ask about earlier postictal episodes |
| **Ictal psychosis / non-convulsive status** | Against: continuous for 8 years, presumably clear consciousness. Complex partial status shows fluctuating delirium |
| **Forced normalisation / alternative psychosis** | Ask whether psychosis worsens when seizures are controlled (e.g., after a new ASM) |
| **ASM-induced psychosis** | Review the drug history: levetiracetam, topiramate, vigabatrin, tiagabine, perampanel. Usually resolves on withdrawal |
| Aura misinterpreted as psychosis | Fear and déjà vu are ictal, brief and stereotyped. Delusional elaboration of aura content is possible (Pond) |
| Other organic causes | Structural lesion (tumour, dysplasia), encephalitis sequelae |

### 3. Assessment [1, 2]
**History (patient and informant):**
- **Seizures:**
  - aura sequence, automatisms, dystonic posturing, postictal confusion or dysphasia;
  - frequency, clustering, status;
  - febrile convulsions in childhood (hippocampal sclerosis);
  - head injury, encephalitis.
- **Temporal relation of psychosis to seizures:**
  - lucid intervals after clusters (PIP);
  - changes with seizure control (forced normalisation).
- **Drug history:** every ASM and antipsychotic, response, adverse effects, levels.
- **Reasons for non-adherence:**
  - **side-effects**;
  - **lack of insight** (from psychosis);
  - cognitive problems;
  - stigma;
  - practical barriers.
- **Risk:**
  - **suicide**: epilepsy carries an SMR of 3.5, a psychiatric history ninefold, and adolescent onset a higher risk;
  - SUDEP and injury with uncontrolled GTCS;
  - risk to others.
- **Family history** of psychosis or epilepsy.
- **Social:** driving, employment, marriage, pregnancy plans (a woman of child-bearing age).

**Mental state:**
- first-rank symptoms, insight;
- depression (common comorbidity);
- **cognition** (left TLE gives verbal memory deficits).

**Investigations:**
- **EEG** (sleep-deprived if needed) and **video-EEG telemetry** if the diagnosis is doubtful or for surgical work-up;
- **MRI** (coronal hippocampal views): **hippocampal sclerosis**, dysplasia, tumour;
- **ASM levels** (adherence and toxicity);
- FBC, LFTs, U&E (sodium), **folate**, prolactin, metabolic baseline before antipsychotics.

### 4. Management [1, 2]
**Principles:**
- **multidisciplinary, joint care with neurology**;
- treat both conditions together; neither in isolation.

**(a) Engagement and adherence:**
- psychoeducation for patient and family about both illnesses and their interaction;
- simplify regimens; once-daily drugs where possible;
- address side-effects; supervised medication; epilepsy nurse specialist.
- **Long-acting injectables:**
  - Lishman: **avoid depots if possible**, because they cannot be withdrawn quickly if seizures worsen [1];
  - Maudsley: "depots need extreme care" [2];
  - ⚠️ given this patient's non-adherence, weigh this risk explicitly.

**(b) Seizure control (the foundation):**
- **Carbamazepine or lamotrigine** for focal epilepsy (SANAD: lamotrigine had fewer treatment failures).
- Avoid ASMs with psychiatric liability (levetiracetam, topiramate, vigabatrin).
- Note **interactions**: **carbamazepine induces enzymes and lowers many antipsychotic levels**. **Do not combine carbamazepine with clozapine.**
- **Refractory (two ASMs failed):** refer for **epilepsy surgery evaluation**. A psychiatric history is not a contraindication, but:
  - **preoperative psychosis usually persists** even if seizures stop;
  - **postictal psychosis implies bilateral disease**;
  - comorbid dissociative seizures must be excluded.

**(c) Antipsychotic treatment (treat as schizophrenia):**
- Choose **low seizure-risk** drugs:
  - **risperidone, haloperidol, sulpiride, trifluoperazine** [1];
  - **amisulpride/sulpiride, aripiprazole, risperidone** [2].
- **Start low, go slow.**
- **Avoid** chlorpromazine (high dose) and loxapine.
- **Clozapine** for treatment resistance:
  - start in hospital;
  - replace carbamazepine;
  - **lamotrigine is the ASM of choice** alongside clozapine [1, 2].
- Monitor for seizure exacerbation. If it occurs, adjust the ASM or change the antipsychotic.

**(d) Comorbidity:**
- treat depression (SSRIs safe; ECT not contraindicated);
- monitor suicide risk.

**(e) Women's health:**
- contraception: **enzyme-inducing ASMs reduce oral contraceptive efficacy** [2];
- pre-conception counselling;
- **avoid valproate** in women of child-bearing potential [2];
- folic acid.

**(f) Psychosocial:**
- rehabilitation, employment;
- driving advice (seizure-free period required);
- carer support;
- stigma.

**Prognosis** [1]:
- interictal psychosis outcome is broadly similar to schizophrenia (one-third remit, one-third improve; often chronic);
- seizure control improves quality of life only if seizure-free.

**Conclusion.** This is **TLE with secondarily generalised seizures** complicated by a **chronic interictal schizophrenia-like psychosis** and non-adherence. After excluding postictal, ictal, alternative and drug-induced psychoses, management combines:
- optimal ASM treatment (lamotrigine or carbamazepine; surgical evaluation if refractory);
- a low-seizure-risk antipsychotic (clozapine with lamotrigine if refractory);
- adherence support, attention to interactions, contraception and suicide risk;
- joint neuropsychiatric follow-up.

## In depth

### Why the aura matters [1]
- **Ictal fear** is often linked to **non-dominant mesial temporal** foci spreading to orbitofrontal and cingulate cortex.
- Patients may **incorporate aura experiences into delusions** (Slater).
- **Experiential auras** are over-represented in psychotic epileptics (three studies).
