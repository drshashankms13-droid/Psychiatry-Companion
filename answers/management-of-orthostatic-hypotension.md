## Exam answer

### Definition and importance [1]
- **Orthostatic (postural) hypotension:** usually a **fall of 20 mmHg or more systolic and/or 10 mmHg or more diastolic within 3 minutes of standing**.
- **Common** cardiovascular adverse effect of **antipsychotics and some antidepressants**; usually **acute during titration**, but can be **chronic**.
- **Consequences:** poorer quality of life; **falls**; cardiovascular disease, **depression and death**.
- **Symptoms:**
  - dizziness, light-headedness, weakness (asthenia), headache, visual disturbance;
  - patients may **under-report**, and subjective dizziness **correlates weakly** with the measured drop, so **confirm with BP measurement** on standing.

### Causes relevant to psychiatry and old age [1, 2]
**Drugs** [1]:
- **Antipsychotics with high α1-adrenergic affinity:**
  - **quetiapine (27%)**, **clozapine (24%)**, iloperidone (19.5%);
  - **low-potency phenothiazines** (chlorpromazine);
  - lowest: **lurasidone and asenapine (under 2%)**.
- **Tricyclic antidepressants** and **MAOIs** (postural hypotension); **trazodone** (dose-limiting in the elderly).
- **Antidepressants in older people**: postural hypotension and falls, with consequences worsened by SSRI-related osteopenia.
- **Antihypertensives** (α-blockers, β-blockers, diuretics; the **number** of antihypertensives may matter more than the class).

**Patient factors** [1, 2]:
- **old age** (the young get tachycardia instead);
- **dehydration**; **cardiovascular disease**;
- **autonomic dysfunction**: **Parkinson's disease** [1]; **dementia with Lewy bodies** (OH and unexplained loss of consciousness) [2];
- **Addison's disease**: OH with postural dizziness in **90%**, with **salt craving** [2];
- Parkinson's disease patients are particularly susceptible to **TCA-induced OH** [2].

**Treatment factors** [1]:
- **IM administration** (rapid, high peaks); **rapid dose increases**;
- **antipsychotic polypharmacy**;
- interactions (TCAs, antihypertensives).

### Assessment [1, 2]
1. **BP measurement** to confirm (a fall within 3 minutes of standing) [1].
2. **BP at baseline** and **frequently during titration and after dose changes** of antipsychotics (Table 1.6) [1].
3. **Medication review** (psychotropic and antihypertensive load).
4. Look for **dehydration**, cardiovascular disease and **autonomic disorders** (PD, DLB) [1, 2]. Consider **adrenal insufficiency** [2].
5. In dizziness after head injury, **exclude OH** as a cause of syncopal feelings [2].
6. **Falls risk.**

### Management [1]
**1. Minimise the risk of treatment (Table 1.41):**
- **start low and titrate slowly** according to tolerability: **most people develop tolerance** to the hypotensive effect;
- **temporary dose reduction** if OH develops;
- **reduce peak levels**: smaller, more frequent doses or **modified-release** preparations;
- avoid polypharmacy and rapid IM loading where possible;
- **switch** if postural hypotension is symptomatic (Table 1.6). Lower-risk antipsychotics (Table 1.48):
  - **suggested:** amisulpride, aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, cariprazine, lurasidone;
  - **alternatives:** haloperidol, sulpiride, trifluoperazine;
- **avoid TCAs in dementia** (falls via OH; anticholinergic cognitive harm) [1, p. 673].

**2. Non-pharmacological measures:**
- **rise slowly**: sit on the edge of the bed for several minutes before standing, especially in the morning;
- **abdominal binders and compression stockings**;
- **fluids 1.25–2.5 L/day** if not fluid-restricted.

**3. Pharmacological** (only with a compelling need for the causative drug, e.g., **clozapine**, and failure of other measures):
- **sodium chloride** supplements (may help **antidepressant-induced** OH);
- **fludrocortisone**: used for **clozapine-induced OH** when other measures fail. **Monitor electrolytes and BP**; **not for long-term use**;
- **midodrine** (an **α1 agonist**): one small case series. Linked to **acute dystonia with antipsychotics**. Considered **second line** for clozapine-induced OH or where fludrocortisone is contraindicated or poorly tolerated;
- **etilefrine**: benefit shown but an **unfavourable risk–benefit** profile, so not recommended. Other sympathomimetics **lack evidence** in psychotropic-induced OH.

**4. Treat the underlying cause** where present (e.g., hormone deficiency in Addison's disease: *treatment details are not in the source*) [2].

**Conclusion.** Orthostatic hypotension (a fall of 20/10 mmHg or more within 3 minutes of standing) is common with α1-blocking psychotropics (clozapine, quetiapine, chlorpromazine, TCAs, trazodone), especially in older people, those with Parkinson's disease or DLB, and those who are dehydrated or on antihypertensives. It causes falls, morbidity and death. Management:
- confirm with BP measurement on standing;
- review medication;
- titrate slowly (tolerance usually develops), divide doses or use modified-release forms;
- switch to low-α1 drugs (amisulpride, aripiprazole, lurasidone);
- use simple measures (rising slowly, stockings or binders, 1.25–2.5 L fluid);
- only for a compelling drug such as clozapine: salt, fludrocortisone, then midodrine (beware dystonia).
