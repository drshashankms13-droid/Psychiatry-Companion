## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- Eating disorders have the **highest mortality rate among psychiatric disorders** [2].
- In AN, life-threatening medical complications can arise from **electrolyte disturbance and inadequate nutrition** [1].

### 1. Cardiac [1]
- Patients are at **increased risk of arrhythmias**, secondary to electrolyte disturbance and the cardiac complications of inadequate nutrition.
- Put an **alert in the prescribing record**.
- Avoid **QT-prolonging** drugs where possible; do an **ECG** if a cardiotoxic drug is essential.

### 2. Electrolyte and metabolic [1]
- **Hypokalaemia** and other electrolyte disturbances may be **asymptomatic and develop slowly**.
- **Re-feeding:**
  - electrolyte disturbances often resolve with re-feeding, but **rapid correction is hazardous**;
  - it can cause **re-feeding-precipitated hypophosphataemia**.
- **Monitoring:** U&Es, **bicarbonate, calcium, phosphate, magnesium**, and ECG.
- **Relative hypercholesterolaemia** [2].
- **Vitamin deficiencies:**
  - multivitamin and multimineral supplements are recommended; **vitamin D** may be needed [1];
  - **thiamine:** AN is a recognised cause of **Wernicke's encephalopathy**, and re-feeding without vitamin supplementation can precipitate it [3, 4].

### 3. Endocrine and reproductive [2]
- **Hypothalamic hypogonadism**, causing **menstrual irregularity or amenorrhoea**.
- **Low IGF-1.**
- **Decreased leptin, ghrelin and peptide YY.**
- Similar changes have been noted in BN in limited studies.
- **Treatment:** pulsatile GnRH for hypothalamic amenorrhoea; oestrogen.
- **Differential diagnosis:** hyperprolactinaemia and pituitary disease can resemble AN, and vice versa (Lishman). Amenorrhoea is a presenting feature of both [4].

### 4. Bone [1, 2]
- **Bone loss and osteoporosis:** a major complication with serious consequences. Decreased bone mass and **impaired bone microarchitecture** [2].
- **Treatment:**
  - limited and conflicting evidence for oestrogen, DHEA, combined oral contraceptives and bisphosphonates;
  - for long-term low weight with low bone mineral density, **17β-oestradiol with cyclic progesterone** (or oestrogen at 13–17 years), and **bisphosphonates** for women over 18 [1].
- **Prolactin-raising antipsychotics** (risperidone, amisulpride, sulpiride) **worsen bone loss** and should be avoided. SSRIs can sometimes raise prolactin [1].

### 5. Immune and inflammatory [2]
- **Increased:** TNF-α, IL-2, TGF-β. **Decreased:** IFN-γ.
- Low-grade inflammation with raised TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β.

### 6. Gastrointestinal and microbiome [2]
- **Bacteroidetes are reduced** in underweight AN.
- **Starvation stress and high cortisol increase intestinal permeability.**

### 7. Neurological and brain [2, 4]
- **White matter:** abnormalities in thalamocortical tracts, the left superior longitudinal fasciculus and the inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus.
- **Grey matter:** decreased density in the anterior cingulate, frontal operculum and precuneus [2].
- **Wernicke's encephalopathy** (thiamine) [3, 4].

### 8. Related to substance and medication misuse [1]
- Patients may misuse **laxatives, diuretics, ADHD stimulants, slimming pills, semaglutide and caffeine** to control weight.
- Time medicines with **purging** in mind.

### Management principles [1]
- Weight restoration by careful **re-feeding**, with oral supplementation and **close biochemical and ECG monitoring**.
- Avoid drugs that compromise cardiac function or raise prolactin.
- **Bupropion is contraindicated** in AN.

## In depth

### Medicines with physical risks in AN [1]
| Issue | Avoid or caution |
|---|---|
| QT and arrhythmia | QT-prolonging drugs; ECG if essential |
| Osteoporosis | Risperidone, amisulpride, sulpiride; monitor prolactin with SSRIs |
| Seizures and weight loss | Bupropion is contraindicated |
| Over-rapid correction | Re-feeding hypophosphataemia |
