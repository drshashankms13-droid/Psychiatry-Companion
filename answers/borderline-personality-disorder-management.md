## Exam answer

**Definition.** Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is a pervasive pattern of **instability of affect, self-image and interpersonal relationships with marked impulsivity**, beginning by early adulthood. DSM-5 requires **5 of 9** features: frantic efforts to avoid abandonment; unstable, intense relationships (idealisation–devaluation); identity disturbance; impulsivity in two or more self-damaging areas; recurrent suicidal behaviour or self-harm; affective instability; chronic emptiness; intense anger; transient stress-related paranoia or dissociation [1]. ICD-10 calls it emotionally unstable personality disorder, borderline type; **ICD-11** records a personality disorder by severity with an optional **borderline pattern** qualifier [2].

### Key facts

- Prevalence about **2%** in the general population, **10%** of outpatients, **20%** of inpatients; about **75%** of diagnosed cases are women (DSM-5) [1].
- Suicide mortality is estimated at **50 times** the general population [3].
- **Course:** instability and suicide risk peak in young adulthood and wane in the 30s–40s; the diagnosis is more common in younger samples, consistent with natural maturation and remission [1].

### Mechanism

**Linehan's biosocial model:** emotional vulnerability (high sensitivity, high reactivity, slow return to baseline) × **invalidating environment** → pervasive **emotion dysregulation** → self-harm, impulsivity, anger and unstable relationships as attempts to regulate emotion [3]. Childhood sexual abuse is reported by 40–70% but is neither necessary nor sufficient (meta-analytic r = 0.28) [3]. The **mentalizing model** (Bateman and Fonagy): under attachment stress, the capacity to understand one's own and others' mental states collapses [4]. BPD is about **five times** more common in relatives of probands [1].

### Assessment

- Detailed history including **collateral**; longitudinal pattern, not a single crisis.
- **Risk assessment at every contact** — suicide, self-harm, substance use, risky sex, violence.
- Comorbidity: depression, substance use, bulimia, PTSD, ADHD, other personality disorders [1].
- Differentiate from bipolar disorder, recurrent depression, complex PTSD and identity problems of adolescence [1].
- Structured tools: SCID-5-PD, Zanarini Rating Scale for BPD, McLean Screening Instrument.

### Management principles

1. **Psychotherapy is the primary treatment**; no drug is approved for BPD [3].
2. Clear diagnosis and **psychoeducation** — explain that BPD is common, understandable and treatable.
3. **Structured, consistent care** with one coordinating clinician; agreed **crisis plan**.
4. **Avoid iatrogenic harm:** prolonged admissions, polypharmacy, frequent changes of therapist, punitive responses to self-harm.
5. Clinicians watch for **countertransference**, avoid giving direct advice, and do not believe the "untreatable" myth [1].

### Psychotherapies with evidence

| Therapy | Core idea | Format | Key evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT)** | Balance **acceptance and change**; skills for emotion regulation | Weekly individual + group skills training + phone coaching + therapist consultation team; about 1 year | First RCT 1991; more than 10 RCTs; Cochrane (2012) — most supported treatment; greatest effect on **suicidal and self-harming behaviour** [3] |
| **Mentalization-based treatment (MBT)** | Strengthen mentalizing within attachment relationships | Partial hospital or outpatient; individual + group; about 18 months | Partial hospital RCT: NNT about 2; at **8-year follow-up** suicidality 23% vs 74%, BPD diagnosis 13% vs 87% [4] |
| Transference-focused psychotherapy | Integrate split object representations | Twice-weekly individual | RCT evidence |
| Schema therapy | Change early maladaptive schemas | Individual or group | RCT evidence |
| STEPPS, Good Psychiatric Management | Systems training; generalist, case-management model | Group / outpatient | Effective, less resource-intensive |

### Pharmacotherapy (adjunctive only)

- Target **symptom domains**, not the disorder: **mood and anxiety dysregulation** (SSRIs/SNRIs); **impulsive aggression and affective lability** (lithium, lamotrigine, valproate, topiramate); **cognitive-perceptual symptoms** (low-dose second-generation antipsychotics, e.g. aripiprazole, quetiapine) [1].
- **Treat comorbid disorders** (major depression, PTSD, ADHD, substance use) on their own merits.
- Prescribe **time-limited**, **single** agents; review and stop if ineffective; avoid **benzodiazepines** (disinhibition, dependence) [1]; limit quantities of drugs dangerous in overdose (TCAs, lithium).

### Crisis management and admission

- Brief crisis contact; validate distress; collaborative safety plan; involve family with consent.
- **Short, goal-directed admission** only for acute suicidal risk or comorbid severe illness; avoid long admissions, which increase regression and self-harm.

**Conclusion.** BPD is a disorder of emotion regulation and mentalizing with a better long-term course than once believed. Management rests on a clear diagnosis, structured evidence-based psychotherapy — DBT and MBT have the strongest evidence — consistent case management and crisis planning, with medication used sparingly for specific symptom domains and comorbidity.

## In depth

### Background

- **Stern (1938)** described a "border line group"; **Kernberg (1975)** defined **borderline personality organisation** — identity diffusion, primitive defences (splitting, projective identification) and intact but fragile reality testing [1].
- Gunderson and others operationalised BPD, leading to its inclusion in DSM-III (1980).
- **Linehan** developed DBT in the late 1970s–1980s for chronically suicidal women; Bateman and Fonagy developed MBT in the 1990s [3, 4].
- ICD-11 initially proposed dropping BPD; the **borderline pattern** qualifier was retained for continuity of care and access to specific psychotherapies [1, 2].

### Epidemiology and course

- 30–60% of patients with personality disorders meet BPD criteria [1].
- Prospective cohorts (McLean Study of Adult Development, Collaborative Longitudinal Personality Disorders Study) show symptomatic remission in most patients within 10 years, with functional recovery lagging behind (author's knowledge).
- Physical and sexual abuse, neglect, hostile conflict and early parental loss are more common in histories of people with BPD; first-degree relatives have more antisocial personality disorder, substance use and mood disorders [1].

### Mechanism in detail

| Model | Core deficit | Treatment target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biosocial (Linehan) | Emotion dysregulation from vulnerability × invalidation | Skills, validation, behavioural change [3] |
| Mentalizing (Bateman & Fonagy) | Loss of mentalizing under attachment stress | Restore mentalizing in the therapeutic relationship [4] |
| Object relations (Kernberg) | Splitting, identity diffusion | Transference interpretation [1] |
| Psychobiological (Cloninger) | High novelty seeking + harm avoidance, low self-directedness and cooperativeness | Character development [1] |

DBT's model groups BPD criteria into five dysregulation domains: **emotion** (affective instability, anger), **behaviour** (impulsivity, self-injury), **cognition** (dissociation, paranoia), **interpersonal** (chaotic relationships, fear of abandonment) and **self** (identity disturbance, emptiness) [3].

### Clinical features in detail

- Associated features: undermining oneself close to a goal; feeling safer with pets or objects than people [1].
- Stress-related psychotic-like experiences (brief hallucinations, ideas of reference, body-image distortions) [1].
- Impairment: frequent job loss, interrupted education, broken relationships [1].

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
| --- | --- |
| Bipolar disorder / cyclothymia | Mood episodes last days–weeks, with sleep and energy change; BPD mood shifts last hours and are reactive [1] |
| Recurrent depression | Episodic; lacks abandonment fears, identity disturbance, chronic emptiness [1] |
| Complex PTSD | Stable negative self-concept and avoidance of relationships rather than unstable ones; required trauma exposure |
| Histrionic, narcissistic, antisocial PD | Attention-seeking, grandiosity, exploitation respectively; overlap common [1] |
| ADHD | Lifelong inattention and hyperactivity from childhood |
| Substance-induced states | Temporal link |
| Identity problems of adolescence | Limited to a developmental stage [1] |

### Management details

**DBT treatment hierarchy:** (1) life-threatening and self-injurious behaviour; (2) therapy-interfering behaviour; (3) quality-of-life-interfering behaviour; (4) skills acquisition [3]. Four stages: behavioural control → emotional experiencing (including trauma work) → ordinary problems of living → completeness and joy [3].

**MBT structure:** a phase of assessment and psychoeducation (MBT-I), then weekly individual and group sessions with a "not-knowing" stance, focus on current mental states, and avoidance of over-interpretation [4].

**Pharmacotherapy by symptom domain** (adapted from Table 26–24 of K&S) [1]:

| Domain | Preferred | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emotional lability | Lithium, lamotrigine, valproate | TCAs |
| Affective aggression | Lithium, SSRIs, anticonvulsants | Benzodiazepines (disinhibition) |
| Chronic anxiety | SSRIs | Benzodiazepines (abuse) |
| Cognitive–perceptual symptoms | Low-dose atypical antipsychotic | Long-term use without review |

NICE guidance (UK) advises against using drugs specifically for BPD or its individual symptoms outside crisis or comorbidity (author's knowledge).

### Evidence table

| Study | Design | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linehan et al., 1991 | First DBT RCT | DBT superior to treatment as usual in women with BPD [3] |
| Linehan et al., 2006 | RCT vs expert community therapy | Halved suicide attempts (author's knowledge) |
| Meta-analysis of 16 studies (8 RCTs) | DBT | Largest effects on suicidal and self-injurious behaviour [3] |
| Bateman & Fonagy, partial hospital RCT | 18-month MBT | Large benefit; NNT about 2; gains increased during follow-up [4] |
| Bateman & Fonagy, 8-year follow-up | — | Suicidality 23% vs 74%; still diagnosed 13% vs 87% [4] |
| Outpatient MBT RCT | vs structured clinical management | MBT superior, especially in long term and in patients with more PD diagnoses [4] |
| Adolescent MBT RCT | Self-harming adolescents | Recovery 44% vs 17% [4] |

### Critical appraisal

- Most RCTs are small, conducted by treatment developers, with short follow-up and high dropout.
- Head-to-head comparisons of DBT, MBT, TFP and schema therapy show **similar outcomes**; common factors (structure, coherent model, therapist support) may matter more than brand.
- Pharmacological trials are small and inconsistent; antidepressants are widely prescribed with little evidence.
- Diagnostic reliability for BPD has been low (kappa 0.34 in one study cited by K&S) [1].
- Stigma among clinicians ("manipulative", "attention-seeking") harms care and is itself a target for training [3].

### Indian context

- BPD is under-diagnosed in India; presentations often come through emergency services after self-harm, or are labelled depression or "hysteria".
- Families are central — family psychoeducation and involvement in DBT skills (as in DBT for adolescents) suit Indian settings.
- Structured psychotherapy services are scarce; **Good Psychiatric Management** and group-based DBT skills are scalable options.
- The **Mental Healthcare Act 2017** (Section 115) presumes severe stress in attempted suicide and requires the government to provide care; the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023** has no general offence of attempted suicide (author's knowledge).

### Recent advances

- DBT skills training alone is being tested as a transdiagnostic treatment for emotion dysregulation [3].
- MBT is being extended to antisocial and avoidant personality disorders, eating disorders and adolescents [4].
- ICD-11 severity-based diagnosis is expected to change how BPD is recorded and studied [2].
