## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 10-year-old boy presents with **nocturnal enuresis**. He is also:
- **absent-minded** at school, **frequently misplacing pencils and sharpeners**;
- **poor in studies**, with **consistently low marks in mathematics and science**;
- **likes playing with younger children**.

### Provisional formulation
This is a **cluster of neurodevelopmental and elimination problems**. The principal hypotheses (which may co-exist) are:
1. **Intellectual disability (mild) or borderline intellectual functioning.**
   - Global academic weakness, especially in abstract subjects (maths, science).
   - **Preference for younger playmates**, suggesting social and mental age below chronological age.
   - Enuresis persisting at 10.
   - Inattention "better explained by a lack of comprehension" [1, 2].
2. **ADHD, predominantly inattentive presentation.**
   - Forgetfulness, losing things, distractibility.
   - Enuresis is **commoner in ADHD** [general knowledge].
   - Symptoms must be "**inappropriate for mental age**" if ID is present [1].
3. **Specific learning disorder with impairment in mathematics (dyscalculia).**
   - Consistently poor maths and science, if IQ is average or above (at least 85, as used for certification) [3].
4. **Nocturnal enuresis:**
   - **primary** (never dry for 6 months) or **secondary** (relapse after at least 6 months dry, suggesting a stressor or medical cause);
   - **monosymptomatic** or **non-monosymptomatic** (daytime symptoms) [general knowledge].
   - **DSM-5-TR:** voiding into bed or clothes at least twice a week for 3 months, or with distress or impairment, at **chronological or developmental age 5 or older**, not due to a substance or medical condition [general knowledge].

**Also consider:**
- **ASD** (preference for younger children, social immaturity);
- **absence epilepsy** ("absent-minded"; also nocturnal seizures causing enuresis);
- **obstructive sleep apnoea** (causes enuresis, inattention and poor school performance);
- **hypothyroidism**;
- **diabetes mellitus or insipidus** (polyuria);
- **UTI**, constipation;
- spina bifida occulta;
- **sensory deficits** (vision, hearing);
- **depression or anxiety** (secondary enuresis; poor concentration);
- **child abuse** (secondary enuresis);
- **environmental and educational deprivation**; medium-of-instruction mismatch [3].

### (a) Evaluation
**1. Enuresis history:**
- primary or secondary; frequency; **daytime symptoms** (urgency, frequency, dribbling, holding manoeuvres);
- **constipation or encopresis**;
- fluid intake; nocturia; snoring;
- family history (enuresis is strongly familial: about 44% if one parent had it, 77% if both [general knowledge]);
- previous treatment; **parental attitude** (punishment, shame);
- impact on self-esteem and peer life (sleepovers, hostel).

**2. Developmental history:**
- milestones (motor, language, social, toilet training); hearing and vision;
- **antenatal and perinatal risk factors** (prematurity, asphyxia, infections, alcohol, maternal hypothyroidism);
- neonatal jaundice; seizures [2].

**3. Behavioural and academic history:**
- inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity **since early childhood**, in 2 or more settings;
- **academic profile across subjects**: reading, writing, spelling, maths; copying from the board; number sense; calculation errors; is performance **variable** (ADHD) or **persistent** (SLD/ID)? [1]
- teacher reports; report cards;
- school type, medium of instruction, absenteeism, repeated grades.

**4. Adaptive functioning [2]:**
- self-care, money and time concepts, safety, social judgement, independent travel;
- play preferences (younger children) and peer relationships.

**5. Family and psychosocial:**
- consanguinity; family history of ID, SLD, ADHD;
- parenting; stressors; **abuse**; school pressure.

**6. Examination:**
- growth, head circumference, **dysmorphism** (fragile X: large ears, macro-orchidism after puberty; FASD), neurocutaneous markers;
- **spine** (sacral dimple, hair tuft: occult dysraphism); **neurological examination of the lower limbs**;
- abdomen (faecal loading); genitalia;
- **BP**; **tonsils** (OSA);
- thyroid; vision and hearing screening.

**7. Mental state examination and observation:** attention, activity, comprehension, language, social reciprocity, mood.

**8. Investigations:**
- **urine routine and culture**, **urine specific gravity**, **blood glucose**;
- **TSH**;
- ultrasound of kidneys, ureters, bladder and post-void residual **if daytime symptoms are present** [general knowledge];
- **EEG** if absences or nocturnal seizures are suspected; sleep study if OSA is suspected;
- **genetic testing** (fragile X, chromosomal microarray) if ID with dysmorphism [2].

**9. Psychological assessment (stepwise) [1–3]:**
- **IQ:** MISIC, **Binet–Kamat**, WISC-IV (verbal vs performance IQ; ACID profile in SLD) [3];
- **adaptive behaviour:** **Vineland Social Maturity Scale (VSMS)**, giving the social quotient. "Wherever IQ and SQ indicate different severity, decisions are taken in favour of SQ" [2];
- **ADHD rating scales** (Conners, Vanderbilt) from parents and teachers [1];
- **SLD:** **NIMHANS Index for SLD (Level II, 8–12 years)**, WRAT, curriculum-based assessment [3]. **Assess SLD after ADHD symptoms are controlled** [1];
- **ASD screen** (ISAA, SCQ) if social deficits are prominent.

### (b) Management
**Communicate a formulation** to the parents: enuresis is **involuntary, common and treatable**, **not laziness or naughtiness**. Explain the link with neurodevelopment.

**Enuresis [general knowledge unless cited]:**
1. **Treat underlying causes:** constipation, UTI, OSA, diabetes.
2. **Basic advice:**
   - **no punishment**;
   - regular daytime voiding (5–7 times);
   - adequate daytime fluids, **restricted 1–2 hours before bed**;
   - avoid caffeine and fizzy drinks;
   - void before sleep;
   - easy toilet access and a night light;
   - **star chart** (rewarding agreed behaviours, not dry nights alone).
3. **Enuresis alarm:**
   - **first-line** for motivated families;
   - success about 65–75%, with **lower relapse** than drugs;
   - continue until **14 consecutive dry nights**;
   - overlearning reduces relapse.
4. **Desmopressin:**
   - first-line alternative, or when rapid dryness is needed (camps);
   - **0.2–0.4 mg tablet** or 120–240 µg melt at bedtime;
   - **fluid restriction from 1 hour before to 8 hours after** a dose (**risk of hyponatraemia and water intoxication**);
   - high relapse on stopping; structured withdrawal reduces relapse.
5. **Combined alarm plus desmopressin** for partial responders.
6. **Tricyclics:**
   - **imipramine** (licensed 6–17 years) or **amitriptyline** (licensed 6–17, UK MHRA/EMA) [4];
   - third line, because of **cardiotoxicity and overdose risk**.
   - **Oxybutynin** (anticholinergic) with desmopressin for non-monosymptomatic enuresis.
7. **Comorbid ADHD:** treating ADHD may improve adherence to the alarm programme. **Atomoxetine** has reported benefit for enuresis [general knowledge].

**If ID or borderline intellectual functioning is confirmed [2]:**
- explain the diagnosis and its severity (IQ and SQ);
- **special education or inclusive education** with an **Individualised Education Plan (IEP)**;
- **life-skills** and **vocational** planning;
- behavioural training (toilet training programmes);
- treat comorbidities (epilepsy, ADHD: **methylphenidate effective in ADHD with ID** [4]);
- **genetic counselling** if a cause is found;
- **disability certification under RPwD Act 2016**: mild ID (IQ/SQ 55–69) = 50%; borderline (70–84) = 25%, which is not benchmark [2];
- welfare benefits (Niramaya insurance, the National Trust) [general knowledge].

**If ADHD (inattentive) [1]:**
- environmental modifications at home and school: structure, a "place for everything", planners, seating near the teacher;
- parent training;
- **methylphenidate** if impairment persists;
- **adjust for mental age** if ID co-exists.

**If SLD (dyscalculia) [3]:**
- **remedial education** (2–3 times a week for 3–4 years; concrete-to-abstract maths teaching; multisensory methods);
- **accommodations:** calculator, extra time, a scribe; lower-level mathematics; NIOS options; board provisions;
- **certification** (benchmark disability under RPwD Act 2016) [3].

**Psychosocial:**
- protect self-esteem;
- treat secondary anxiety or depression;
- address bullying;
- **school liaison** with consent;
- **parent counselling** about realistic expectations.

**Follow-up:** dryness diary; academic progress; reassess IQ/SLD after ADHD control [1].

**Prognosis:**
- Enuresis **resolves spontaneously in about 15% a year**; about 1–2% persist into adolescence [general knowledge].
- The academic and social prognosis depends on the underlying neurodevelopmental diagnosis and on early remediation.

**Conclusion.** Enuresis at 10, inattention and disorganisation, global (especially maths and science) academic difficulty and a preference for younger playmates suggest an underlying **neurodevelopmental disorder**: **mild ID or borderline intellectual functioning, ADHD (inattentive) or SLD (dyscalculia)**, alone or together. Medical causes of enuresis and of inattention (OSA, absences, hypothyroidism, sensory deficits) must be excluded. Management combines **enuresis treatment (advice, alarm, desmopressin)** with diagnosis-specific educational, behavioural and pharmacological interventions and certification [1–4].

## In depth

### Background
- **Enuresis** comes from the Greek "to void urine". Toilet control is a developmental milestone, which explains the developmental-age criterion.
- **Neurodevelopmental clustering:** ADHD, ID, SLD and enuresis commonly co-occur ("ESSENCE", Gillberg) [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology
- **Enuresis:** about 15–20% at 5 years, **about 5% at 10 years**, about 1–2% at 15. Boys outnumber girls 2:1 [general knowledge].
- **ADHD:** 5–7% of school-aged children [1]. About 20–30% of children with enuresis have ADHD symptoms [general knowledge].
- **ID:** about 2.5% globally (by IQ distribution); Indian point prevalence varies widely [2].
- **SLD:** 2–10% (Sahoo 2015) [3]. ADHD co-occurs in 10–60% [3].

### Classification and criteria
- **ID [2]:**
  - deficits in **intellectual functioning and adaptive behaviour**, onset in the developmental period;
  - **ICD-10 severity by IQ**: mild 50–69, moderate 35–49, severe 20–34, profound below 20;
  - **DSM-5** severity by adaptive functioning (IQ about 65–75 cut-off).
- **SLD:** DSM-5 overarching category with specifiers (reading, written expression, mathematics); persistence despite 6 months of targeted help [3].
- **Enuresis (DSM-5-TR):** nocturnal only, diurnal only, nocturnal and diurnal.

### Mechanism [general knowledge]
- **Enuresis:**
  - **nocturnal polyuria** (reduced nocturnal ADH secretion);
  - **detrusor overactivity / small functional bladder capacity**;
  - **arousal deficit**: failure to wake to bladder signals. This explains the link with ADHD (brainstem arousal and attention circuits).
  - Genetic loci on chromosomes 12, 13 and 22.
- **ID:** genetic (Down syndrome, fragile X), prenatal (FASD, TORCH), perinatal (asphyxia, prematurity), postnatal (meningitis, malnutrition, iodine deficiency, lead) [2].

### Clinical features: academic profiles
| | ID | SLD | ADHD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IQ | Below 70 (borderline 70–84) | Average or above | Variable |
| Academics | Global, consistent | Specific domain(s), persistent | Variable, inconsistent [1] |
| Adaptive behaviour | Impaired | Intact | May be impaired (executive function) |
| Play | Younger peers | Age-appropriate | Impulsive play |

### Assessment: Indian tools [2, 3]
- **IQ:** MISIC, Binet–Kamat, WISC-IV, Raven's progressive matrices (non-verbal).
- **Adaptive behaviour:** VSMS (the only standardised Indian measure) [2].
- **SLD:**
  - NIMHANS Index for SLD (Level I 5–7 years; Level II 8–12 years) [3];
  - **GLAD** (Grade Level Assessment Device, NIEPID; grades I–IV) [3].

### Differential diagnosis: medical causes of enuresis
- **UTI**; **diabetes mellitus**; **diabetes insipidus**; **chronic kidney disease** (polyuria).
- **Constipation.**
- **OSA**; **epilepsy**.
- Neurogenic bladder (spinal dysraphism).
- Posterior urethral valves (boys with poor stream).
- **Medications:** clozapine, lithium, valproate, risperidone, diuretics [general knowledge; Maudsley clozapine section].

### Management details: alarm vs desmopressin [general knowledge]
| | Alarm | Desmopressin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism | Conditioning (arousal) | Reduces nocturnal urine |
| Onset | Weeks | Days |
| Success | 65–75% | 30–70% |
| Relapse | Low (about 15–30%) | High (up to 70%) |
| Best for | Normal-capacity bladder, motivated family | Nocturnal polyuria, rapid need |
| Adverse effects | Disturbs sleep | Hyponatraemia, headache |

### Critical appraisal
- Indian IQ tests are **outdated** (MISIC norms from 1969) [3].
- **Borderline intellectual functioning** falls between services: it is not a benchmark disability, yet it is impairing [2].
- Enuresis is often dismissed in psychiatric settings. Its impact on self-esteem is under-appreciated.

### Indian context
- **Cultural attitudes:** shaming or punishment for bedwetting; use of home remedies.
- Alarms are not widely available or are costly; **desmopressin** is available.
- **RPwD Act 2016:** benchmark disabilities (ID at least 40%; SLD) entitle the child to free education and exam accommodations [2, 3].
- Hostel settings (residential schools) heighten distress.

### Recent advances
- **Desmopressin melt** formulations.
- **Solifenacin** for refractory overactive bladder (off-label).
- **Exome sequencing** in ID [2].
- **Digital screening tools** for SLD [3].
