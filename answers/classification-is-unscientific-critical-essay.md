## Exam answer

### Introduction

Classification organizes symptoms and syndromes to support communication, treatment, research, epidemiology, and service planning. DSM and ICD are widely used, but their scientific status is debated because many disorders lack definitive biomarkers, overlap, and vary across individuals and cultures. The criticism is partly justified but too absolute.

### Arguments supporting the criticism

- **No clear biological boundaries:** Most diagnoses rely on clinical symptom patterns rather than specific laboratory or imaging tests.
- **Heterogeneity:** People with the same diagnosis may differ substantially in symptoms, mechanisms, and course.
- **Comorbidity/overlap:** Anxiety, depression, trauma, substance use, and personality pathology commonly co-occur.
- **Reliability is not validity:** Operational criteria may improve agreement without proving a distinct natural disease entity.
- **Thresholds:** Many symptoms are continuous; cut-offs between distress and disorder depend on duration, impairment, and context.
- **Culture and society:** Language, social expectations, trauma, and inequality shape presentation and interpretation.
- **Historical change:** Categories evolve with evidence and social values.

### Arguments for classification

It provides shared language, supports evidence-based decisions, epidemiology, research recruitment, service planning, and access to care. Standardized criteria improve reliability and communication. ICD-11 emphasizes international applicability and clinical utility; DSM offers detailed operational criteria.

### Alternatives/complements

- **Dimensional approaches:** Rate symptoms and traits by severity.
- **HiTOP:** Hierarchical organization of psychopathology spectra and dimensions.
- **RDoC:** Research framework studying dimensions across genes, molecules, circuits, behaviour, and self-report; not a clinical diagnostic manual.
- **Network models:** Treat symptoms as interacting elements.
- **Clinical formulation:** Integrates biological, psychological, developmental, interpersonal, and cultural factors.

### Implications

Use diagnoses pragmatically and transparently; record severity, course, functioning, risk, comorbidity, and context. Evaluate classification for validity, reliability, utility, fairness, and cultural applicability. Research should examine mechanisms that cross diagnostic boundaries.

### Conclusion

Psychiatric classification is an evolving, imperfect scientific tool. It is not wholly unscientific. Categorical diagnoses aid communication but may not map neatly onto discrete biological entities. Diagnosis, dimensional measurement, formulation, and mechanism-focused research should be used together.
