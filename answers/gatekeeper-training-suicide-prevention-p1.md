## Exam answer

### Definition

Gatekeeper training is a structured suicide-prevention strategy that trains selected people to recognise warning signs, ask about suicidal thoughts, provide initial support and facilitate referral to professional services.

### Target groups

Teachers, college staff, peers, healthcare workers, ASHA workers, police, social workers, workplace supervisors, community leaders and family members may act as gatekeepers.

### Objectives

-   Improve knowledge and reduce myths and stigma.
-   Recognise warning signs and risk factors.
-   Increase confidence in asking directly about suicide.
-   Provide compassionate initial support.
-   Facilitate timely referral.
-   Strengthen community-based prevention systems.

### Major programmes

-   **QPR:** Question, Persuade, Refer.
-   **ASIST:** Applied Suicide Intervention Skills Training.
-   **safeTALK:** suicide alertness and connection to trained helpers.
-   **Mental Health First Aid:** broader mental health first aid that
    includes response to suicide risk.

### Core skills

1.  Recognise verbal and behavioural warning signs, such as
    hopelessness, withdrawal, talking about death or marked behavioural
    change.
2.  Ask directly and sensitively about suicidal thoughts.
3.  Listen without judgement, validate distress and avoid minimising the
    person's experience.
4.  Encourage professional help and connect the person to crisis and
    mental health services.
5.  If risk is imminent, facilitate urgent assessment, do not leave the
    person alone when safe to remain, and reduce access to lethal means
    where feasible and safe.
6.  Follow up within appropriate role boundaries.

Gatekeepers are not substitutes for trained clinicians and should not independently manage high-risk situations.

### Evidence and limitations

Training generally improves knowledge, confidence and willingness to intervene. Evidence for reducing suicide attempts or deaths is less consistent. Knowledge gains may not translate into action if referral services are inaccessible. Refresher training, supervision and clear pathways are necessary.

### Indian context

India's National Suicide Prevention Strategy (2022) sets a target of reducing suicide mortality by 10% by 2030. Gatekeeper training can be integrated into schools, colleges, primary care, workplaces and community programmes. Tele-MANAS and District Mental Health Programme services may support referral pathways.

### Conclusion

Gatekeeper training is an important component of a multisectoral suicide-prevention strategy. Its impact depends on sustained training, safe intervention skills and accessible referral services.
