## Exam answer

**Definition.** SSRI-resistant (treatment-resistant) OCD is failure to respond adequately (usually **<25–35% reduction in Y-BOCS**) to **at least two adequate SSRI trials**: maximum tolerated or licensed dose (often higher than antidepressant doses) for **≥8–12 weeks**, with good adherence. Many definitions also require failure of an adequate course of **exposure and response prevention (ERP)**. Staging (Pallanti & Quercioli 2006): response ≥35% Y-BOCS reduction; partial response 25–35%; non-response <25%; refractory = no change or worsening despite all available treatments. About **40–60%** of patients do not respond fully to a first SSRI [1,2].

### Mechanism (rationale for strategies)
OCD involves hyperactivity of the **cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC)** loop: orbitofrontal cortex, anterior cingulate, caudate, thalamus. SRIs act slowly (5-HT downregulation of OFC autoreceptors) and need high doses. Non-response may reflect **dopaminergic** involvement (tic-related OCD responds to D2 blockers), **glutamatergic** excess (NAC, memantine), poor insight, comorbidity, or inadequate dosing, duration or ERP [1,2].

### Step 1 — Before calling it "resistant" (pseudo-resistance)
Confirm the diagnosis (vs psychosis, OCPD, autism, tics, BDD). Check dose and duration (e.g., fluoxetine 60–80 mg, sertraline 200 mg, fluvoxamine 300 mg, paroxetine 60 mg, escitalopram 20–40 mg, for ≥12 weeks). Check adherence and plasma levels. Was ERP done properly? Address comorbidity (depression, bipolar disorder, ADHD, substance use, tics), family accommodation, and poor insight [1,2].

### Step 2 — Evidence-based strategies

| Strategy | Detail | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Add / intensify ERP (CBT)** | Therapist-guided ERP 16–20 sessions; intensive or residential; family involvement | Simpson 2008/2013: ERP augmentation of SRI superior to stress management and to risperidone augmentation |
| **Higher-than-licensed SSRI dose** | E.g., sertraline 250–400 mg (case reports up to 650 mg), escitalopram 30–50 mg; ECG | Ninan 2006: sertraline 250–400 mg better than 200 mg [1] |
| **Switch to another SSRI** | — | About 25–50% respond to a second SSRI |
| **Clomipramine** | 150–250 mg (start 25 mg; ECG, seizure threshold, anticholinergic); oral or IV | Possibly slightly more effective than SSRIs; second-line after 2 SSRIs (NICE) [1] |
| **Clomipramine + SSRI (citalopram/fluvoxamine)** | Monitor clomipramine levels (fluvoxamine inhibits CYP1A2 → raised clomipramine); ECG | NICE option [1] |
| **Antipsychotic augmentation** | **Risperidone 0.5–2 mg** or **aripiprazole 2.5–10 mg** (best evidence); haloperidol (especially with tics); 6–8 weeks trial; stop if no response | Bloch 2006: about 1 in 3 responds (NNT ≈ 5); greater benefit with tics [1,2] |
| **Glutamatergic agents** | N-acetylcysteine 2.4–3 g; memantine 10–20 mg; topiramate; lamotrigine; riluzole | Small RCTs; mixed results [2] |
| **Other augmentations** | Ondansetron (QT risk), pregabalin, mirtazapine, celecoxib, minocycline | Small trials [2] |
| **Neuromodulation** | **rTMS:** LF right DLPFC (moderate), HF bilateral DLPFC, LF SMA; deep TMS (H7, FDA-cleared 2018); 10–30 sessions | IPS CPG: moderate evidence [3] |
| **Deep brain stimulation** | Ventral capsule/ventral striatum, STN, nucleus accumbens | About 60% response in refractory cases; FDA humanitarian exemption [2] |
| **Ablative neurosurgery** | Anterior capsulotomy, cingulotomy, gamma-knife | About 50–60% response; irreversible; ethics review |
| ECT | Not effective for core OCD | Only for comorbid severe depression |
| Ketamine IV | Rapid but transient | Case reports and small RCT [2] |

### Assessment and monitoring
Y-BOCS (or CY-BOCS) every 4 weeks; Brown Assessment of Beliefs Scale (insight); dimensional OCD scale (DOCS); comorbidity screening; family accommodation scale; ECG with clomipramine or high-dose citalopram/escitalopram; metabolic monitoring with antipsychotics [1,2].

### Key evidence
- **Bloch 2006 meta-analysis** (9 RCTs): antipsychotic augmentation gave response in about 32% vs 0% with placebo; tics predict response.
- **Dold 2013/2015:** risperidone and aripiprazole best supported; quetiapine and olanzapine not consistently effective.
- **Simpson 2013 (JAMA Psychiatry):** ERP augmentation (80% response) superior to risperidone (23%) and placebo (15%).
- **Denys 2010 (DBS, n=16):** about 46% response at 21 months (VC/VS/NAc).
- **IPS rTMS CPG:** moderate positive evidence; Y-BOCS SMD 0.50–0.79 [3].

**Conclusion.** SSRI-resistant OCD is common. Address pseudo-resistance first (dose, duration, adherence, ERP quality, comorbidity). Proven next steps are adding or intensifying ERP, raising the SSRI dose, switching to clomipramine, and augmenting with low-dose risperidone or aripiprazole. rTMS, glutamatergic agents, and finally DBS or ablative surgery are options for truly refractory cases.

## In depth

### Background
- Clomipramine (1960s; Fernandez-Cordoba) was the first drug shown effective in OCD. The serotonin hypothesis followed (Insel 1980s).
- Meyer (1966) developed ERP; Foa refined it.
- **Augmentation era (1990s):** McDougle's haloperidol trial in tic-related OCD (1994) and risperidone augmentation (2000).
- **Neurosurgery:** cingulotomy and capsulotomy from the 1950s; DBS (Nuttin 1999); FDA humanitarian device exemption 2009.
- **rTMS and deep TMS:** H7 coil FDA-cleared 2018 [3].

### Epidemiology
- Lifetime prevalence of OCD ~2–3%; NMHS India 0.8%. Onset in two peaks (childhood and early adulthood).
- First SSRI response about 40–60%. Full remission is uncommon (about 25%).
- About 10% remain refractory to all treatment.
- Comorbidity: depression 60%; tics 20–30%; bipolar disorder 10–20%. Poor insight in about 15–30%.

### Classification and criteria
- **ICD-11 6B20 OCD:** in the **obsessive-compulsive or related disorders** grouping, with insight specifier (fair to good; poor to absent).
- DSM-5-TR: OCD in the OCRD chapter, with good/fair, poor or absent insight, and tic-related specifiers.
- **Note:** the user's study notes state that ICD-11 kept OCD under anxiety disorders and placed BDD under "body image disorders" and hoarding under impulse control. In the published ICD-11, OCD, BDD, olfactory reference disorder, hypochondriasis, hoarding and body-focused repetitive behaviour disorders are all in the **OCRD grouping (6B20–6B25)**. Please verify against ICD-11 CDDR.
- **Treatment response staging (Pallanti):** full response ≥35% Y-BOCS reduction and CGI-I 1–2; partial 25–35%; non-response <25%; remission Y-BOCS ≤12 [1].

### Mechanism in detail
- **CSTC model:** direct (excitatory) vs indirect (inhibitory) pathway imbalance; hyperactive OFC–caudate loop ("worry circuit"); error-related negativity enhanced; reduced cognitive flexibility.
- **Serotonin:** SRIs desensitise OFC 5-HT autoreceptors, which needs high doses and long duration.
- **Dopamine:** tic-related OCD; stimulants can worsen it; D2 antagonists augment [2].
- **Glutamate:** raised CSF glutamate; SLC1A1 (glutamate transporter) gene association; this supports NAC, memantine, riluzole [2].
- **Immune:** PANDAS/PANS in children.
- **Neuromodulation targets:** OFC (LF inhibition), SMA (motor inhibition), DLPFC; DBS targets VC/VS, STN, ITP [3].

### Clinical features in detail — predictors of resistance
Early onset; male sex; long duration; poor insight; hoarding symptoms; symmetry or ordering with tics; sexual or religious obsessions (variable); comorbid schizotypy or personality disorder; severe family accommodation; comorbid depression, bipolar disorder or substance use.

### Differential — apparent resistance vs other diagnoses

| Condition | Clue |
| --- | --- |
| Psychosis / schizo-obsessive | Delusional conviction beyond poor insight; other psychotic symptoms |
| OCPD | Ego-syntonic perfectionism, no true obsessions |
| Autism spectrum | Restricted interests and routines, ego-syntonic |
| Tic disorder | "Just right" compulsions with tics |
| BDD, hoarding, ORS | Specific foci needing tailored therapy |
| Depressive rumination | Mood-congruent, not neutralised |
| Bipolar disorder | SSRI-induced activation; cycling |

### Management details
- **Clomipramine:** start 25 mg; increase to 150–250 mg over weeks; plasma clomipramine + desmethylclomipramine ~450 µg/L. Risks: seizures (>250 mg), cardiac conduction, anticholinergic effects, weight gain, sexual dysfunction. IV clomipramine for refractory cases.
- **SSRI + clomipramine:** careful; fluvoxamine raises clomipramine levels (CYP1A2) and reduces desmethylclomipramine. Low-dose clomipramine with fluvoxamine is better tolerated [2].
- **Antipsychotic augmentation:** low doses (aripiprazole 2.5–5 mg; risperidone 0.5–2 mg); review at 6–8 weeks; stop if ineffective [2]. Antipsychotics alone are not effective for OCD [2].
- **Duration:** continue at least 1–2 years after response. Relapse after stopping is 80%; continuing for 2 years halves relapse (25–40%) [1].
- **ERP practicalities:** hierarchy; exposure without rituals; homework; family involvement to reduce accommodation; intensive formats (daily 3-hour sessions for 3 weeks).
- **Neurosurgery and DBS:** strict criteria — severe (Y-BOCS ≥28), duration ≥5 years, failed ≥3 SRIs including clomipramine, ≥2 augmentations, and ≥20 hours of ERP; ethics board review.
- **Special groups:**
  - Children: CBT first; sertraline or fluvoxamine; aripiprazole augmentation [2].
  - Pregnancy: CBT; sertraline.
  - Elderly: lower doses.
  - Bipolar comorbidity: mood stabiliser first; cautious SSRI.

### Critical appraisal
- Antipsychotic augmentation helps only a minority and brings metabolic and EPS costs. Long-term efficacy is unclear.
- Many glutamatergic and other augmentation trials are small and single-centre, with risk of publication bias.
- ERP access is limited; treatment-resistance often reflects a lack of good ERP rather than biology.
- Definitions of resistance vary across studies.
- Neurosurgery ethics: irreversibility (ablation), consent, long-term effects.

### Indian context
- **IPS CPG for OCD (Janardhan Reddy et al., 2017):** SSRIs first line with adequate dose and duration; ERP; clomipramine; antipsychotic augmentation; rTMS; DBS and surgery in specialist centres.
- **NIMHANS OCD clinic:** large Indian cohorts; research on intensive ERP, rTMS (SMA), DBS and ablative surgery; family-based ERP.
- Religious and contamination obsessions are common; cultural norms of purity and ritual may enhance accommodation; involve families.
- Clomipramine and SSRIs are inexpensive; ERP therapists are scarce outside major centres. Use family-assisted and telepsychiatry-delivered ERP.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** deep TMS H7 coil; aripiprazole augmentation [2,3].
- **Emerging:** accelerated rTMS and theta burst (TBS ineffective so far) [3]; internet-based ERP; DBS target refinement; MR-guided focused ultrasound capsulotomy.
- **Investigational:** ketamine; psilocybin; troriluzole (phase 3 failed in 2024 — check status); anti-inflammatory agents.
