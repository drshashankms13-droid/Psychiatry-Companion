## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 12-year-old boy was referred by the school counsellor for:
- **lying**;
- **forging his father's signature** on his mark card;
- recent **physical fights**;
- being **caught smoking ganja (cannabis) on school premises**.

His **father works abroad**; he lives with his mother.

### Provisional diagnosis
**Conduct disorder (CD), probably childhood-onset or adolescent-onset type to be clarified, with comorbid cannabis use (harmful use or use disorder to be assessed).**

**Rationale [1]:**
- **DSM-5-TR CD** requires **3 or more of 15** behaviours in **12 months** (at least 1 in the last 6 months), across 4 groups:
  1. aggression to people and animals (7);
  2. destruction of property (2);
  3. **deceitfulness or theft** (3);
  4. **serious violation of rules** (3).
- This boy shows:
  - **deceitfulness** (lying to obtain goods or favours, or to avoid obligations; **forgery** to evade consequences);
  - **aggression** (initiating fights);
  - possibly **rule violations** (substance use on campus is a serious school-rule violation, though not itself a DSM criterion).
- **Subtype by onset:** **childhood-onset** (at least 1 symptom before 10 years) vs **adolescent-onset**. Ask about earlier behaviour [1].
- **Specifier "with limited prosocial emotions"** (2 or more of 4: lack of remorse or guilt; callous lack of empathy; unconcerned about performance; shallow or deficient affect) [1].
- **ICD-11:** conduct-dissocial disorder (6C91), childhood- or adolescent-onset, with or without limited prosocial emotions.
  - ICD-10 subtypes: **unsocialised** vs **socialised**, and confined to the family context. Any single category, **if marked**, could suffice; isolated dissocial acts do not [1].

**Differentials and comorbidities [1]:**
| Condition | Points |
| --- | --- |
| **ADHD** | Most common comorbidity; ADHD symptoms predate defiance [1, 3] |
| **ODD** | Angry or irritable mood, argumentativeness, vindictiveness; without serious violations (often a precursor) [1] |
| **Mood disorder** (depression, bipolar), **DMDD** | Irritability; change from baseline [1] |
| **Adjustment reaction** | To parental absence or migration (father abroad) [1] |
| **Substance-induced disorder** | Cannabis-related irritability and aggression |
| **Specific learning disorder / ID** | Academic failure leads to forging the mark card [general knowledge] |
| **ASD** | Social misreading leads to fights [1] |
| **IED** | Episodic disproportionate aggression without other conduct problems [1] |
| **Subcultural deviance** | Peer-group norms [1] |
| **Victim of abuse or bullying**; **PTSD** | Must be explored |
| **ASPD** | Not diagnosed before 18 [1] |

### (a) Evaluation
**1. History from multiple informants:** the boy (alone, with confidentiality limits explained), the **mother**, the **father** (by video call), teachers and the counsellor.
- **Conduct symptoms:** onset, frequency, settings; aggression (weapons, cruelty to animals, bullying); stealing; fire-setting; truancy; running away; staying out at night.
- **Substance use:**
  - cannabis (onset, frequency, source, peers, route, dependence features);
  - **tobacco, alcohol, inhalants** (whitener, petrol), other drugs.
  - Use **CRAFFT** and ASSIST-Y [general knowledge].
- **Academic:** marks, failures (why forge?), SLD signs, attendance.
- **Developmental:** temperament (difficult, negative emotionality, poor self-regulation, restlessness), milestones, language, IQ [1].
- **Family [1]:**
  - **father's absence** (attachment, discipline shift, the "Gulf syndrome" of migrant families) [general knowledge for the term];
  - mother's stress and depression; parenting style (inconsistent, harsh, permissive, **ignoring positive behaviour, paradoxical attention, negative-reinforcement trap**);
  - marital conflict and violence; **maltreatment** (corporal punishment);
  - family psychopathology and substance use; finances (remittances, unsupervised money).
- **Peers:** deviant peer affiliation; peer rejection; gang involvement [1].
- **Neighbourhood and school** factors [1].
- **Legal:** police contact. If he is a "child in conflict with law", the **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015** applies [general knowledge].
- **Strengths:** sports, interests, positive relationships.

**2. Mental state examination:**
- mood (depression often co-exists);
- hostility; remorse and empathy (**callous-unemotional traits**);
- psychotic symptoms (cannabis);
- suicidal ideation;
- cognition and estimated IQ.

**3. Physical examination and investigations:**
- signs of intoxication or injury; nutrition; self-harm;
- **urine drug screen** (with consent and assent);
- other investigations as indicated.

**4. Rating scales and testing:**
- **CBCL**, **SDQ**, Vanderbilt/Conners (ADHD), **ICU** (Inventory of Callous-Unemotional traits);
- **IQ** (MISIC/WISC) and **SLD** assessment (NIMHANS Index) if academic difficulty is present;
- CGAS [general knowledge].

### (b) Management
**General principles of treatment [1]:**
1. The aim is to **increase compliance** and make the child more **cooperative and better accepted**.
2. **Structured psychosocial intervention is the only effective treatment.**
3. Involve **both parents** (here, the father via video); improve **parenting skills and parent–child interaction**.
4. **Identify and treat comorbidities** (ADHD, depression, SLD, substance use).
5. Address **parental depression, psychosis, substance use and marital discord**.
6. Build on **strengths** of the child and family.
7. **De-stress the family** as an initial step.
8. Intervene for **learning difficulties** and involve the **school** as needed.

**Parent management training (PMT): six steps [1]:**
1. Promote a **child-centred approach** (special time, positive attention).
2. **Increase acceptable behaviour** (praise, rewards, token economy).
3. Set **clear expectations** (house rules, effective commands).
4. **Reduce unacceptable behaviour** (planned ignoring, time-out, loss of privileges, consistent consequences, monitoring whereabouts).
5. **Strategies for avoiding trouble** (anticipating high-risk situations, supervision, peers).
6. **Identify and rectify long-standing maladaptive patterns.**

**Evidence-based programmes [general knowledge]:**
- Webster-Stratton **Incredible Years**; **Triple P**; Parent–Child Interaction Therapy (younger children);
- for adolescents: **Multisystemic Therapy (MST)**, **Functional Family Therapy (FFT)**, **Multidimensional Treatment Foster Care**;
- **child-focused:** problem-solving skills training (Kazdin), anger management (Coping Power), social-skills training.

**Substance use:**
- **motivational interviewing**; CBT;
- **Multidimensional Family Therapy (MDFT)** and the **Adolescent Community Reinforcement Approach** [general knowledge];
- psychoeducation on cannabis risks (psychosis, cognition, school performance);
- reduce access (money, peers);
- the school's response should be **supportive, not punitive expulsion**.

**School:**
- liaison (with consent);
- a behaviour plan; a daily report card;
- remedial teaching if SLD;
- avoid suspension (it increases unsupervised time);
- mentoring.

**Pharmacotherapy (adjunctive only) [2; general knowledge]:**
- **Treat comorbid ADHD:** methylphenidate or atomoxetine. This also reduces conduct symptoms [general knowledge].
- **Risperidone** is the **only drug licensed for conduct disorder** (UK MHRA/EMA, 5–18 years) [2]. It is used for **persistent severe aggression** short-term, after psychosocial measures, at low dose (0.25–1.5 mg), with metabolic and prolactin monitoring [general knowledge for dosing].
- **Lithium and valproate:** some RCT evidence for aggression in hospitalised children with CD [2]. Valproate should be avoided in post-pubertal girls [2].
- **No drug for cannabis use** in adolescents. Treat comorbid depression if present (SSRI).

**Legal and protective:**
- if there is abuse or neglect, the **Child Welfare Committee** (JJ Act 2015);
- if there is offending, the **Juvenile Justice Board**, with a focus on rehabilitation [general knowledge];
- the **NDPS Act**: possession by a juvenile goes to the JJ system [general knowledge; verify].

**Prognosis [1; general knowledge]:**
- Long-term course: irritable, headstrong temperament, then ODD, then CD, then **aggressive CD with callous-unemotional traits**, which may become ASPD [1].
- **Poor prognostic factors:**
  - **childhood onset**; **callous-unemotional traits**;
  - aggression in multiple settings; comorbid ADHD;
  - low IQ; **parental criminality**; deviant peers;
  - **early substance use**.
- About 40% of children with CD develop ASPD (Robins) [general knowledge].
- Adolescent-onset ("adolescence-limited", Moffitt) has a better outcome.

**Conclusion.** The boy meets provisional criteria for **conduct disorder with cannabis use**, against the backdrop of an **absent father** and probable inconsistent supervision. Evaluation must establish onset type, callous-unemotional traits, comorbid ADHD, SLD, depression and substance use, and family functioning. **Structured psychosocial interventions** (PMT, family therapy or MST, school liaison, substance-use treatment) form the core of treatment. Medication is reserved for comorbidity or severe aggression [1, 2].

## In depth

### Background
- **Externalising disorders:** ADHD, ODD, CD [1].
- Antisocial behaviour outside socially acceptable norms that violates others' rights; repetitive and persistent problems with self-control of emotions and behaviour [1].
- The anger spectrum runs ODD, IED, CD, ASPD [1].

### Epidemiology [1]
- Overall prevalence **5–10%**. **ODD is commoner in younger children; CD in adolescents.**
- **Gender** is the strongest predictor (with parenting style). Boys outnumber girls: ODD 2:1 in younger children; CD 3:1 to 7:1 in adolescents.
- **SES and area:** 5 times higher in the lowest SES; 2–3 times higher in deprived areas.
- **Worldwide:** Bangladesh 8.9% > Yemen > Brazil > Britain > Italy > **India 0.4%**.
- **High-risk populations:** maltreated children, residential and foster care, IDD, young offenders.
- Rates **doubled between 1970 and 2000** [1].
- CD comorbidity in SLD is about 6% (workbook table, Chaudhary and Meghwal 2015) [4].

### Classification
- **DSM-5-TR:**
  - ODD: 4 of 8 symptoms in 3 groups (angry or irritable mood; argumentative or defiant; vindictive). Under 5 years: most days for 6 months; 5 years and over: at least weekly for 6 months [1].
  - CD: 3 of 15 in 12 months; limited prosocial emotions specifier (2 of 4) [1].
- **ICD-10:** a list of behaviours (fighting or bullying, cruelty, destructiveness, fire-setting, stealing, lying, truancy, tantrums, defiance, disobedience). **Any one, if marked**, may suffice; at least 6 months. Subtypes unsocialised, socialised, ODD [1].

### Mechanism: aetiology [1]
- **Individual:**
  - **genetic** effects on antisocial behaviour, especially with ADHD, CU traits and physical aggression;
  - specific genes: **MAOA** (×maltreatment interaction, Caspi 2002 [general knowledge]);
  - pregnancy and perinatal complications (alcohol, smoking);
  - **temperament** (difficult, negative emotionality, poor self-regulation);
  - **brain function**: "hot" paralimbic system (amygdala, VMPFC, superior temporal lobe);
  - **language, IQ and educational deficits**; **executive dysfunction**;
  - **ANS and HPA hypoarousal**: bradycardia, low cortisol, low skin conductance (fearlessness / stimulation-seeking);
  - **low theory of mind**.
- **Family:**
  - ignoring positive behaviour; **paradoxical attention**; **negative-reinforcement trap** (Patterson's coercive cycle);
  - insecure or disorganised attachment;
  - interparental conflict and violence;
  - maltreatment (with a child-effect component).
- **Outside the family:** peer rejection, **deviant peers**, school, **poverty**, neighbourhood [1].

### Clinical features: developmental course [1]
- An irritable, headstrong, hurtful temperament with outbursts leads to ODD (defiant, disobedient). This then branches:
  - to anxiety and depression (the irritable dimension), or
  - to CD (the headstrong dimension), and then aggressive CD with CU traits.

### Assessment: callous-unemotional traits
- These are important because they predict **severe, stable aggression** and **poorer response to standard PMT**. Warmth-focused and reward-based approaches work better than punishment for these children [general knowledge].

### Differential: ODD vs CD vs ADHD
| | ODD | CD | ADHD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Negativistic, defiant | Violates others' rights or major norms | Inattention, hyperactivity |
| Intent | Defiance of authority | Deliberate antisocial acts | Not deliberate |
| Age | Younger | Older | Before 12 |

### Management details: MST [general knowledge]
- Intensive home- and community-based work (3–5 months).
- Targets the family, peers, school and neighbourhood ("social ecology").
- Reduces re-offending and out-of-home placement in juvenile offenders.

### Evidence table [general knowledge unless cited]
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Patterson (coercion theory) | Parent–child reinforcement cycles maintain aggression |
| Webster-Stratton Incredible Years RCTs | Reduced conduct problems |
| Henggeler MST trials | Reduced arrests and placements |
| Pringsheim 2015 (cited in [2]) | Stimulants and risperidone reduce conduct problems and aggression in ADHD/ODD/CD |
| Campbell 1995; Malone 2000 (cited in [2]) | Lithium reduced aggression in hospitalised children with CD |
| Maudsley Table 5.1 [2] | Risperidone licensed for CD, 5–18 years (UK/EMA) |

### Critical appraisal
- CD is a **heterogeneous** category (CU vs non-CU; reactive vs proactive aggression).
- Diagnosis risks **medicalising social adversity**.
- Few Indian intervention trials exist.
- Antipsychotic use in children carries significant **metabolic** risk.

### Indian context
- **Low reported prevalence (0.4%)** probably reflects under-recognition [1].
- **Migrant fathers** (Gulf migration, especially from Kerala) are linked to the stress of left-behind families [general knowledge].
- **Juvenile Justice Act 2015:** rehabilitation focus; 16–18-year-olds may be tried as adults for heinous offences after a preliminary assessment [general knowledge].
- **Substance use:** school-based cannabis and inhalant use; COTPA bans tobacco sale within 100 yards of schools [general knowledge].
- **MHCA 2017, s87:** admission of minors [general knowledge].

### Recent advances
- **CU-trait-targeted** interventions (emotion-recognition training).
- **Digital parenting programmes.**
- Neuroimaging of amygdala hypo-reactivity in CU traits.
- Polygenic risk research.
