## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 35-year-old unmarried engineer has used **alprazolam almost daily for 5 years** (started 10 years ago for anxiety). He has **panic attacks in performance situations** (presentations), **marked discomfort with unfamiliar people**, **restricted social life**, **severe work impairment** and plans to quit his job. Alprazolam **no longer gives relief (tolerance)**. He has been **shy and inhibited since childhood**, with a **punitive upbringing**.

**Provisional diagnosis.**
1. **Social anxiety disorder** (ICD-11 6B04; DSM-5 social anxiety disorder), with situationally bound panic attacks — the primary disorder.
2. **Benzodiazepine (sedative-hypnotic) dependence** / sedative, hypnotic or anxiolytic use disorder — moderate to severe.
3. Possible **avoidant (anxious) personality traits/disorder** — ICD-11 personality difficulty or disorder with prominent **negative affectivity and detachment** features.
4. Rule out comorbid **depression** (he wants to give up his job — check hopelessness, suicidality).

### 1. Evaluation and diagnosis

**A. History**
- **Social anxiety:** feared situations (performance vs interactional), fear of negative evaluation and humiliation, anticipatory anxiety, avoidance, safety behaviours (rehearsing, avoiding eye contact), age of onset (usually adolescence), course, impairment.
- **Panic attacks:** only in social/performance situations (cued) or also unexpected — distinguishes social anxiety from **panic disorder**; agoraphobic avoidance.
- **Alprazolam use pattern:** dose escalation, daily dose now, source (prescription, over-the-counter, multiple prescribers), route, **tolerance**, **withdrawal** between doses (inter-dose rebound anxiety, insomnia, tremor), **past withdrawal seizures or delirium**, attempts to stop, craving, use despite harm, time spent obtaining it — map onto **ICD-11 dependence** (impaired control, increasing priority, physiological features) [author's knowledge].
- **Other substances:** alcohol (dangerous combination, cross-tolerance), cannabis, opioids, z-drugs, gabapentinoids.
- **Comorbidity:** depression, suicidal ideas, GAD, OCD, body dysmorphic disorder, ADHD.
- **Personality:** pervasive shyness, feelings of inadequacy, hypersensitivity to criticism, avoidance of relationships unless sure of acceptance since childhood → **avoidant personality disorder** overlaps strongly with generalised social anxiety.
- **Developmental and family history:** punitive upbringing, behavioural inhibition in childhood, bullying, family history of anxiety or substance use.
- **Functioning:** work, relationships, marriage plans; motivation for change.

**B. Mental state examination**
- Anxiety, avoidance of eye contact, low self-esteem, depressive cognitions, suicidal ideation; signs of intoxication or withdrawal; cognition (benzodiazepines impair memory and attention) [1].

**C. Rating scales**
- **Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS)**, Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN); Panic Disorder Severity Scale; HAM-A, PHQ-9/HAM-D; **Severity of Dependence Scale (SDS)** and Benzodiazepine Dependence Questionnaire.

**D. Investigations**
- Urine drug screen; LFT (affects benzodiazepine metabolism and choice of taper drug); TFT (anxiety mimic); FBC; ECG if indicated.

**E. Differential diagnosis**

| Condition | Distinguishing point |
| --- | --- |
| Panic disorder with agoraphobia | Unexpected attacks; fear of the attack itself, not of scrutiny |
| GAD | Worry across many domains, not centred on evaluation |
| Avoidant personality disorder | Pervasive, lifelong, broader inadequacy — often co-diagnosed |
| Depression with social withdrawal | Loss of interest rather than fear of scrutiny |
| Benzodiazepine withdrawal/inter-dose rebound | Anxiety tied to dose timing [1] |
| Schizoid personality | Lack of desire for relationships (he wants them) |
| Body dysmorphic disorder | Fear centred on perceived appearance defect |

### 2. Pharmacological intervention

**A. Treat the primary disorder — social anxiety [1]**

| Line | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| **First line** (in order) | **SSRIs** (escitalopram, sertraline, paroxetine, fluvoxamine; try a second SSRI if the first fails) → **venlafaxine XR 75–225 mg/day** |
| Second line | Olanzapine 5–20 mg; clonazepam 0.3–6 mg (prn; sertraline + clonazepam); gabapentin 900–3600 mg; pregabalin 150–600 mg; **moclobemide 600 mg** and **phenelzine** (NICE-recommended) |
| Performance anxiety | **Atenolol/propranolol** prn reduces autonomic symptoms in performance situations, probably not effective for generalised social anxiety |

- Start the SSRI at a **low dose and titrate**; anxious patients are sensitive to early activation; assess at 2–4 weeks; full effect over 8–12 weeks; continue for **at least 6–12 months** after response.
- Benzodiazepines are a second-line option in social anxiety in general, but **not appropriate for this patient** because of established dependence and tolerance; avoid gabapentinoids/pregabalin as substitutes because of their own dependence and misuse risks [1].

**B. Benzodiazepine withdrawal (tapering) [1]**

Principles:
- **Never stop abruptly** — risk of **seizures, delirium, psychosis**; withdrawal can include akathisia and new suicidality [1].
- Withdrawal should be **agreed with the patient**, not imposed; prepare with education and support (psychological therapy, self-help groups) [1].
- **Start the SSRI first** and allow it to work before tapering, so that underlying anxiety is covered.

Steps:
1. **Stabilise** on a fixed, scheduled dose (not prn) — establish total daily dose.
2. **Switch to diazepam** (long half-life, smoother withdrawal) — **stepwise switch**, one dose at a time over 1–2 weeks; approximate equivalence **alprazolam 0.5 mg ≈ diazepam 10 mg** (Ashton; alprazolam is not in the Maudsley equivalence table [1]) — adjust to symptoms. Direct taper of alprazolam is an alternative but needs multiple daily doses [1].
3. **Taper hyperbolically:** reduce by about **10% of the most recent dose every 2–4 weeks / monthly**, so reductions get smaller as the dose falls; final doses may be **below 1 mg diazepam** [1].
4. Expected duration: **at least 10 weeks**, often many months [1].
5. **Inpatient detoxification** if very high doses, past withdrawal seizures, alcohol co-dependence or repeated failure as an outpatient.
6. Monitor withdrawal symptoms (CIWA-B), mood and suicidality at each step; slow or pause if symptoms are severe; **distinguish withdrawal from relapse** — withdrawal symptoms appear soon after a reduction and new symptoms (e.g. paraesthesia, sensory hypersensitivity) favour withdrawal [1].

**C. Adjuncts**
- Short-term propranolol for autonomic symptoms during taper; sleep hygiene rather than z-drugs; treat comorbid depression with the same SSRI.

### 3. Psychosocial intervention, long-term prognosis and strategy

**A. Psychosocial interventions**
1. **Psychoeducation and motivational interviewing:** link between alprazolam, tolerance, rebound anxiety and worsening avoidance; build motivation for taper; collaborative goal setting.
2. **CBT for social anxiety — first-line treatment** (NICE: individual CBT, Clark & Wells or Heimberg model) — more effective than drugs, with more durable gains:
   - Cognitive model: **negative self-image, self-focused attention, safety behaviours, anticipatory and post-event processing (rumination)**.
   - Techniques: video feedback, attention training, behavioural experiments dropping safety behaviours, **graded exposure** to social and performance situations, cognitive restructuring of beliefs ("people will think I'm incompetent").
3. **CBT for benzodiazepine discontinuation** — improves cessation rates when combined with tapering (author's knowledge); relaxation, coping with withdrawal anxiety.
4. **Social skills training** and assertiveness training if skills deficits exist.
5. **Schema-focused or psychodynamic therapy** for long-standing avoidant traits and the impact of punitive upbringing (shame, defectiveness schema).
6. **Occupational intervention:** discourage resigning during acute treatment; graded return to presentations; workplace accommodation if needed.
7. **Self-help groups** and family involvement.

**B. Long-term strategy**
- Phase 1 (0–8 weeks): engagement, SSRI start, stabilise benzodiazepine, CBT begins.
- Phase 2 (2–6 months): hyperbolic taper to zero with ongoing CBT exposure work.
- Phase 3 (6–24 months): maintain SSRI for 12 months after response then slow taper; booster CBT; relapse prevention plan; watch for **protracted withdrawal** (can last over a year) [1] and substitution with alcohol or other sedatives.
- No further benzodiazepine prescriptions; coordinate with GP and pharmacy; avoid fixed-dose SSRI–benzodiazepine combinations.

**C. Prognosis**

| Favourable | Unfavourable |
| --- | --- |
| Educated, employed, help-seeking | **Early-onset, chronic** social anxiety (childhood inhibition) |
| Insight; recognises loss of alprazolam effect | **Avoidant personality features** — slower response |
| No mention of other substances | **10 years of benzodiazepine use**, tolerance — difficult taper |
| CBT and SSRIs effective | Punitive upbringing; low self-esteem |
| | Severe functional impairment; risk of depression and alcohol misuse |

- Social anxiety untreated is **chronic** with low spontaneous remission; with CBT ± SSRI most patients improve substantially though residual symptoms are common; relapse is higher after stopping drugs than after CBT (author's knowledge).
- Benzodiazepine cessation: about 90% get withdrawal symptoms and many fail at first attempts, but gradual tapering improves success and **mental state often improves after withdrawal** [1].
- **Overall: fair** — good chance of meaningful improvement with combined CBT, SSRI and a slow taper; personality traits mean longer therapy and residual shyness.

**Conclusion.** The core problem is long-standing social anxiety disorder with avoidant traits, complicated by iatrogenic and self-maintained benzodiazepine dependence that now worsens his anxiety. Treat the anxiety with an SSRI and CBT, then withdraw alprazolam by a stepwise switch to diazepam and a slow hyperbolic taper, with psychological support, occupational rehabilitation and a long-term relapse-prevention plan.

## In depth

### Background

- Alprazolam is widely prescribed for panic disorder in some countries; it is short-acting and high-potency, with more severe withdrawal and inter-dose rebound than diazepam [1].
- **Physical dependence** results from repeated exposure and does not require misuse; **addiction** (craving, compulsion) occurs in about **2%** of users [1]. This patient shows features of both — tolerance plus self-escalation.
- Benzodiazepine use rose sharply; prescriptions of gabapentinoids rose seven-fold between 2010 and 2020 [1].

### Epidemiology

- Social anxiety disorder: lifetime prevalence about 7–12% in Western surveys, lower in Indian community studies; onset in early adolescence; chronic course; high comorbidity with depression and substance (especially alcohol and sedative) use (author's knowledge).

### Mechanism

- **Behavioural inhibition** (temperament) plus a **critical/punitive upbringing** → negative self-schemas and fear of evaluation.
- **Benzodiazepines** act as positive allosteric modulators at GABA-A receptors; long-term use → receptor down-regulation → tolerance and withdrawal; relationship between dose and receptor occupancy is **hyperbolic** — a 5 mg reduction from 50 mg diazepam lowers occupancy by 2.3 points, but from 5 mg by 18.3 points [1].
- **Negative reinforcement:** alprazolam relieves anxiety immediately, maintaining avoidance and preventing the learning that occurs in exposure.

### Long-term problems with benzodiazepines [1]

| Domain | Effects |
| --- | --- |
| Emotional | Depression, emotional numbing, paradoxical anxiety and panic, irritability |
| Cognitive | Memory, attention, reaction time (some persist after stopping) |
| Physical | Ataxia, dizziness, slurred speech, autonomic dysfunction |
| Morbidity | Road traffic accidents, falls; possible dementia, cancer, infections |
| Behavioural | Insomnia, avoidance/agoraphobia, disinhibition, suicidality, aggression |

### Withdrawal syndrome [1]

- **Physical:** stiffness, fatigue, GI upset, paraesthesia, flu-like symptoms, visual disturbance, sensory hypersensitivity, tremor, tachycardia, photophobia, **convulsions, confusion/delirium** (with rapid withdrawal), akathisia.
- **Psychological:** anxiety, insomnia, panic, nightmares, depersonalisation/derealisation, depression, paranoia, psychosis (rapid withdrawal), suicidal ideation, mania.
- 90% experience withdrawal; **42% on short half-life vs 32% on long half-life** drugs could not stop because of withdrawal.
- **Protracted withdrawal** may last more than a year (FDA boxed warning); "benzodiazepine-induced neural dysfunction".

### Diazepam equivalents (Maudsley Table 3.24) [1]

| Drug | Dose ≈ diazepam 10 mg |
| --- | --- |
| Chlordiazepoxide | 25 mg |
| Clonazepam | 0.5 mg |
| Lorazepam | 1 mg |
| Lormetazepam | 1–2 mg |
| Nitrazepam | 10 mg |
| Oxazepam | 20 mg |
| Temazepam | 20 mg |
| Alprazolam (Ashton, not in Maudsley table) | 0.5 mg |

- Extra caution in hepatic dysfunction: diazepam and other long-acting drugs may accumulate — consider oxazepam or lorazepam [1].
- Cochrane is lukewarm about switching to diazepam; a stepwise switch is prudent [1].

### Worked taper example (illustrative)

Alprazolam 3 mg/day ≈ diazepam 60 mg (cap and adjust to symptoms; many clinicians start lower, e.g. 30–40 mg, and titrate) → stepwise substitution over 2 weeks → reduce 10% of current dose every 2–4 weeks (60 → 54 → 49 → 44 … ) → below 5 mg use liquid diazepam for small steps → stop from below 1 mg.

### Evidence table

| Evidence | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Meta-analysis of tapering | Gradual dose reduction improves cessation compared with routine care; ≥10 weeks most successful [1] |
| Cluster RCT (educational intervention) | Face-to-face education increases benzodiazepine discontinuation [1] |
| Mayo-Wilson 2014 network meta-analysis | Individual CBT best psychological treatment; SSRIs/SNRIs best drugs for social anxiety [1] |
| SSRI vs venlafaxine vs clonazepam augmentation | Switching SSRI to venlafaxine no better than adding clonazepam [1] |

### Critical appraisal

- Benzodiazepines work for social anxiety in the short term, so stopping is a negotiation; imposing withdrawal reduces engagement [1].
- Equivalence tables are approximate; individual variation is large [1].
- Evidence on the optimal taper speed is limited; hyperbolic tapering is pharmacologically logical but less tested in trials.

### Indian context

- Alprazolam is easily available, sometimes without prescription, and in **fixed-dose combinations** with SSRIs; it is among the commonest sedatives of dependence seen in Indian de-addiction centres (author's knowledge).
- Under the **NDPS Act** benzodiazepines are psychotropic substances; pharmacy regulation is uneven.
- Group CBT and tele-psychotherapy can extend access to CBT for social anxiety; workplace stigma may delay help-seeking.

### Recent advances

- **Hyperbolic tapering** guidance (Maudsley Deprescribing Guidelines, 2024) [1].
- Internet-delivered CBT for social anxiety; virtual-reality exposure.
- D-cycloserine augmentation of exposure — mixed results (author's knowledge).
