## Exam answer

### Framework [1]
- **Baseline risks:** miscarriage **10–20%**; major malformation **2–3% (1 in 40)**.
- **Maternal mental illness itself** independently raises the risk of malformations, stillbirth and neonatal death, and causes long-term child effects. Smoking, alcohol, obesity and illicit drugs **confound** most studies.
- **Outcomes to consider:**
  1. **teratogenicity** (first trimester; organogenesis);
  2. **obstetric/growth effects** (preterm birth, birth weight, gestational diabetes, haemorrhage);
  3. **neonatal toxicity or withdrawal** (late-pregnancy exposure);
  4. **long-term neurodevelopment**.
- Data are **observational** (databases, teratology services, case reports), and conclusions **shift as evidence accumulates**.

### Outcomes by drug class [1]

| Class | Malformations | Pregnancy/growth | Neonatal (late exposure) | Neurodevelopment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **FGAs** | Not major teratogens (haloperidol–limb defects unreplicated) | Gestational diabetes, preterm birth, **low birth weight**, postpartum bleeding | **Somnolence and jitteriness (20%)**, dyskinesia, jaundice (phenothiazines), withdrawal, PPHN (low absolute risk) | No clear effect |
| **SGAs** | Not major teratogens; signals: risperidone, olanzapine (clefts), gastroschisis; **cariprazine** animal malformations (avoid) | **Gestational diabetes** (clozapine, olanzapine, quetiapine), large for gestational age, macrosomia (olanzapine), caesarean section | Withdrawal (15% in one study), **seizures (clozapine)**, theoretical agranulocytosis (clozapine), PPHN | Early delays resolve by 12 months; ADHD/autism linked to maternal illness, not the drug; aripiprazole signal |
| **TCAs** | No apparent harm; weak clomipramine–cardiac link | **Preterm birth** | **Withdrawal**: agitation, irritability, **seizures**, respiratory distress, metabolic disturbance; QT prolongation (case reports) | No adverse effect in limited data |
| **SSRIs** | Not major teratogens; cardiac defect link (paroxetine, fluoxetine) inconsistent and possibly due to illness | Small rise in preterm birth, low birth weight, low Apgar, NICU; pre-eclampsia; **postpartum haemorrhage** (exposure in the month before delivery: MHRA) | **Poor neonatal adaptation syndrome** (more with prematurity and higher dose, **less with sertraline**); **PPHN** (late exposure; small absolute risk) | ASD link not significant after confounder control; no reliable ADHD link; brain-morphology changes of unclear meaning |
| **SNRIs** (venlafaxine, duloxetine) | Not major teratogens (2022 meta-analysis) | Small for gestational age, **postpartum haemorrhage** | Withdrawal, poor adaptation, PPHN (low risk) | — |
| **Mirtazapine, bupropion, trazodone** | No malformation signal; bupropion: small VSD risk | Possible miscarriage (may be the illness) | — | **Bupropion: ADHD** association |
| **MAOIs** | Suspected malformations (avoid) | Hypertensive crisis | — | — |
| **Lithium** | Ebstein's anomaly (smaller than thought), septal defects; **peak risk 2–6 weeks after conception** | Preterm birth, large for gestational age (inconsistent) | Goitre, **hypotonia, lethargy**, arrhythmia, respiratory problems, low Apgar | Probably unaffected |
| **Valproate** | **About 10%**: neural tube defects, cardiac defects, clefts, hypospadias, craniosynostosis; small head | Pre-eclampsia | Low Apgar | **Up to 40% developmental delay**, lower IQ, autism |
| **Carbamazepine** | Neural tube defects (dose-related) | — | Vitamin K needed for mother (third trimester) and neonate; low Apgar | Better than valproate |
| **Lamotrigine** | Within background rates | — | — | No significant effect |
| **Topiramate** | **Oral clefts** | Small for gestational age | Low Apgar | Neurodevelopmental disorders |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Oral clefts (inconsistent) | Caesarean section, miscarriage, preterm birth, low birth weight, small head; **stillbirth and preterm birth with late exposure** | **Floppy infant syndrome**, NICU, ventilatory support | No association |
| **Z-drugs** | Probably none | Preterm birth, low birth weight, small for gestational age; zolpidem: caesarean section | — | No association |
| **Stimulants** | Methylphenidate: small cardiac risk; **modafinil: malformations (avoid; contraception for 2 months after stopping)** | Amfetamines: preterm birth, low birth weight; methylphenidate: miscarriage | — | No increase |

### Minimising neonatal harm [1]
- **Pre-conception planning**:
  - avoid valproate, carbamazepine and topiramate in women who could conceive;
  - **folic acid before conception**;
  - discuss breastfeeding early.
- **First trimester:** avoid drugs where possible; use established drugs at the lowest dose, as **monotherapy**.
- **Do not stop abruptly** in high-risk women: relapse may harm mother and child more than the drug.
- **Fetal screening:** detailed ultrasound or **fetal echocardiography** (lithium, anticonvulsants, antipsychotics).
- **Third trimester:** adjust doses for changed pharmacokinetics (blood volume up 30%, CYP2D6 up, CYP1A2 down); monitor lithium and lamotrigine levels.
- **Delivery:**
  - inform the obstetric and neonatal teams;
  - deliver in a unit with **neonatal/paediatric intensive care**;
  - stop lithium in labour;
  - give **vitamin K** with carbamazepine.
- **After birth:**
  - **monitor the neonate for withdrawal and poor adaptation** (NICE: extra monitoring after SSRI/SNRI exposure);
  - continuing the same drug through breastfeeding may **soften neonatal withdrawal** (except clozapine and lithium: avoid breastfeeding).

### Update from IPS CPG 2022 [2]
**Weighing risk (four considerations):**
1. the risks of untreated maternal illness;
2. the benefits of avoiding exposure;
3. the benefits of controlling the illness;
4. the risks of the medication.

**Untreated depression or SMI** brings:
- poor antenatal care, self-neglect and **neglect of the infant**;
- suicidal or **infanticidal** risk; poor nutrition;
- domestic violence, substance use;
- **preterm labour, low birth weight and stillbirth**.

**"Confounding by indication":** registry data suggest that some adverse outcomes come from the **underlying illness, not the drug**.

**Labelling:**
- the FDA letter categories (1979–2015) were criticised as **simplistic and misleading**;
- they were replaced in June 2015 by the **Pregnancy and Lactation Labelling Rule**, with three headings: pregnancy, lactation, and females and males of reproductive potential.
- Old categories (Table 7) are given for reference:
  - **B:** clozapine, zolpidem, bupropion, buspirone;
  - **C:** most antipsychotics and antidepressants (except paroxetine), lamotrigine;
  - **D:** lithium, valproate, carbamazepine, paroxetine, most benzodiazepines;
  - **X:** temazepam, estazolam, flurazepam.

**Risks by class (Table 8):**

| Class | Risks |
| --- | --- |
| Antidepressants | **No increase in major malformations** (data mostly SSRIs). Spontaneous abortion, gestational hypertension or pre-eclampsia, low Apgar, low birth weight. **Third-trimester neonatal withdrawal**. These risks are "minimal, not clinically significant, and confounded by underlying depression" |
| SSRIs | **Cardiac septal defects** with first-trimester exposure (mostly **paroxetine**). **PPHN** with third-trimester exposure, absolute risk **3 per 1000**. Slight rise in **postpartum haemorrhage**. No rise in aneuploidy. **Sertraline has the least placental exposure**; avoid paroxetine |
| TCAs | Fetal exposure is **high**. **Avoid doxepin** |
| MAOIs | Malformations and hypertensive crisis; avoid |
| SGAs | No rise in major malformations. **Maternal weight gain, gestational diabetes, higher birth weight**. Monitor weight, OGTT and fetal growth scans. **Clozapine:** floppy infant; monitor for agranulocytosis |
| FGAs | No major malformation. Low birth weight, preterm delivery. **Third-trimester EPS and withdrawal**. High-potency drugs preferred |
| Lithium | **Ebstein's anomaly 1/1000** |
| Valproate | Major anomalies; **neural tube defects 6–9%**; **intellectual disability** in the child |
| Carbamazepine | Increased malformations |
| Lamotrigine | No rise in major malformations. Needs **therapeutic drug monitoring** |
| Benzodiazepines | Perinatal toxicity, low Apgar, hypotonia, poor feeding, **cleft** defects. **Floppy infant** if given just before delivery. Taper; intermittent use is less likely to cause withdrawal; prefer lorazepam |

**Practical steps:**
- **anomaly scans at 10–12 weeks (level I) and 16–18 weeks (level II)** for all mothers on psychotropics;
- **fetal echocardiography** with lithium, SSRIs or other drugs with a cardiac risk;
- **plan delivery** where neonatal intensive care is available;
- **folic acid 5 mg/day** for all women planning pregnancy.

**Developmental delay** in exposed infants is usually **mild and reversible**, catching up after weaning. It is commoner in low-birth-weight or premature infants and with older mothers. Advise **sensorimotor stimulation** (massage, oil bath, lullabies, talking, toys) and an extra attachment figure if the mother is sedated or has negative symptoms.

⚠️ **Discrepancies (not resolved):**
- **Valproate:** IPS gives neural tube defects at 6–9%; Maudsley gives about 10% for all major malformations. These are different measures.
- **Lithium:** IPS gives Ebstein's at 1/1000; Maudsley says only "much smaller than previously estimated".
- **FGAs:** IPS's table says "high potency preferred", but its text says FGAs are **not initiated** in pregnancy "because there is less safety data". This is internally inconsistent, and Maudsley's data (large US and Nordic studies) show no meaningful malformation increase.

**Conclusion.** Most antipsychotics and antidepressants are not major teratogens. Their main neonatal risks are transient poor neonatal adaptation and withdrawal, PPHN (SSRIs, late exposure, low absolute risk), preterm birth and lower birth weight, and metabolic effects (SGAs). The drugs with clear teratogenic and neurodevelopmental harm are **valproate, carbamazepine and topiramate**, with lithium's cardiac risk now thought smaller. Benzodiazepines in late pregnancy cause floppy infant syndrome. Maternal illness itself confounds and contributes to many of these outcomes, so the risks of the drug must be weighed against the risks of untreated illness.
