## Exam answer

### Definition [1]
- **RLS** (Ekbom 1945) is a sensorimotor disorder with:
  - **deep, creeping, unpleasant or unbearable dysaesthesiae** (also cold, discomfort or weakness), **mostly in the calves**;
  - an **irresistible urge to move the legs** and inability to keep them still;
  - **evening and pre-sleep worsening**, sometimes persisting into light sleep;
  - **temporary relief** from rapid exercise, walking, rubbing or kicking.
- Curiously, symptoms may be **abolished by fever**.

### Epidemiology and course [1]
- Onset usually in the **second decade**; slowly progressive with wide fluctuations.
- **Family history in up to 60%**; may be **autosomal dominant**.
- Severity increases with **age, sleep deprivation and stress**.
- CSF hypocretin-1 (orexin) is **raised**, especially in early-onset cases.

### Causes and associations [1]
- **Primary/familial.**
- **Secondary:**
  - **anaemia** and deficiency states, malnutrition;
  - **uraemia**;
  - **diabetes**, subclinical sensory neuropathy, amyloidosis;
  - motor neurone disease, poliomyelitis;
  - **pregnancy**.
- **Drug and substance triggers:** **caffeine** and other CNS stimulants, **alcohol**, **antidepressants**.
- **Periodic limb movement syndrome (PLMS)** is very strongly associated with both familial and non-familial RLS.

### Consequences [1]
- **Sleep-onset insomnia** and **daytime sleepiness**.
- Severe discomfort with **emotional disturbance**.
- **Bed partners** kicked or struck (with PLMS).

### Periodic limb movement syndrome [1]
- About **3 times commoner** than RLS.
- **Brief (0.5–5 s) stereotyped movements every 20–40 s**, in runs of minutes to an hour; **NREM only**, **not at sleep onset**, not during waking.
- **2% of the elderly; up to 15% of insomnia patients.** Onset rarely before middle age; prevalence rises with age.
- Arousals cause **insomnia and excessive daytime sleepiness**; decreased stage 4.
- **Differentiate from:** epileptic myoclonus; jerking and restlessness in **sleep apnoea**. **EMG** helps.

### Differential diagnosis [1, 2]
- **Neuroleptic-induced akathisia:** whole-body restlessness. RLS has **prominent sensory discomfort and motor "impatience"**, affects the **legs (less often arms)**, and is evening-predominant. Akathisia usually involves the **whole body** [1]. There is a phenomenological overlap between akathisia and RLS, and possibly an overlap in pathophysiology [2].
- **Nocturnal cramps**, neuropathy, positional discomfort.
- PLMS (often coexists); sleep apnoea.
- Anxiety-related restlessness.

### Assessment [1]
- **History:** urge, sensations, evening pattern, relief by movement; family history; **drugs** (antidepressants, antipsychotics), caffeine, alcohol; pregnancy.
- **Medical screen:** **anaemia and deficiency states**, renal failure, diabetes, neuropathy. *(Ferritin threshold not in source.)*
- **Polysomnography / EMG** if PLMS or apnoea is suspected.

### Management [1, 2]
1. **Treat underlying causes:** anaemia and deficiencies, uraemia, neuropathy.
2. **Remove aggravators:** caffeine, alcohol, stimulants; review **antidepressants**.
3. **Sleep hygiene;** avoid sleep deprivation.
4. **Non-drug measures** (PLMS/RLS): **warm bath at bedtime**, **gentle leg stretches**, **relaxation** before sleep [1].
5. **Dopaminergic drugs** benefit some patients [1]. *(Specific agents, doses and augmentation are not in the source.)*
6. **CKD / haemodialysis:** **gabapentin** has been used for restless legs; dosing adjusted to renal function [2].
7. **Akathisia vs RLS** in psychiatric patients: recognise akathisia (reduce or switch the antipsychotic) rather than misattributing it [2].

**Conclusion.** RLS is a common, often familial, sensorimotor disorder: an evening urge to move the legs with deep dysaesthesia, relieved by movement, causing sleep-onset insomnia. It is strongly linked to periodic limb movements. Secondary causes (anaemia/deficiency, uraemia, neuropathy, pregnancy) and aggravators (caffeine, alcohol, antidepressants) must be sought. In psychiatric practice it must be distinguished from neuroleptic akathisia. Management is the cause, sleep hygiene, physical measures, and dopaminergic drugs (gabapentin in renal failure). **The modern treatment hierarchy (iron, α2δ ligands, augmentation) is not covered by the sources.**
