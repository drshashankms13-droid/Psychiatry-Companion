## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- **Definition:** tauopathies are neurodegenerative disorders defined by **intracellular aggregates of abnormal, hyperphosphorylated tau** [1].
- **Grouping by protein:** Lishman groups them by **shared molecular pathology** (Lee 2001), in contrast to synucleinopathies (PD, DLB, MSA) and amyloidopathies (prion disease, AD).
- **Why it matters:**
  - it classifies by **mechanism** rather than symptoms;
  - **one pathology can produce very different clinical pictures** depending on location (e.g., PSP vs FTD);
  - it suggests **shared therapies** (e.g., tau-aggregation inhibitors).

### Tau biology [1]
- **Normal tau:**
  - a **microtubule-associated protein**, mainly axonal;
  - stabilises microtubules, which are essential for **fast axonal transport** (the synapse depends on proteins made in the cell body).
- **Phosphorylation:**
  - regulates microtubule binding;
  - kinases include **GSK-3** (inhibited by lithium).
- **Isoforms:**
  - **six**, from alternative splicing of one gene (*MAPT*);
  - **3 or 4 microtubule-binding repeats (3R/4R)**, with 0–2 N-terminal inserts;
  - the normal brain has **3R:4R about 1:1**.
- **Pathogenesis:**
  - hyperphosphorylated or mutant tau **detaches from microtubules**, redistributes from the axon to the cell body, aggregates into insoluble filaments, and disrupts **axonal transport**, leading to neuronal dysfunction and loss.
  - **Braak:** increased phosphorylation and somatodendritic redistribution occur **years to decades before** clinical dementia.
- ***MAPT* mutations:**
  - **in or near the microtubule-binding domains** impair function;
  - **exon 10 splice-site mutations** alter the **3R:4R ratio**;
  - so the *ratio*, not absolute amount, appears critical.

### Classification of tauopathies [1, 2]

| Disorder | Tau isoform and lesion | Clinical picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alzheimer's disease** (secondary tauopathy; also amyloidopathy) | 3R + 4R; **neurofibrillary tangles** (paired helical filaments), neuropil threads, dystrophic neurites | Amnestic dementia (tangles correlate best with severity) |
| **Pick's disease** (FTD subtype) | **3R**; **Pick bodies** (tau- and ubiquitin-positive), ballooned neurones, gliosis | Behavioural or language FTD |
| **FTDP-17** (*MAPT* mutations) | Variable (3R/4R imbalance) | Familial FTD with parkinsonism |
| **Progressive supranuclear palsy** | **4R** (3–4× excess); **straight-filament** tangles (not paired helical), **glial tau**; basal ganglia, brainstem, cerebellar nuclei; cortex spared; **H1/H1 haplotype** (89% vs 65%) | Vertical (down-) gaze palsy, axial rigidity, backward falls, pseudobulbar palsy, **subcortical dementia**, apathy, disinhibition |
| **Corticobasal degeneration** | **4R**; achromatic **ballooned neurones**, globose tangles, basophilic inclusions; perirolandic cortex plus basal ganglia; **H1** (93%) | **Asymmetric** akinetic-rigid syndrome, **limb apraxia, alien limb**, myoclonus, cortical sensory loss; depression; dementia in about one-third |
| **Dementia pugilistica** (repetitive head injury, boxers) | Tau aggregates resembling AD, plus Aβ | Dementia after repeated blows |
| **Gerstmann–Sträussler–Scheinker disease** | PrP plaques **plus neurofibrillary tangles** | Ataxia, extrapyramidal signs, dementia |
| **Postencephalitic parkinsonism**, **parkinsonism–dementia complex of Guam** | Neurofibrillary tangles (rather than Lewy bodies) | Parkinsonism ± dementia |

**Tau-negative FTD:** many FTD cases are **tau-negative, ubiquitin-positive**. DSM-5-TR identifies **TDP-43 (*TARDBP*)**, *GRN*, *C9orf72* and *FUS*; these are *not* tauopathies [2].

**PSP and CBD** are now considered **FTD-related**. They share 4R tau and H1 predisposition but are pathologically **distinct entities** [1].

### Clinical and therapeutic implications [1, 2]
- **Clinical overlap:** FTD ↔ PSP ↔ CBD ↔ MND. Overlapping cognitive (frontal) and language deficits.
- **Diagnosis:**
  - clinical syndrome plus imaging (frontotemporal atrophy; PSP midbrain atrophy under 17 mm; asymmetric CBD atrophy and PET);
  - **genetic testing** (*MAPT*) in familial cases.
- **Treatment:**
  - **no disease-modifying therapy** yet;
  - **levodopa responses poor** in PSP and CBD;
  - NICE advises **no AChE-Is or memantine in FTD**.
- **Future directions:**
  - **GSK-3 inhibitors** to break the amyloid–tau link in AD;
  - **tau-aggregation inhibitors** potentially useful across tauopathies [1].

**Conclusion.** Tauopathies are disorders in which abnormal, hyperphosphorylated tau aggregates disrupt microtubule-dependent axonal transport. They include:
- **AD** (3R + 4R tangles, with amyloid);
- **Pick's disease** (3R);
- **PSP and CBD** (4R; H1 haplotype);
- **FTDP-17** (*MAPT* mutations);
- dementia pugilistica and GSS.

The concept groups clinically diverse syndromes (dementia, aphasia, parkinsonism, gaze palsy, apraxia) by shared molecular mechanism. It overlaps with other proteinopathies, and points to future tau-targeted therapies.
