## Exam answer

Marriage may provide support, practical help, and social integration, but can also involve conflict, coercion, violence, caregiver burden, and stigma. The relationship is bidirectional: symptoms can affect communication, intimacy, finances, parenting, sexuality, and household roles; marital conflict, abuse, separation, or bereavement can worsen symptoms. Mania may involve impulsivity and irritability; depression may reduce energy and desire; psychosis may affect trust; substance use can contribute to conflict.

**Assessment** should cover relationship quality, support, conflict, coercion, violence, sexual health, parenting, finances, and the partner's understanding of illness. Screen privately for intimate-partner violence when appropriate. Do not assume marriage is inherently therapeutic. Couple/family work may help when both parties consent and safety permits.

### Capacity and consent

Capacity is decision-specific and time-specific. Diagnosis alone does not establish incapacity. Assess ability to understand relevant information, appreciate consequences, reason about options, and communicate a choice. Severe mania, psychosis, intoxication, or cognitive impairment may temporarily affect decision-making, but assessment must be individualized. Marriage requires free and informed consent; family pressure cannot replace the person's choice.

### Indian legal/ethical context

The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 and Special Marriage Act, 1954 contain their own statutory conditions and must be interpreted in their legal context. Mental illness is not synonymous with legal incapacity. The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 reinforces equality and non-discrimination. Avoid blanket statements that a person with a psychiatric diagnosis cannot marry. Seek legal advice for case-specific questions.

### Role of psychiatrist

Explain diagnosis, prognosis, relapse signs, treatment, medication and pregnancy considerations when relevant. Encourage honest communication while respecting confidentiality and applicable law. Avoid issuing a simplistic "fitness to marry" certificate without a clear purpose and adequate assessment. Offer psychoeducation, couple intervention, safety planning, and referral as indicated.

### Conclusion

A rights-based approach emphasizes autonomy, informed consent, safety, individualized capacity assessment, and supportive relationships–-not diagnosis-based exclusion.
