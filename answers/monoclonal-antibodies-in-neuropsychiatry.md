## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs)** are laboratory-produced identical immunoglobulins (usually IgG). Each binds a single specific epitope. Their names indicate their type:
- **-ximab:** chimeric.
- **-zumab:** humanised.
- **-umab:** fully human.

Because they are highly target-specific with few off-target effects, they work as **precise therapeutic tools and as experimental probes** of disease mechanisms. Unlike NSAIDs, which have off-target effects (e.g., celecoxib modulates glucocorticoid receptors), mAbs can **directly test whether a specific inflammatory pathway causes psychopathology** [1].

In neuropsychiatry, mAbs are used in three ways:
1. **Established neurological therapy:** migraine, MS, NMOSD, myasthenia, autoimmune encephalitis.
2. **Disease modification in Alzheimer's disease:** anti-amyloid antibodies.
3. **Experimental immunotherapy in psychiatric disorders:** depression, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder.

They also cause **neuropsychiatric adverse effects**.

### Mechanism
- **Binding and neutralising** a soluble target (cytokine, CGRP, Aβ), or **blocking a receptor** (IL-6R, CGRP receptor, α4-integrin).
- **Depleting cells** (anti-CD20 B-cell depletion) through complement or antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.
- **Clearing aggregates** by microglial phagocytosis (anti-amyloid).
- **Blood-brain barrier:** large IgG molecules cross poorly (about 0.1%). Many act peripherally (CGRP, cytokines), or through the high doses and peripheral sink needed for CNS targets.

### Main uses

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Migraine prevention** | **Erenumab** (CGRP receptor), **fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab** (CGRP ligand) | CGRP pathway | Approved; about 50% responder rates; monthly or quarterly injection |
| **Multiple sclerosis** | **Natalizumab** (α4-integrin), **ocrelizumab, ofatumumab, ublituximab** (CD20), **alemtuzumab** (CD52) | Lymphocyte trafficking or depletion | Approved; natalizumab carries **PML** risk (JC virus) |
| **NMOSD** | **Eculizumab, ravulizumab** (C5), **inebilizumab** (CD19), **satralizumab** (IL-6R) | Complement, B cells, IL-6 | Approved |
| **Myasthenia gravis** | Eculizumab, ravulizumab, zilucoplan (peptide); rozanolixizumab, efgartigimod (FcRn) | Complement, FcRn | Approved |
| **Autoimmune encephalitis** (anti-NMDAR, LGI1) | **Rituximab** (2nd line); tocilizumab in refractory cases | CD20, IL-6R | Standard second-line; improves outcomes and reduces relapse |
| **Alzheimer's disease** | **Lecanemab, donanemab** (aducanumab withdrawn) | Aβ protofibrils or plaque | Approved for early AD; slows decline about 25–35%; **ARIA** risk |
| **Depression (experimental)** | **Infliximab** (TNF-α); sirukumab, tocilizumab (IL-6) | Cytokines | **Raison 2013:** no overall benefit in TRD, but **benefit in high-CRP (>5 mg/L) subgroup** [1] |
| **Schizophrenia (experimental)** | **Tocilizumab** (IL-6R), siltuximab (IL-6), **canakinumab** (IL-1β; CATS), **natalizumab** | Cytokines, trafficking | Open-label tocilizumab: improved **cognition** (n=6) [1]; later RCT (Girgis 2018) negative for symptoms |
| **Bipolar depression (experimental)** | Infliximab | TNF-α | McIntyre 2019 RCT: no overall benefit; benefit with childhood maltreatment |
| **Substance use (experimental)** | Anti-cocaine, anti-methamphetamine, anti-nicotine antibodies or vaccines | Sequester drug in blood | Failed or limited |

### Inflammation hypothesis in psychiatry [1]
- **Meta-analyses** show ↑ IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β and CRP in schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and MDD, with a **similar pattern across disorders** and **state-dependent elevations** during acute episodes (Goldsmith 2016).
- **Cytokine-based treatments** (interferon-α for hepatitis C, melanoma or MS) commonly induce **depression and psychosis**. This supports causality.
- **Adjunctive NSAIDs** (celecoxib) improved symptoms in some trials, but results are inconsistent. A key reason may be that **inflammation was not an inclusion criterion**.

### Trial design lessons [1]
- **Enrich for inflammation** (e.g., CRP-stratified inclusion) to improve response and signal-to-noise.
- Consider **illness phase**: acute episodes vs stable illness are inflammatory states that should be studied separately.
- Decide on **treatment duration and goals**: lowering baseline symptoms, or speeding response to standard drugs?
- Weigh **long-term risks**: demyelination, infections, ulcers, malignancy.

### Neuropsychiatric adverse effects of mAbs
- **Natalizumab:** PML (cognitive decline, behavioural change).
- **Anti-TNF:** demyelination, depression (rare).
- **Anti-amyloid:** ARIA (headache, confusion, seizures).
- **Checkpoint inhibitors** (ipilimumab, nivolumab): autoimmune encephalitis, hypophysitis, mood change.
- **Interferon** (not a mAb): depression and suicidality.
- **CAR-T/bispecifics:** ICANS (immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity).

**Conclusion.** Monoclonal antibodies have **transformed neurology**: migraine prevention, MS, NMOSD, myasthenia and autoimmune encephalitis. They have brought the **first modest disease modification in Alzheimer's disease**. In psychiatry they remain **investigational**, but they offer a uniquely precise way to test the **inflammation hypothesis**, especially in an **inflamed subgroup** (e.g., high-CRP depression). Future trials must stratify by biomarkers, consider illness phase, and weigh long-term immune risks. Psychiatrists also need to recognise mAb-related neuropsychiatric adverse effects.

## In depth

### Background
- **1975:** Köhler and Milstein developed **hybridoma technology** (Nobel Prize 1984).
- **1986:** the first therapeutic mAb (muromonab-CD3).
- **Humanisation (1990s)** reduced immunogenicity.
- **Neurology:** natalizumab (2004; withdrawn and reintroduced over PML); CGRP mAbs (2018); lecanemab (2023).
- **Psychiatry:** Raison et al. 2013 (infliximab in TRD) was a landmark proof-of-concept trial [1].

### Epidemiology: inflammation in psychiatric disorders
- About **a quarter to a third of people with depression have low-grade inflammation** (CRP >3 mg/L; Osimo 2019 meta-analysis).
- Elevated CRP is associated with treatment resistance.
- In schizophrenia, about 20–30% are estimated to have elevated inflammatory markers.

### Classification and criteria: mAb nomenclature
| Suffix | Source | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -omab | Mouse | Muromonab |
| -ximab | Chimeric | Infliximab, rituximab |
| -zumab | Humanised | Natalizumab, tocilizumab, ocrelizumab, fremanezumab |
| -umab | Fully human | Adalimumab, erenumab, canakinumab, lecanemab |

(A new 2021 WHO naming scheme uses -tug, -bart, -mig, -ment; for example, donanemab-azbt.)

### Mechanism in detail: cytokines → psychopathology
Peripheral cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β) reach the brain by several routes:
- humoral pathways (leaky circumventricular organs, active transport);
- neural pathways (vagal afferents);
- cellular pathways (monocyte trafficking).

They then cause:
- **microglial activation**;
- **IDO induction**, which diverts tryptophan from serotonin to kynurenine and quinolinic acid (NMDA agonist, neurotoxic);
- reduced BDNF and neurogenesis;
- HPA dysregulation (glucocorticoid resistance);
- **basal ganglia dopamine reduction**.

The clinical result is **anhedonia, fatigue, psychomotor slowing and cognitive dysfunction** ("sickness behaviour").

### Clinical features in detail: who might benefit
- The **"inflamed depression" phenotype:** high CRP, obesity, metabolic syndrome, childhood maltreatment, treatment resistance, prominent anhedonia, fatigue and psychomotor slowing.
- **Schizophrenia with inflammation:** cognitive impairment, negative symptoms, treatment resistance.

### Assessment in detail: before mAb therapy (neurology use)
- Infection screen: **TB (IGRA)**, hepatitis B and C, HIV, JC virus antibody (natalizumab).
- Vaccination status; pregnancy plans.
- Baseline MRI (MS, anti-amyloid).
- **APOE genotyping** (anti-amyloid).
- Psychiatric history: depression and suicidality (some agents).

### Differential: anti-inflammatory strategies in psychiatry
| Agent | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Celecoxib (adjunct, MDD) | Meta-analyses positive but small and heterogeneous (Köhler 2014) [1] |
| Minocycline | Mixed; negative large schizophrenia trial (BeneMin) |
| N-acetylcysteine | Mixed |
| Omega-3 fatty acids | Mixed |
| Statins | Modest adjunct evidence |
| mAbs | Experimental; biomarker-stratified [1] |

### Management details: practical psychiatry interface
- **Screen for depression** before interferon or other immunotherapy; monitor during treatment; treat promptly (SSRIs).
- **Interactions:** mAbs have few pharmacokinetic interactions with psychotropics, but **infection risk** with immunosuppression interacts with clozapine-related neutropenia.
- **Autoimmune encephalitis** presenting as psychosis: involve neurology early; rituximab is often needed. Use antipsychotics cautiously (NMS-like reactions).
- **Anti-amyloid therapy:** psychiatrists may assess eligibility, capacity and consent, and manage BPSD and ARIA-related confusion.

### Critical appraisal
- **Psychiatric evidence is preliminary:** small trials, heterogeneous populations, inconsistent results.
- **Biomarker uncertainty:** CRP is a crude marker. The optimal threshold and target cytokine are unknown.
- **Safety and cost:** infections, malignancy, demyelination; very high cost [1].
- **Brain penetrance:** most mAbs barely cross the BBB, so are effects peripheral or central?
- **Strength:** mAbs offer **mechanistic precision**, so even negative trials inform pathophysiology [1].

### Indian context
- **Neurology:** CGRP mAbs (erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab) and anti-CD20 agents are available in India at high cost. Biosimilar rituximab is widely used, including for autoimmune encephalitis.
- **Alzheimer's:** donanemab launched in India in 2026; access is limited by cost and PET/MRI availability.
- **Psychiatry:** no mAb is approved for psychiatric indications. Research groups (e.g., NIMHANS neuroimmunology) study autoimmune psychosis and inflammatory markers.
- **TB and hepatitis B screening** is essential before immunosuppressive mAbs in India (high prevalence).

### Recent advances
- **Established:** CGRP mAbs; anti-CD20 in MS; complement and FcRn agents; anti-amyloid mAbs.
- **Emerging:**
  - **Brain-shuttle antibodies** (transferrin-receptor bispecifics such as trontinemab), which improve BBB penetration and reduce ARIA in early data.
  - Anti-tau antibodies (mixed results).
  - Biomarker-stratified immunopsychiatry trials (e.g., IL-6 inhibition in high-CRP depression; the INSPIRE trial).
- **Investigational:** canakinumab in schizophrenia (CATS); sirukumab in MDD (development stopped) [1].
