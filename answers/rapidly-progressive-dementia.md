Modern content (the definition, the **VITAMINS** framework, autoimmune encephalitis, RT-QuIC) has been added from **general knowledge** and tagged as such. ## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- **What it is:** most primary dementias evolve over **many months or years**. A **short duration** at presentation "immediately raises the possibility" of a **secondary** dementia or a prion disease [1].
- **Definition** (not given in Lishman) [general knowledge]:
  - a dementia that develops **within 1–2 years of the first symptom**, usually **over weeks to months**;
  - some authors use **less than 1 year to dementia** or **to death**.
- **Why it matters:** in tertiary RPD series (UCSF), about **60% were prion disease**. Among non-prion cases, **autoimmune encephalitis and other treatable causes** were common. **Every RPD is a neurological emergency until a treatable cause is excluded** [general knowledge].

### Causes suggested by the source [1]

| Category | Causes named in Lishman |
| --- | --- |
| **Prion** | **Sporadic CJD** (death usually within 2 years; myoclonus, ataxia, visual and pyramidal signs; psychiatric prodrome); **variant CJD** (young, psychiatric onset); familial and iatrogenic CJD |
| **Structural** | **Cerebral tumour** (including frontal meningioma without focal signs); **subdural haematoma** (even after slight head injury; fluctuation); covert **cerebral infarction**; **cerebral abscess** |
| **Infective / inflammatory** | **AIDS**; **general paresis** (neurosyphilis); **subacute encephalitis**; **collagen-vascular disease/vasculitis**; multiple emboli in **subacute bacterial endocarditis**; Whipple's disease; sarcoid |
| **Metabolic / toxic / anoxic** | Renal or hepatic failure, **hypoglycaemia**, myxoedema, parathyroid disease, **B12 and folate deficiency**, **Wernicke–Korsakoff**, **alcohol and drugs**, heavy metals, carbon monoxide, cerebral anoxia (anaemia, heart or lung disease), anaesthesia |
| **Neoplastic (remote)** | **Carcinoma of the lung** presenting with dementia without metastases |
| **Neurodegenerative with rapid course** | **DLB** (mean 1.8 years to death in one series; fluctuation, hallucinations; **lethal neuroleptic reactions**); FTD with MND |
| **Mimics** | **Delirium** (fluctuation, clouding); **depressive pseudodementia**; Ganser state; hysterical pseudodementia (the CJD case was first labelled this) |

### Modern framework: the "VITAMINS" mnemonic [general knowledge]
Geschwind's mnemonic organises the differential:

| Letter | Category | Key examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **V** | Vascular | Multi-infarct; strategic infarct; **CNS vasculitis** (primary or secondary); **cerebral venous thrombosis**; PRES; dural AV fistula; amyloid angiopathy-related inflammation |
| **I** | Infectious | **Neurosyphilis**; HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder; **PML (JC virus)**; HSV and other viral encephalitis; **TB and cryptococcal meningitis**; Whipple's; SSPE |
| **T** | Toxic-metabolic | **Wernicke** (thiamine); **B12**; hypothyroidism; uraemia; hepatic encephalopathy; **hyponatraemia**; hypercalcaemia; lithium, bismuth, mercury, arsenic, alcohol |
| **A** | **Autoimmune** | **Anti-LGI1** (faciobrachial dystonic seizures; hyponatraemia), **anti-NMDAR**, CASPR2, GABA-B, AMPA, **IgLON5**; **SREAT/Hashimoto**; neurosarcoid; **SLE**; Sjögren's; coeliac |
| **M** | Metastases / neoplasm | **Primary CNS lymphoma**; gliomatosis; intravascular lymphoma; metastases; **paraneoplastic (anti-Hu, Ma2, CV2)** limbic encephalitis |
| **I** | Iatrogenic / inborn errors | Drugs (anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids, methotrexate); adult-onset metabolic disorders (Wilson's, Kufs, leucodystrophies) |
| **N** | Neurodegenerative | **Prion (sCJD, vCJD, genetic, iatrogenic)**; rapid AD; **DLB**; FTD-MND; CBD; PSP |
| **S** | Systemic / seizures / structural / psychiatric | **Non-convulsive status epilepticus**; NPH; **subdural haematoma**; tumour; **depressive pseudodementia**; catatonia; delirium [1] |

### Approach [1]
**1. History (with informant):**
- **Tempo:**
  - is the onset truly sudden, or just newly noticed (holidays, infrequent contact)?
  - a **definite onset date** suggests tumour or infarction;
  - **fluctuation or remission** suggests delirium, subdural haematoma, drugs, vascular disease or hypoglycaemia.
- **First symptoms:** **non-memory onset** (personality, psychiatric) should raise suspicion (FTD, HD, frontal tumour, general paresis, CJD prodrome).
- **Red flags:**
  - headache, vomiting, visual disturbance (space-occupying lesion);
  - focal seizures;
  - head injury (subdural haematoma);
  - fits, faints or collapses;
  - cough or weight loss (lung cancer);
  - fever (encephalitis, abscess, vasculitis, endocarditis);
  - HIV risk; alcohol, drugs, occupational toxins;
  - gastrectomy or diet (B12);
  - **family history** (familial CJD, HD, FTD, early AD).

**2. Examination:**
- general: fever, myxoedema, malnutrition, endocarditis, malignancy;
- **neurological:**
  - **myoclonus** (CJD);
  - **ataxia** (CJD, Wernicke, vCJD);
  - focal or pyramidal signs (tumour, vascular);
  - parkinsonism (DLB);
  - chorea (HD);
  - Kayser–Fleischer rings (Wilson's);
  - pupils (general paresis);
  - papilloedema;
  - neuropathy;
  - **cortical blindness**.

**3. Mental state:**
- **clouding** points to delirium;
- psychiatric prodrome (CJD);
- **depression** (pseudodementia);
- inconsistent performance (Ganser or hysterical).

**4. Investigations:**
- **bloods:** FBC, urea and electrolytes, LFT, TFT, B12/folate, **syphilis serology**; HIV if indicated by history;
- urine microscopy and culture; CXR;
- **MRI** (DWI for CJD; tumour; subdural; vascular; pulvinar sign);
- **EEG** (periodic triphasic complexes in CJD; slowing in delirium and encephalopathy; normal in FTD);
- **CSF:** cells, protein, pressure, xanthochromia (subdural), syphilis serology, **14-3-3 and tau** (CJD), subacute encephalitis;
- special tests:
  - caeruloplasmin (Wilson's);
  - fresh blood film (neuroacanthocytosis);
  - arylsulphatase-A (metachromatic leucodystrophy);
  - biopsies (Kufs, Whipple's, mitochondrial, sarcoid);
  - genetic testing (PRNP, HTT);
  - **modern additions** [general knowledge]: **CSF RT-QuIC** (prion; specificity close to 100%); **serum and CSF autoimmune encephalitis panels** and paraneoplastic antibodies; anti-TPO; **CT chest, abdomen and pelvis or PET-CT** for occult malignancy; **continuous EEG** for non-convulsive status; brain biopsy if all else is unrevealing.

### Management [1]
- **Treat the remediable condition** identified:
  - tumour, subdural haematoma, NPH;
  - general paresis;
  - metabolic, endocrine and vitamin deficiencies;
  - drug or toxin effects;
  - corticosteroids for vasculitis [1].
  - The source does not detail specific treatments.
- **Suspected autoimmune encephalitis** [general knowledge]: start **empirical immunotherapy** (IV methylprednisolone, IVIg or plasma exchange) once infection is excluded. **Do not wait for antibody results** when the clinical picture fits (Graus 2016 criteria). Search for tumours.
- **Empirical thiamine** for every patient with possible Wernicke's [general knowledge].
- **DLB:** **avoid antipsychotics** (fatal sensitivity); cholinesterase inhibitors.
- **CJD and other prion disease:** supportive dementia care; prevent iatrogenic transmission; surveillance notification (see the CJD answer).
- **All patients:** general dementia management (information, carer support, legal planning, nursing care); **regular review and readiness to re-investigate** when diagnosis is uncertain or the course unusual.

**Conclusion.** A short history or rapid course in dementia should prompt a broad search for **secondary and treatable causes** (structural, infective, inflammatory, metabolic, toxic, neoplastic) and for **prion disease** (myoclonus, ataxia, periodic EEG, DWI-MRI, CSF 14-3-3). Delirium must be distinguished, and depression excluded. Management targets the cause; for prion and rapidly progressive degenerative disease, it is supportive.

## In depth

### Background
- Lishman's principle: "a short duration at presentation immediately raises the possibility" of a secondary dementia. This underlies the whole RPD concept [1].
- **The CJD surveillance era (1990s, after BSE)** created large referral cohorts of "suspected CJD". Many turned out to have **treatable non-prion disease** [general knowledge].
- **Geschwind's UCSF series** (about 2007 onwards) formalised RPD and the **VITAMINS** mnemonic [general knowledge].
- **Discovery of antibody-mediated encephalitides:**
  - anti-NMDAR (Dalmau 2007);
  - **LGI1** (2010);
  - IgLON5 (2014).
  These transformed the differential: some "CJD-like" cases are **immunotherapy-responsive** [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology [general knowledge]
- **Tertiary prion centre (UCSF):** about **62% prion disease**. Of non-prion RPD:
  - neurodegenerative (FTD, CBD, AD, DLB) about 40%;
  - **autoimmune about 20–30%**;
  - others: infections, malignancy, toxic-metabolic, vascular.
- **General hospital and community series** (Germany, India): **non-prion causes predominate**. Autoimmune, infectious, vascular and toxic-metabolic causes make up a large treatable fraction.
- **sCJD incidence:** about **1–2 per million per year** worldwide.
- **In India**, infectious causes are much more prominent:
  - **neurosyphilis**;
  - **HIV** (HAND, PML, cryptococcus, toxoplasma);
  - **tuberculous meningitis**;
  - **SSPE** (measles; adolescents);
  - **neurocysticercosis**;
  - **Japanese encephalitis** sequelae.

### Classification
**By tempo [1; general knowledge]:**
| Tempo | Think of |
| --- | --- |
| Hours to days (acute) | **Delirium**, stroke, encephalitis, non-convulsive status, Wernicke, hypoglycaemia, intoxication |
| Weeks to months (subacute: true RPD) | **Prion disease**, autoimmune or paraneoplastic encephalitis, CNS lymphoma, vasculitis, PML, neurosyphilis, subdural haematoma, NPH (subacute), metabolic causes |
| Months to 2 years | Rapid AD, DLB, FTD-MND, CBD, PSP; tumours |
| Fluctuating or stepwise | Subdural haematoma; vascular; delirium; **DLB**; drugs; hypoglycaemia [1] |

**By treatability [general knowledge]:**
- **Reversible or treatable:**
  - autoimmune (immunotherapy);
  - infections;
  - toxic-metabolic (thiamine, B12, thyroid, sodium, uraemia);
  - structural (subdural, NPH, tumour);
  - **NCSE**;
  - vasculitis;
  - **primary CNS lymphoma** (chemotherapy; **avoid steroids before biopsy**);
  - **psychiatric (depression, catatonia)**.
- **Untreatable:** prion disease; most neurodegenerative RPD (symptomatic care).

### Mechanism in detail [1; general knowledge]
- **Prion disease:** templated misfolding of PrPC to protease-resistant PrPSc. This produces **exponential** spread, which explains the rapid course (median sCJD survival about **4–6 months**) [1].
- **Autoimmune encephalitis:**
  - **antibodies against neuronal surface antigens** (NMDAR, LGI1, CASPR2, AMPAR, GABA-B) cause **reversible receptor internalisation or channel dysfunction**, so the outcome is good with immunotherapy;
  - **intracellular antigen antibodies** (Hu, Ma2) mark **T-cell-mediated neuronal destruction**, which is less treatable and strongly paraneoplastic.
- **Vasculitis and SLE:** small-vessel inflammation, multifocal infarcts [1].
- **Metabolic:** cerebral energy failure (Wernicke: thiamine-dependent enzymes in periventricular regions); osmotic shifts.
- **Neurodegenerative RPD:** "rapid AD" may involve distinct Aβ conformers (prion-like strains) and co-pathology.

### Clinical features in detail: diagnostic clues
| Clue | Suggests |
| --- | --- |
| **Myoclonus**, startle | sCJD; also AD (late), DLB, AE, metabolic, lithium or bismuth toxicity [1] |
| **Cerebellar ataxia early** | sCJD (MV2, VV2 subtypes), vCJD, Wernicke, paraneoplastic (anti-Yo), GSS [1] |
| **Cortical blindness, visual agnosia** | **Heidenhain variant of sCJD**; PRES; posterior cortical atrophy [1; general knowledge for Heidenhain] |
| **Psychiatric prodrome in a young person, painful sensory symptoms** | **vCJD** [1] |
| **Faciobrachial dystonic seizures; hyponatraemia** | **Anti-LGI1** [general knowledge] |
| **Psychosis then seizures, dyskinesia, autonomic instability, coma; young woman** | **Anti-NMDAR** (ovarian teratoma) [general knowledge] |
| **Sleep disorder (parasomnia, stridor), bulbar signs** | **Anti-IgLON5**; **fatal familial insomnia** [general knowledge; 1 for FFI] |
| **Neuromyotonia, insomnia, pain, autonomic signs** | **CASPR2** (Morvan syndrome) [general knowledge] |
| **Fluctuation, visual hallucinations, parkinsonism** | DLB [1] |
| **Gait apraxia, incontinence** | NPH |
| **Headache, papilloedema, focal signs** | Tumour, abscess, CVT, subdural [1] |
| **Ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion; alcohol or malnutrition** | **Wernicke** [1] |
| **Pupils (Argyll Robertson), grandiosity** | **General paresis** [1] |
| **Steroid-responsive, very high anti-TPO** | **SREAT** [general knowledge] |
| **Diarrhoea, arthralgia, oculomasticatory myorhythmia** | **Whipple's disease** [1; general knowledge for myorhythmia] |

### Assessment: tiered investigation
**Tier 1 (all)** [1; general knowledge]:
- FBC, ESR/CRP; urea and electrolytes (**sodium**), calcium, glucose, LFT, ammonia;
- **TFT, B12/folate**;
- **syphilis serology, HIV**;
- urinalysis; CXR; ECG;
- **MRI brain with DWI/ADC and FLAIR**:
  - sCJD shows **cortical ribboning and/or caudate-putamen restricted diffusion**;
  - vCJD shows the **pulvinar sign** [1];
- **EEG**:
  - **periodic sharp-wave complexes** (sCJD; late and about 65% sensitive) [1];
  - NCSE; encephalopathic slowing;
  - **extreme delta brush** (anti-NMDAR) [general knowledge];
- **CSF**:
  - cells, protein, glucose, OCB, IgG index;
  - cultures and PCR (HSV, VZV, JC);
  - VDRL;
  - **14-3-3 and total tau** (sensitive, not specific; raised in any rapid neuronal death) [1];
  - **RT-QuIC** [general knowledge].

**Tier 2 (selected)** [general knowledge]:
- **autoimmune panels** (serum and CSF): NMDAR, LGI1, CASPR2, AMPAR, GABA-B, GAD65, IgLON5, DPPX, onconeural antibodies;
- anti-TPO; ANA, ENA, ANCA; ACE (sarcoid); coeliac serology;
- **body CT, PET-CT**, testicular or pelvic ultrasound (teratoma);
- **continuous EEG**;
- **MR or catheter angiography** (vasculitis);
- **PRNP sequencing** (genetic prion disease);
- **caeruloplasmin**, fresh film for acanthocytes [1];
- heavy metals.

**Tier 3:** **brain biopsy** (non-dominant frontal, with meninges), for suspected vasculitis, lymphoma or infection when non-invasive tests fail. **Prion precautions** are required [1; general knowledge].

**Diagnostic criteria:**
- **sCJD (CDC/EU 2018)** [general knowledge]:
  - **probable** = progressive cognitive impairment **plus** RT-QuIC positive; **or** at least 2 of (myoclonus, visual or cerebellar signs, pyramidal or extrapyramidal signs, akinetic mutism) **plus** a typical EEG, or positive 14-3-3 with duration under 2 years, or typical MRI.
- **Autoimmune encephalitis (Graus 2016)** [general knowledge]:
  - **possible AE** = subacute onset (<3 months) of working-memory deficits, altered mental status or psychiatric symptoms;
  - **plus** at least 1 of: new focal CNS findings, unexplained seizures, CSF pleocytosis, MRI features of encephalitis;
  - **plus** reasonable exclusion of alternatives.
  - This allows **immunotherapy before antibody results**.

### Differential diagnosis: prion disease vs autoimmune encephalitis [general knowledge]
| Feature | sCJD | Autoimmune encephalitis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age | 60s | Any (NMDAR: young women; LGI1: older men) |
| Course | Relentless; death in months | Subacute; may fluctuate; responds to immunotherapy |
| Seizures | Uncommon (late) | Common (FBDS in LGI1) |
| MRI | Cortical ribboning, striatal DWI | Normal or medial temporal FLAIR hyperintensity |
| CSF | Acellular; 14-3-3 and tau high; **RT-QuIC positive** | Pleocytosis or OCB in many; antibodies |
| Sodium | Normal | **Low** in LGI1 |
| EEG | Periodic sharp-wave complexes | Temporal epileptiform; extreme delta brush |

**Psychiatric mimics [1]:**
- **depressive pseudodementia** (rapid onset, depressive history, "don't know" answers, variable effort);
- **catatonia** (rapid regression; lorazepam challenge) [general knowledge];
- **Ganser and functional cognitive presentations**;
- Lishman's CJD case was first labelled "hysterical pseudodementia" [1].

### Management details
**Immediate** [general knowledge]:
- **thiamine** IV;
- correct metabolic derangements;
- antiseizure medication for NCSE;
- **empirical acyclovir** if HSV is possible;
- **avoid steroids before biopsy** if lymphoma is suspected (steroids "melt" lymphoma and spoil the biopsy).

**Autoimmune encephalitis** [general knowledge]:
- **first line:** IV methylprednisolone 1 g/day for 5 days ± **IVIg** (2 g/kg) or **plasma exchange**;
- **second line:** **rituximab** or cyclophosphamide;
- **tumour removal** (teratoma);
- psychiatric symptoms: low-dose antipsychotics with caution (**NMS-like reactions in anti-NMDAR**); **benzodiazepines for catatonia**; **ECT** for refractory catatonia;
- **outcomes:** about 80% of NMDAR patients recover substantially; relapse in about 12%.

**Prion disease [1; general knowledge]:**
- no disease-modifying treatment;
- **palliative care**: myoclonus (**clonazepam, levetiracetam, valproate**); agitation; feeding decisions;
- **infection control**: instrument quarantine; notify the surveillance unit;
- genetic counselling for familial cases.

**Neurodegenerative RPD [1]:**
- **DLB:** avoid antipsychotics; cholinesterase inhibitors [1];
- symptomatic care.

**Communication and ethics** [general knowledge]:
- rapid decline leaves **little time** for advance care planning;
- address capacity, nominated representatives (MHCA 2017), end-of-life wishes and **organ or tissue donation restrictions** (prion disease).

### Critical appraisal
- **Referral bias:** prion-centre series over-represent CJD. Community series under-ascertain prion disease [general knowledge].
- **14-3-3** is non-specific (stroke, encephalitis, seizures) [1].
- **Antibody testing:** false positives (low-titre serum-only results, cell-based vs tissue assays). Clinical judgement must guide interpretation [general knowledge].
- **Lishman (2009)** predates most autoimmune encephalitis knowledge and RT-QuIC. This answer's modern sections are drawn from later literature.

### Indian context [general knowledge]
- **Infections dominate** treatable RPD:
  - neurosyphilis (rising with HIV);
  - **TB meningitis** (subacute dementia with cranial neuropathies and hydrocephalus);
  - cryptococcal meningitis;
  - PML with HIV;
  - **SSPE** in adolescents (myoclonus, periodic EEG; mimics CJD).
- **Wernicke encephalopathy** is under-recognised (alcohol, hyperemesis, malnutrition, bariatric surgery).
- **Autoimmune encephalitis panels** are now available commercially but are costly. Many centres treat empirically.
- **Prion surveillance** is limited; CJD is probably under-diagnosed. RT-QuIC is available in few centres (NIMHANS).
- **Psychiatric presentation** is common: AE and SSPE often present to psychiatrists first.

### Recent advances [general knowledge]
- **Second-generation RT-QuIC** (CSF, olfactory mucosa, skin).
- **α-synuclein seed amplification assays** (DLB, PD) and **plasma p-tau217** (AD) refine the neurodegenerative RPD work-up.
- **Plasma neurofilament light** as a non-specific marker of rapid neuronal loss.
- **Newly identified antibodies** (e.g., KLHL11 in testicular cancer; anti-GFAP astrocytopathy).
- **Autoimmune psychosis criteria** (Pollak 2020) for psychiatric presentations.
