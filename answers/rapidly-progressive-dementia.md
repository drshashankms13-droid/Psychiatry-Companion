## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- **What it is:** most primary dementias evolve over **many months or years**. A **short duration** at presentation "immediately raises the possibility" of a **secondary** dementia or a prion disease [1].
- **Definition:** a formal one (e.g., dementia within 1–2 years of onset) is *not given* in the source.

### Causes suggested by the source [1]

| Category | Causes named in Lishman |
| --- | --- |
| **Prion** | **Sporadic CJD** (death usually within 2 years; myoclonus, ataxia, visual and pyramidal signs; psychiatric prodrome); **variant CJD** (young, psychiatric onset); familial and iatrogenic CJD |
| **Structural** | **Cerebral tumour** (including frontal meningioma without focal signs); **subdural haematoma** (even after slight head injury; fluctuation); covert **cerebral infarction**; **cerebral abscess** |
| **Infective / inflammatory** | **AIDS**; **general paresis** (neurosyphilis); **subacute encephalitis**; **collagen-vascular disease/vasculitis**; multiple emboli in **subacute bacterial endocarditis**; Whipple's disease; sarcoid |
| **Metabolic / toxic / anoxic** | Renal or hepatic failure, **hypoglycaemia**, myxoedema, parathyroid disease, **B12 and folate deficiency**, **Wernicke–Korsakoff**, **alcohol and drugs**, heavy metals, carbon monoxide, cerebral anoxia (anaemia, heart or lung disease), anaesthesia |
| **Neoplastic (remote)** | **Carcinoma of the lung** presenting with dementia without metastases |
| **Neurodegenerative with rapid course** | **DLB** (mean 1.8 years to death in one series; fluctuation, hallucinations; **lethal neuroleptic reactions**); FTD with MND |
| **Mimics** | **Delirium** (fluctuation, clouding); **depressive pseudodementia**; Ganser state; hysterical pseudodementia (the CJD case was first labelled this) |

### Approach [1]
**1. History (with informant):**
- **Tempo:**
  - is the onset truly sudden, or just newly noticed (holidays, infrequent contact)?
  - a **definite onset date** suggests tumour or infarction;
  - **fluctuation or remission** suggests delirium, subdural haematoma, drugs, vascular disease or hypoglycaemia.
- **First symptoms:** **non-memory onset** (personality, psychiatric) should raise suspicion (FTD, HD, frontal tumour, general paresis, CJD prodrome).
- **Red flags:**
  - headache, vomiting, visual disturbance (space-occupying lesion);
  - focal seizures;
  - head injury (subdural haematoma);
  - fits, faints or collapses;
  - cough or weight loss (lung cancer);
  - fever (encephalitis, abscess, vasculitis, endocarditis);
  - HIV risk; alcohol, drugs, occupational toxins;
  - gastrectomy or diet (B12);
  - **family history** (familial CJD, HD, FTD, early AD).

**2. Examination:**
- general: fever, myxoedema, malnutrition, endocarditis, malignancy;
- **neurological:**
  - **myoclonus** (CJD);
  - **ataxia** (CJD, Wernicke, vCJD);
  - focal or pyramidal signs (tumour, vascular);
  - parkinsonism (DLB);
  - chorea (HD);
  - Kayser–Fleischer rings (Wilson's);
  - pupils (general paresis);
  - papilloedema;
  - neuropathy;
  - **cortical blindness**.

**3. Mental state:**
- **clouding** points to delirium;
- psychiatric prodrome (CJD);
- **depression** (pseudodementia);
- inconsistent performance (Ganser or hysterical).

**4. Investigations:**
- **bloods:** FBC, urea and electrolytes, LFT, TFT, B12/folate, **syphilis serology**; HIV if indicated by history;
- urine microscopy and culture; CXR;
- **MRI** (DWI for CJD; tumour; subdural; vascular; pulvinar sign);
- **EEG** (periodic triphasic complexes in CJD; slowing in delirium and encephalopathy; normal in FTD);
- **CSF:** cells, protein, pressure, xanthochromia (subdural), syphilis serology, **14-3-3 and tau** (CJD), subacute encephalitis;
- special tests:
  - caeruloplasmin (Wilson's);
  - fresh blood film (neuroacanthocytosis);
  - arylsulphatase-A (metachromatic leucodystrophy);
  - biopsies (Kufs, Whipple's, mitochondrial, sarcoid);
  - genetic testing (PRNP, HTT).

### Management [1]
- **Treat the remediable condition** identified:
  - tumour, subdural haematoma, NPH;
  - general paresis;
  - metabolic, endocrine and vitamin deficiencies;
  - drug or toxin effects;
  - corticosteroids for vasculitis [1].
  - The source does not detail specific treatments.
- **DLB:** **avoid antipsychotics** (fatal sensitivity); cholinesterase inhibitors.
- **CJD and other prion disease:** supportive dementia care; prevent iatrogenic transmission; surveillance notification (see the CJD answer).
- **All patients:** general dementia management (information, carer support, legal planning, nursing care); **regular review and readiness to re-investigate** when diagnosis is uncertain or the course unusual.

**Conclusion.** A short history or rapid course in dementia should prompt a broad search for **secondary and treatable causes** (structural, infective, inflammatory, metabolic, toxic, neoplastic) and for **prion disease** (myoclonus, ataxia, periodic EEG, DWI-MRI, CSF 14-3-3). Delirium must be distinguished, and depression excluded. Management targets the cause; for prion and rapidly progressive degenerative disease, it is supportive.
