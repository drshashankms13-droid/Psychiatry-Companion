## Exam answer

**Introduction [1].**
- The pandemic brought a rise in mental morbidity in the general population.
- There is a **high prevalence of common mental disorders during active infection** and **high psychiatric morbidity after recovery**.
- In the 6 months after admission, a **neurological or psychiatric diagnosis** occurred in about **34%** of patients admitted to COVID wards and **46%** of those admitted to COVID ICUs.
- **Existing mental disorder raises COVID-19 mortality:** OR **1.67** (meta-analysis of 16 studies, n = 19,086).

### A. Acute phase: new-onset syndromes [1]
- **Acute stress reaction** and **panic attacks**.
- **Depressive symptoms and disorders**; **suicidal ideation and attempts**.
- **Acute psychosis.**
- **Delirium:** common in **severe** COVID, especially in the **ICU**. It is occasionally the **presenting complaint**, so screen everyone.
- **Sleep disturbance.**
- **Substance withdrawal:** patients in COVID wards lack access to substitution or detoxification.

### B. Acute phase: in pre-existing mental disorders [1]
- **Exacerbation** of previously controlled **OCD, psychosis, anxiety and depression**.
- More health worries, anger, impulsivity and **intense suicidal ideation** than in others.
- **Discrimination:** patients are refused COVID-ward admission despite equal COVID severity, because of fear of unpredictable behaviour and suicide. The psychiatrist must **advocate** for admission.
- **Clozapine:** higher risk of **aspiration pneumonia**, so the dose is generally **halved** during infection.

### C. Medication-related syndromes [1]
- **Steroid-associated psychiatric disorders.**
- Establish the **temporal link** with anti-COVID drugs: **hydroxychloroquine, remdesivir, favipiravir, dexamethasone, prednisolone, tocilizumab, convalescent plasma**. Steroids, **interferons**, hydroxychloroquine and **ritonavir** are named for depression and psychosis.
- **Interactions:** **QTc prolongation** from COVID drugs plus antipsychotics; **hyponatraemia**; benzodiazepine **respiratory depression**.

### D. Post-COVID (recovery) phase [1]
- **Sufficient evidence** exists for:
  - **depression**;
  - **anxiety**;
  - **PTSD** (from ICU stay, ventilation, seeing others die, traumatic procedures, delirium, near-death experiences);
  - **mild cognitive impairment** and **dementia**.
- **Emerging:** **prolonged fatigue** and **cognitive deficits** up to **6 months**.
- Also reported: **psychosis, cognitive deficits, dementia, substance use disorders**.
- **Long COVID symptoms** listed: persistent fatigue, confusion, loss of smell and taste, breathlessness, chest pain, sleep problems, anxiety, headache (see the separate long COVID answer).
- **Grief:** from deaths of relatives, often **without the usual rituals**.
- **Stigma:** anticipated stigma on returning to society.
- **Distress in families** and caregivers.

### Contributing factors [1]
- **Isolation:** fear of being alone or away from family; fear of death.
- **Social and economic:** lockdown distress, constant media exposure, unemployment and financial burden, **bereavement**.
- **Care environment:**
  - **feeling uncared for**, or a perceived lack of empathy from staff;
  - **unfriendly wards**;
  - **seeing co-patients die**.
- **Poor coping.**

### Assessment (summary) [1]
- **Baseline assessment at diagnosis** (Table 1):
  - prior mental disorder and medication; **substance use (last intake)**; cognition; sleep; coping; frustration tolerance; personality; lifetime and current suicidality;
  - MSE;
  - screens: **PHQ-9/PHQ-4/PHQ-2** and **GAD-7**; **delirium screen**.
- **In the ward** (Table 2):
  - **CAM/CAM-ICU**; **RASS**; MMSE, HMSE or MoCA; pain; sleep;
  - contextual stressors (food, internet and devices, contact with family, deaths in the ward);
  - the doctor–patient relationship.
- **Daily surveillance** of mood, anxiety, stress, sleep and suicidality, even without baseline problems (Table 3).
- **Special groups** (Table 4):
  - **older people** (disability, sensory deprivation, so delirium risk);
  - **peripartum women** (fear of transmission, breastfeeding, domestic violence);
  - **children** (staying alone, screen use, guilt about infecting others).

### Management principles [1]
- **Psychological first aid for all** (Table 6):
  - reassure (mortality is not very high); allow ventilation;
  - answer questions; encourage **adaptive coping** (prayer, mindfulness, talking to loved ones, sleep, exercise, hobbies, yoga);
  - discourage maladaptive coping (constant news, screens, substances, repeatedly checking vitals).
- **Don't rush to drugs:** much acute anxiety and depression **settles with reassurance as respiratory symptoms improve**.
- **Use drugs at once for:** imminent self-harm, agitation, delirium or marked worsening, with a **high-risk protocol** and 24×7 monitoring.
- **Start low, go slow.** Consider:
  - oxygen needs; renal and hepatic function;
  - COVID complications (coagulopathy, bleeding, stroke);
  - **QTc** and **sodium**.
- **Syndrome-specific plans:**

| Syndrome | Plan (Table 7) |
| --- | --- |
| Acute stress, panic | Reassure; look for stressors and drug causes; explain masks, oxygen and **BiPAP**; breathing exercises; short-acting BZDs (clonazepam, etizolam, alprazolam); SSRI if persistent |
| Depression | Mild: supportive therapy, CBT. Moderate to severe: **SSRI/SNRI** (check interactions); short-term BZD; add an antipsychotic if psychotic; high-risk protocol if suicidal |
| Delirium | See the COVID delirium answer |
| New psychosis | Link to illness or drugs (steroids, HCQ); **rule out delirium**; **SGA** with QTc monitoring |
| Withdrawal | Usual detoxification with an eye on **oxygen saturation**. **Lorazepam** for alcohol (watch respiration); analgesics or supervised OST for opioids; **NRT** for tobacco |

- **Liaison:**
  - **with families:** baseline contact, regular updates, involve them in monitoring;
  - **with the COVID team:** risk communication; staff mental health;
  - **breaking bad news:** SPIKES, ABCDE or BREAKS, preferably by **video**.
- **Post-COVID:**
  - ask every new psychiatric presentation about **recent COVID in self or family, and COVID deaths**;
  - include a mental health professional in **post-COVID clinics**.

**Conclusion.** COVID-19's neuropsychiatric burden falls into four groups:
1. acute new-onset syndromes (stress, panic, depression, suicidality, psychosis, **delirium**);
2. relapse of pre-existing illness;
3. **drug-induced** syndromes (steroids) and interactions;
4. **post-COVID** depression, anxiety, PTSD, **cognitive impairment, fatigue** and grief.

[Certain, per the source] Mental disorder also raises COVID mortality. The IPS approach is **universal screening and psychological first aid**, delirium screening in severe illness, cautious "start low" prescribing that watches QTc and drug interactions, and continued follow-up after recovery.

## In depth

### Post-COVID assessment battery (Table 5) [1]
- **Depression:** PHQ-9/4/2. **Anxiety:** GAD-7. **PTSD:** primary-care PTSD screen; **IES-R** for severity.
- **Cognition:** MMSE, HMSE, MoCA, **IQCODE**. **Sleep:** Pittsburgh scale. **Fatigue:** Fatigue Assessment Scale.
- **Suicidality:** C-SSRS, Beck scales.
- **Function, coping, relationships and finances.**

### Post-COVID management (Table 8) [1]
| Condition | Management |
| --- | --- |
| Anxiety/depression | Tailor to severity. Supportive therapy or CBT (with antidepressants if needed); **SSRIs/SNRIs**; short-term low-dose BZD; relaxation and yoga; activity scheduling; thought diary |
| Sleep | Hygiene; low-dose melatonin, zolpidem or BZD |
| PTSD | **SSRIs**; short-term BZD; relaxation; **trauma-focused psychotherapy**; supportive sessions |
| Cognitive deficits/dementia | Look for **stroke or TIA**; dementia work-up; **MRI**; neuropsychology. **Cognitive rehabilitation**; **donepezil** may be started; **memantine** if vascular risk factors; behavioural interventions |
| New psychosis | Rule out organic causes (fever, inflammatory markers, imaging if needed) before diagnosing **acute psychosis**; SGA; BZD for agitation |

### Grief work [1]
- Facilitate adaptation to loss; allow **emotional outbursts and crying without interruption**.
- Explore **missed death rituals**, and help create **new rituals** to stay connected.
- Speak slowly and calmly; avoid speculation.

### Not covered by the sources (deliberately left blank)

- Neurological syndromes and their frequencies.
- Paediatric multisystem inflammatory syndrome; vaccine-related neuropsychiatric events.
