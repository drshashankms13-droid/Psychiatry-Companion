## Exam answer

### Introduction

Gender-affirming procedures are interventions that help align a person's physical characteristics with their gender identity. They may include chest, genital, facial, voice and other procedures. Gender diversity itself is not a mental disorder; ICD-11 classifies gender incongruence under conditions related to sexual health.

### Psychiatric assessment

Assessment should be individualised, respectful and non-pathologising. It includes:
- Understanding the person's gender identity, goals and desired procedures.
- Exploring persistence and personal meaning of gender incongruence.
- Assessing expectations, benefits, limitations and possible complications.
- Evaluating decision-making capacity and informed consent.
- Assessing depression, anxiety, trauma, substance use, psychosis and suicide risk.
- Exploring family, relationship, occupational, financial and social supports.
- Discussing fertility, sexuality, postoperative care and realistic outcomes.

Mental illness does not automatically contraindicate gender-affirming surgery. Conditions that impair decision-making or interfere with safe perioperative care should be addressed collaboratively.

### Role of the mental health professional

-   Provide supportive, affirming assessment.
-   Help clarify goals and expectations.
-   Assess capacity and relevant comorbidities.
-   Facilitate informed decision-making without unnecessary gatekeeping.
-   Coordinate with surgical and endocrine teams.
-   Provide perioperative support and postoperative follow-up.
-   Address adjustment difficulties, complications or social stressors.

### Outcomes and concerns

Many studies report improved gender congruence, body satisfaction and quality of life after appropriately indicated procedures. However, outcomes vary, and surgery does not resolve every psychosocial difficulty. Complications, dissatisfaction, regret and requests for revision are possible and should be discussed without stereotyping.

### Ethical and legal issues in India

Care should respect autonomy, dignity, confidentiality and informed consent. Relevant frameworks include the NALSA judgment (2014), Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019 and Rules 2020. WPATH Standards of Care Version 8 (2022) provides international clinical guidance.

### Conclusion

Psychiatric involvement should facilitate safe, informed and affirming care rather than act as a blanket barrier. Assessment must be comprehensive, individualised and respectful of autonomy.
