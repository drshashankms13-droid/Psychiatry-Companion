## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Transcranial electrical stimulation (tES)** is a family of non-invasive, **non-convulsive** neuromodulation techniques. They deliver weak electrical currents (typically 1–2 mA) to the scalp through electrodes [1]. Types:
- **Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS):** constant, unidirectional current.
- **Transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS):** sinusoidal current at a chosen frequency, to entrain oscillations.
- **Transcranial random noise stimulation (tRNS).**
- **High-definition tDCS (HD-tDCS):** small electrodes in arrays (e.g., 4×1 ring) for **focal** stimulation.

tDCS is cheap, portable, well tolerated and **sub-threshold**: it changes neuronal excitability but does not fire neurons [1].

### Mechanism
- Battery-driven current flows from **anode to cathode** [1]:
  - **Anodal** → depolarises the resting membrane → ↑ cortical excitability and spontaneous firing.
  - **Cathodal** → hyperpolarises → ↓ excitability [1].
- **After-effects:** with 10–20 minutes or more of stimulation, effects outlast the session (up to about 90 minutes). They are **NMDA-dependent LTP/LTD-like plasticity**, blocked by dextromethorphan and enhanced by D-cycloserine.
- Effects also involve GABA reduction under the anode, BDNF and network connectivity.
- **Why repeated sessions matter:** daily sessions accumulate plastic changes. This is why courses of 10–20 sessions are used.
- **tACS:** entrains endogenous oscillations (e.g., alpha, gamma).
- **HD-tDCS:** focal current under the centre electrode, giving better spatial precision than the diffuse current of two large sponges.

### Montages and indications

| Condition | Montage | Evidence (Lefaucheur 2017 guideline) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Major depression (non-drug-resistant)** | **Anode left DLPFC (F3)**, cathode right supraorbital/F4 | **Level B (probably effective)** [1] |
| Drug-resistant depression | Same | Not recommended (probably ineffective) |
| **Addiction / craving** | Anode right DLPFC, cathode left DLPFC | **Level B** [1] |
| **Fibromyalgia / chronic pain** | Anode left M1, cathode right supraorbital | **Level B** [1] |
| **Auditory verbal hallucinations** | Cathode left temporoparietal junction, anode left DLPFC | Level C / mixed [1] |
| Negative symptoms, cognition in schizophrenia | Anode left DLPFC | Investigational |
| OCD | Cathodal over pre-SMA / bilateral SMA | Investigational [1] |
| Post-stroke aphasia, motor rehabilitation | Perilesional anodal | Level B for some |

### Typical parameters
- 1–2 mA (up to about 4 mA in recent trials).
- 20–30 minutes per session.
- Saline-soaked sponge electrodes, 25–35 cm².
- Daily on weekdays for 2–6 weeks, then tapering maintenance.
- **Home-based tDCS** with remote supervision is increasingly used.

### Assessment and safety
- **Screen for:** metal implants in the head, cochlear implants, skin lesions at electrode sites, epilepsy (relative), pregnancy (limited data), **bipolar disorder** (risk of switching), and the patient's ability to attend daily or use a home device.
- **Adverse effects:** tingling, itching, erythema, mild headache, fatigue. **Skin burns** occur rarely if technique is poor. **Hypomania** or mania switches have been reported. No serious adverse events in more than 33,000 sessions (Bikson 2016).

### Key evidence in depression
- **SELECT-TDCS** (Brunoni 2013, n=120): tDCS + sertraline 50 mg > either alone > placebo.
- **ELECT-TDCS** (Brunoni 2017, NEJM, n=245): tDCS was **inferior** to escitalopram 20 mg, though better than placebo (more new-onset mania in the tDCS arm).
- **Home-based tDCS** (Woodham 2024, Nat Med, n=174): active tDCS gave higher response and remission than sham at 10 weeks.
- **Meta-analyses** (e.g., Brunoni 2016, individual patient data): active > sham for response and remission (moderate effect, NNT about 7).

**Conclusion.** tDCS is a safe, cheap, portable, sub-convulsive way to modulate cortical excitability. It has **probable efficacy (level B)** in non-resistant depression, craving and fibromyalgia, but its effects are modest. It is less effective than antidepressants or ECT, and it is not established in treatment-resistant depression. HD-tDCS and tACS improve focality and frequency-specificity but remain research tools. In practice tDCS is best used as an **adjunct**, for example combined with an SSRI, or where medication is not tolerated, and home-based protocols may widen access in India.

## In depth

### Background
- Electrical treatment is old [1]:
  - Scribonius Largus (1st century) used **torpedo fish** for headache.
  - Aldini (1804) applied galvanic current to people with melancholia.
- **Modern tDCS:**
  - Priori et al. 1998 showed modulation of motor evoked potentials [1].
  - **Nitsche & Paulus (2000)** established polarity-specific excitability changes of the motor cortex.
- **Clinical development in depression:** Fregni (2006) and Boggio; then Brunoni and Loo's RCTs; the 2017 **Lefaucheur et al. evidence-based guideline** (*Clinical Neurophysiology*) [1].
- **HD-tDCS:** developed by Datta and Bikson (2009) through computational current-flow modelling.

### Epidemiology of use
- Mainly used in research and specialist neuromodulation clinics. Low cost (devices cost a few hundred dollars) makes it attractive in LMICs.
- **Unregulated "DIY" consumer devices** exist; this is a safety and ethical concern.

### Classification and criteria
| Technique | Current | Main principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tDCS | Direct, 1–2 mA | Polarity-dependent excitability shift |
| HD-tDCS | Direct, multiple small electrodes | Focal current |
| tACS | Alternating, at a set frequency | Entrainment of oscillations |
| tRNS | Random-noise frequencies (0.1–640 Hz) | Stochastic resonance; ↑ excitability |
| CES (cranial electrotherapy stimulation) | Pulsed, low intensity, ear-clip electrodes | FDA-cleared for anxiety and insomnia (weak evidence) |
| tPCS, TNS | Pulsed / trigeminal | Investigational |

### Mechanism in detail
- **Field strength** in the cortex is about 0.2–0.5 V/m at 2 mA. This changes membrane potential by under 1 mV, enough to shift firing probability but not to trigger action potentials.
- **Factors affecting dose at the cortex:** neuron orientation (pyramidal soma–dendrite axis), gyral folding and individual anatomy (skull thickness, CSF). Computational modelling helps standardise dose.
- **Non-linearity:** longer or stronger stimulation may reverse polarity effects (e.g., 2 mA cathodal can become excitatory). Response varies a great deal between individuals.
- **Network effects:** DLPFC stimulation modulates connectivity with subgenual ACC and the default mode network.

### Clinical features in detail
- **Onset in depression:** response emerges over 2–6 weeks. Combination with an SSRI or with cognitive training (e.g., during CBT tasks) may be synergistic.
- **Cognitive effects:** small, inconsistent improvements in working memory. No cognitive adverse effects, which is a key advantage over ECT.

### Assessment in detail
- Baseline HAM-D/MADRS; YMRS if there is bipolar risk; C-SSRS.
- Skin inspection.
- Electrode placement with the 10–20 EEG system (F3 = left DLPFC; Beam F3 method).
- Impedance checks.
- Training and adherence monitoring for home use.

### Differential: tES vs other neuromodulation
| Feature | tDCS | rTMS | ECT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neuronal firing | No (sub-threshold) | Yes (supra-threshold) | Generalised seizure |
| Focality | Low (HD better) | High | — |
| Cost and portability | Very low; portable / home use | High; clinic | Anaesthesia; hospital |
| Evidence in depression | Level B (non-resistant) | Level A (HF left DLPFC) [1] | Strongest (60–90% response) [1] |
| Cognitive adverse effects | None | None | Yes |

### Management details
- **Protocol example** (depression): anode F3, cathode F8 or right supraorbital; 2 mA for 30 minutes; 15 sessions over 3 weeks; then twice-weekly maintenance.
- **Combining with medication:** SSRIs may enhance tDCS effects. Benzodiazepines and anticonvulsants (sodium/calcium channel blockers) can reduce after-effects.
- **Special groups:**
  - **Pregnancy:** an attractive non-drug option, with small trials showing safety (e.g., Vigod 2019 pilot RCT).
  - **Children:** use lower current and closer monitoring.
  - **Bipolar depression:** watch for hypomania; small RCTs (Sampaio-Junior 2018) were positive.
- **Schizophrenia:** cathodal TPJ for AVH (Brunelin 2012 positive, later trials mixed); anodal DLPFC for negative symptoms (Valiengo 2020 positive) [1].

### Evidence table
| Study | Design | n | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nitsche & Paulus 2000 (J Physiol) | Physiological | — | Polarity-specific excitability change |
| Brunoni et al. 2013 (JAMA Psychiatry), SELECT-TDCS | 2×2 factorial RCT | 120 | Combined tDCS + sertraline best |
| Brunoni et al. 2017 (NEJM), ELECT-TDCS | Non-inferiority RCT | 245 | tDCS inferior to escitalopram; better than placebo |
| Lefaucheur et al. 2017 (Clin Neurophysiol) | Evidence-based guideline | — | Level B: non-resistant MDD, addiction/craving, fibromyalgia [1] |
| Brunoni et al. 2016 (Br J Psychiatry) | Individual patient data meta-analysis | 289 | Active > sham for response and remission |
| Woodham et al. 2024 (Nat Med) | Home-based RCT | 174 | Active home tDCS superior to sham |
| Bikson et al. 2016 (Brain Stimul) | Safety review | >33,200 sessions | No serious adverse effects at ≤4 mA, ≤40 min |

### Critical appraisal
- **Modest and variable effects;** many trials are small and use heterogeneous montages and doses. Publication bias exists.
- **Sham blinding** is fairly good (ramp-down sham), but tingling and skin redness can unblind.
- **Negative large trials:** ELECT-TDCS (vs escitalopram); Loo 2018 (n=130, no benefit over sham in depression).
- **Inter-individual variability** in anatomy and baseline state means a one-size dose is crude. Modelling-based individualisation is promising but unproven.
- **DIY and consumer use:** unregulated, with a risk of burns and false claims.
- **HD-tDCS and tACS:** mechanistically appealing, but clinical RCT evidence is scarce.

### Indian context
- Research groups at **NIMHANS** (e.g., tDCS for auditory hallucinations, negative symptoms, OCD and depression), CIP Ranchi and PGIMER have published RCTs. NIMHANS has contributed influential tDCS work in schizophrenia, including cathodal TPJ for hallucinations.
- Low cost and portability make tDCS feasible in **resource-limited settings** and for home-based or tele-supervised care. Regulatory clarity for devices (CDSCO medical device rules) is still evolving.
- **MHCA 2017:** no specific restriction on tDCS (unlike ECT or psychosurgery), but standard informed consent applies.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** level B recommendations [1]; home-based, remotely supervised tDCS (Woodham 2024).
- **Emerging:**
  - HD-tDCS for focal targeting.
  - **Closed-loop tACS** (phase-locked to brain rhythms).
  - Combining tDCS with psychotherapy or cognitive training.
  - Individual current-flow modelling.
  - **Temporal interference stimulation** for non-invasive deep targeting (investigational).
- **Investigational:** transcutaneous auricular VNS (taVNS); trigeminal nerve stimulation (FDA-cleared for paediatric ADHD in 2019).
