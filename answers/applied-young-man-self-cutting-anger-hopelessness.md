## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 22-year-old unmarried college student has had **low mood, difficulty sustaining friendships and self-cutting with a blade for 2 years**, with **significant relationship problems with his parents and frequent anger outbursts**. He has been **irregular with treatment and psychotherapy** and says **"everything will end soon"**. He **appears euthymic** but expresses **hopelessness and worthlessness** and reports **active suicidal ideation**.

**Key issue.** A **high immediate suicide risk** — hopelessness, active ideation, a statement of finality, repeated self-harm, poor engagement — in a young man with a probable **personality disorder with borderline pattern** and/or a **depressive disorder** [1–3]. A euthymic appearance does **not** reduce risk; it may signal a decision to die.

### 1. Differential diagnosis

| Diagnosis | For | Against / to clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Personality disorder with borderline pattern (BPD)** | 2 years of self-harm, unstable relationships, anger outbursts, worthlessness, poor engagement | Need pervasive pattern since adolescence; abandonment fears, identity disturbance, impulsivity, emptiness [1] |
| **Recurrent depressive disorder / persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia)** | Low mood, hopelessness, worthlessness | Euthymic appearance; check biological symptoms, duration, episodicity |
| **Bipolar disorder (II) or cyclothymia** | Anger outbursts, mood instability | Episodes of hypomania lasting days [1] |
| **Complex PTSD** | Self-concept and relational disturbance | Requires trauma exposure — ask [2] |
| **Substance use disorder** | Young male, impulsivity | Alcohol, cannabis, other drugs |
| **ADHD** | Impulsivity, anger, academic difficulty | Childhood onset of inattention and hyperactivity |
| **Other personality disorders** (antisocial, narcissistic, avoidant) | Anger, relationship problems | Pattern of rule-breaking, grandiosity or avoidance [1] |
| **Adjustment disorder** | Family conflict | Duration and pervasiveness argue against |
| **Emerging psychosis** | Withdrawal, "everything will end soon" | Check for delusional or nihilistic meaning, hallucinations |

**Most likely:** personality disorder with borderline pattern, with comorbid depressive disorder, at high suicide risk.

### 2. Factors determining prognosis

**Poor prognostic factors (present here)**
- **Active suicidal ideation, hopelessness and statement of finality** — the strongest predictor of suicide in the short term.
- **Male sex and young age** — higher lethality of attempts.
- Repeated self-harm over 2 years.
- **Poor treatment adherence and engagement** [1].
- Family conflict and possible **invalidating environment** [3].
- Unknown comorbidity — depression, substance use, ADHD worsen outcome.
- Social isolation (inability to maintain friendships).

**Good prognostic factors to look for**
- **Natural course of BPD** — symptoms and suicide risk peak in young adulthood and wane with age [1].
- Still in college; intelligence and education.
- Family still involved — potential to change family relationships.
- Absence of substance use and antisocial traits.
- Engagement with a structured, evidence-based therapy (DBT/MBT) and a stable therapeutic alliance [3, 4].
- Treatable comorbid depression.

**Prognostic factors from the literature:** severity (number of PD diagnoses), comorbid substance use, childhood abuse, poor premorbid functioning and early dropout predict worse outcome; in MBT trials, patients with more PD diagnoses needed more intensive treatment [4].

### 3. Detailed psychosocial intervention plan

**Phase 1 — Immediate safety (days)**
- **Comprehensive suicide risk assessment**; consider **admission** given active ideation, hopelessness and statement of intent — under MHCA 2017 as **independent admission** if he consents with capacity, or **supported admission (Section 89)** if he lacks capacity and is at risk (author's knowledge).
- **Collaborative safety plan**: warning signs, internal coping strategies, people and places for distraction, family and professional contacts, crisis line (**Tele-MANAS 14416**), means restriction (remove blades, medicines; family supervises).
- Treat any **comorbid depression** (SSRI with limited supply; supervised dispensing).
- Engage family immediately (with consent, or without if there is serious risk).

**Phase 2 — Engagement and formulation (weeks 1–4)**
- **Psychoeducation** for him and his parents — the biosocial model of emotion dysregulation; self-harm as a way of regulating unbearable emotion, not "attention-seeking" [3].
- **Explore reasons for poor engagement** (stigma, mistrust, previous therapist mismatch, practical barriers); use motivational interviewing.
- Shared formulation and goals ("a life worth living").
- Treatment contract: attendance, crisis plan, how to contact the team.

**Phase 3 — Structured psychotherapy (6–12 months)**
- **DBT** (preferred, given self-harm and suicidality) [3]:
  - Weekly individual therapy — diary cards, chain analysis of self-cutting and anger outbursts, solution analysis.
  - Weekly skills group — **mindfulness, distress tolerance** (alternatives to cutting), **emotion regulation** (opposite action), **interpersonal effectiveness** (with parents and friends).
  - **Telephone coaching** for skills use before self-harm (24-hour rule after self-harm).
  - Therapist consultation team.
- Alternative: **MBT** — focuses on loss of mentalizing during family conflict; evidence for reducing self-harm and suicidality in adults and adolescents [4].
- If neither is available: **Good Psychiatric Management** or structured clinical management with regular sessions and case management.

**Phase 4 — Family intervention**
- **Family sessions** to reduce criticism and invalidation; teach validation and DBT "walking the middle path" skills to parents [3].
- Address anger outbursts with a family crisis plan.

**Phase 5 — Educational and social functioning**
- Liaison with college counsellor (with consent) for academic support and attendance flexibility.
- Graded social activity; peer groups; structured routine, exercise, sleep hygiene.
- Address substance use if present (motivational interviewing, relapse prevention).

**Phase 6 — Maintenance and relapse prevention**
- Regular review of risk; updated safety plan.
- Continuity of one clinician; planned endings to avoid abandonment crises.
- Consider DBT stage II for trauma if relevant; vocational planning.

**Conclusion.** This young man is at high immediate suicide risk despite appearing euthymic. The differential centres on a borderline-pattern personality disorder with comorbid depression. Prognosis depends on engaging him in structured psychotherapy, treating comorbidity and changing family dynamics. The psychosocial plan moves from safety and engagement to DBT or MBT, family work and educational rehabilitation, with continuous risk review.

## In depth

### Background

- BPD is more often diagnosed in women, but clinical samples under-represent men, who may present with anger, substance use and contact with the criminal justice system (author's knowledge); DSM-5 reports about 75% of diagnosed cases are female [1].
- Suicide mortality in BPD is estimated at 50 times the general population [3].

### Classification

- **ICD-11:** personality disorder rated mild/moderate/severe, with trait domains (likely negative affectivity, disinhibition) and borderline pattern [2].
- **DSM-5:** BPD requires 5 of 9 criteria [1].

### Mechanism in detail

Emotional vulnerability × invalidating family environment → emotion dysregulation → self-cutting relieves intense emotion (negative reinforcement) → anger outbursts → family criticism → further invalidation (transactional cycle) [3]. Mentalizing collapses during family conflict, so he misreads parents' intentions and acts on feelings [4].

### Assessment in detail

| Tool | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale | Ideation, intent, behaviour |
| Beck Hopelessness Scale | Hopelessness — predicts suicide |
| SCID-5-PD / Zanarini Rating Scale | BPD diagnosis and severity |
| PHQ-9 / HAM-D | Depression |
| AUDIT / ASSIST | Substance use |
| Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale | ADHD screen |

### Evidence table

| Intervention | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| DBT | Most RCT support for BPD; largest effects on suicidal and self-injurious behaviour [3] |
| MBT (outpatient) | Superior to structured clinical management, especially with more PD diagnoses [4] |
| MBT (adolescents with self-harm) | Recovery 44% vs 17% [4] |
| MBT 8-year follow-up | Suicidality 23% vs 74% [4] |

### Critical appraisal

- Statements like "everything will end soon" must be clarified — they may be suicidal intent, nihilism or hopelessness.
- Admission can be both protective and harmful in BPD; short, goal-directed admissions are preferred [1].
- Evidence in young men specifically is limited; most DBT trials recruited women [3].

### Indian context

- Family conflict and academic pressure are common stressors in Indian students; families may see anger and self-harm as disobedience.
- College counselling and Tele-MANAS improve access.
- MHCA 2017 supports supported admission and treatment where capacity is impaired; Section 115 addresses attempted suicide as a health concern.
- Firearm access is low, but medicines and pesticides are common means — restrict access.

### Recent advances

- DBT for adolescents and young adults, including family skills training [3].
- MBT adaptations for adolescents and antisocial personality disorder [4].
- Brief contact interventions and safety planning after self-harm reduce repetition (author's knowledge).
