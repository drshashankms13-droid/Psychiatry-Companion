## Exam answer

**Introduction.** The COVID-19 pandemic (declared March 2020) affected mental health in four overlapping groups [1]:
1. **The general population:** lockdown, fear, economic loss, isolation.
2. **People infected with SARS-CoV-2:** in the acute phase and the post-COVID ("long COVID") phase.
3. **People with pre-existing mental disorders:** relapse, loss of services, higher COVID mortality.
4. **Healthcare workers (HCWs):** see `psychological-impact-of-covid-on-healthcare-workers`.

**WHO** stated early that **mental health must be an integral part of pandemic response** [1].

### 1. General population
- **What people experienced:**
  - **anxiety, fear, nervousness**;
  - **quarantine/isolation**: stress, anger, irritability, loneliness (Brooks 2020 rapid review) [1];
  - grief, often complicated by the inability to perform funeral rituals [1];
  - **infodemic** and media over-exposure;
  - **stigma** (against infected people, survivors, HCWs, certain communities);
  - **unemployment and financial loss** (migrant workers' exodus in India);
  - **domestic violence**, child abuse;
  - **increased alcohol** use in some groups and **withdrawal** when liquor shops closed (India: withdrawal seizures, delirium, suicides reported in March–April 2020) [general knowledge];
  - **screen time and gaming** in children; disrupted schooling.
- **Burden:**
  - global prevalence of **major depressive disorder rose about 28%** (+53 million cases) and **anxiety disorders about 26%** (+76 million) in 2020 (GBD COVID-19 Mental Disorders Collaborators, Santomauro 2021) [general knowledge];
  - WHO: about a **25% increase** in anxiety and depression in the first year [general knowledge].
- **Suicide:** early data showed **no overall rise** in high-income countries in the first months, with variation by group and later waves [general knowledge].
- **Risk factors:**
  - female sex; young age;
  - pre-existing illness;
  - low income; job loss;
  - living alone;
  - **bereavement**;
  - frontline work;
  - excessive news exposure [1, general knowledge].

### 2. People with COVID-19 infection [1]
- **Acute phase (ward or home isolation):**
  - **acute stress reaction, panic, depression**;
  - **suicidal ideation and attempts**;
  - **acute psychosis**;
  - **delirium** (especially ICU);
  - insomnia;
  - **substance withdrawal** in wards without detox or OST.
- **Contributors:**
  - fear of death; isolation from family;
  - seeing co-patients die;
  - perceived lack of care or empathy; unfriendly wards;
  - masks and BiPAP;
  - **drug effects** (steroids, hydroxychloroquine, interferons, ritonavir);
  - drug interactions [1].
- **Post-COVID phase:**
  - **depression, anxiety, PTSD**;
  - **cognitive impairment / dementia**;
  - **fatigue**; psychosis; substance use;
  - **long COVID** symptoms [1] (see `long-covid`).
- **Burden:** **6-month incidence of a neurological or psychiatric diagnosis: about 34% overall (46% after ICU admission)** (Taquet 2021) [1].

### 3. People with pre-existing mental illness [1]
- **Relapse or exacerbation** (OCD, psychosis, anxiety, depression) due to:
  - lockdown;
  - closure of in-person services;
  - medicine supply disruptions.
- **Higher COVID-19 mortality:** **OR 1.67** in a meta-analysis of 16 studies (n = 19,086) [1].
- **Higher infection risk; discrimination** in admission to COVID wards. Psychiatrists must **advocate** for them [1].
- **Clozapine:** aspiration pneumonia risk and level changes (infection and inflammation reduce CYP1A2), so **halve the dose** during infection [1].

### Management framework (IPS Clinical Practice Guidelines 2022) [1]
**At the population level:**
- **psychological first aid** and helplines (e.g., the NIMHANS helpline and **Kiran 2020**, later **Tele-MANAS 2022**) [general knowledge];
- **anti-stigma campaigns** for survivors and HCWs [1];
- reliable information, avoiding the infodemic;
- social and economic support;
- **telepsychiatry**: the **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (March 2020)** and **Telepsychiatry Operational Guidelines (May 2020)** enabled continuity of care (see `telepsychiatry-guidelines-india-2020`).

**For infected patients:**
1. **Baseline mental health assessment** at diagnosis [1]:
   - pre-existing illness; psychotropics; substance use and last intake;
   - cognition; sleep; coping; personality;
   - suicide risk; MSE;
   - **PHQ-9/PHQ-4/PHQ-2, GAD-7**.

   This guides the choice between admission and home isolation (a "mild" COVID case with high suicide risk may need admission).
2. **Daily surveillance:** mood, anxiety, sleep, suicidality, fear of death, grief. Self-rated scales can be sent electronically; nurses can monitor [1].
3. **Screen everyone for delirium (CAM/CAM-ICU)**, sedation (RASS) and cognition (MMSE/HMSE/MoCA) [1].
4. **Psychological first aid for all** [1]:
   - reassure (mortality is not very high);
   - let them ventilate;
   - answer questions;
   - encourage adaptive coping (prayer, mindfulness, contact with family, sleep, hobbies, yoga);
   - discourage maladaptive coping (excessive news, constant checking of saturation, substances);
   - relaxation.
5. **Pharmacotherapy:**
   - **don't rush to medicate** new symptoms; many settle with reassurance and as respiratory symptoms improve;
   - when needed, **start low, go slow**;
   - check **QTc** (additive with HCQ, azithromycin, antipsychotics), **hyponatraemia**, oxygen saturation (**benzodiazepine respiratory depression**), drug interactions, and renal and hepatic function [1].
6. **Disorder-specific plans (Table 7)** [1]:
   - **Panic/acute stress:** reassurance; breathing techniques; short-acting benzodiazepines as needed; SSRIs if persistent.
   - **Depression:** CBT or supportive therapy; SSRI or SNRI if moderate–severe; suicide precautions.
   - **Delirium:** find and treat the cause; reorientation; melatonin; haloperidol or quetiapine (QTc); dexmedetomidine; lorazepam only for alcohol withdrawal.
   - **New-onset psychosis:** rule out delirium and steroid effects; SGA.
   - **Substance withdrawal:** lorazepam for alcohol; OST for opioids; NRT.
7. **Liaison with family** (regular updates, involving them in monitoring) and **with the COVID team**. **Breaking bad news** by video using SPIKES, ABCDE or BREAKS [1].
8. **Post-COVID clinics** with a mental health professional:
   - screen for depression, anxiety, PTSD, cognition, sleep, fatigue, suicidality;
   - provide **grief work**;
   - treat according to standard guidelines (Table 8) [1].

**For psychiatric services:**
- continuity through tele-consultation;
- longer prescriptions;
- home delivery of medicines;
- depot antipsychotics;
- **ECT precautions** (screening, PPE, airway management);
- separate COVID facilities for psychiatric patients.

**Conclusion.** COVID-19 caused a **"second pandemic" of mental distress**:
- rises in depression and anxiety in the general population;
- high psychiatric morbidity in infected people (delirium acutely; depression, PTSD, cognitive deficits and long COVID afterwards);
- relapse and excess mortality in people with mental illness;
- burnout in HCWs.

The Indian response combined psychological first aid, consultation-liaison guidelines (IPS CPG 2022), telepsychiatry legalisation, and helplines that grew into Tele-MANAS. These are lessons for future pandemic preparedness.

## In depth

### Background
- **Earlier epidemics** (SARS 2003, MERS, Ebola) showed post-traumatic stress, depression and stigma in survivors and HCWs. Quarantine longer than 10 days predicted worse outcomes (Brooks 2020) [1].
- **India:**
  - national lockdown from 25 March 2020;
  - second (Delta) wave in April–May 2021, with oxygen shortages and mass bereavement [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology
- **Indian online survey (Grover 2020):** about 2 in 5 respondents had anxiety or common mental disorder symptoms during lockdown [Likely; check figures in source].
- **Children:** school closures for about 18 months in India; learning loss; rising screen use [general knowledge].

### Classification of pandemic-related psychiatric presentations
| Group | Presentations |
| --- | --- |
| General population | Adjustment disorder, anxiety, depression, insomnia, alcohol use, health anxiety, OCD (contamination), grief |
| Infected (acute) | Delirium, acute stress, panic, depression, psychosis (steroids), withdrawal |
| Infected (post) | Depression, anxiety, PTSD, cognitive deficits, fatigue, long COVID |
| Pre-existing illness | Relapse; non-adherence; COVID mortality |
| HCWs | Burnout, moral injury, depression, anxiety, insomnia, PTSD, substance use |

### Mechanism
- **Psychosocial:** fear, uncertainty, isolation, grief, economic stress, stigma.
- **Biological (infected):**
  - systemic inflammation (cytokines IL-6, TNF-α);
  - hypoxia;
  - endothelial injury and microthrombi;
  - possible neurotropism;
  - **iatrogenic** (steroids, ICU sedation);
  - sleep deprivation in ICU.

  See `covid-19-neuropsychiatric-syndromes`.

### Clinical features: the special populations checklist [1]
- **Elderly:** physical disability, sensory deprivation (delirium risk), comorbidities, polypharmacy.
- **Peripartum women:** worries about transmission to fetus and newborn; delivery and breastfeeding; **domestic violence**; guilt.
- **Children:** ability to stay alone; ID and developmental disorders; screen and gaming use; guilt about infecting others; family members admitted together.

### Assessment tools [1]
- PHQ-9/4/2; GAD-7; Perceived Stress Scale; CAM/CAM-ICU; RASS; MMSE/HMSE/MoCA; IES-R (PTSD); C-SSRS; IQCODE; Fatigue Assessment Scale; Pittsburgh Insomnia Scale.

### Differential diagnosis
- Delirium vs primary psychosis.
- Steroid-induced mania or psychosis.
- Hypoxia-related confusion.
- Withdrawal states.
- Grief vs depression.
- Long COVID fatigue vs depression.

### Management: services during pandemics (lessons)
- **Tele-mental health** as default for follow-up.
- **Essential service status** for mental health.
- Medicine supply chains (DMHP drugs).
- **Integrating mental health into COVID care** pathways (CL psychiatry) [1].
- Volunteer helplines (e.g., NIMHANS psychosocial helpline 080-46110007, 2020) [general knowledge].

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Brooks 2020, *Lancet* | Quarantine: stress, anger, confusion; longer duration, fears, frustration, finances worsen outcomes [1] |
| Taquet 2021, *Lancet Psychiatry* | 6-month neurological or psychiatric diagnosis: 34% (46% after ICU) [1] |
| Meta-analysis of 16 studies, n = 19,086 (cited in [1]) | Mental disorders associated with higher COVID mortality (OR 1.67) |
| Santomauro 2021, *Lancet* (general knowledge) | MDD +28%, anxiety +26% globally in 2020 |
| IPS CPG (Grover 2022) | Assessment and management framework [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- Early studies relied on **online convenience samples** (selection bias, no baseline), which overestimated prevalence.
- **Longitudinal cohorts** showed rises in distress early in the pandemic that **partly returned to baseline** by mid-2020 for many groups (resilience) [general knowledge].
- **Inequities:** the worst effects fell on the poor, migrants, women and children, who are under-represented in research.
- **Indian data** were largely cross-sectional.

### Indian context
- **Policy and services:**
  - **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines** (25 March 2020) and **TOG** (May 2020);
  - **MoHFW "Minding our minds during COVID-19"** guidance [general knowledge];
  - **Kiran helpline** (MoSJE, 2020);
  - **Tele-MANAS** (2022) as a legacy;
  - **IPS CPG supplement 2022** [1].
- **Social impact:** liquor ban and withdrawal crises; migrant mental health; stigma against HCWs (evictions) [general knowledge].

### Recent advances
- **Pandemic preparedness planning** that includes mental health (WHO).
- **Long COVID** clinics.
- **Digital mental health** scale-up.
- Research on **neuroinflammatory mechanisms** and their treatment implications.
