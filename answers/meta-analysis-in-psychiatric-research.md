## Exam answer

### Definition

Meta-analysis statistically combines results from independent studies addressing a related question. It is commonly conducted within a systematic review, which uses explicit methods to identify, select, appraise, and synthesize evidence. Psychiatric applications include treatment effects, diagnostic accuracy, prevalence, associations, and risk factors.

### Steps

1. Formulate a focused PICO/PECO question.
2. Register/publish a protocol and prespecify eligibility and outcomes.
3. Search multiple databases and grey literature; document strategy.
4. Screen records, ideally in duplicate.
5. Extract characteristics and
results using standardized methods.
6. Assess risk of bias with
design-appropriate tools.
7. Select effect measures (e.g., standardized
mean difference, risk ratio, odds ratio, prevalence).
8. Choose
fixed-effect or random-effects model based on assumptions and question.
9. Assess heterogeneity, publication bias, and robustness; justify
subgroup/meta-regression analyses.
10. Interpret certainty, clinical
importance, harms, and applicability, beyond p-values.

### Advantages

Increases precision and power; clarifies apparently conflicting findings; estimates average effects; explores heterogeneity; identifies evidence gaps; and informs guidelines. Subgroup and meta-regression analyses can generate hypotheses but are vulnerable to confounding and ecological fallacy.

### Limitations

- Clinical/methodological heterogeneity in participants, interventions, comparators, outcomes, and designs.
- Publication bias and missing negative studies; funnel plots and tests are imperfect.
- Poor primary studies remain biased when pooled.
- Selective outcomes and analytic flexibility can inflate false positives.
- Multiple reports from the same sample may be mistakenly treated as independent.
- A pooled average may not apply to an individual or local service.
- Statistical significance may not imply clinically meaningful benefit.

### Models

A fixed-effect model assumes one common underlying effect, with observed variation mainly from sampling error. A random-effects model allows true effects to vary across studies and estimates a distribution. Random effects are not automatically preferable. Report heterogeneity (including I²) with clinical interpretation, not as a mechanical threshold.

### Reporting/certainty

PRISMA 2020 improves transparent reporting; the Cochrane Handbook provides methods; AMSTAR 2 appraises systematic reviews; GRADE assesses certainty across outcomes. These tools have different purposes.

### Conclusion

Meta-analysis is powerful when studies are comparable and methods transparent. Conclusions depend on quality, completeness, and applicability of primary evidence. Interpret effect size, uncertainty, heterogeneity, bias, harms, and clinical relevance.
