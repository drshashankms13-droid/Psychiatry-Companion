## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 16-year-old boy has refused food and secretly thrown it away for 6 months.
- He lost **10 kg** and fears regaining it.
- He **exercises 2 hours a day**, wants his **ribs visible**, and **still feels fat**.
- His **mood is low**.
- **Past history:**
  - 1 year ago: daily cannabis (about 5 joints) and tobacco for 6 months, then stopped on his own;
  - 2 years ago: **excessive online gaming**, missed 4 months of school.
- **Childhood:**
  - **over-active and very talkative** in class, but good grades;
  - **fussy about food and clothes** as an infant;
  - a **loner without friends**;
  - **rigid about how his things are kept**; lets no one into his room.
- **Father:** monotonous speech, circumstantial.

### Provisional diagnosis
1. **Anorexia nervosa, restricting type** (with compulsive exercise): ICD-11 **6B80**, **with significantly low or dangerously low body weight** depending on BMI centile (ICD-11 uses BMI below 18.5 in adults, or below the 5th BMI-for-age centile in children and adolescents; "dangerously low" is below 14 in adults or below the 0.3rd centile) [1; centile thresholds general knowledge].
   - Supporting features: deliberate restriction, significant weight loss, **intense fear of weight gain**, **body image disturbance** ("still fat"), compensatory exercise [1].
2. **Comorbid / underlying:**
   - **Autism spectrum disorder, without intellectual disability** (probable). Evidence:
     - lifelong social difficulty (loner, no friends);
     - **insistence on sameness and rigidity** (things kept particular, room off-limits);
     - **sensory sensitivity** (fussy about food textures and clothes as an infant);
     - circumscribed, intense interests (gaming);
     - **broader autism phenotype in the father** (monotonous prosody, circumstantiality).
   - **ADHD** (possible): childhood over-activity and talkativeness. Needs confirmation (onset before 12, more than one setting, impairment).
   - **Depressive episode** (low mood). It may be secondary to starvation, so reassess after weight restoration.
   - **Cannabis and tobacco use, in early remission** (harmful pattern or dependence; clarify); history of **problematic gaming / gaming disorder** (remitted).
3. **Rule out:**
   - **ARFID** (avoidance due to sensory or fear-of-consequences reasons, *without* weight or shape concern). The infant food fussiness hints at an earlier ARFID-like pattern, but the current shape and weight cognitions point to **AN**;
   - **OCD**;
   - **medical causes of weight loss** (hyperthyroidism, diabetes, IBD, coeliac disease, TB, malignancy).

### Points to note
- **Male AN** is under-recognised. The clinical sex ratio is about 13:1 in clinical samples [1].
  - Males more often pursue **muscularity and leanness** via exercise.
  - Androgen and testosterone fall instead of amenorrhoea.
- **AN–ASD overlap:**
  - about 20–30% of people with AN have high autistic traits [general knowledge];
  - shared **cognitive rigidity, weak central coherence, poor set-shifting** and social-emotional processing difficulties [1];
  - ASD is associated with a **poorer outcome** and needs adapted treatment.
- **Trajectory of reward-seeking and compulsive behaviours** (gaming → cannabis → exercise and restriction): consider a **common factor** (rigidity, emotion regulation through behaviour, ADHD impulsivity).

### (a) Further evaluation
1. **Eating-disorder history:**
   - daily intake (24-hour recall), restriction rules, calorie counting;
   - **fluid restriction**;
   - bingeing, **vomiting, laxatives, diuretics, steroids or supplements** (muscle-building);
   - exercise pattern;
   - weighing and body checking;
   - **drive for muscularity** vs thinness;
   - triggers (teasing, sport, social media).
2. **Weight history:** premorbid weight, rate of loss, **BMI and BMI centile (or %mBMI)**, growth charts (height velocity: stunting risk).
3. **Mood and risk:** depressive symptoms, **suicidal ideation**, self-harm.
4. **Neurodevelopmental assessment:**
   - developmental history (speech, social reciprocity, play, repetitive behaviours, sensory issues);
   - **ADI-R/ADOS-2** (interpret with caution during starvation, which itself increases rigidity);
   - **ADHD:** Conners or Vanderbilt, teacher reports.
5. **Substance use:** current status, relapse risk; caffeine and energy drinks, appetite suppressants.
6. **Family:** functioning, communication, parental eating attitudes, expressed emotion, family psychiatric history.
7. **Physical examination:**
   - **temperature, HR and BP lying and standing** (orthostatic changes);
   - **SUSS test** (Sit-Up-Squat-Stand, for proximal muscle weakness);
   - hydration, lanugo, acrocyanosis, oedema, bradycardia;
   - Tanner stage; parotid swelling and Russell's sign (purging).
8. **Investigations:**
   - CBC; **electrolytes including K, Mg, PO4**; glucose; urea and creatinine; LFT; albumin;
   - TFT; CK;
   - **ECG (bradycardia, QTc)**;
   - **testosterone, LH, FSH**; vitamin D;
   - **DEXA** if more than 6–12 months of low weight;
   - stool and urine; coeliac screen if indicated.
9. **Risk stratification** (MEED 2022: Medical Emergencies in Eating Disorders):
   - %mBMI below 70%;
   - HR below 40 (awake), postural tachycardia above 30;
   - systolic BP below 90; syncope;
   - temperature below 35.5 °C;
   - low K, PO4, Na or glucose;
   - QTc above 460;
   - rapid weight loss.

   Any of these indicates **inpatient medical stabilisation** [general knowledge].

### (b) Management
**1. Medical stabilisation and refeeding (if high risk):**
- admit to a paediatric or medical bed, with joint psychiatry–paediatrics care;
- **refeeding:** start at about **1,400–2,000 kcal/day** (MEED: avoid underfeeding) and increase by 200–400 kcal every 1–2 days;
- **monitor PO4, K and Mg daily for the first week** for **refeeding syndrome**; supplement phosphate and thiamine;
- nasogastric feeding if oral intake fails (MHCA 2017 for treatment without consent if needed, via a nominated representative for a minor);
- supervised meals; activity restriction while unstable;
- **target weight gain:** 0.5–1 kg/week (inpatient); 0.5 kg/week (outpatient).

**2. Psychological treatment:**
- **Family-based treatment (FBT, Maudsley approach): first-line for adolescent AN**:
  - Phase 1: **parents take charge of refeeding**;
  - Phase 2: control is gradually returned to the adolescent;
  - Phase 3: adolescent issues and identity.
- **CBT-E** (enhanced CBT) for adolescents if FBT is unsuitable; adolescent-focused psychotherapy.
- **ASD adaptations** (e.g., the **PEACE pathway**, King's College):
  - sensory-friendly environment;
  - predictable routine;
  - explicit, concrete communication;
  - visual schedules;
  - **cognitive remediation therapy (CRT)** for rigidity and set-shifting;
  - address sensory food aversions separately.
- Approaches with emerging evidence (mainly adult or older-adolescent AN) [1]:
  - **Radically Open DBT** (over-control);
  - **MANTRA** (adults; MI style);
  - **REaCH**;
  - compassion-focused therapy.
- **Motivational interviewing** throughout, given the egosyntonic nature of AN.

**3. Pharmacotherapy (adjunctive only):**
- **Olanzapine 2.5–7.5 mg**: superior to placebo for weight gain (Attia 2019); helps anxiety, rumination and rigidity [1];
- **aripiprazole**: may reduce eating preoccupation and rituals [1];
- **antidepressants are not effective in the underweight state**. Treat depression mainly through weight restoration; **SSRI after restoration** if depression or OCD persists;
- **ADHD:** defer stimulants while underweight (appetite suppression). Reconsider after restoration; atomoxetine with caution;
- **bone health:** calcium and vitamin D. Bisphosphonates are not recommended in adolescents. Weight restoration is the main treatment.

**4. Substance use relapse prevention:** MI, CBT; cannabis can be used to suppress appetite or anxiety.

**5. Gaming and screen time:** monitor for relapse into gaming as an avoidance strategy.

**6. School:** liaison, graded return, academic support, social skills support (ASD), anti-bullying.

**7. Long-term follow-up:** relapse prevention; transition to adult services at 18; monitor growth and bone density.

**Prognosis:**
- **Favourable:** adolescent onset, short duration, family engagement, early FBT.
- **Unfavourable:** **ASD comorbidity**, male sex (mixed evidence), low BMI, purging, substance use, depression, chronicity.
- Outcomes overall: about 50% recover, 30% improve, 20% become chronic. AN has the highest mortality of any psychiatric disorder (SMR about 5–6) [general knowledge].

## In depth

### Background
- **Classification history** [1]:
  - DSM-I: AN as a neurotic reaction;
  - DSM-III: bulimia added;
  - DSM-IV: EDNOS residual category;
  - **DSM-5 (2013): amenorrhoea removed from AN criteria** (allowing diagnosis in males and premenarchal girls), **ARFID** introduced, BED made a full diagnosis.
- **ICD-11 (6B80–6B85):**
  - AN specifiers: significantly low weight, **dangerously low weight**, in recovery with normal weight;
  - BN; BED; **ARFID**; pica; rumination-regurgitation [1].

### Epidemiology [1]
- **AN:** lifetime prevalence about 0.6%; female:male about **10–20:1**; median onset about 18 years.
- **BN:** about 1%. **BED:** 1.9%, female:male about 3:1 (community).
- **ARFID:** 5–15% in clinical feeding samples; more common in males; childhood onset.
- **India:** AN is reported less often. Atypical presentations ("non-fat-phobic" AN) are common in Asian populations [general knowledge].

### Classification and criteria (DSM-5-TR AN)
1. Restriction of energy intake leading to **significantly low body weight**.
2. **Intense fear of weight gain** or persistent behaviour interfering with weight gain.
3. **Disturbance in experience of body weight or shape**, undue influence on self-evaluation, or lack of recognition of seriousness.

- Subtypes: restricting; binge-eating/purging.
- Severity (adults): BMI 17 or above (mild), 16–16.99 (moderate), 15–15.99 (severe), below 15 (extreme). For children, use BMI percentiles.

### Mechanism in detail [1]
- **Neurobiology:**
  - reduced grey matter (ACC, precuneus);
  - white-matter changes;
  - **insula** interoceptive abnormalities (body image);
  - increased dorsal striatal activity when choosing low-calorie foods (**habit formation**);
  - **reward hyporesponsiveness**.
- **Neurotransmitters:**
  - **5-HT1A ↑, 5-HT2A ↓** (persisting into recovery);
  - **D2/D3** altered;
  - low glutamate in ACC.
- **Genetics:**
  - **heritability 48–74%**;
  - female relatives have 11× the risk;
  - **GWAS locus on chromosome 12**, with genetic correlations with metabolic traits, so AN is reconceptualised as a **metabolic–psychiatric** disorder (Watson 2019) [1].
- **Epigenetics:** DAT promoter hypermethylation; OXTR methylation correlating with BMI [1].
- **Endophenotypes:**
  - **cognitive rigidity, poor set-shifting, weak central coherence**;
  - social-emotional processing difficulties;
  - altered theory of mind [1].

  These overlap with ASD.
- **Personality:** high persistence, harm avoidance, perfectionism, over-control.
- **Socio-cultural:**
  - thin (or muscular) ideal;
  - social media;
  - sports (endurance, weight-category, aesthetic);
  - objectification [1].
- **Gut microbiome:** reduced Bacteroidetes; raised inflammatory markers (TNF-α, IL-6) [1].

### Clinical features: medical complications (relevant to monitoring)
- **Cardiac:**
  - **bradycardia**, hypotension, QTc prolongation, arrhythmia;
  - mitral valve prolapse; pericardial effusion.
- **Metabolic:**
  - hypoglycaemia; electrolyte disturbance;
  - **refeeding syndrome** (hypophosphataemia, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, thiamine deficiency, fluid shifts, heart failure).
- **Endocrine:**
  - **hypothalamic hypogonadism** (↓ LH, FSH, testosterone in males);
  - **low IGF-1**; raised cortisol; **sick euthyroid** (low T3);
  - decreased leptin [1].
- **Bone:** **osteopenia and osteoporosis**, which may be irreversible if onset is before peak bone mass [1].
- **Haematological:** pancytopenia; bone marrow gelatinous transformation.
- **GI:** delayed gastric emptying, constipation, SMA syndrome, raised transaminases.
- **CNS:** pseudoatrophy (reversible), cognitive slowing.
- **Growth:** stunting in adolescents.

### Assessment tools [1]
- **SCOFF** (screen);
- **EDE-Q**;
- EDI-3;
- **EAT-26**;
- Morgan–Russell outcome scale;
- BITE (bulimia).

### Differential diagnosis
| Condition | Distinguishing features |
| --- | --- |
| ARFID | No weight or shape concern; sensory, fear-of-aversive-consequence or low-interest pattern |
| Depression with appetite loss | No drive for thinness; weight loss unwanted |
| OCD | Food rituals without weight or shape focus |
| Muscle dysmorphia (BDD) | Belief of being too small; muscle-building; anabolic steroids |
| ASD with restricted diet | Sensory-based restriction, longstanding |
| Psychosis | Delusional food beliefs (poisoning) |
| Medical illness | Hyperthyroidism, IBD, diabetes, malignancy, TB |

### Management details
- **NICE NG69 (2017):**
  - FBT first-line for children and young people;
  - CBT-ED as an alternative;
  - for adults: CBT-ED, **MANTRA** or **SSCM** (specialist supportive clinical management) [general knowledge].
- **Medication:** none licensed. Olanzapine has modest evidence for weight [1].
- **Other options** [1]:
  - **neuromodulation** (rTMS to DLPFC; DBS for severe enduring AN) is experimental;
  - **telemedicine / internet CBT** is better for binge-type disorders than restriction.

| Study | Finding |

### Critical appraisal
- **Low weight inflates autistic traits** (starvation increases rigidity and social withdrawal). An ASD diagnosis is best confirmed with developmental history and after weight restoration. Here, the **infant sensory fussiness and the father's phenotype** support true ASD.
- **Male AN** research is sparse. Instruments were designed around female thinness ideals and miss muscularity concerns.
- **FBT evidence** comes from Western families. Adaptation to Indian joint families (grandparents' feeding roles) is needed.

### Indian context
- **Under-recognition:**
  - presentations are often somatic (abdominal pain, "gastric");
  - **non-fat-phobic AN** (religious fasting, "loss of appetite") is common;
  - males are rarely considered.
- **Services:** few specialised eating-disorder services (NIMHANS child psychiatry, AIIMS, some private centres).
- **MHCA 2017:** admission and treatment of minors (Section 87) and **nasogastric feeding without consent** require careful legal framing [general knowledge].

### Recent advances
- **Metabo-psychiatric** reconceptualisation of AN (GWAS) [1].
- **PEACE pathway** for autistic people with eating disorders [general knowledge].
- **Psilocybin** pilot trials in AN (Peck 2023) [general knowledge].
- **Higher-calorie refeeding** protocols (MEED 2022) are shown to be safe with monitoring [general knowledge].
