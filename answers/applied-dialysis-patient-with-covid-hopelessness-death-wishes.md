## Exam answer

**Case summary.**
- **Patient:** a 50-year-old single man, an auto driver.
- **Medical history:** hypertension, diabetes, and **CKD on haemodialysis three times a week**. He is recently **HCV positive**.
- **Now:** **COVID-19 positive**, with fever and breathlessness, and shifted to a COVID hospital.
- **Family:** **seven family members** (parents, brother and his family) have been quarantined, and they are **angry with him**.
- **Mental state:** he has developed **sadness, hopelessness and death wishes**.

### 1. Factors contributing to his distress [1, 2]
- **COVID-related:** the CPG lists these:
  - fear of being alone or away from family in an isolation centre;
  - **fear of death**;
  - financial burden and unemployment (relevant for a daily-earning auto driver);
  - feeling **not cared for** by the team;
  - an unfriendly ward;
  - **seeing co-patients die**;
  - poor coping.
- **Interpersonal:**
  - **family blame and anger** for their quarantine;
  - the CPG specifically flags **guilt about infecting others** and "blaming the other person for suffering" when families are affected together.
- **Medical burden:**
  - multiple chronic illnesses;
  - **dialysis dependence**: dialysis survival is about **80% at 1 year and 35% at 5 years** [2];
  - a new HCV diagnosis.
- **Organic contributors:**
  - **uraemic encephalopathy** (irritability, insomnia, cognitive and attentional deficits, progressing to delirium) [2];
  - **delirium**, common in severe COVID [1];
  - **hypoxia**;
  - drugs used for COVID (**dexamethasone/prednisolone**, hydroxychloroquine and others) [1].
- **Social:** he is **single**, so support may be limited, and the family is quarantined elsewhere.

### 2. Assessment [1]
**Baseline assessment**:
- pre-existing mental disorder and past or current psychotropics;
- **substance use** (type, quantity, last intake, withdrawal);
- cognition and past delirium;
- sleep, coping, frustration tolerance, personality;
- **lifetime suicidal behaviour** and current ideation, plans and recent attempts;
- **MSE**;
- expectations from treatment.

**Screening tools:** PHQ-9 (or PHQ-2/4), GAD-7, and **CAM / CAM-ICU** for delirium. In the ward, also use the Richmond scale and MMSE, HMSE or MoCA.

**Context**:
- the doctor–patient relationship;
- food, cleanliness, phone or Internet access, and whether he can **communicate with family**;
- deaths of co-patients.

**Aetiology:**
- link the onset of symptoms in time to anti-COVID drugs (especially **steroids**);
- check **oxygen saturation, electrolytes, renal parameters and QTc** [1];
- ask about **dialysis adherence** (missed or shortened sessions) and uraemic symptoms; an EEG can help if delirium or uraemia is suspected [2].

**Suicide risk:**
- He has **death wishes and hopelessness**.
- Assess plans and intent; use the CSSR-S or Beck scales.
- Look for **cognitive symptoms of depression**: hopelessness, helplessness, **feeling a burden** [1].

**Daily surveillance**: mood, anxiety, sleep, **suicidality, fear of death**, worries about family health.

### 3. Provisional diagnosis
- **Depressive episode** (or adjustment disorder with depressed mood) in the setting of acute COVID-19 and ESRD, **with death wishes**.
- **Differentials:**
  - **delirium** (hypoactive delirium can mimic depression);
  - **uraemic encephalopathy**;
  - **steroid- or medication-induced** mood disorder;
  - **acute stress reaction**.

### 4. Management
**Immediate safety [1]:**
- If there is **imminent risk of self-harm**, start the **high-risk management protocol** at once:
  - activate all safety and security measures;
  - **24×7 monitoring**;
  - start pharmacotherapy;
  - explain the situation to the family;
  - obtain consent from the patient or family according to his capacity.
- Advocate for appropriate admission. The CPG notes that persons with mental illness may be **discriminated against** for COVID ward admission.

**Psychological first aid for all patients** [1]:
- reassure him (the COVID mortality rate is not very high);
- let him **ventilate** fears and anger;
- answer his queries (length of stay, testing, family);
- encourage **adaptive coping**: prayer, mindfulness, talking to loved ones, sleep, hobbies;
- discourage maladaptive coping: constant news, screen time, substances, repeatedly checking vitals;
- relaxation, yoga and meditation as COVID severity permits.

**Do not rush psychotropics** when there is no immediate threat: acute anxiety and depression often settle with reassurance and as respiratory symptoms subside [1].

**Depression management** [1]:
- **Mild:** supportive sessions and CBT techniques.
- **Moderate to severe:** **SSRIs/SNRIs**, mindful of drug interactions; short-term low-dose benzodiazepines (remember **respiratory depression**).
- **If suicidal:** high-risk management and close surveillance.
- Add an antipsychotic if psychotic features are present.
- If **steroids** or other drugs are implicated, discuss reduction or change with the physician.

**General prescribing in the medically ill** [1]:
- **start low, go slow**;
- consider oxygen requirement, COVID drugs, **renal and hepatic function**, coagulopathy, **QTc interaction** (anti-COVID drugs plus antipsychotics) and **hyponatraemia**.

**Renal-specific prescribing [2, 3]:**
- **Maudsley** (renal impairment): no antidepressant is clearly preferred. Reasonable choices:
  - **sertraline** (poor efficacy data in renal disease);
  - **citalopram** (QTc; higher sudden-death risk on haemodialysis vs other SSRIs);
  - fluoxetine (long half-life).
- **Anxiolytic or hypnotic:** **lorazepam or zopiclone**. Sedation is more likely in renal impairment.
- **Antipsychotic, if needed:** haloperidol 2–6 mg/day or olanzapine 5 mg/day. Avoid sulpiride and amisulpride.
- **Evidence for antidepressants in CKD is lacking**; depression is poorly treated on haemodialysis. **CBT, exercise and relaxation** probably reduce depressive symptoms on dialysis [3].
- **Avoid highly anticholinergic** and QTc-prolonging drugs, given electrolyte shifts [3].
- K&S (renal dosing): desvenlafaxine at most 50 mg on alternate days in severe renal insufficiency; duloxetine to be avoided; mirtazapine and paroxetine at reduced dose [2].

**Delirium, if present** [1]: treat the cause, give re-orientation cues, and use **melatonin, haloperidol or quetiapine** (with QTc monitoring) or dexmedetomidine.

**Family liaison** [1]:
- Establish a relationship with the family, who are themselves distressed and quarantined.
- Give **regular updates**.
- Address **blame and guilt**, and involve them in telephonic or video support.
- Psychological first aid can be extended to family members.

**Liaison with the COVID and nephrology teams** [1, 2]:
- Inform nurses and physicians of his mental state and risk.
- Ensure **dialysis continues**: treating depression can **improve dialysis adherence** [2].

**Post-COVID phase** [1]:
- reassess for depression, anxiety, **PTSD** (ICU stay, seeing deaths), cognitive deficits and fatigue;
- address **anticipated stigma** on returning to society;
- address financial and relationship issues;
- **grief work** if a family member is lost.

## In depth

### Why he is high-risk [1, 2]
| Domain | Finding | Source point |
|---|---|---|
| Mood | Hopelessness, death wishes | Cognitive symptoms of depression; assess suicidality |
| Social | Single; family angry and quarantined | Family blame, guilt about infecting others; isolation from family |
| Medical | ESRD on dialysis, DM, HTN, HCV | Organic contributors; dialysis survival 35% at 5 years |
| Occupational | Daily-wage auto driver | Unemployment and financial burden as COVID stressors |
| Treatment | Possible steroids, QTc-interacting drugs | Medication-induced symptoms; prescribing constraints |

### CPG at a glance [1]
- **Acute stress / panic:**
  - reassurance and validation;
  - check whether masks or BiPAP are the trigger;
  - short-acting benzodiazepines; SSRIs if panic persists.
- **Delirium:** CAM-ICU; melatonin, haloperidol or quetiapine; lorazepam only for alcohol withdrawal.
- **Insomnia:** sleep hygiene; low-dose melatonin or zolpidem.
- **Substance withdrawal:** standard detoxification protocols, respecting oxygen saturation; NRT for tobacco.
