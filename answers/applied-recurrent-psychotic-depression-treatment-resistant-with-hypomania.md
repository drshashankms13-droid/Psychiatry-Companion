## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 40-year-old man has had **severe depression for 6 months** with **agitation, suspiciousness, auditory hallucinations and intense suicidal ideas**. Adequate trials of **sertraline 150 mg (2 months)** and **escitalopram 20 mg (6 weeks)** and a course of **rTMS** have failed. He has had **3 similar episodes over 10 years**, all with **psychotic features and high suicide risk needing admission**, full inter-episode recovery, and **one episode followed by hypomania**.

**Provisional diagnosis.** **Recurrent severe depressive episode with psychotic symptoms** — most likely **bipolar disorder (bipolar II, or bipolar I if the "hypomania" was in fact mania)**, current episode depressed, severe, with psychotic features; differential **recurrent depressive disorder with psychotic features** if the hypomania was purely antidepressant-induced and brief. The current episode is **treatment-resistant psychotic depression** — and resistance is partly **pseudo-resistance**, because SSRI monotherapy and rTMS are not the right treatments for psychotic depression [1].

### 1. Additional information required for diagnosis

**A. To settle unipolar versus bipolar**
- **The hypomanic episode:** symptoms, duration (≥4 days hypomania, ≥7 days mania), severity, need for admission, psychotic features — mania would make it **bipolar I**.
- **Relationship to antidepressants:** did it start during antidepressant treatment, and did it **persist beyond the drug's physiological effect**? (A full syndrome persisting after stopping counts towards bipolar disorder.)
- Other subthreshold hypomanic periods, irritability, reduced need for sleep, overspending — use **collateral history** and the **Mood Disorder Questionnaire / HCL-32**.
- **Bipolarity indicators:** early onset, **psychotic depression**, recurrent brief severe episodes, atypical features, postpartum episodes, antidepressant non-response or switching, **family history of bipolar disorder, suicide or psychosis**.
- **Mixed features now:** the "agitation" may be **mixed depression** — ask about racing thoughts, pressured speech, increased energy, risk-taking.

**B. About the psychotic features**
- Content of voices and suspiciousness — **mood-congruent** (guilt, deserved punishment, ruin) or **mood-incongruent**; **command hallucinations** to self-harm.
- Psychosis confined to mood episodes (supports mood disorder) versus psychosis persisting between episodes (consider **schizoaffective disorder**).

**C. Treatment history (pseudo-resistance check)**
- Previous medications, doses, durations, response — especially whether **antipsychotics, lithium or ECT** helped in past episodes (best predictor of current response).
- **Adherence**, current dosing, drug levels if available, side effects.
- rTMS parameters (target, number of sessions).

**D. Exclusions and comorbidity**
- **Substance use** (alcohol, cannabis, stimulants, benzodiazepines).
- **Medical/organic causes:** thyroid function, B12, folate, calcium, HIV, VDRL; neurological signs; **neuroimaging** given psychosis at 40 and recurrent severe episodes; EEG if indicated.
- Personality, psychosocial stressors, losses, social support.

**E. Risk**
- Detailed suicide risk: plans, preparations, previous attempts and lethality, access to means, hopelessness, command hallucinations, protective factors; risk to others if paranoid.

### 2. Management strategies

**A. Immediate**
1. **Admit** — psychotic depression with intense suicidal ideas, agitation and failure of outpatient care. Under the **Mental Healthcare Act 2017**: independent admission if he has capacity; otherwise **supported admission (Section 89)** with nominated representative.
2. **Suicide precautions:** close observation, remove means, frequent risk review.
3. Baseline: FBC, LFT, RFT, electrolytes, TFT, glucose, lipids, ECG, weight; anaesthetic fitness for ECT.

**B. ECT — first-line choice here**
- Indications fulfilled: **psychotic depression**, **high suicide risk**, **severe agitation**, **failure of several treatments** and need for **rapid response** [1].
- ECT is effective in MDD and may be **more efficacious in psychotic depression** and **more protective against relapse** in it [1]; effective in **bipolar depression** too.
- Usually bitemporal or right unilateral high-dose, twice/thrice weekly, 6–12 sessions; informed consent or advance directive / nominated representative per MHCA 2017; **no ECT without anaesthesia and muscle relaxant**.

**C. Pharmacotherapy (concurrently or if ECT declined)**

| Situation | Choice [1, 2] |
| --- | --- |
| Psychotic depression, unipolar | **Antidepressant + antipsychotic** combination — strongest evidence for **olanzapine + fluoxetine**; combination better than either alone; if monotherapy must be used, a **TCA** |
| **Bipolar depression (likely here)** | **Avoid antidepressant monotherapy** (switch risk); use **olanzapine + fluoxetine**, **quetiapine**, **lurasidone** or **cariprazine**; add **lithium** |
| Suicidality | **Lithium** — reduces suicide and attempts (about 80%) in bipolar and unipolar disorder [2] |
| Agitation | Short-term lorazepam/clonazepam |

Practical plan: **stop escitalopram (taper) → start olanzapine (10–20 mg) with fluoxetine (20–40 mg)**, or quetiapine if bipolarity is confirmed and SSRIs are to be avoided → **add lithium** (target 0.6–0.8 mmol/L; augmentation of antidepressants in unipolar TRD also uses lithium) [2] → ECT course alongside.

**D. If still resistant**
- Change antipsychotic; TCA + antipsychotic (unipolar); **lithium + quetiapine**; **ketamine/esketamine** — possibly effective in psychotic depression but evidence is limited (case series) and not first line [1]; **mifepristone** reduced psychotic symptoms in pooled trials [1]; clozapine in exceptional cases.
- Re-examine diagnosis: schizoaffective disorder, substance use, organic cause.

**E. Continuation and maintenance**
- Continue the **antidepressant–antipsychotic combination for at least 4–6 months after remission**; **STOP-PD II**: stopping olanzapine from sertraline + olanzapine raised relapse from **20% to 55%**, mostly in the first 2 months [1]. Then taper the antipsychotic slowly only after weighing metabolic risk against past relapse and psychosis [1].
- With bipolarity and 4 severe psychotic episodes: **long-term lithium** (first-line prophylaxis; anti-suicidal) ± quetiapine/olanzapine; **avoid long-term antidepressants** if bipolar [2].
- **Continuation/maintenance ECT** if relapse occurs despite drugs (maintenance ECT + nortriptyline superior to nortriptyline alone at 2 years) [1].
- Monitoring: lithium level, eGFR, TFT every 6 months; metabolic monitoring on SGAs.

**F. Psychosocial**
- Psychoeducation of patient and family (nature of illness, early signs, adherence, suicide warning signs); family-focused therapy; CBT for residual symptoms; IPSRT and sleep regularity if bipolar; crisis plan; occupational rehabilitation; **advance directive** under MHCA 2017 for future episodes.

### 3. Long-term course and outcome

**Course**
- Psychotic depression is **recurrent**: once psychotic features occur, **they tend to recur in future episodes** [1] — he has already had four.
- Compared with non-psychotic depression: **greater severity, impairment and longer episodes**, **poorer long-term outcome** and poorer response to combined drug and psychological treatment [1].
- **Diagnostic shift:** a sizeable proportion of patients with psychotic depression are later re-diagnosed with **bipolar disorder** (author's knowledge — early psychotic depression is a known predictor); his hypomania already points this way. Each further episode raises recurrence risk.
- Inter-episode **full recovery** so far is reassuring, but residual cognitive and functional deficits may accumulate.

**Outcome**
- **Suicide risk** is much higher than in non-psychotic depression [1] — the dominant long-term risk; lithium and ECT reduce it.
- Good acute response to **ECT** and antidepressant–antipsychotic combinations is common.

| Favourable | Unfavourable |
| --- | --- |
| Full inter-episode recovery | Psychotic features in every episode |
| Good premorbid functioning (presumed) | High suicide risk each episode |
| Episodic course; ECT/lithium responsiveness likely | Four episodes in 10 years; current treatment resistance |
| Family support (to be confirmed) | Possible bipolarity with antidepressant-related switching |
| | Long current episode (6 months) |

**Overall:** good chance of remission of the current episode with ECT and appropriate combination pharmacotherapy; long-term prognosis is **guarded** — high recurrence and suicide risk — but substantially improved by maintenance lithium (± antipsychotic), adherence, maintenance ECT where needed and early relapse detection.

**Conclusion.** This man most likely has bipolar (or recurrent) depression with psychotic features, made "treatment-resistant" by treatment that does not suit psychotic depression. The key additional information is the nature of the hypomania, family history, treatment history and organic exclusion. Management is admission for safety, ECT, and an antidepressant–antipsychotic (or bipolar-appropriate) combination with lithium, continued for months after remission, plus long-term lithium-based prophylaxis and psychoeducation.

## In depth

### Background

- Psychotic depression has a **lifetime prevalence of about 1%**, occurs in both MDD and bipolar disorder, and is **often under-diagnosed** — missed at academic centres in many cases — although it needs a different treatment approach [1].
- **ICD-11:** "depressive episode, severe, with psychotic symptoms" within single/recurrent depressive disorder or bipolar disorders; a single hypomanic episode with a depressive history is **bipolar type II** in ICD-11 [3].

### Mechanism

- **HPA-axis hyperactivity** (high cortisol, dexamethasone non-suppression) is more common in psychotic depression — the basis for glucocorticoid-receptor blockade with mifepristone [1].
- Dopaminergic overactivity with depression explains the need for combined antidepressant and antipsychotic action.

### Why SSRI and rTMS failed — pseudo-resistance

| Factor | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Monotherapy | SSRI alone is less effective than antidepressant + antipsychotic in psychotic depression [1] |
| Bipolarity | Antidepressant monotherapy is ineffective and risky in bipolar depression [2] |
| rTMS | Evidence base largely excludes psychotic depression (author's knowledge) |
| Duration/dose | Adequate here (2 months sertraline 150 mg; 6 weeks escitalopram 20 mg) |

### Evidence on pharmacotherapy [1]

- **Oliva et al. 2024 network meta-analysis** (14 RCTs, acute phase): SSRI + SGA — particularly **fluoxetine + olanzapine** — gave the highest response rate and a good efficacy–tolerability balance; combination beat either monotherapy.
- Among monotherapies, **TCAs (amoxapine, imipramine)** beat venlafaxine and mirtazapine.
- Limitation: trials only up to 2013; continuation studies excluded.
- **NICE 2022** also advises an antidepressant–antipsychotic combination.

### ECT evidence [1]

- Pooled sham-controlled trials: real ECT better than sham at 4 weeks in delusional depression, no difference at 6 months — hence the need for continuation treatment.
- Small RCT: maintenance ECT + nortriptyline superior to nortriptyline alone at 2 years.

### Ketamine and other options [1]

- Ketamine 0.5 mg/kg IV over 40 minutes (1–7 infusions over 3–8 weeks) or 30–120 mg intranasal at 3–7-day intervals in small series; reduced depressive and occasionally psychotic symptoms; no worsening of psychosis reported, but samples tiny. Esketamine case series (n = 4) in TRD with psychotic features.
- **Mifepristone** (pooled 5 trials, n = 833 vs 627 placebo, 7 days): reduced psychotic symptoms; better response with plasma level ≥1637 ng/mL.
- Anecdotal: methylphenidate, lamotrigine, phenelzine + aripiprazole + quetiapine, minocycline.

### Lithium [2]

- Lithium augmentation is effective in unipolar TRD, best at plasma level ≥0.5 mmol/L; it also protects against suicide in unipolar depression.
- In bipolar disorder, prophylactic level 0.6–0.8 mmol/L; abrupt discontinuation causes early relapse.

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
| --- | --- |
| Bipolar depression with psychotic features | History of hypomania/mania (present here) |
| Recurrent depressive disorder with psychotic features | Hypomania only during antidepressant and brief |
| Schizoaffective disorder | Psychosis persisting without mood symptoms |
| Mixed episode / depression with mixed features | Agitation with racing thoughts, increased energy |
| Substance-induced | Temporal link; urine screen |
| Organic (thyroid, neurological) | Physical signs; investigations |

### Critical appraisal

- Evidence on maintenance duration of the antipsychotic beyond 4–6 months is lacking; decisions rest on relapse history and adverse effects [1].
- Psychotic depression is excluded from most antidepressant and neuromodulation trials, so evidence is thinner than for non-psychotic depression.
- Distinguishing true hypomania from antidepressant-induced activation is often retrospective and unreliable.

### Indian context

- ECT is available in most teaching hospitals and is cost-effective for severe psychotic depression; MHCA 2017 (Section 95) prohibits ECT without anaesthesia and muscle relaxants.
- Olanzapine, fluoxetine, quetiapine and lithium are inexpensive generics.
- Family involvement in suicide observation and adherence is culturally natural and valuable.

### Recent advances

- **Esketamine/ketamine** in TRD (and limited evidence in psychotic depression) [1].
- **Mifepristone** and glucocorticoid-receptor strategies [1].
- 2024 network meta-analysis refining drug choice [1].
