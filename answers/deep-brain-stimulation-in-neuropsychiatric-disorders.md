## Exam answer

### Definition [1]
- DBS is a neurosurgical procedure:
  - an **electrode is implanted stereotactically** into a specific brain target;
  - a **pacemaker-like stimulator** delivers **continuous electrical stimulation**.
- It is an alternative to destructive **lesioning** surgery. Stimulation-related side effects **can be reversed** [3].

### Targets studied in depression [1]
- **Subcallosal cingulate gyrus (SCG)**.
- **Nucleus accumbens (NAc)**.
- **Ventral capsule/ventral striatum (VC/VS)**.
- **Inferior thalamic peduncle (ITP)**.
- **Ventral anterior limb of the internal capsule (vALIC)**.
- **Medial forebrain bundle (MFB)**.
- **Lateral habenular complex (LHb)**.

### Psychiatric indications [1, 2, 4, 5, 6]

| Disorder | Evidence in the sources |
| --- | --- |
| **Treatment-resistant depression** | Only **VC/VS and SCG** have had **multicentre, randomised, sham-controlled trials**, with **inconclusive** results. DBS is confined to a few centres and remains **strictly experimental** [1]. In a network meta-analysis, **bilateral rTMS was more effective than DBS** in TRD [4]. Maudsley lists DBS among non-drug TRD options but considers it **beyond its scope** [2] |
| **Treatment-resistant OCD** | "**Could be effective**" for resistant OCD (observational study; listed in Maudsley's OCD in children and adolescents section) [2]. Still an **experimental** treatment [5]. Future **CSTC-circuit-guided personalised neuromodulation** [6]. Psychosurgery for severe OCD works by **interrupting frontal connections** [3] |
| **OCD with bipolar disorder** | One case: OCD improved but **mania worsened** with **bilateral anterior-limb internal capsule DBS** [5] |
| **Treatment-resistant schizophrenia** | **Nucleus accumbens and subgenual ACC** DBS was effective in **4 of 7** patients in a pilot randomised crossover trial [2]. Physical treatments other than ECT (rTMS, tDCS, DBS) are "**still largely experimental**" [2] |
| **Aggression** (historical psychosurgery) | Stereotactic **amygdalotomy**, temporal tractotomy and posterior cingulectomy were reported to help aggressive patients, many with epilepsy or learning disability. Bilateral temporal lobectomy is contraindicated (amnesia) [3] |

### Neurological and neuropsychiatric indications [2, 3]

| Disorder | Evidence in the sources |
| --- | --- |
| **Parkinson's disease / essential tremor** | **Thalamic (ventral intermediate nucleus)** lesions relieve tremor, including **essential tremor**; **DBS is an alternative**. **Bilateral stimulation is more effective** than unilateral, but **dysarthria** can be a problem. **Thalamic lesions vs stimulators: equal tremor control, more adverse events with lesions.** Thalamic DBS does not help **non-tremor symptoms**. Best results in **idiopathic, levodopa-responsive** disease [3] |
| **Dystonia** | Stereotactic surgery, including DBS, has positive effects but the **optimal site is unclear**. **Pallidal stimulation** is an alternative to bilateral pallidotomy [3] |
| **Tardive dyskinesia** | Mostly **globus pallidus internus** stimulation. Limited evidence; a possible role in **severe, distressing, drug-refractory TD** [2] |

### Update from Continuum 2022: DBS in movement disorders [7]
**Parkinson's disease:**
- **Three indications:** troublesome **dyskinesias**, troublesome **off-time** (frequent dosing), **medication-resistant tremor**.
- **Selection (CAPSIT-PD):**
  - idiopathic PD for at least **5 years**;
  - at least **33% UPDRS-III improvement** on levodopa or apomorphine challenge;
  - MRI and **neuropsychological testing within 1 year**;
  - assessment by a **multidisciplinary team including a psychiatrist and neuropsychologist**.
- **Ideal candidate:** younger, little or no cognitive impairment, fluctuating motor symptoms, high function when "on". Many centres avoid those aged 80 or over. **Dementia generally excludes surgery**; pronounced executive or memory deficits raise the risk of post-operative cognitive decline.
- **What responds:** levodopa-responsive symptoms, plus **rest tremor even when levodopa-refractory**. **Gait, balance, speech and freezing respond poorly.**
- **Outcomes:**
  - STN stimulation improved off-time in **69.1%** and dyskinesias in **62.5%**;
  - **STN allows more medication reduction**; **GPi is better for dyskinesias**;
  - long-term motor control is similar between targets.
- **Risks (1–5%):** infection, **haemorrhage**, **cognitive decline**, **worsening of gait or mood**, hardware problems. Programming takes about **6 months**.
- **Open-loop** today; **closed-loop/adaptive DBS** (sensing beta oscillations) is under research.
- **Not disease-modifying.**

**Neuropsychiatric effects of STN-DBS** [7]:
- possible **worsening of depression and anxiety**, and **impulse control disorders**;
- **apathy increases** after STN-DBS, especially in those with pre-operative ICDs and independent of dopaminergic dose changes;
- patients with **pre-operative apathy** are at higher risk of **new ICDs** afterwards;
- conversely, DBS can be considered for refractory **ICDs**, because it allows **substantial reduction of dopamine replacement therapy**.

**Essential tremor** [7]:
- **VIM thalamic DBS** is the most-used surgery: reversible, can be bilateral, controls arm and head tremor;
- alternative: **MRI-guided focused ultrasound** (unilateral, incisionless lesion).

**Dystonia** [7]:
- **Indications:** severe medication-refractory **generalized or segmental dystonia (class 1 evidence)**, refractory **cervical dystonia**, severe focal dystonia, and **status dystonicus** (DBS or intrathecal baclofen).
- **Targets:**
  - **GPi** (posteroventral; **20% non-responders**; slow response; stimulation-induced bradykinesia);
  - **STN** (similar efficacy);
  - **VIM thalamus** (dystonic tremor).
- **Better outcome:** isolated generalized (especially **DYT-TOR1A**), young, short duration, mobile dystonia, normal MRI.
- **Worse outcome:** acquired or complex dystonia, fixed contractures, **functional dystonia** (wrongly selected), **significant cognitive or neuropsychiatric comorbidity**.
- **Tardive dystonia** is listed among appropriate DBS indications.
- **DBS hardware failure** is a common iatrogenic cause of **dystonic storm**.

**Chorea, Huntington's disease and tardive dyskinesia** [7]:
- **GPi** DBS has antichoreic effects but remains **investigational**;
- the cognitive and psychiatric features of HD **limit its use**.

**MSA** [7]: GPi DBS or pallidotomy for refractory dystonia gave **mixed results**. A rapid decline after STN-DBS for presumed PD may in retrospect reveal MSA.

### Psychiatric complications of DBS [3, 5]
- **Mania/hypomania**: OCD–bipolar case with internal capsule stimulation [5].
- **Compulsive use of a subthalamic nucleus stimulator**, akin to dopamine dysregulation syndrome in Parkinson's disease [3].
- **STN-DBS**: worsening depression and anxiety, impulse control disorders, **increased apathy** [7].
- Cognitive decline and mood worsening are among the 1–5% serious surgical risks [7].

### Practical interactions [4]
- **DBS is a contraindication to rTMS** if the coil is within **10 cm** of the leads.
- TMS in a patient with DBS only for **concrete scientific or medical reasons**, with **ethics committee** oversight.
- Turning the **implanted pulse generator off** may give some protection.

**Conclusion.** DBS delivers continuous stimulation through stereotactically implanted electrodes; it is adjustable and reversible, unlike lesional psychosurgery.
- **Established in neurology**:
  - **PD** (dyskinesia, off-time, refractory tremor): STN (more medication reduction) or GPi (better for dyskinesia); selection by CAPSIT-PD; psychiatric and neuropsychological work-up; dementia excludes;
  - **VIM** DBS for essential tremor;
  - **GPi/STN** for generalized and refractory focal dystonia;
  - investigational for chorea and tardive dyskinesia.
- **Psychiatric effects of STN-DBS:** depression, anxiety, impulse control disorders and **apathy**. A psychiatrist belongs on the DBS team.
- **Experimental in psychiatry**:
  - treatment-resistant depression (SCG and VC/VS sham-controlled trials inconclusive; bilateral rTMS performed better);
  - refractory OCD (promising; risk of inducing mania in bipolar patients);
  - treatment-resistant schizophrenia (NAc/sgACC pilot, 4 of 7 responders).
- **Psychiatric adverse effects** include mania and compulsive stimulator use.
- DBS is a contraindication to nearby rTMS.
