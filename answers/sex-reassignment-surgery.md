## Exam answer

### Introduction and terminology

The term sex reassignment surgery is increasingly replaced by **gender-affirming surgery**, reflecting patient-centred care and recognition that these procedures affirm gender rather than simply reassign sex.

### Types

-   **Masculinising procedures:** chest reconstruction, hysterectomy,
    salpingo-oophorectomy, metoidioplasty, phalloplasty and
    scrotoplasty.
-   **Feminising procedures:** breast augmentation, orchiectomy,
    penectomy, vaginoplasty, vulvoplasty and facial feminisation.
-   Other procedures may include voice-related and facial procedures.

### Assessment and preparation

Assessment includes goals, expectations, capacity, informed consent, physical health, fertility considerations, mental health, support systems and ability to participate in postoperative care. Gender diversity is not a mental disorder. Psychiatric comorbidity does not automatically preclude surgery but may require concurrent treatment.

### Role of psychiatrist

The psychiatrist provides affirming assessment, supports informed decision-making, assesses relevant comorbidity and capacity, coordinates care and offers perioperative and postoperative support. Assessment should not become unnecessary gatekeeping.

### Outcomes and risks

Many people report improved gender congruence and quality of life. Risks include surgical complications, altered sensation, sexual or urinary difficulties, need for revision and possible dissatisfaction. Regret and detransition are complex and should be discussed without assuming they are common or inevitable.

### Indian context

Relevant legal frameworks include NALSA v Union of India (2014), the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019 and Rules 2020. Care should uphold dignity, autonomy and informed consent.

### Conclusion

Gender-affirming surgery is an individualised healthcare intervention. Multidisciplinary, respectful assessment and informed consent are essential.
