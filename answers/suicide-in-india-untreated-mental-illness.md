## Exam answer

**Introduction: the burden [1, 2].**
- More than **1 lakh** suicide deaths a year; the rate rose from **10.2 to 11.3 per 100,000**.
- Suicide is the **commonest cause of death at 15–29 years**.
- India's share of global suicides: **36.6%** of women, **24.3%** of men.
- Deaths by year: **131,666 (2014)**, 133,623 (2015), **230,314 (2016)** [2].
- About **25 attempts per death**, roughly **5.75 million attempts in 2016** [2].

### Arguments FOR the statement
1. **Psychiatric illness is a major recognised risk factor.** NSPS lists depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, anxiety and personality disorders first among biopsychosocial risk factors [1].
2. **High lifetime suicide rates in psychiatric disorders** [3]: bipolar 15.5%, mixed drug abuse 14.7%, **MDD 14.6%**, anxiety 7.2%, schizophrenia 6.0%.
3. **Substance use** is a common, **modifiable** risk factor, involving all substances and all patterns of use. Treatment infrastructure is limited and poorly integrated with suicide prevention, so much is **untreated** [1].
4. **The treatment gap:**
   - NSPS lists **barriers to accessing mental health and substance-use care** and **stigma about help-seeking** as risk factors;
   - it lists **access to effective clinical care** as protective;
   - its objective is suicide-prevention OPDs in every district through DMHP [1].
5. **Effective treatments exist:** lithium, clozapine, ECT and antidepressants reduce suicidal risk [3]. Untreated illness therefore means a lost chance to prevent.

### Arguments AGAINST: "mainly" is not supported
1. **The leading recorded causes are psychosocial** (NCRB 2020) [1]:
   - **family problems 34%**, **illness 18%**;
   - marital problems, love affairs, **bankruptcy and debt**, substance abuse;
   - "**mental illness**" is not the dominant recorded reason.
2. **Socioeconomic gradient** [1]:
   - most suicides are among people earning **under Rs 1 lakh a year**;
   - **daily-wage earners** form the largest occupational group;
   - **60%** had not completed school (graduates about 4%);
   - **family suicide pacts** are linked to **extreme poverty and debt**, humiliation and superstition.
3. **Clinical data on attempters (Kumar et al.)** [2]:
   - only **48%** had a diagnosable psychiatric disorder;
   - the commonest diagnosis was **adjustment disorder (23.5%)**, then MDD (14.5%);
   - attempts were mostly **impulsive (80%)**, of **low intent (58%)** and **low lethality (68%)**;
   - drivers: marital conflict, family stress, low SES, finances, alcoholism.
4. **Means and environment:** **hanging and poisoning** account for more than 80% of deaths [1]. **Pesticide ingestion** was 50% of attempts [2]. **Easy access to lethal means** is an independent, modifiable risk.
5. **Sociocultural factors:** isolation, stigma, cultural beliefs, **media sensationalisation**, clusters, violence and abuse [1].
6. **The law's choice of words:** MHCA s115 presumes **"severe stress"**, not mental illness. Parliament replaced "mental illness" (2013 draft) with "severe stress", partly because of stigma, while still recognising the need for mental-health intervention [2].
7. **Gender pattern:** males die by suicide more often, but **family problems and illness** lead in both sexes [1].
8. **Data limitations:** police-based NCRB data; cause undocumented in about **10%**, method in about 5%. Under-recognition of psychiatric illness is possible in both directions [1].

### Synthesis
- **Untreated mental illness and substance use are important, modifiable contributors.** They are a key target, especially for **lethal** and repeated suicidal behaviour.
- **But Indian data point to a multifactorial causation.** Family conflict, illness, poverty, debt, low education, impulsivity, access to pesticides and hanging, and stigma make it inaccurate to say suicides are "**mainly**" due to untreated mental illness.
- **Policy implication:** NSPS adopts a **multisectoral REDS strategy** and the WHO LIVE LIFE interventions [1]:
  - means restriction;
  - responsible media;
  - life skills for the young;
  - early identification and follow-up;
  - stronger health services;
  - community resilience;
  - surveillance.
- **Medical treatment of mental illness is necessary but not sufficient.**

**Conclusion.** The statement is **partly true but overstated**. Treating mental illness (especially depression, bipolar disorder and substance use) is essential, but India's high suicide rates reflect an interplay of **psychosocial, economic, cultural and access-to-means factors**. Prevention must be biopsychosocial and multisectoral.

## In depth

### NSPS objectives [1]
| Objective | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Effective suicide surveillance | 3 years |
| Psychiatric OPD with suicide prevention services in all districts (DMHP) | 5 years |
| Mental well-being curriculum in all educational institutions | 8 years |
| Reduce suicide mortality by 10% (vs 2020) | By 2030 |
