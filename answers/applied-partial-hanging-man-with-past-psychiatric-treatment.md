Hypoxic-brain-injury sequelae and drug choices are drawn from Maudsley 15e and **general knowledge**, marked as such. The question gives no sub-parts; the answer covers evaluation and management.

## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 35-year-old married man was brought **unconscious after partial hanging**. He was referred to psychiatry **10 days after regaining consciousness**. In the preceding **2 months** he had:
- stopped going to work;
- looked stressed;
- withdrawn socially;
- slept poorly.

He had **psychiatric treatment before marriage**, on unknown medication.

### Provisional formulation
1. **Suicide attempt by partial hanging.** This is a **high-lethality method** in the context of a probable psychiatric disorder. Treat it as **high intent until proved otherwise**.
2. **Most likely:** **current depressive episode, probably severe**, possibly **without or with psychotic features**. It is most likely a **recurrence** of an earlier disorder:
   - **recurrent depressive disorder**, or
   - **bipolar affective disorder, current episode depression**. Prior treatment before marriage raises this; ask about past highs and lithium or valproate use.
3. **Differentials and comorbidities to exclude:**
   - **schizophrenia or another psychotic disorder** (relapse after stopping medication; command hallucinations; persecutory beliefs);
   - **alcohol or substance use disorder**;
   - adjustment disorder (precipitant: work or marital stress);
   - **OCD**;
   - **personality disorder**.
4. **Medical:** **hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury** after partial hanging. It can cause:
   - cognitive impairment (memory, executive function);
   - **post-hypoxic delirium**;
   - seizures;
   - delayed **post-hypoxic leukoencephalopathy**;
   - myoclonus (Lance–Adams);
   - and, from the neck injury, laryngeal or cervical injury and aspiration pneumonia [general knowledge].
5. **Possible psychosocial precipitants:**
   - job loss or occupational stress; financial strain;
   - **marital discord**, including concealment of his illness before marriage;
   - stigma; stopping medication after marriage.

### (a) Evaluation
**1. Medical and neurological status (with the medical team):**
- GCS course, duration of unconsciousness, ventilation, seizures;
- **neck examination** (ligature mark, hyoid or laryngeal injury, cervical spine), aspiration;
- **neurological examination**; **MRI brain** (hypoxic injury of basal ganglia, hippocampi, cortex) [general knowledge];
- EEG if seizures or myoclonus;
- bloods: CBC, LFT, RFT, electrolytes, TSH, glucose, B12, **urine drug screen**, blood alcohol on arrival.

**2. Medico-legal:**
- **MLC registration** [1].
- **Inform him and the family of MHCA s115**: he is presumed to have "severe stress"; he will not be tried or punished; the Government has a duty of care [1, 2].
- **Inquiry into abetment** (IPC 306/107 and 116; BNS 108): check for cruelty or harassment [1].
- Document carefully: history in his own words, collateral history, mental state examination, capacity.

**3. Detailed history (patient alone, and wife and family separately) [3]:**
- **The attempt:**
  - **SIS components:** planning, timing, precautions against discovery, note or communication, final acts, expected lethality, **reaction to survival** (relief vs regret);
  - how he was found (rescued or interrupted);
  - alcohol before the attempt.
  - Ask: "Can you describe what happened? What did you think would happen? How did you feel afterwards? Has your view of things changed since?" [3]
- **Current episode:**
  - depressive symptoms (pervasive low mood, anhedonia, guilt, worthlessness, hopelessness, early-morning awakening, appetite and weight loss, retardation);
  - **psychotic symptoms** (nihilistic, guilt or persecutory delusions; command hallucinations);
  - **mixed features**; anxiety, OCD, PTSD;
  - **current suicidal ideation** after recovering consciousness.
- **Past psychiatric history (critical):**
  - diagnosis, **medicines** (obtain old prescriptions), response, adherence, **why they stopped**;
  - hospitalisations, previous attempts;
  - **any manic or hypomanic episodes**;
  - ECT.
- **Substance use:** alcohol (AUDIT), cannabis, tobacco; benzodiazepine misuse.
- **Family history:** mood disorder, suicide, psychosis.
- **Personal and social:**
  - marriage (duration, whether the illness was disclosed, discord, domestic violence, children);
  - occupation (work stress, debt);
  - support systems;
  - premorbid personality.
- **Risk factors** (seminar SAD PERSONS: male, depression, previous attempt, ethanol, rational-thinking loss, organised plan, sickness) and **protective factors** (children, religiosity, reasons for living) [3].

**4. Mental state examination:**
- appearance (ligature mark);
- psychomotor activity; mood and affect;
- **hopelessness**; thought content (guilt, nihilism, persecution);
- perceptual disturbances;
- **cognition** (orientation, attention, memory: **MMSE/HMSE, MoCA**, which matter for hypoxic injury);
- insight; **capacity** for treatment decisions (MHCA s4).

**5. Rating scales [3]:**
- **Beck SIS** (attempt intent);
- **C-SSRS** (current ideation and behaviour);
- **BHS** (hopelessness);
- **HAM-D or MADRS** (depression);
- **YMRS** if there are mixed or manic features;
- **BPRS** if psychotic;
- Perceived Stress Scale [1];
- **reasons for living** [3].
- Scales support clinical judgement but have **low predictive value** [3].

**6. Family assessment:** expressed emotion, wife's understanding and distress, safety at home (means).

### (b) Management
**Setting:**
- **Inpatient care is indicated:**
  - a recent high-lethality attempt;
  - probable severe depression;
  - possible bipolar or psychotic disorder;
  - possible cognitive sequelae.
- **MHCA 2017 route:**
  - **s86 independent admission** if he has capacity and consents;
  - **s89 supported admission** (up to 30 days) if he lacks capacity, has a severe disorder and is at risk of self-harm, with the **nominated representative**. The **Mental Health Review Board is informed** [1; general knowledge for section details];
  - in a general hospital ward before transfer, **s94 emergency treatment for up to 72 hours** [1].
- **Observation:** close or one-to-one; **remove ligature points** and belts; reassess risk frequently, with reducing frequency as risk falls [1].

**Pharmacological (guided by diagnosis) [4]:**
- **Unipolar severe depression:**
  - an **SSRI** (sertraline or escitalopram), or **mirtazapine** (useful for insomnia and appetite);
  - **avoid TCAs** (lethal in overdose);
  - **dispense limited quantities**; the family holds medicines;
  - monitor for activation.
  - **If there are psychotic features:** antidepressant plus antipsychotic, **or ECT**.
- **Bipolar depression:** quetiapine, lurasidone, olanzapine–fluoxetine or lamotrigine. **Add lithium**: **anti-suicidal** effect in mood disorders [3]. Avoid antidepressant monotherapy.
- **Schizophrenia relapse with suicidality:** antipsychotic. **Clozapine** reduces suicidal behaviour in schizophrenia (InterSePT) [general knowledge].
- **Short-term:** a hypnotic or anxiolytic, carefully. Avoid benzodiazepines if he has an alcohol history.
- **ECT [3]:**
  - for **acutely suicidal** patients, where delay is life-threatening;
  - psychotic depression; catatonia; poor oral intake.
  - **Caution:** check post-hypoxic brain status. Neurology clearance and MRI should be done first; ECT is not contraindicated by hypoxic injury per se [general knowledge].
- **Ketamine/esketamine:** rapid reduction of suicidal ideation (an emerging option) [3].
- Alcohol withdrawal management with thiamine, if relevant.

**Psychological:**
- **crisis intervention**; supportive psychotherapy;
- **collaborative safety planning** (warning signs, coping, contacts, **means restriction**);
- **CBT for suicide prevention** / problem-solving therapy;
- **interpersonal therapy** (role transition, marital dispute);
- couple therapy;
- **psychoeducation** for him and his wife: illness, the need for continued medication, early warning signs, what to do in a crisis [1].

**Rehabilitation and social [1, 2]:**
- **s115(2)**: rehabilitation is a State duty;
- graded **return to work**; vocational rehabilitation; disability support if cognition is impaired;
- financial counselling;
- address marital conflict and the stigma of concealment;
- **cognitive rehabilitation** for hypoxic deficits.

**Discharge plan [1]:**
- risk assessment;
- treatment of comorbidity;
- contingency supports for stressors;
- psychoeducation on alcohol and **securing lethal means**;
- a supportive person at home;
- a **24-hour contact number**; Tele-MANAS 14416;
- **early follow-up (within 7 days)**;
- **caring contacts** (calls or messages).
- **Recurrence is about 10%** [1]. The highest suicide risk is in the weeks after discharge [general knowledge].

**Prognosis:**
- **Poor indicators:**
  - a high-lethality method;
  - male sex;
  - a recurrent disorder with non-adherence;
  - substance use;
  - poor support;
  - cognitive sequelae;
  - persistent hopelessness.
- **Good indicators:**
  - treatable depression;
  - a supportive wife;
  - prior good response to treatment.

**Conclusion.** This is a **high-risk suicide attempt** in a man with a probable **recurrent mood (or psychotic) disorder** that relapsed after stopping treatment. Evaluation must cover **medical and hypoxic sequelae**, intent, the past diagnosis (especially **bipolarity**), substances, family and legal issues (**MHCA s115**, abetment). Management needs **inpatient care**, definitive treatment (antidepressant or mood stabiliser, with **lithium and ECT** as anti-suicidal options), safety planning, family work, rehabilitation and close follow-up [1–4].

## In depth

### Background: partial hanging
- **Partial hanging:** the body is partly supported (feet or knees touching the ground). Death occurs through venous and arterial occlusion and vagal reflexes, rather than cervical fracture.
- **Survivors** often have **hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy**, cerebral oedema, pulmonary oedema (negative-pressure), aspiration and seizures [general knowledge].
- Prognosis after near-hanging depends on **GCS at presentation** and **duration of hypoxia** [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology relevant to the case [1, 2]
- In India, **hanging and poisoning account for over 80% of suicide methods** [2].
- **65% of suicides are aged 18–45 years** [2].
- **Family problems (34%)** and **illness (18%)** are the leading recorded causes [2].
- In an Indian series of attempts: hanging 10%; **48% had a diagnosable psychiatric disorder** [1].
- Lifetime suicide risk estimates (Harris and Barraclough, as presented in the seminar): bipolar disorder 15.5%, MDD 14.6%, schizophrenia 6.0% [3].

### Classification of the attempt (C-SSRS) [3]
- actual attempt; interrupted attempt; aborted (self-interrupted) attempt; preparatory acts.
- A rescue during hanging would be an **interrupted** attempt that still caused injury.

### Mechanism: why he may have attempted
- **Theories applied [3]:**
  - **IMV:** defeat and entrapment (job, marriage), then intention; access to a ligature turns intention into action.
  - **Interpersonal theory:** **perceived burdensomeness** (not earning) and thwarted belongingness (withdrawal).
  - **Baumeister:** escape from self-awareness of failure.
- **Biological:** recurrence of mood disorder after stopping medication. Serotonergic dysfunction.

### Clinical features: clues to bipolarity
- early onset (before marriage, in his 20s);
- recurrence;
- **family history of bipolar disorder**;
- **hypersomnia** or atypical features;
- psychotic depression;
- antidepressant-induced switching in the past;
- prior prescription of lithium, valproate or antipsychotics.

### Assessment: hypoxic brain injury
| Domain | Look for | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memory | Anterograde amnesia (hippocampal CA1 vulnerability) | HMSE/MoCA; Rey AVLT |
| Executive | Apathy, disinhibition | FAB, Trail Making Test |
| Motor | Myoclonus, parkinsonism | Neurological examination |
| Delayed deterioration | Days to weeks later (post-hypoxic leukoencephalopathy) | MRI (diffuse white matter) |

### Differential diagnosis
- Psychotic depression vs schizophrenia with post-psychotic depression.
- Bipolar mixed state (high suicide risk).
- Alcohol-induced depressive disorder.
- Adjustment disorder (if no full syndrome).
- Organic mood disorder secondary to hypoxic injury (if symptoms started **after** the attempt).

### Management details: lithium and clozapine as anti-suicidal agents [3, general knowledge]
- **Lithium:** meta-analyses show about a 60% reduction in suicide in mood disorders (Cipriani 2013). It is **toxic in overdose**, so supervise supply and check levels.
- **Clozapine:** InterSePT (Meltzer 2003) showed fewer suicide attempts than olanzapine in schizophrenia.
- **ECT:** rapid relief of suicidal ideation in depression (CORE data) [general knowledge].

### Critical appraisal
- The **predictive value of risk scales is low** [3]. Management should be needs-based, not score-based.
- **Coercive admission** must balance safety against autonomy (MHCA rights; least restrictive option).
- Indian services often discharge attempters from medical wards **without psychiatric review**, despite the expectation of mandatory referral [1].

### Indian context
- **s115 MHCA**; the police still register an MLC [1, 2].
- **Disclosure of illness before marriage:** a common marital-conflict theme. Legal issues (annulment under the Hindu Marriage Act s12 for concealment) can arise [general knowledge].
- **Tele-MANAS**; DMHP follow-up [2].

### Recent advances
- **Safety Planning Intervention plus follow-up calls** (Stanley 2018): about a 45% reduction in suicidal behaviour [general knowledge].
- **Esketamine** for MDD with suicidal ideation.
- **Brief contact interventions** (postcards, SMS).
- **Psychological autopsy** registries [1].
