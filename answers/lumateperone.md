## Exam answer

**Introduction.** **Lumateperone** (ITI-007; brand **Caplyta**) is an atypical antipsychotic. It is classed by some as a "**fourth-generation**" or "**novel multimodal**" agent because of its combined serotonergic, dopaminergic and glutamatergic actions.
- **FDA approval** [general knowledge]:
  - **schizophrenia** in adults (December 2019);
  - **bipolar I and II depression**, as monotherapy and as an adjunct to lithium or valproate (December 2021);
  - adjunctive **MDD** (2025) [general knowledge; check].
- **Dose:** **42 mg once daily** (licensed maximum 42 mg/day). Maudsley lists an approximate equivalent dose of **21 mg**, estimated, on the same scale as olanzapine 10 mg and risperidone 4 mg, **no titration**, taken in the evening [1].

### Pharmacology [general knowledge]
- **5-HT2A antagonism** with very high affinity: **about 60-fold greater than its D2 affinity**. This gives a high 5-HT2A:D2 ratio.
- **D2 receptor:**
  - **presynaptic partial agonist / postsynaptic antagonist**;
  - **low D2 occupancy (about 40%) at 42 mg**;
  - this is thought to explain the **minimal EPS and prolactin effects**;
  - it acts preferentially on **mesolimbic and mesocortical** dopamine.
- **D1-mediated enhancement of glutamatergic (NMDA and AMPA) signalling** (via mTOR), the proposed basis for effects on negative and depressive symptoms.
- **Serotonin transporter (SERT) inhibition**: possible antidepressant effect.
- Low affinity for **H1, muscarinic and α1** receptors, so less sedation, weight gain and orthostasis than many SGAs.
- **Metabolism:** **CYP3A4**. Avoid strong inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine) and reduce the dose with strong inhibitors. Not recommended in moderate–severe hepatic impairment.

### Efficacy [general knowledge]
- **Schizophrenia:**
  - **Study 301 (Lieberman 2016, *Biol Psychiatry*)** and **Study 303 (Correll 2020, *JAMA Psychiatry*)**: 42 mg was **superior to placebo** on PANSS total at 4 weeks;
  - effect size **modest (about 0.3)**, similar to other SGAs;
  - **Study 302** failed (high placebo response);
  - improvement in prosocial factor and depressive symptoms in post-hoc analyses.
- **Bipolar depression:**
  - **Study 404 (Calabrese 2021, *Am J Psychiatry*)**: monotherapy better than placebo on MADRS in bipolar I and II depression;
  - the adjunctive study (402) was positive;
  - Study 401 was not significant.
  - It is one of few agents licensed for **bipolar II depression** [general knowledge].
- **Meta-analysis (Peng 2024):** efficacy in bipolar depression and schizophrenia, with favourable safety [1].

### Safety and tolerability
- **Metabolic:** weight, glucose and lipid changes **similar to placebo** in short-term trials; long-term open-label data show stable or reduced weight [general knowledge].
- **Prolactin:** **no clinically significant rise** [1].
- **EPS and akathisia:** low, **similar to placebo** [general knowledge].
- **Sexual dysfunction:** **none reported** in trials [1].
- **NMS:** no reports linked to lumateperone as of mid-2024 [1].
- **Common adverse effects:** **somnolence/sedation** (main), dizziness, nausea, dry mouth [general knowledge].
- **QTc:** no significant prolongation [general knowledge].
- **Class warnings:**
  - increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis;
  - suicidality warning (antidepressant indication) in young people.

### Place in therapy
- **Advantages:** a **tolerability-first** option for people with **metabolic risk, hyperprolactinaemia or EPS** concerns. Also bipolar depression (including bipolar II). No titration.
- **Limitations:**
  - modest efficacy;
  - **no data in TRS**;
  - **no LAI**;
  - limited long-term and real-world data;
  - **cost**;
  - **not available in India** [Guessing; check];
  - one of three schizophrenia RCTs failed.

**Conclusion.** Lumateperone is a novel antipsychotic: potent **5-HT2A antagonism**, **low D2 occupancy with pre- and postsynaptic actions**, SERT inhibition and glutamatergic modulation. These produce **efficacy comparable to other SGAs** with an unusually **benign metabolic, prolactin and motor profile**, plus a licence for **bipolar I and II depression**. It is a useful option where tolerability dominates, but it does not replace clozapine for resistant illness.

## In depth

### Background
- Developed by **Intra-Cellular Therapies**. It came from the work of Nobel laureate **Paul Greengard's** group on intracellular dopamine signalling (DARPP-32) [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology / use
- Mainly used in the US.

### Classification
- **NbN-style description:** a serotonin and dopamine receptor antagonist with SERT inhibition.

### Mechanism
- **About 40% striatal D2 occupancy** on PET at 60 mg, which contrasts with 65–80% for most antipsychotics. The **5-HT2A blockade** is near-saturating [general knowledge].

### Clinical features: who might benefit
- Young patients concerned about weight or sexual function.
- Patients with bipolar depression intolerant of quetiapine (sedation, metabolic effects) or lurasidone (akathisia).

### Assessment / monitoring
- Standard antipsychotic monitoring: weight, glucose, lipids, BP, EPS, prolactin if symptomatic.
- Check for CYP3A4 interactions.

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding (general knowledge unless noted) |
| --- | --- |
| Lieberman 2016; Correll 2020 | Superior to placebo in schizophrenia (4 weeks) |

### Critical appraisal
- Short trials; industry-funded; a failed trial.
- Low D2 occupancy raises the question of efficacy in severe or acute psychosis.
- No head-to-head trials against active comparators (other than a risperidone arm used for assay sensitivity).

### Indian context
- **Not marketed in India** as far as known [Guessing; verify]. Exam answers should focus on mechanism and place in therapy.

### Recent advances
- **Adjunctive MDD** approval (2025) [general knowledge; check].
- Trials in **mixed features**, **generalised anxiety** and **paediatric bipolar depression** [general knowledge].
