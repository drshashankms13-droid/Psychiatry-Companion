## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 56-year-old housewife with well-controlled hypertension on **losartan** has a 2-month **severe depressive episode with agitation, anxious rumination ("fearing relatives would have a mishap"), death wishes, insomnia, poor eating and self-neglect**. She had one prior episode 30 years ago lasting 14–15 months with full recovery. Premorbidly she is **organised, meticulous, rule-bound (anankastic traits)** without impairment. An SSRI + benzodiazepine failed at 4 weeks; on an **SNRI** she improved, then developed **talkativeness, singing, disinhibition, delusional jealousy and auditory hallucinations** — an **antidepressant-emergent manic episode with psychotic features**. On **lithium + olanzapine** she developed **extreme restlessness, inability to stand still, repetitive questioning and checking**.

**Diagnosis.** **Bipolar disorder (type I)**, most recent episode **manic with psychotic features**, following a severe depressive episode with agitation; the current restlessness is most likely **antipsychotic-induced akathisia**, with lithium toxicity, a mixed state and anxiety/obsessional symptoms as differentials.

### 1. Short-term and long-term management plan

**A. Immediate assessment of the restlessness**

| Possible cause | Check |
| --- | --- |
| **Akathisia** (olanzapine, possibly residual SNRI effect) | Subjective inner restlessness, urge to move, shifting feet, rocking; onset after starting/raising antipsychotic; Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale |
| **Lithium toxicity** — **losartan (an angiotensin II receptor antagonist) raises lithium levels**; age, poor intake, dehydration add risk [1] | Urgent **lithium level**, U&E, eGFR, sodium; coarse tremor, ataxia, confusion, GI symptoms |
| Mixed state / ongoing mania | Mood, speech, sleep, thought content |
| Agitated depression re-emerging | Low mood, hopelessness, suicidal ideas |
| Anxiety with obsessional checking (premorbid anankastic traits) | Ego-dystonic, anxiety-relieving checking |
| Delirium | Fluctuating attention, disorientation — check electrolytes, infection |
| Serotonin syndrome (SNRI recently stopped) | Clonus, hyperreflexia, fever — usually within 24 hours |

**B. Short-term (acute phase)**
1. **Setting:** admission advisable — psychotic mania, suicide risk in the recent depression, self-neglect, poor nutrition; MHCA 2017: independent admission if she has capacity, otherwise supported admission (Section 89) with nominated representative.
2. **Stop all antidepressants** (already stopped) [1].
3. **Lithium:** check level 12 hours post-dose; target **0.6–0.8 mmol/L**, upper end acceptable in acute mania but at 56 with an ARB aim conservatively and monitor weekly; liaise with her physician about **switching losartan to an antihypertensive that does not raise lithium** (e.g. a calcium-channel blocker such as amlodipine) [1].
4. **Akathisia:** reduce olanzapine dose if possible; add **propranolol 10–40 mg/day** (watch BP — already on antihypertensive), or **mirtazapine 15 mg** or a short course of **benzodiazepine (clonazepam/lorazepam)**; if persistent, switch to an antipsychotic with lower akathisia risk such as **quetiapine** (olanzapine itself is low risk, so confirm the diagnosis) [2].
5. **Antimanic and antipsychotic cover:** combination of lithium + antipsychotic is more effective and faster than either alone in mania [1]; continue lowest effective dose.
6. **Sleep, nutrition, hydration, hygiene**; monitor fluid intake (lithium).
7. **Physical monitoring:** weight, glucose, lipids (olanzapine), ECG (age, hypertension), TSH, calcium, eGFR [1].
8. **ECT** if mania is severe, psychotic features persist, intake remains poor or drugs are not tolerated.
9. **Psychoeducation for husband and family**, including the meaning of the jealousy delusion (not her real belief) to protect the marriage.

**C. Long-term (maintenance)**
1. **Diagnosis revised to bipolar I** — explain to patient and family.
2. **Lithium maintenance** (first line) for at least several years; target **0.6–0.8 mmol/L**, lower (0.4–0.6) if poorly tolerated; for older adults aim conservatively [1]. Monitor lithium, eGFR, U&E, TFTs every 6 months; calcium; weight [1].
3. Taper and stop olanzapine after sustained remission if lithium alone suffices; otherwise continue the combination (combinations protect better) [1].
4. **Avoid antidepressants** in future depressive episodes where possible; for bipolar depression use quetiapine, lurasidone, lamotrigine or lithium optimisation [1].
5. **Psychoeducation**, early warning signs (reduced sleep, talkativeness, irritability), relapse plan, sleep regularity.
6. Liaison with physician for **drug interactions** (ARBs, ACE inhibitors, thiazides, NSAIDs) [1]; avoid over-the-counter NSAIDs.
7. Address possible **anankastic traits** with supportive CBT if they cause distress.
8. Regular follow-up; cognitive screening over time (late-onset bipolar disorder can signal vascular or neurodegenerative change).

### 2. Precautions and side effects of lithium [1]

**Pre-treatment work-up:** eGFR, urea and electrolytes, **calcium**, TFTs, weight/BMI; **ECG** if cardiovascular risk factors (she has hypertension); pregnancy status where relevant.

**Prescribing:** start low (e.g. 200–400 mg at night in older adults); level after 7 days and after every dose change, 12 hours post-dose; specify liquid strength clearly.

**Precautions**
- **Drug interactions** raise lithium levels: **ACE inhibitors and angiotensin II receptor antagonists (losartan)** — up to fourfold rise, sevenfold increase in hospitalisation for toxicity in the elderly; **thiazides** (25–400% rise within 10 days); **NSAIDs** (10% to >400%); carbamazepine and SSRIs via hyponatraemia.
- **Dehydration, low-salt diet, vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, heat** → toxicity.
- Elderly, renal impairment, heart failure, Addison's disease.
- **Do not stop abruptly** — rebound mania; taper over at least 1 month, preferably 3 months.
- Teratogen (contraception in women of child-bearing age).
- Give written information on toxicity and interactions, repeated at intervals.

**Side effects**

| Type | Effects |
| --- | --- |
| Common, dose-related | Nausea, diarrhoea, **fine tremor** (propranolol helps), **polyuria/polydipsia**, metallic taste, weight gain, ankle oedema |
| Renal | **Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus**; reduced eGFR; chronic kidney disease (risk higher with levels >0.8 mmol/L, hypertension) |
| Endocrine | **Hypothyroidism** (up to 20% in middle-aged women — relevant here); rarely hyperthyroidism; **hyperparathyroidism and hypercalcaemia** (~4%) |
| Skin | Acne, psoriasis worsening |
| Cardiac | T-wave changes, sinus node dysfunction (rare) |
| Neurological | Cognitive dulling; neurotoxicity even at normal levels |
| **Toxicity** | **>1.5 mmol/L:** anorexia, nausea, diarrhoea, coarse tremor, ataxia, drowsiness, confusion, twitching; **>2 mmol/L:** disorientation, seizures, coma, death; **>3 mmol/L:** dialysis often needed |

### 3. Prognosis

**Favourable factors**
- Full inter-episode recovery for nearly 30 years; good premorbid functioning and personality integration.
- Supportive marriage; no substance use; no family history.
- Lithium-responsive profile likely (episodic course with full remission).

**Unfavourable factors**
- **Late age** at second episode with a **long first episode (14–15 months)** — suggests severe, protracted episodes.
- **Psychotic features** in mania; severe depression with suicidal ideas.
- **Antidepressant-induced switching** — greater risk of mixed states and rapid cycling if antidepressants are reused.
- Medical comorbidity (hypertension, ARB interaction) complicating lithium; possible **vascular contribution** in late life.
- Possible drug intolerance (akathisia).

**Overall:** good chance of remission from the current episode with mood stabiliser treatment; risk of further episodes is substantial now that bipolarity has declared itself, but maintenance lithium, avoidance of antidepressants and good adherence give a **fair to good long-term prognosis**.

**Conclusion.** This woman's illness is bipolar I disorder unmasked by an antidepressant-induced manic switch; her current restlessness must be assessed urgently for akathisia and lithium toxicity (losartan interaction). Management combines stopping antidepressants, careful lithium-plus-antipsychotic treatment with akathisia management, medical liaison over the antihypertensive, and long-term lithium maintenance with monitoring and psychoeducation.

## In depth

### Background — antidepressant-emergent mania

- DSM-5 counts a full manic episode that persists beyond the physiological effect of an antidepressant as evidence of bipolar disorder; ICD-11 similarly considers antidepressant-induced mania within bipolar disorders when symptoms persist (author's knowledge).
- Switch risk is higher with **TCAs and SNRIs (venlafaxine)** than with SSRIs or bupropion (author's knowledge); this case shows a switch after starting an SNRI.

### Epidemiology

- Late-onset bipolar disorder (after 50) accounts for a minority of cases; it has fewer family histories and more medical/neurological comorbidity (author's knowledge).
- About 15% of people with bipolar disorder die by suicide; lithium reduces attempted and completed suicide by about 80% [1].

### Classification

- **ICD-11:** bipolar type I disorder, current episode manic with psychotic symptoms; the earlier severe depressive episode would be coded within the bipolar diagnosis [3].
- **DSM-5:** bipolar I disorder, current or most recent episode manic, severe, with psychotic features (a jealousy delusion is usually mood-incongruent unless it arises from grandiosity — specify accordingly).

### Mechanism — akathisia

- D2 blockade in mesocortical/striatal pathways with noradrenergic over-activity; also caused by SSRIs/SNRIs; propranolol (noradrenergic), 5-HT2A antagonists (mirtazapine) and benzodiazepines are treatments.
- Akathisia is linked to agitation, impulsivity and suicidality — must not be mistaken for worsening mania or anxiety (author's knowledge, consistent with Maudsley EPS section).

### Management details

| Step | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Lithium dosing in older adults | Start 200 mg; ISBD/IGSLi: aim 0.4–0.6 mmol/L in the elderly, at most 0.7–0.8 at age 65–79 [1] |
| Mania doses | Olanzapine 10–20 mg; quetiapine IR up to 800 mg; risperidone 2–6 mg; lorazepam up to 4 mg [1] |
| Lithium in acute mania | Plasma level 0.8–1.0 mmol/L for acute treatment; antipsychotic if faster onset needed [1] |
| Monitoring on lithium | Lithium, eGFR, U&E, TFTs every 6 months; more often with interacting drugs or in the elderly [1] |
| Stopping lithium | Over at least 1 month, preferably 3; avoid level drops >0.2 mmol/L [1] |

### Differential diagnosis of the final presentation

| Condition | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Akathisia | Restlessness, cannot stand still, after antipsychotic | Repetitive checking less typical |
| Lithium toxicity | ARB, age | Needs tremor, GI, confusion; check level |
| Mixed state | Recent mania + depression | Need mood/energy features |
| Anxious/obsessive symptoms | Anankastic traits, checking | New onset with drug change |
| Delirium | Age, drugs | Check attention, orientation |

### Evidence table

| Evidence | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Cipriani 2005 meta-analysis | Lithium reduces suicide and attempts by about 80% [1] |
| Lithium discontinuation studies | Abrupt stop → high early manic relapse even after 5 years' stability [1] |
| Combination in mania | Antipsychotic + mood stabiliser more effective and quicker than either alone [1] |
| ACE inhibitor/ARB interaction | Up to fourfold rise in lithium; sevenfold risk of hospitalisation for toxicity in elderly [1] |

### Critical appraisal

- Diagnosis was revised only after the switch; screening for bipolarity (prior hypomania, family history) before prescribing antidepressants could have prevented it.
- The first depressive episode lasting 14–15 months on treatment suggests treatment resistance — lithium augmentation might have been considered then.
- Choosing lithium in a hypertensive woman on an ARB requires coordination; valproate is an alternative after menopause, and quetiapine monotherapy another.

### Indian context

- Many such patients are seen in general hospital psychiatry units; physician liaison for antihypertensive changes is practical.
- Lithium monitoring may be difficult in rural areas — ensure access to a laboratory before starting.
- Family members often misinterpret jealousy delusions; couple psychoeducation prevents marital breakdown.
- **MHCA 2017:** capacity assessment for consent to admission and ECT; nominated representative involvement.

### Recent advances

- Lithium remains the gold standard in 2024 [1]; interest in its anti-dementia and neuroprotective potential (mixed evidence) [1].
- Long-acting injectable antipsychotics for maintenance in non-adherent patients [1].
- Valbenazine-type drugs are for tardive dyskinesia, not akathisia; evidence for mirtazapine and propranolol in akathisia continues to grow (author's knowledge).
