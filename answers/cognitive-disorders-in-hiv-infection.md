## Exam answer

### Introduction [1, 2]
- **HIV-1 infection** is "the most common, preventable and treatable cause of cognitive impairment in those aged under 50" [1].
- **Mechanism:** mainly **direct brain invasion** by HIV (infected macrophages and microglia; viral proteins gp120, Tat, Vpr; cytokines and excitotoxicity). Correlations with pathology are imperfect [1].
- **History of names:** subacute encephalitis → **AIDS dementia complex** (Navia 1986) → **HAD and MCMD** (AAN 1991) [1] → **HAND** (ANI, MND, HAD) [2].

### Classification and criteria [1, 2]
**HIV-associated dementia (Box 7.1; modified ICD-10 dementia criteria):**
1. Acquired abnormality in **at least 2 cognitive domains** (attention, speed of processing, abstraction, visuospatial, memory, language) for **at least 1 month**, **impairing work or ADLs**.
2. **At least one of:** acquired **motor** abnormality, **or** decline in motivation, emotional control or social behaviour.
3. **No clouding of consciousness.**
4. **No other cause** (opportunistic infection, malignancy, psychiatric disorder, substance misuse).

**MCMD (Box 7.2):** milder cognitive, motor or behavioural symptoms (at least 2 for at least 1 month) with **mild functional impairment**, not meeting dementia criteria.

**Neuropsychological impairment:** test abnormality without functional impact.

**Delirium** is also recognised.

### Epidemiology and risk factors [1, 2]
- **Pre-ART:** over 60% of those dying of AIDS had some dementia. Navia found the dementia complex in 38% at post-mortem; it **preceded other AIDS features in a quarter**.
- **Prevalence by stage:** 1% asymptomatic, 3% at first AIDS illness, 5–10% symptomatic, **10–20% advanced** disease.
- **Annual incidence** 7–14% in symptomatic patients; about 9 years from seroconversion.
- **HAART:**
  - **incidence of HAD fell about 50%**, but **prevalence** of HAD + MCMD remains high (**37%** in one cohort) because of longer survival and poor CNS drug penetration;
  - HAD now occurs at **CD4 counts above 200** too (previously 70% below 200).
- **Asymptomatic infection:** median neuropsychological impairment **35% vs 12%** in seronegative controls. Impaired asymptomatic patients have **more unemployment** (eightfold work disability).
- **Risk factors:**
  - **high plasma viral load** (above 30,000 copies, HR 9.1); **CD4 below 200** (HR 3.4);
  - anaemia, low BMI, constitutional symptoms; **older age**;
  - **injection drug use** (cocaine increases BBB entry); methamphetamine; **hepatitis C**;
  - female sex and low education (suggested);
  - early psychomotor slowing, MCMD, extrapyramidal signs, **depression**;
  - possibly **APOE ε4**;
  - CSF viral load and chemokines; plasma TNF-α; CSF MCP-1.

### Clinical features of HAD [1]
A **subcortical dementia**: cognitive, motor and behavioural.
- **Cognitive:**
  - **memory loss and poor concentration** (commonest early);
  - **mental slowing**; confusion;
  - focal cortical signs (apraxia, agnosia) appear **late**;
  - **insight relatively preserved** until late.
- **Motor:**
  - imbalance, **ataxia**, leg weakness (may precede cognitive change);
  - loss of fine coordination, **deteriorating handwriting**, tremor, clumsiness;
  - impaired rapid eye and limb movements; hyperreflexia; **frontal release signs** (snout);
  - later spastic paraparesis, clonus, dysarthria;
  - peripheral neuropathy common.
- **Behavioural:**
  - **apathy, social withdrawal, loss of spontaneity, psychomotor slowing**: easily **mistaken for depression**;
  - irritability, emotional lability;
  - opening feature in about a quarter.
- **Psychiatric:** psychotic episodes resembling **schizophrenia, paranoid states, mania or psychotic depression**.
- **Course:**
  - steadily progressive with abrupt accelerations; half reach severe dementia within 2 months (pre-ART);
  - survival about **6 months** after diagnosis pre-ART;
  - end stage: **akinetic mutism**, incontinence, paraplegia, myoclonus, with **consciousness preserved**;
  - HAD remains a risk factor for death even with HAART (linked to poor adherence).

**Childhood HIV progressive encephalopathy:**
- developmental delay or loss of milestones, **acquired microcephaly**, spastic quadriparesis, **basal ganglia calcification**;
- 75% present before age 3.

### Investigations [1, 2]
1. **Screening:** cognitive screening is recommended for all people living with HIV (**MoCA** or the three-item **Cognitive Concerns Questionnaire**) [2]. Neuropsychology: psychomotor speed and executive tests are sensitive [1].
2. **MRI:**
   - atrophy, ventricular dilatation, **diffuse white-matter T2 hyperintensities**;
   - its **essential role is to exclude treatable opportunistic disease** (toxoplasmosis, lymphoma, PML, CMV periventriculitis) [1];
   - **MRS:** reduced NAA, raised myo-inositol and choline;
   - **PET:** early subcortical hypermetabolism, later hypometabolism;
   - SPECT and DTI changes.
3. **CSF (essential):**
   - excludes opportunistic infection and **neurosyphilis**;
   - may show mild protein rise, lymphocytes, oligoclonal bands, HIV;
   - **β2-microglobulin, neopterin, quinolinic acid, MCP-1** correlate with severity but are non-specific;
   - CSF viral load is less useful than hoped.
4. **EEG:** normal early, diffuse slowing later.
5. **Bloods:** CD4, plasma viral load, metabolic screen, B12, syphilis serology; drug and toxicology review.

### Differential diagnosis [1]
- **Depression** (apathy and slowing), anxiety, fatigue, systemic illness.
- **Delirium:** metabolic causes, drug toxicity (patients are sensitive to CNS-active drugs).
- **Opportunistic CNS disease:** **CMV** (retinitis, colitis, adrenalitis, periventricular MRI), **toxoplasmosis**, **cryptococcal or TB meningitis**, **PML**, **primary CNS lymphoma**, **neurosyphilis**.
- **Primary psychosis** coexisting with HIV.
- Other dementias in older patients; substance-related impairment; hepatitis C.

### Management [1, 2]
1. **Antiretroviral therapy (cornerstone):**
   - **combination ART** reduces HAD incidence and can improve cognition;
   - **zidovudine** (crosses the BBB; marrow suppression);
   - other CNS-penetrating drugs: abacavir, stavudine, nevirapine, efavirenz, indinavir [1];
   - Maudsley: ART with **high CNS penetration-effectiveness** (e.g., **raltegravir**), aiming for therapeutic CNS levels with minimal neurotoxicity [2];
   - **uncertain** whether CNS-penetrating regimens are necessary: Letendre supports, Antinori does not [1];
   - **efavirenz neurotoxicity:** dizziness, agitation, **hallucinations**, amnesia, insomnia, **nightmares**, worsening psychiatric illness; false-positive cannabinoid screen [1].
2. **Adherence support:** dementia predicts poor adherence and death [1].
3. **Cognitive rehabilitation:** "an essential treatment component" [2].
4. **Treat comorbidities** [1, 2]:
   - **depression**: SSRIs preferred (fluoxetine raises ritonavir levels); imipramine/desipramine; mirtazapine; **psychostimulants** (methylphenidate, dexamfetamine, modafinil) for apathy and fatigue; testosterone (fatigue) in depressed HIV-positive men; IPT;
   - substance use; physical comorbidity.
5. **Adjunctive agents:** psychostimulants, modafinil, memantine, lithium and valproate have been studied, but there is **no licensed treatment for HAND** [2].
6. **Psychosis or mania:**
   - **low-dose, slow-titration antipsychotics** (high **EPS sensitivity**); watch metabolic effects and interactions (ritonavir lowers olanzapine);
   - valproate (concern about HIV replication not confirmed); carbamazepine and lamotrigine interact with ART;
   - quetiapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole or ziprasidone for secondary mania (no trial evidence) [1, 2].
7. **Supportive care:** counselling for patient, partner and family; safe-sex and needle-use advice; capacity and future planning; prophylaxis of opportunistic infections; self-help groups [1].

**Conclusion.** HIV is a leading treatable cause of cognitive impairment in younger adults. The spectrum runs from asymptomatic neuropsychological impairment, through MCMD/MND, to **HIV-associated dementia**: a subcortical syndrome of slowing, memory and concentration loss, apathy (mimicking depression), motor signs and later akinetic mutism with preserved consciousness. ART has halved HAD incidence, but milder impairment remains common. Diagnosis is by exclusion: MRI and CSF to rule out opportunistic infections, lymphoma and neurosyphilis, plus neuropsychology. Management is optimised CNS-penetrant ART with adherence support, cognitive rehabilitation, treatment of depression and substance use, and cautious psychotropic use mindful of EPS sensitivity and ART interactions.
