## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 63-year-old retired man with long-standing **hypertension and diabetes**.
- **Months** of **forgetfulness and episodes of wandering**.
- **In the last month:** **several falls** and **urinary incontinence in bed twice**.
- **Bereavement:** his wife died of COVID-19 pneumonia last year.

### 1. Differential diagnosis
1. **Normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH)**: the most important **treatable** cause to exclude [1].
   - The **Hakim–Adams triad**:
     - **gait disturbance** (falls; magnetic, wide-based, short-stepped gait; difficult initiation; en bloc turns);
     - **urinary incontinence**;
     - **dementia** (frontal-subcortical).
   - "If an elderly patient has gait disturbance + incontinence + dementia + enlarged ventricles on CT, **think NPH first**."
   - NPH may follow prior **head injury, subarachnoid haemorrhage or meningitis** [2].
2. **Subcortical vascular dementia (small-vessel disease, Binswanger type)** [1, 2]:
   - hypertension and diabetes;
   - **gait disturbance, urinary symptoms and frontal-executive deficits**;
   - marche à petit pas, pseudobulbar palsy;
   - **mixed AD plus vascular** pathology is very common.
3. **Subdural haematoma:** with **falls**, even a slight head injury can lead to a subdural, especially in elderly or arteriosclerotic people, sometimes after a latent interval [1, 2].
4. **Depression / grief with cognitive impairment (pseudodementia)**, after his wife's death [1, 3]. Depression also often accompanies vascular dementia.
5. **Alzheimer's disease** with wandering (BPSD) [1].
6. **Other reversible causes:** drugs (hypoglycaemics, causing **hypoglycaemia**; antihypertensives, causing falls), **B12 deficiency**, hypothyroidism, infection (urinary infection with incontinence or delirium) [1].

### 2. Assessment [1, 2]
- **History** from his son:
  - onset and progression of each feature, and **which came first** (in NPH, gait usually precedes incontinence and dementia);
  - stepwise vs gradual course;
  - head injury with the falls;
  - **mood and grief**;
  - glycaemic control and hypoglycaemic episodes;
  - medications;
  - alcohol.
- **Examination:**
  - **gait** (magnetic vs parkinsonian; NPH has no tremor or rigidity at initiation);
  - focal signs;
  - postural BP;
  - pseudobulbar signs.
- **Cognition:** MMSE or MoCA, frontal-executive tests; **Hachinski Ischaemic Score** (7 or more suggests VaD; 4 or less suggests AD) [1].
- **Investigations:**
  - **CT or MRI brain**: **ventricles disproportionately enlarged** (Evans index over 0.3) with tight high-convexity sulci (**DESH**) in NPH; infarcts and white-matter hyperintensities in VaD; **subdural collection** [1];
  - **CSF tap test** (removing 30–50 mL; gait improvement within 24–72 hours) or extended lumbar drainage to predict shunt response [1];
  - bloods: glucose, HbA1c, electrolytes, renal function, TSH, B12, urine culture.

### 3. Management
**If NPH [1]:**
- **Ventriculoperitoneal shunt** is the definitive treatment.
- **Best outcomes:** short duration, gait-predominant symptoms, a **positive tap test**.
- **Expected improvement:** **gait (50–80%) > continence (50–60%) > cognition (25–40%)**.
- **Complications:** over-drainage (subdural), infection, shunt failure.

**If vascular dementia [1]:**
- **Control risk factors:** BP under 130/80, HbA1c, statin, smoking cessation; antiplatelet therapy.
- Cholinesterase inhibitors or memantine have modest evidence.

**If subdural haematoma:** neurosurgical referral [2].

**Depression or grief:** antidepressant if depressive episode; bereavement support [3].

**Safety:**
- **falls** prevention; **wandering** (supervision, identification);
- review hypoglycaemic and antihypertensive drugs;
- continence care.

**Caregiver support:** his son; assess caregiver burden (Zarit) [1].

**Conclusion.** Forgetfulness with recent falls and urinary incontinence in a vascular-risk patient should raise **NPH** (treatable with a shunt) alongside **subcortical vascular dementia** and **subdural haematoma**. Plan:
- neuroimaging and a tap test;
- correct reversible causes;
- control vascular risk;
- address grief and safety.

## In depth

### NPH vs subcortical vascular dementia vs AD [1]
| Feature | NPH | Subcortical VaD | AD |
|---|---|---|---|
| First symptom | Gait | Executive or gait | Memory |
| Incontinence | Early | Common | Late |
| Imaging | Ventriculomegaly, tight vertex sulci | White-matter hyperintensities, lacunes | Hippocampal or medial temporal atrophy |
| Treatment | VP shunt | Vascular risk control | ChEIs, memantine |
