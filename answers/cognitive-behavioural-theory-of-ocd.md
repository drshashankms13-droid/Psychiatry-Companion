## Exam answer

### Introduction

Cognitive-behavioural models explain OCD through the interaction of intrusive thoughts, maladaptive interpretations and behaviours that temporarily reduce distress but maintain the disorder.

### Cognitive model

Intrusive thoughts are common in the general population. In OCD, they are interpreted as unusually important, dangerous or morally significant. Key cognitive distortions include:
- Inflated responsibility.
- Overestimation of threat.
- Thought–action fusion.
- Intolerance of uncertainty.
- Perfectionism and need for control.
- Over-importance of thoughts and excessive attempts to suppress them.

These appraisals produce anxiety, guilt or disgust and motivate compulsions or mental rituals.

### Behavioural model

Classical conditioning may associate neutral stimuli with fear. Compulsions are maintained through negative reinforcement: performing a ritual reduces distress temporarily, increasing the likelihood of repeating it. Avoidance similarly prevents corrective learning.

### Integrated maintenance cycle

Intrusion → catastrophic interpretation → distress → compulsion or avoidance → temporary relief → reinforcement of the cycle.

### Application in treatment

**Cognitive Behaviour Therapy (CBT)** includes:
1. Psychoeducation about intrusive thoughts and the OCD cycle.
2. Identification of triggers and maladaptive appraisals.
3. Cognitive restructuring and behavioural experiments.
4. Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP).
5. Reduction of reassurance-seeking and family accommodation.
6. Relapse prevention and maintenance planning.

ERP involves planned, graded exposure to feared situations or thoughts while refraining from compulsions. Imaginal exposure may be used when feared consequences cannot be directly tested.

### Evidence and limitations

CBT with ERP is a first-line psychological treatment for OCD. It can be effective alone or combined with medication. Barriers include limited therapist availability, distress during exposure, poor adherence and cultural or family factors that reinforce rituals.

### Conclusion

Cognitive-behavioural theory explains how maladaptive appraisals and negatively reinforced rituals maintain OCD. CBT, particularly ERP, directly targets these mechanisms and is a central evidence-based treatment.
