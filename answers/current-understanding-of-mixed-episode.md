## Exam answer

**Definition.** A mixed state is the simultaneous presence, or very rapid alternation, of manic and depressive symptoms within a single episode. ICD-11 retains the **mixed episode**, defined in a way conceptually equivalent to ICD-10, with guidance on the typical **contrapolar symptoms** seen when manic or depressive symptoms predominate; a mixed episode indicates **bipolar type I** [1]. DSM-5 replaced the DSM-IV mixed episode with a **"with mixed features" specifier** applicable to manic, hypomanic and depressive episodes.

### Evolution of the concept

| Period | Concept |
| --- | --- |
| Kraepelin (1899), Weygandt | Mixed states as combinations of disturbances in mood, thought and activity — **six mixed states** (e.g. depressive mania, excited depression, manic stupor) |
| ICD-10 (1992) | Bipolar affective disorder, current episode mixed: manic and depressive symptoms mixed or rapidly alternating, both prominent for most of at least **2 weeks** |
| DSM-IV (1994) | Mixed episode: full criteria for **both** mania and major depression nearly every day for 1 week — too restrictive, rarely met |
| **DSM-5 (2013)** | **"With mixed features" specifier**: full criteria for the index episode + **at least 3 non-overlapping symptoms of the opposite pole**; applies also to unipolar depression |
| **ICD-11 (2022)** | **Mixed episode retained** (ICD-10 concept) with contrapolar symptom guidance; indicates bipolar I [1] |

### ICD-11 vs DSM-5

| Feature | ICD-11 mixed episode | DSM-5 mixed features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Status | An episode type | A specifier |
| Threshold | Several prominent manic and depressive symptoms, simultaneous or alternating rapidly (day to day or within a day) | Full index episode + ≥3 opposite-pole symptoms |
| Duration | At least 2 weeks (or less if hospitalisation) | Duration of the index episode |
| Overlapping symptoms | Considered | **Excluded** (irritability, distractibility, psychomotor agitation, insomnia) |
| Unipolar depression | Not applicable | MDD with mixed features allowed |
| Implication | **Bipolar type I** [1] | Mania with mixed features = bipolar I; depression with mixed features can be MDD or bipolar |

### Clinical features

- **Depression-predominant mixed state:** depressed mood with racing thoughts, pressured speech, increased energy, reduced need for sleep, risk-taking.
- **Mania-predominant mixed state:** elevated or irritable mood with dysphoria, hopelessness, guilt, suicidal ideation.
- **Agitation, irritability, anxiety, insomnia** are common but non-specific (excluded from the DSM-5 count).
- Often occur at the **transition between episodes**, after antidepressant use, with substance misuse, in rapid cycling, and in women and younger patients.

### Clinical significance

- **Suicide risk is highest** — despair combined with energy and impulsivity.
- More comorbidity (anxiety, substance use), more rapid cycling, more psychotic features, longer episodes, poorer treatment response and poorer outcome.
- **Antidepressants can induce or worsen mixed states**.

### Assessment

Ask actively about the opposite pole in every depressed or manic patient → longitudinal history (episode pattern, switching on antidepressants, family history of bipolar disorder) → rule out substances (stimulants, alcohol withdrawal), thyroid disease, akathisia, agitated depression, borderline personality disorder → rate with YMRS and MADRS together, or the Koukopoulos Mixed Depression Rating Scale.

### Management

1. **Stop antidepressants** [2].
2. **Safety:** suicide risk assessment; admission if severe.
3. **Antipsychotics with evidence in mixed states** [2]: **olanzapine**, **aripiprazole**, **risperidone** and **asenapine** (licensed for mixed episodes or states in the US); **cariprazine** improves both manic and depressive symptoms in mania with mixed features; **lurasidone** and **lumateperone** for depression with mixed features; quetiapine widely used.
4. **Mood stabilisers:** **valproate** often preferred (avoid in women of child-bearing potential); **lithium may be somewhat less effective in mixed states** [2]; combinations with an antipsychotic.
5. **ECT** for severe, psychotic, suicidal or treatment-resistant mixed states.
6. Short-term **benzodiazepine** for agitation and insomnia; sleep stabilisation.
7. **Maintenance:** continue the effective combination; psychoeducation, regular routines, avoid substances.

**Conclusion.** Mixed states are common, dangerous and frequently missed. Current understanding views them as a dimensional mixture of manic and depressive features rather than a rare overlap of two full syndromes — captured by DSM-5's mixed-features specifier and ICD-11's retained mixed episode. Recognition changes treatment: stop antidepressants, use antipsychotics or valproate-based combinations, consider ECT and manage suicide risk closely.

## In depth

### Background

- Kraepelin derived mixed states from his three-domain model (mood, thought, activity), each of which could be independently elevated or depressed.
- DSM-IV's strict definition made mixed episodes rare in research and practice; the DSM-5 specifier increased recognition, but by excluding overlapping symptoms it may miss the most typical features (agitation, irritability) — a major critique (Koukopoulos, Vieta).
- ICD-11 kept the broader ICD-10 concept because evidence supported its validity (Maj, 2012) [1].

### Epidemiology

- Mixed features occur in roughly 20–40% of bipolar episodes and in about a quarter or more of patients with major depression, depending on definitions (author's knowledge).
- Depression with mixed features predicts later conversion to bipolar disorder.

### Mechanism in detail

- Independent dysregulation of mood, energy/activity and cognition (Kraepelin; dimensional models).
- Neurobiology: combined monoaminergic and circadian disruption; hyperarousal; antidepressant-induced activation.
- Triggers: sleep loss, antidepressants, stimulants, alcohol withdrawal, steroids, postpartum period.

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing point |
| --- | --- |
| Agitated (anxious) depression | No elevated energy, pressured speech or reduced need for sleep |
| Akathisia | Motor restlessness after antipsychotic or SSRI start/increase; subjective urge to move |
| Borderline personality disorder | Mood shifts over hours, triggered by interpersonal events; chronic pattern |
| ADHD | Lifelong, not episodic |
| Substance intoxication/withdrawal | Temporal link; urine screen |
| Rapid cycling | Distinct episodes ≥4 per year rather than simultaneous symptoms |

### Management details [2]

- FDA-licensed antipsychotics for mixed episodes or states: aripiprazole, asenapine, olanzapine, risperidone.
- Cariprazine: post hoc pooled analysis of three trials in mania with mixed features showed benefit on manic and depressive symptoms; low weight gain.
- Lurasidone: RCT in MDD with mixed features (Suppes 2016).
- Lumateperone: efficacy in bipolar depression with mixed features.
- Iloperidone may be effective but data are insufficient.
- Valproate vs lithium in mania or mixed mania compared in an open trial (Bowden); lithium may be less effective in mixed states.
- In rapid cycling (often overlapping with mixed states): withdraw antidepressants, check thyroid and alcohol, optimise and combine mood stabilisers; quetiapine has the best supporting data.

### Evidence table

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Maj, 2012 (ICD-11 options paper) | Supported retaining the ICD-10 mixed episode [1] |
| McIntyre et al. (cariprazine, pooled 3 trials) | Effective in mania with mixed features [2] |
| Suppes et al., 2016 (lurasidone) | Effective in MDD with mixed features [2] |
| STEP-BD analyses | Antidepressants associated with worse outcomes in mixed and rapid-cycling patients [2] |

### Critical appraisal

- **DSM-5 specifier excludes overlapping symptoms**, so it may under-diagnose the agitated mixed depression seen clinically.
- **ICD-11 vs DSM-5 divergence** complicates research and comparison of prevalence.
- Few trials recruit mixed-state patients specifically; most evidence is post hoc.
- The relationship between mixed states, rapid cycling and borderline features remains unclear.

### Indian context

- Mixed presentations are frequently misdiagnosed as unipolar depression with "anxiety", leading to antidepressant-plus-benzodiazepine prescribing; screening for hypomanic symptoms is essential.
- Valproate use in young women is common in India despite teratogenicity warnings; olanzapine and quetiapine are affordable alternatives.
- ECT is accessible in many teaching hospitals for severe mixed states.

### Recent advances

- Dimensional assessment tools (Koukopoulos Mixed Depression Rating Scale, Clinical Assessment Rating Scale for mixed states).
- Newer agents with evidence in mixed features: cariprazine, lurasidone, lumateperone [2].
- Interest in the "mixed features" construct as a predictor of antidepressant non-response and bipolar conversion.
