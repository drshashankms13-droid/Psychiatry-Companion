## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 35-year-old school teacher has had episodic anxiety for years (palpitations, breathlessness, dry mouth, dizziness), with low mood for 3 months since her mother's death and occasional headaches. Family history: sister with **bipolar disorder and migraine who died by suicide**; father with alcohol dependence and diabetes. Her husband lives in another city and comes home at weekends. **Hb 10 g/dL** (mild anaemia) and **TSH 7 mIU/L** (subclinical, or mild overt, hypothyroidism). The task is to clarify the diagnosis among **panic disorder/GAD**, **depression vs normal grief vs early PGD**, **soft bipolarity**, and **medical contributors** (thyroid, anaemia), with high suicide-risk loading.

### (a) What further clarifications are needed in history?

| Domain | Clarifications | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anxiety episodes | Discrete, sudden, peaking within minutes (panic) vs persistent worry (GAD); cued vs uncued; frequency; anticipatory anxiety; avoidance (agoraphobia); fear of dying or losing control; nocturnal attacks | Panic disorder vs GAD vs other [2] |
| Timeline | Onset relative to life events (marriage, husband's job move); whether anxiety preceded the bereavement | Primary vs secondary |
| Mood | Pervasive low mood, anhedonia, sleep and appetite change, guilt, worthlessness, **suicidal ideation** (crucial given sister's suicide), psychotic features | MDD vs grief |
| Grief | Yearning, preoccupation with her mother, guilt, avoidance of reminders, function at school; cultural mourning practices | Normal grief vs PGD (too early by ICD-11 criteria at 3 months) [3] |
| **Past hypomania** | Periods of elevated or irritable mood, decreased need for sleep, overactivity, over-spending; brief episodes (days); antidepressant-induced activation; postpartum episodes; atypical depression (hypersomnia, hyperphagia); early-onset depression; multiple brief depressions; seasonal pattern | **Soft bipolarity/bipolar II** [4,5] |
| Treatment history | Previous antidepressants or benzodiazepines (and response or switch), alprazolam use | Bipolarity; dependence |
| Substances | Caffeine (teachers drink tea or coffee often), alcohol (father dependent), tobacco, benzodiazepines | Anxiety triggers, genetic risk |
| Headaches | Migraine features (aura, unilateral, photophobia) — sister had migraine; **migraine–bipolar comorbidity**; analgesic overuse | Comorbidity, treatment choice |
| Physical | Thyroid symptoms (weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, menorrhagia), anaemia causes (menorrhagia, diet, worms), cardiac symptoms, perimenopause unlikely at 35 | Medical causes of anxiety and low mood |
| Gynaecological | Menstrual pattern, contraception, pregnancy plans, premenstrual exacerbation | Drug choice (valproate avoidance); PMDD |
| Marital and social | Husband away (loneliness, marital stress), children, support, workload | Maintaining factors |
| Personality | Anxious, dependent; hyperthymic or cyclothymic temperament | Prognosis; spectrum [4] |
| Family | Sister's illness course and treatments (lithium response?); others with mood disorder; father's alcohol and its effect | Genetic risk (first-degree relative with bipolar disorder: about 7–10× risk) [6] |

**Investigations:** repeat TSH with free T4 and anti-TPO antibodies; CBC with indices, serum ferritin, B12 and folate; glucose and HbA1c (father diabetic); ECG (palpitations); electrolytes; lipid profile; urine pregnancy test before drugs. Screening instruments: GAD-7, PDSS, PHQ-9, **MDQ / HCL-32**, C-SSRS, PG-13-R if grief persists.

### (b) Differential diagnosis

| Possibility | Supporting | Against / to clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Panic disorder** (± agoraphobia) | Episodic palpitations, breathlessness, dizziness for years | Need discrete, unexpected attacks plus ≥1 month of concern [2] |
| **GAD** | Chronic anxiety, somatic symptoms | Need excessive worry over months [2] |
| **Depressive episode** (single, after bereavement) | Sadness for 3 months | Must distinguish from grief: pervasive anhedonia, worthlessness, global hopelessness [3] |
| **Normal grief / uncomplicated bereavement** | 3 months after mother's death | Usually improving, focused on the loss |
| **PGD** | — | Too early (ICD-11 ≥6 months; DSM ≥12 months) [3]; monitor |
| **Bipolar II / bipolar spectrum (soft bipolarity)** | Sister with bipolar disorder (C1 criterion of Ghaemi's spectrum); anxiety comorbidity common in bipolar disorder | Need history of hypomania or ≥2 soft signs (atypical, early, brief or recurrent depression, antidepressant switch, postpartum) [4,5] |
| **Mood or anxiety disorder due to hypothyroidism** | TSH 7 (low mood, fatigue, possibly anxiety) | Usually mild; check free T4 and antibodies |
| **Anaemia-related symptoms** | Hb 10 → palpitations, breathlessness, dizziness, fatigue | Only partly explains a long history |
| Migraine with comorbid anxiety/depression | Headaches, family migraine | — |
| Substance-related (caffeine, benzodiazepine withdrawal, alcohol) | — | History |
| Adjustment disorder with mixed anxiety and depressed mood | Stressor (mother's death, husband away) | Exclude if criteria for MDD or panic disorder are met |
| Cardiac arrhythmia, hyperthyroidism | — | ECG; TSH is high, not low |

### (c) Approach to management
1. **Safety:** assess suicide risk carefully (family suicide, bereavement, depression, female). Agree a safety plan, restrict means, give Tele-MANAS 14416.
2. **Medical correction:**
   - **Anaemia:** find the cause (menorrhagia, diet); oral iron; deworming; gynaecology referral if menorrhagia.
   - **TSH 7:** repeat in 6–8 weeks with free T4 and anti-TPO. Treat with levothyroxine if free T4 is low, antibodies are positive, she is symptomatic, or she plans pregnancy. Optimising thyroid status matters for mood, especially in bipolar disorder.
3. **Psychoeducation:** explain the panic cycle and normal grief; involve her husband (weekend sessions).
4. **Psychological (first line):**
   - CBT for panic or GAD (interoceptive and in-vivo exposure, cognitive restructuring) [2].
   - Grief-focused supportive therapy; monitor for PGD [3].
   - Behavioural activation for low mood.
   - Address loneliness and work stress; sleep hygiene; reduce caffeine.
5. **Pharmacotherapy — guided by the bipolarity screen:**
   - **No bipolar features:** SSRI (sertraline 25 → 50–100 mg) for panic, GAD and depression; short-term clonazepam only if severe [7].
   - **Bipolar spectrum suspected** (positive MDQ, soft signs) [4,5]: avoid antidepressant monotherapy. Consider lamotrigine (depression prevention; safe in women; no weight gain) or quetiapine (anxiety + depression). If an SSRI is used, combine with a mood stabiliser and monitor for activation [8].
   - **Migraine:** lifestyle and triggers; prophylaxis compatible with her mood state (propranolol helps anxiety and migraine, but caution in depression; amitriptyline is risky if bipolar; topiramate is an option; avoid valproate in women of childbearing age).
6. **Follow-up:** every 2–4 weeks initially, with mood charting and scales; reassess the diagnosis at 6 months (PGD, bipolarity).
7. **Genetic counselling** about family history; advise family about early warning signs [6].

**Conclusion.** The most likely picture is a **long-standing anxiety disorder (panic disorder or GAD)** with a **recent depressive episode or complicated bereavement**, aggravated by anaemia and possible hypothyroidism. A **bipolar diathesis** must be actively excluded because of her sister's illness and suicide. Treat the medical factors, offer CBT and grief support, and choose medication cautiously: avoid unopposed antidepressants if soft bipolarity is present.

## In depth

### Background
This case tests the integration of several concepts:
- **Kraepelin's** broad manic-depressive spectrum [5].
- **Akiskal's soft bipolarity**, including bipolar II½ (depression with cyclothymic temperament) and anxious-sensitive cyclothymia [4].
- **Grief vs depression:** DSM-5 removed the bereavement exclusion; ICD-11 and DSM-5-TR introduced PGD [3].
- **Medical mimics:** thyroid and anaemia [7].

### Epidemiology
- Anxiety disorders are about twice as common in women; panic disorder lifetime 2–5% [2].
- Comorbid anxiety occurs in over 50% of people with bipolar disorder; panic disorder raises suicide risk in bipolar disorder.
- Subclinical hypothyroidism affects about 4–10% of adult women (higher in India, ~9–11%). Iron-deficiency anaemia affects about 50% of Indian women (NFHS-5: 57% of women aged 15–49).
- PGD affects about 3–10% of bereaved people; risk is higher with closeness to the deceased [3].
- Up to half of clinic depressions may be on the bipolar spectrum (broad criteria) [4].

### Classification and criteria
- **Panic disorder:** ICD-11 6B01; **GAD:** 6B00 [2].
- **Depressive episode:** 6A70. ICD-11 notes that **grief alone** is not a depressive episode unless criteria are met, with careful judgement during bereavement.
- **PGD:** 6B42 (≥6 months) [3].
- **Bipolar II:** 6A61. **Cyclothymia:** 6A62.
- **Secondary mood syndrome due to hypothyroidism:** 6E62.
- **Ghaemi's bipolar spectrum criteria:** see the antidepressants answer [4,5].

### Mechanism in detail
- **Thyroid and mood:** hypothyroidism reduces central serotonergic and noradrenergic function, causing fatigue, depression and cognitive slowing. T3 augments antidepressants; hypothyroidism is associated with rapid cycling.
- **Anaemia:** reduced oxygen delivery causes compensatory tachycardia and dyspnoea, which can mimic or trigger panic; iron deficiency is linked to fatigue, depression and restless legs.
- **Grief:** reward-pathway yearning and attachment disruption [3].
- **Bipolar diathesis:** high heritability; antidepressant exposure may unmask hypomania [4,6].

### Clinical features in detail — clues to bipolarity in an anxious, depressed woman
Early onset; recurrent brief depressions; atypical features; postpartum depression; mood lability ("ups and downs without cause"); hyperthymic or cyclothymic temperament; antidepressant activation, insomnia or irritability; family history (the strongest clue here); migraine comorbidity [4].

### Differential diagnosis (additional)
- **PMDD:** cyclical anxiety and irritability in the luteal phase.
- **Phaeochromocytoma / paroxysmal arrhythmia:** if attacks are with severe hypertension or documented arrhythmia.
- **Health anxiety:** if the focus is fear of illness.
- **Borderline personality disorder:** if there is chronic emptiness, unstable relationships, self-harm.

### Management details
- **SSRIs in possible bipolar spectrum:** prefer sertraline or escitalopram at low dose; combine with a mood stabiliser if bipolar II is confirmed; stop at any sign of hypomania; avoid TCAs and venlafaxine [8].
- **Lamotrigine:** 25 mg for 2 weeks → 50 mg for 2 weeks → 100 mg → 200 mg; watch for rash. Combined oral contraceptives lower its levels.
- **Quetiapine:** 50–300 mg; helps anxiety, sleep and depression; metabolic monitoring.
- **Lithium:** consider if bipolar II is confirmed with high suicide risk, given the family history. Lithium is anti-suicidal [9].
- **Benzodiazepines:** short term only; she has a family history of addiction (father) [7].
- **Thyroid:** levothyroxine 25–50 µg if indicated; recheck TSH in 6–8 weeks.
- **Iron:** ferrous sulphate 100–200 mg elemental iron daily, or alternate-day dosing; IV iron if intolerant.
- **Psychotherapy:** CBT for panic [2]; IPT (grief, role transitions); grief-focused CBT if PGD emerges at 6 months or later [3]; couple sessions.
- **Work:** teacher stress; consider brief leave; school counsellor support.

### Critical appraisal
- **Risk of over-diagnosing bipolarity:** family history alone is not diagnostic. Over-diagnosis exposes her to mood stabilisers unnecessarily [4,5].
- **Risk of under-diagnosing:** missing bipolarity and giving SSRI monotherapy may cause switch, mixed states and suicide [4].
- **TSH 7:** whether to treat subclinical hypothyroidism for mood is debated. Treat if TPO antibodies are positive, she is symptomatic, or pregnancy is planned.
- **Grief:** avoid pathologising grief at 3 months, but do not miss MDD or suicide risk [3].

### Indian context
- Anaemia and thyroid dysfunction are common in Indian women and often missed in psychiatric settings.
- Teachers experience high workload; husbands working in other cities (migration for work) is common, leading to loneliness and role overload.
- Hindu, Muslim and Christian mourning rituals provide support; check whether these were possible [3].
- Antidepressants are often prescribed by GPs without a bipolarity screen [4].
- Tele-MANAS; IPS CPGs on depression, anxiety and bipolar disorder.

### Recent advances
- **Emerging:** digital mood tracking to detect hypomania; MDQ validated in Indian languages.
- **Investigational:** polygenic risk for bipolar disorder in relatives (not clinically useful yet) [6]; naltrexone for PGD [3].
