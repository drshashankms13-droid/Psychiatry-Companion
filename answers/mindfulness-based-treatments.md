## Exam answer

### Definition

Mindfulness refers to purposeful, non-judgemental awareness of present-moment experience. Mindfulness-based interventions cultivate attention, acceptance and a less reactive relationship to thoughts, emotions and bodily sensations.

### Major interventions

-   **Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR):** structured programme
    involving meditation, body scan and mindful movement.
-   **Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT):** combines mindfulness
    practices with cognitive therapy, particularly for prevention of
    depressive relapse.
-   **Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention (MBRP):** applies mindfulness
    to substance-use relapse prevention.
-   **DBT mindfulness skills:** support emotion regulation and distress
    tolerance.
-   **Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT):** uses acceptance,
    present-moment awareness and values-based action.

### Proposed mechanisms

-   Improved attentional regulation.
-   Decentring from thoughts.
-   Reduced rumination and reactivity.
-   Greater emotional regulation and acceptance.
-   Improved awareness of triggers and urges.
-   Possible changes in functional connectivity and stress-related
    neurobiology; these remain research findings rather than routine
    biomarkers.

### Clinical applications

-   **Recurrent depression:** MBCT can reduce relapse risk in selected
    patients.
-   **Anxiety disorders:** may reduce worry and reactivity as part of
    treatment.
-   **Substance use:** MBRP may support coping with craving and relapse
    triggers.
-   **Bipolar disorder:** may be used as an adjunct in stable patients,
    not as a substitute for mood stabilisers.
-   **Chronic pain:** mindfulness can help coping and distress.
-   **Borderline personality disorder:** mindfulness skills are part of
    DBT.
-   **Psychosis:** requires caution and adaptation, as intensive
    practice may worsen distress in some individuals.

### Evidence and limitations

Evidence is strongest for MBCT in recurrent depression and for mindfulness as part of structured interventions. Effects vary by population and comparator. Mindfulness is not universally benign; some individuals experience increased anxiety, dissociation or distress. Instructor competence and appropriate patient selection matter.

### Conclusion

Mindfulness-based treatments are useful adjunctive approaches for selected psychiatric conditions, particularly recurrent depression, anxiety and substance-use problems. They should be delivered in structured, evidence-informed formats and integrated with standard care.
