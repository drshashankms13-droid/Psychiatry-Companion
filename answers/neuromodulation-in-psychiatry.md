## Exam answer

**Definition.** Neuromodulation means changing neural activity by delivering electrical, magnetic or other energy to specific brain targets or networks. Unlike drugs, it acts on a defined circuit. In psychiatry the techniques fall into three groups:
- **Convulsive:** ECT, magnetic seizure therapy (MST).
- **Non-invasive, non-convulsive:** rTMS/theta burst stimulation, deep TMS, tDCS, cranial electrotherapy, trigeminal nerve stimulation.
- **Invasive/surgical:** vagus nerve stimulation (VNS), deep brain stimulation (DBS), ablative surgery (anterior capsulotomy, cingulotomy) [1,2].

The statement "current neuromodulation approaches hold much promise" is **true for ECT and rTMS in depression, partly true for OCD and schizophrenia, and not yet true for most invasive methods**. Promise must be weighed against effect sizes, durability, blinding problems and access.

### Mechanism
Energy delivery → depolarisation (TMS, ECT) or a shift in resting membrane potential (tDCS) → synaptic plasticity (LTP/LTD-like), neurotrophic change (BDNF), neurotransmitter release → **normalisation of dysfunctional networks**:
- Depression: hypoactive left DLPFC, hyperactive subgenual cingulate.
- OCD: cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) hyperactivity.
- Hallucinations: hyperactive left temporoparietal cortex.

ECT adds a generalised seizure → hippocampal neurogenesis, increased GABA, raised seizure threshold (anticonvulsant hypothesis) and HPA normalisation [1,2].

### Techniques compared

| Technique | How | Main indication / evidence | Key limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ECT** | Brief-pulse current under GA, induces 20–25 s seizure; RUL ultrabrief or bitemporal; 2–3/week, 6–12 sessions | TRD, psychotic depression, catatonia, suicidality, mania, NMS; response 60–80%, remission ~50% (UK ECT Review Group 2003) | Anaesthesia; retrograde amnesia; relapse ~50% at 6 months without continuation; stigma |
| **rTMS / iTBS** | Coil over scalp; 10 Hz left DLPFC or 1 Hz right; iTBS 3 min | Unipolar depression (strong), TRD, peripartum, post-stroke; OCD, PTSD, GAD, negative symptoms (moderate) — IPS CPG 2023 | Daily visits 4–6 weeks; response 30–50%; large sham effect |
| **Deep TMS** | H-coil | MDD (H1), OCD (H7), smoking (H4) | Less focal; more headache |
| **tDCS** | 1–2 mA anodal over left DLPFC, 20–30 min | Depression (modest), negative symptoms, hallucinations | Small, inconsistent effects; inferior to escitalopram (ELECT-TDCS) |
| **MST** | Magnetic seizure, more focal | Depression; ECT-like efficacy with fewer cognitive effects in early trials | Investigational |
| **VNS** | Implanted pulse generator on left vagus | Chronic TRD (FDA 2005); slow, cumulative response over 1–2 years | Surgery, cost; hoarseness; weak RCT evidence |
| **DBS** | Electrodes in sgACC (Cg25), ventral capsule/striatum, MFB, NAcc | Refractory OCD (FDA humanitarian exemption 2009), TRD | Two large TRD RCTs failed; surgical risk |
| **Ablative surgery** | Gamma-knife capsulotomy, cingulotomy | Highly refractory OCD/depression | Irreversible; ethical oversight |

### Assessment before neuromodulation
- Confirm diagnosis and **true** treatment resistance (adequate dose, duration, adherence).
- Severity scales (HAM-D/MADRS, Y-BOCS, PANSS).
- Medical work-up: seizure history, metal implants (TMS), cardiac and anaesthetic fitness (ECT), baseline cognition (MoCA/MMSE).
- Capacity and informed consent.

### Management — how to place them (for depression)
Two adequate antidepressant trials fail → optimise or augment (lithium, aripiprazole, T3) **+ psychotherapy** → **rTMS/iTBS** if non-psychotic, not acutely suicidal, outpatient → **ECT** (first-line if psychotic, catatonic, acutely suicidal, not eating, or prior ECT response) → **ketamine/esketamine** for rapid response → continuation/maintenance (pharmacotherapy ± maintenance ECT/rTMS) → **VNS/DBS** only in specialist centres for chronic, multiply-resistant illness [1–3].

### Key evidence
- **UK ECT Review Group 2003, Lancet:** real ECT > sham (effect size about 0.9); bilateral > unilateral.
- **Mutz 2019 network meta-analysis:** rTMS SMD 0.83 vs sham; bitemporal ECT had higher response than HF rTMS [1].
- **THREE-D 2018:** iTBS non-inferior to 10 Hz rTMS.
- **ELECT-TDCS 2017** (n=245): tDCS > placebo but inferior to escitalopram.
- **BROADEN (sgACC) and RECLAIM (VC/VS) DBS trials:** stopped for futility. Long-term open follow-up shows sustained response in some patients.
- **SAINT 2022:** 78.6% remission with accelerated fMRI-guided iTBS in a small RCT.

**Conclusion.** The promise is real but uneven. **ECT** remains the most effective acute antidepressant treatment. **rTMS** has moved from experimental to established second-line care for depression. tDCS, VNS and DBS remain adjuncts or research tools. The best use of neuromodulation now is targeted, personalised and combined with drugs and psychotherapy, not as a replacement for them. Wider claims should wait for larger, well-blinded and longer trials.

## In depth

### Background
- **1938:** Cerletti and Bini introduced ECT in Rome, after Meduna's chemical convulsive therapy (1934). It was refined by anaesthesia and muscle relaxants (1950s), brief-pulse stimulation (1970s–80s), and ultrabrief pulse right-unilateral ECT (2000s).
- **1985:** Barker's TMS. **1990s:** antidepressant rTMS. **2008:** FDA clearance [1].
- **VNS:** developed for epilepsy (1997 approval); mood improvement noted; FDA approval for chronic TRD in 2005.
- **DBS:** from movement disorders. Mayberg (2005) reported sgACC (area 25) DBS in 6 TRD patients; Nuttin (1999) described capsular stimulation in OCD.
- **tDCS:** galvanic stimulation of the 19th century, revived by Nitsche and Paulus (2000).
- **Psychosurgery:** the discredited prefrontal leucotomy era (Moniz, Freeman) left a legacy of caution that still shapes ethics review for DBS and ablation.

### Epidemiology of use
- About 30% of patients with depression do not respond to two antidepressant trials (STAR*D: cumulative remission about 67% after four steps). This is the population for which neuromodulation is considered.
- The IPS guideline notes that 20–60% of patients across psychiatric disorders do not respond to standard treatment, which drives interest in rTMS [1].
- ECT use in India is higher than in the West, often in acute psychosis and catatonia. Unmodified ECT was banned by the MHCA 2017.

### Classification and criteria
- **By invasiveness:** non-invasive (TMS, tDCS, tACS, transcutaneous VNS, focused ultrasound) vs invasive (DBS, implanted VNS, epidural cortical stimulation).
- **By seizure:** convulsive (ECT, MST) vs non-convulsive.
- **By target strategy:** scalp-based (5 cm rule, Beam F3), neuronavigated, connectivity-guided, closed-loop/responsive.
- Regulatory status differs. The FDA "clears" devices (510(k)) rather than approving them like drugs. Clearance for rTMS (MDD, OCD, smoking, anxious depression) differs from evidence grades in guidelines.

### Mechanism in detail

| Technique | Proposed mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| ECT | Generalised seizure → increased GABA and seizure threshold (anticonvulsant theory); hippocampal neurogenesis and BDNF; HPA normalisation; increased functional connectivity changes in DMN; dopaminergic sensitisation (relevant to catatonia, Parkinson's) |
| rTMS | Frequency-dependent LTP/LTD; network propagation from DLPFC to sgACC; dopamine release in caudate; BDNF |
| tDCS | Subthreshold: anodal depolarises (raises excitability), cathodal hyperpolarises; after-effects NMDA-dependent |
| VNS | Afferent vagal fibres → nucleus tractus solitarius → locus coeruleus (noradrenaline) and dorsal raphe (serotonin) → limbic regions |
| DBS | High-frequency stimulation → functional inhibition of the local node and modulation of white-matter tracts (sgACC, VC/VS, MFB) |

### Clinical features in detail — indications beyond depression
- **OCD:** rTMS (LF right DLPFC moderate; LF SMA, HF bilateral DLPFC low); deep TMS H7 coil FDA-cleared; DBS of VC/VS or STN for severe refractory OCD, with about 50–60% response in open series [1].
- **Schizophrenia:** LF rTMS over left temporoparietal cortex for persistent auditory hallucinations (low grade); HF left DLPFC for negative symptoms (moderate); ECT augmentation of clozapine in clozapine-resistant illness; tDCS (fronto-temporal montage) for hallucinations is promising [1,2].
- **Catatonia:** benzodiazepines then ECT; ECT is still the treatment of choice when lorazepam fails [2].
- **Substance use:** HF left DLPFC rTMS reduces craving and smoking (low grade) [1].
- **Dementia:** HF DLPFC rTMS improves global cognition and BPSD in Alzheimer's (moderate) but not in MCI [1].
- **Pain, tinnitus, migraine, insomnia:** motor cortex rTMS for fibromyalgia and migraine; LF right DLPFC for insomnia [1].

### Assessment in detail
- **Treatment resistance staging:** Thase–Rush staging, Maudsley Staging Method (MSM), Antidepressant Treatment History Form (ATHF).
- **Cognition:** for ECT, baseline and serial MoCA plus an autobiographical memory measure (AMI-SF).
- **Pre-ECT:** anaesthetic assessment, ECG, electrolytes, review of drugs that alter seizure threshold (benzodiazepines, anticonvulsants, lithium — neurotoxicity risk). Fundoscopy or CT only if raised ICP is suspected.
- **Pre-TMS:** TASS/Rossi questionnaire, motor threshold [1].
- **Pre-DBS/VNS:** neurosurgical and ethics committee review; MRI; neuropsychological testing.

### Differential diagnosis of non-response to neuromodulation

| Cause | Clue | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pseudo-resistance | Poor adherence, sub-therapeutic doses | Optimise first |
| Wrong diagnosis | Bipolar depression, personality disorder, organic cause | Re-evaluate |
| Inadequate dose | Low pulses or intensity, too few sessions, short ECT seizures | Adjust parameters |
| Drug interference | Benzodiazepines, anticonvulsants raise seizure threshold and MT | Reduce before treatment |
| Ongoing stressors | Psychosocial maintaining factors | Add psychotherapy and social input |

### Management details
- **ECT in special groups:** safe in pregnancy (with obstetric monitoring), elderly (consider RUL ultrabrief), adolescents (MHCA 2017 restricts ECT in minors to consent of guardian plus Mental Health Review Board permission).
- **rTMS in pregnancy:** minimal fetal exposure; a good option for antenatal depression [1].
- **Drug–ECT interactions:** lithium (risk of delirium; consider holding), benzodiazepines (raise threshold; use flumazenil if needed), clozapine (ECT augmentation effective; monitor for tachycardia) [2].
- **Continuation:** after successful ECT, continuation pharmacotherapy (lithium + nortriptyline in Sackeim 2001) or continuation ECT (CORE trial: comparable to drugs) is mandatory because relapse is high.
- **rTMS maintenance:** tapered sessions for responders with relapse history; evidence still limited [1].

### Critical appraisal
**Arguments supporting the statement**
- Circuit-based, mechanism-informed treatment that bypasses drug side effects (weight gain, sexual dysfunction).
- rTMS is well tolerated (serious adverse events equal to sham) and safe in pregnancy and the elderly [1].
- Rapid-acting protocols (SAINT) and ECT can save lives in severe depression.
- Biomarkers (connectivity, EEG) promise personalised targeting.

**Arguments against, or for caution**
- **Effect sizes outside depression are modest** and come from small, heterogeneous trials. Many IPS recommendations are graded "low" [1].
- **Blinding:** sham TMS and sham tDCS are imperfect; placebo response in rTMS trials is large (g ≈ 0.8) [1].
- **Durability:** about half of rTMS responders relapse by 12 months; ECT relapse is about 50% at 6 months without continuation.
- **Invasive methods failed pivotal RCTs** (BROADEN, RECLAIM). Open-label successes may reflect selection, expectation and programming adjustments.
- **Cognitive side effects of ECT** and stigma limit acceptance. Ultrabrief RUL reduces but does not abolish them.
- **Access and cost:** daily rTMS sessions, anaesthesia for ECT, and neurosurgery for DBS are concentrated in urban tertiary centres.
- **Publication bias and industry funding** in device trials.

**Verdict:** "Much promise" is justified for depression and reasonable for OCD. For other disorders it is premature. The field's priority is replication with active sham, longer follow-up and cost-effectiveness data from low- and middle-income settings.

**Applying this to the statement "newer modes of brain stimulation are more effective and convenient than ECT"**:
- **Effectiveness: false, as a general claim.**
  - Head-to-head meta-analysis (Ren 2014; 9 RCTs, n=425): **ECT response 64% vs high-frequency rTMS 49%** (RR 1.41).
  - ECT remains the most effective treatment for severe, psychotic, catatonic or suicidal depression (response 60–90%; older age and psychotic or catatonic features predict response).
  - tDCS was inferior to escitalopram (ELECT-TDCS).
  - DBS failed pivotal RCTs [11].
- **Convenience: partly true.** rTMS and tDCS need no anaesthesia, have no cognitive adverse effects, and allow outpatient or home delivery. But rTMS needs daily visits for 4–6 weeks, and response is slower [11].
- **Cognition is the real trade-off.** ECT's cognitive effects increase with bilateral placement, sine-wave stimulation, high dose and frequent sessions. They are minimised with ultrabrief right unilateral ECT, at the cost of a longer time to remission (Sackeim 2008; Galletly 2014) [11].
- **Continuation:** after ECT, relapse prevention by continuation ECT is comparable to lithium + nortriptyline (CORE). In PRIDE, ECT plus medication beat medication alone in elderly people [11].
- **Verdict:** newer modes are **more convenient and cognitively safer**, but they are **less effective** than ECT for severe depression. They complement ECT; they do not replace it. Previous ECT response predicts rTMS response, and ECT non-response predicts poor rTMS response [11].

### Indian context
- **MHCA 2017, Section 95:** prohibits ECT without muscle relaxant and anaesthesia (unmodified ECT). ECT in minors needs guardian consent and Mental Health Review Board permission. Advance directives may refuse ECT.
- IPS clinical practice guidelines exist for rTMS (2023) and ECT, and NIMHANS has a large neuromodulation programme (rTMS, tDCS, ECT research) [1].
- India has a large published trial base: a meta-analysis of 52 Indian studies showed moderate–large rTMS effects [1].
- Barriers: cost of devices, uneven training, rural access. Home-based tDCS and teleconsultation could widen reach, but evidence is still limited.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** iTBS; deep TMS for OCD; ultrabrief RUL ECT.
- **Emerging:** accelerated, connectivity-guided iTBS (SAINT/SNT); EEG-synchronised and closed-loop TMS; ketamine anaesthesia for ECT.
- **Investigational:** transcranial focused ultrasound (deep targets non-invasively); adaptive (closed-loop) DBS using local field potential biomarkers; transcutaneous auricular VNS; temporal interference stimulation; MST.
- **Combination:** pharmacological enhancement of plasticity (D-cycloserine with rTMS); psychotherapy paired with stimulation (e.g., exposure with deep TMS in OCD).
