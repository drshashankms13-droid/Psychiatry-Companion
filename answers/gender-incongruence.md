## Exam answer

### Definition and classification

ICD-11 defines gender incongruence as a marked and persistent incongruence between an individual's experienced gender and assigned sex. It is classified under conditions related to sexual health, not mental disorders. DSM-5-TR uses the diagnosis of gender dysphoria when clinically significant distress or impairment is present.

### Clinical presentations

Gender incongruence may be expressed through a desire to live and be accepted as a gender different from assigned sex, a desire to alter sex characteristics, or other experiences of gender. The person's goals and desired interventions vary widely.

### Aetiology

Gender identity is multifactorial, involving complex developmental and biological influences. There is no single established cause.

### Assessment

Explore gender history, persistence, personal meaning, desired social or medical changes, mental health, capacity, support, safety and expectations. Use respectful names and pronouns.

### Differential diagnosis

Distinguish gender incongruence from gender nonconformity without distress or incongruence, body dysmorphic disorder, psychotic disorders and other conditions where indicated. Sexual orientation is distinct from gender identity.

### Comorbidities

Depression, anxiety, trauma-related disorders, substance use and suicidality may occur, often influenced by stigma, discrimination and minority stress. They are not intrinsic to gender incongruence.

### Management

Offer affirming psychosocial support and discuss social, hormonal and surgical options according to the person's goals and informed consent. Treat comorbid conditions and address family and social support. Avoid conversion practices and unnecessary gatekeeping.

### Conclusion

Gender incongruence is not a mental disorder in ICD-11. Assessment should be respectful and individualised, with care directed toward the person's needs, wellbeing and autonomy.
