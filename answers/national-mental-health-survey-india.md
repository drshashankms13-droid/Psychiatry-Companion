## Exam answer

**Introduction.** The **National Mental Health Survey (NMHS) of India 2015–16** was:
- a large, multicentre, nationally representative study;
- conducted by **NIMHANS** for the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare;
- carried out in **12 states** (two per region) during **2014–16**;
- among adults **18 years and above**, with a pilot adolescent (13–17) sample.

It measured the **prevalence, treatment gap, service use, disability and socio-economic impact** of mental disorders, and assessed **state mental health systems** [1].

### Objectives [1]
1. Estimate the prevalence and burden of mental disorders in a representative population.
2. Identify the treatment gap, care-seeking and service use, disability and impact.
3. Assess mental health facilities, resources and systems (**State Mental Health Systems Assessment, SMHSA**).

### Methodology [1]
- **Pilot:** Kolar district, Karnataka (2014; n=3,190) tested sampling, MINI and MINI-KID, and handheld tablets.
- **States:**
  - North: Punjab, Uttar Pradesh.
  - South: Tamil Nadu, Kerala.
  - East: Jharkhand, West Bengal.
  - West: Rajasthan, Gujarat.
  - Central: Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh.
  - North-east: Assam, Manipur.
- **Sampling:** multi-stage, stratified, random cluster sampling with **probability proportionate to size (PPS)**: state → districts (stratified by poverty ratio) → 2 talukas → clusters (villages or urban wards) → systematic household selection → all eligible members.
- **Sample:** **34,802 adults** and **1,191 adolescents**, covering states with about 60% of India's population.
- **Tools:**
  - **MINI 6.0** (ICD-10/DSM-IV); MINI-KID.
  - Fagerström (tobacco).
  - Screeners for epilepsy (WHO), intellectual disability and autism.
  - Sheehan Disability Scale; socio-economic impact; health-care utilisation.
  - Qualitative components.
- **Data collection:** door-to-door with **handheld tablets**; three-tier quality monitoring; ethics approval [1].

### Key findings

| Indicator | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| **Any mental morbidity (excluding tobacco)** | **Lifetime 13.7%; current 10.6%** [1, 2] |
| People needing care | About **150 million** Indians (projected) [5] |
| Substance use disorders (incl. tobacco) | **22.4%** (tobacco 20.9%, alcohol 4.6%, other drugs 0.5–0.6%) [1] |
| Schizophrenia and other psychoses | Lifetime **1.4%** (current about 0.4%) [1] |
| Mood disorders | Lifetime **5.6%**. Depressive disorder lifetime **5.1%**, current **2.7%** [1] |
| Neurotic and stress-related disorders | Lifetime **3.7%**, current **3.5%**. Agoraphobia 1.6%, GAD 0.6%, panic 0.5%, social phobia 0.5%, OCD 0.3%, PTSD 0.2% [1] |
| Suicide risk (current) | About **0.9% high risk**; higher in women, urban metros and ages 40–49 [5] |
| Adolescents (13–17) | About **7.3%** current prevalence [5]; anxiety and mood disorders commonest; ASD screen positive 1.6% [1] |
| Common vs severe mental disorders | CMDs about **6×** more common (lifetime) and **12×** (current) [1] |
| Comorbidity | 12.3% had two diagnoses; 4.3% had three or more. Depression + alcohol use disorder the commonest pair [1] |
| Urban metros vs rural | Higher prevalence in urban metros (about 13.5% vs 6.9%) [5] |
| Socio-economic gradient | Higher in **lower income, less educated, unemployed**; age **30–49** most affected; roughly equal by sex overall [1] |

### Treatment gap [1]

| Disorder | Treatment gap |
| --- | --- |
| Any mental health problem | **84.5%** |
| Common mental disorders | 85.0% |
| Major depressive disorder | 85.2% |
| Neurotic disorders | 84.0% |
| Severe mental disorders | 73.6% |
| Schizophrenia and other psychoses | 75.5% |
| Bipolar affective disorder | 70.4% |
| **Substance use disorders** | **91.1%** (tobacco 91.8%; alcohol 86.3%; other substances 72.9%) |
| Epilepsy (GTCS) | 31.3% |
| Intellectual disability | 70.5% |

### Service use, disability and cost [1]
- **Delay** from symptom onset to care: median 2.5 months (depression) to 12 months (epilepsy).
- **Government doctors** were the most recent provider for about two-thirds of people with psychosis and alcohol use disorder.
- **Distance** travelled: about 30 km for psychosis and bipolar disorder.
- **Disability:** at least half of those with epilepsy, depression or bipolar disorder reported disability. **Extreme disability** was highest in schizophrenia (20.5–28.2%) and bipolar disorder (11.8–17.6%).
- **Cost:** median monthly treatment cost ₹1,000–2,250. Families lost workdays.
- **Systems (SMHSA):** mental health programmes fragmented and low-priority; DMHP coverage uneven (Kerala full, Punjab under 15%); **under 1% of health budget**; few specialists (psychiatrists 0.2–1.2 per lakh); drug supply irregular [1].

**Conclusion.** NMHS 2015–16 was India's first scientifically robust, nationally representative mental health survey. It showed that about **1 in 10 adults** currently has a mental disorder, that **substance use** (especially tobacco and alcohol) and **common mental disorders** dominate the burden, and that the **treatment gap is 70–92%**. Its findings shaped policy: DMHP expansion, mental health in Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, Tele-MANAS, and the National Suicide Prevention Strategy. Its limitations (12 states only, exclusion of children under 13 and megacities, cross-sectional design) argue for a repeat survey with wider coverage.

## In depth

### Background
**Earlier Indian surveys** (Dube 1970; Nandi; Sethi) were small and regional, used varied methods, and gave prevalence estimates from about 10 to 370 per 1,000.
- **Reddy & Chandrashekar 1998** meta-analysis: 58.2 per 1,000.
- **Ganguli 2000:** 73 per 1,000.

The **WHO World Mental Health Survey (2001–03)** India site (Pondicherry) was not nationally representative.

The **NMHS** was commissioned in 2014 to provide national data, led by **Gururaj and colleagues at NIMHANS** with partner institutions in each state [1].

### Epidemiology: further details
- **Age:** 18–29 years made up the largest share of the sample (34%). People 60+ were slightly over-represented (16% vs 13.1% in Census 2011) [1].
- **Sex:** women were 52.3% of respondents [1].
- **Residence:** rural respondents were 68.8%, close to the national urban-rural split [1].
- **State variation** [1]:
  - Substance use disorders highest in Rajasthan (38.9%), Madhya Pradesh (36.6%), Chhattisgarh (32.4%) and Assam (27.3%).
  - Alcohol use disorder highest in Madhya Pradesh (10.3%).
  - Psychosis highest in West Bengal (1.26%).
  - Mood disorders highest in Jharkhand, West Bengal and Tamil Nadu (about 4.6–4.8%).
  - Neurotic disorders highest in Jharkhand (6.3%).
- **Suicide (NCRB 2014):** national rate 10.6 per lakh; highest in Tamil Nadu (23.4), Kerala and Gujarat (22.4); lowest in Uttar Pradesh (1.7) and Manipur (2.0) [1].

### Classification and criteria
- Diagnoses by **ICD-10 (F10–F48)** through MINI 6.0, with **tobacco use disorder** reported separately because its high prevalence would dominate totals.
- **Severe mental disorders:** psychoses and bipolar disorder (and severe depression in some analyses).
- **Common mental disorders:** depression and anxiety/neurotic disorders.

### Mechanism in detail: why the gap is so large
- **Demand side:** low awareness; stigma; somatic idioms of distress; faith healing; cost and distance.
- **Supply side:** specialist shortage (psychiatrists 0.2–1.2 per lakh; clinical psychologists 0.03–0.6; PSWs 0.03–0.4; psychiatric nurses 0.05–2.0) [3]; uneven DMHP; irregular drugs; poor integration with primary care [1].

### Clinical features in detail: comorbidity patterns
- Among people with dual diagnoses, depression appeared in 4 of the top 5 combinations [1]:
  - depression + alcohol use disorder (17.6%);
  - depression + agoraphobia (13.3%);
  - depression + panic (6.5%);
  - depression + OCD (4.9%).
- Alcohol use disorder + other substance use disorders accounted for 13.0% of dual diagnoses [1].

### Assessment in detail: strengths of the methodology
- Master protocol plus operational guidelines (the "what" and the "how") [1].
- Standardised, translated instruments; tablet-based data capture with minimal data loss.
- Field, state and central monitoring (spot checks, re-interviews).
- Ethical principles: minimise harm; informed consent; anonymity and confidentiality; no deception; right to withdraw.
- **Mixed methods:** quantitative prevalence plus qualitative work on stigma, homelessness and care-seeking [1].

### Differential: NMHS vs other data sources
| Source | Strength | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NMHS 2015–16 | National design, standard tools, treatment gap | 12 states; no under-13s; no megacities |
| GBD India State-Level Disease Burden (Lancet Psychiatry 2020) | Modelled burden for all states | Modelling assumptions |
| NFHS / LASI | Large samples | Screening items only (e.g., CIDI-SF in LASI) |
| MSUI 2019 (Magnitude of Substance Use) | National substance-use estimates | Substances only |
| NCRB | Suicide counts | Under-reporting; police data |

### Management details: policy uses of NMHS
- **Recommendations:**
  - Treat mental health as a development priority.
  - Expand and strengthen the **DMHP**.
  - Integrate mental health into primary care (**Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, CPHC**).
  - Develop human resources.
  - Address substance use, suicide prevention and stigma.
  - Use digital and tele-mental health.
  - Repeat the survey periodically [1].
- **Follow-on actions:** Tele-MANAS (2022); National Suicide Prevention Strategy (2022); mental health in Health and Wellness Centres; DMHP 2.0 redesign (2024) [4].

### Critical appraisal
**Strengths** [1]:
- Appropriate cross-sectional design.
- Clear objectives.
- Pilot study; master protocol; standardised MINI.
- Electronic data capture; rigorous training and monitoring.
- Mixed methods; systems assessment.

**Limitations** [1]:
- **Only 12 states:** no north-eastern state other than Assam and Manipur; several large states missing.
- **Megacities (over 10 million) excluded.**
- **Tribal populations** sampled only opportunistically.
- **Children under 13 excluded**; adolescent sample small (pilot only).
- MINI was not available in every language.
- Cultural variation not specifically addressed.
- Cross-sectional design gives no causality or incidence; self-report and stigma may cause under-reporting; MINI is structured and lay-administered, which may misclassify.
- Treatment gap based on self-reported care.

**Interpretation:** prevalence (10.6%) is lower than in many Western surveys (~17–20%). This may reflect true differences, measurement and cultural idioms, or under-reporting.

### Indian context
- **Planning:** NMHS data underpin NMHP/DMHP planning, MHCA 2017 implementation needs, Tele-MANAS, and state mental health action plans.
- **Second survey:** **NMHS-2** was announced by the government (2023–24) with wider coverage, including children. Check the current status.

### Recent advances
- **Emerging:**
  - NMHS-2 (planned or ongoing).
  - Digital surveillance through the Tele-MANAS and HMIS dashboards.
  - Integration with ABDM.
  - District-level estimates using small-area modelling.
