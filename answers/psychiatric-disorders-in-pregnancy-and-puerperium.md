## Exam answer

### Introduction [1, 2]
- Pregnancy and the year after delivery are a time of **high psychiatric vulnerability**, especially **after delivery**: new psychiatric episodes rise sharply in the **first 3 months**, and at least **80% are mood disorders** [2].
- DSM-5-TR uses the specifier **"with peripartum onset"** (onset in pregnancy or within **4 weeks** of delivery) for:
  - **major depressive, manic and hypomanic episodes**;
  - **brief psychotic disorder** [1].
- **Consequences:**
  - perinatal illness is an **independent risk factor** for **congenital malformations, stillbirth, neonatal death** and **obstetric near misses** (in SMI);
  - **child outcomes persist into adolescence** [2];
  - **perinatal suicide** is linked to **lack of active treatment** [2].

### A. Disorders during pregnancy [1, 2]
1. **Depression**:
   - about **9–10%** of pregnant women [1, 2];
   - about **half** (DSM-5-TR) or **a third** (Maudsley) of "postpartum" depressions **begin before birth** ⚠️ (the sources differ);
   - may slightly raise miscarriage, **low birth weight/small for gestational age**, and **preterm delivery** [2];
   - risks: poor self-care, missed obstetric care, self-harm, neglect [2].
2. **Relapse of bipolar disorder:**
   - pregnancy itself does not raise the risk **unless medication is stopped** [1];
   - **relapse risk is high if mood stabilisers are discontinued** [2].
3. **Psychosis:**
   - **pregnancy does not protect** against relapse;
   - **psychosis in pregnancy predicts postpartum psychosis** [2].
4. **Anxiety disorders and insomnia** are **common**; first-line treatments are **CBT** and **sleep hygiene** [2].
5. **Substance use and smoking:**
   - smoking is more common in pregnant women with mental illness and carries **the largest share of excess risk** of poor pregnancy outcomes;
   - alcohol may be used to self-medicate depression [2].
6. **Organic syndromes in pregnancy** [3]:
   - **hyperemesis gravidarum** → **Wernicke's encephalopathy** (thiamine deficiency);
   - **eclampsia** → **hypertensive encephalopathy** (a medical emergency) and **posterior reversible leucoencephalopathy** (seizures, headache, confusion, visual disturbance; occipitoparietal T2 hyperintensity; **reversible** when the cause is treated).

### B. Disorders of the puerperium [1, 2, 3]

| Condition | Frequency | Onset/course | Key features | Management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Maternity ("baby") blues** | **Much more common** than depression [1] | Shortly after delivery; **resolves within a week** | Sudden tearfulness, lability, sleep disturbance, brief confusion; **no impairment; not a disorder** | No treatment needed [1] |
| **Postpartum (peripartum-onset) depression** | **Just under 7%** in the first year [1] | Pregnancy or within 4 weeks (specifier) | Full major depressive episode; may be psychotic; suicide and infant neglect risk | CBT, SSRIs (sertraline), brexanolone, ECT [2] |
| **Postpartum psychosis** | **1 in 500 to 1 in 1000** [1]; 0.1–0.25% [2] | After delivery (specifier: within 4 weeks) | **Manic or mixed** episode with psychosis; **bipolar I**; infant-directed delusions; **infanticide** risk; catatonia | Antipsychotic, lithium (no breastfeeding), ECT; perinatal services [2] |
| **Bipolar relapse** | About **1 in 5** with bipolar disorder (psychotic relapse); **up to 50%** with high loading; **up to 70%** if lithium is stopped [2] | Postpartum | Mania, mixed states, depression | Continue prophylaxis; mood-stabilising antipsychotic [2] |
| **Postpartum delirium** | — | — | **Fluctuating awareness or attention** [1] | Treat the cause |
| **Sheehan's syndrome** (organic) | Historically the commonest cause of hypopituitarism [3] | Long, insidious | Ischaemic pituitary necrosis after **postpartum haemorrhage**: weakness, fatigue, cold sensitivity, amenorrhoea, loss of libido, psychiatric change [3] | Endocrine replacement (⚠️ details not in source) |
| **Wernicke–Korsakoff** (organic) | — | — | Historically after **puerperal sepsis** [3] | Parenteral thiamine |

**Infanticide** is rare; it is most associated with postpartum psychotic episodes (command hallucinations, delusions that the baby is possessed) but can occur in severe non-psychotic mood episodes [1].

### C. Risk factors [1, 2]
- **Previous perinatal episode**: postpartum psychosis recurs in **30–50%** of later deliveries [1].
- **Bipolar I or schizoaffective disorder**; **family history** of bipolar disorder or postpartum psychosis [1, 2].
- Previous depression (postpartum or not) [2].
- **Discontinuing medication** (lithium; antidepressants: **68% relapse after stopping vs 26% if continued**) [2].
- Primiparity (postpartum psychosis) [1].
- Thyroid and other medical conditions (depression) [1].

### D. Principles of management [2]
1. **Pre-conception:**
   - discuss pregnancy with every woman of child-bearing potential (**half of pregnancies are unplanned**);
   - avoid **valproate, carbamazepine and topiramate**;
   - **perinatal psychiatry advice and a written care plan** for SMI;
   - **folic acid**.
2. **Risk–benefit discussion** with the woman and partner. Untreated illness harms mother and child; ACOG warns against stopping medication **solely because of pregnancy or lactation**.
3. **Drug principles:**
   - avoid drugs in the **first trimester** where possible;
   - **established drugs, lowest dose, fewest drugs**;
   - **do not switch** a well woman (switching risks relapse);
   - **adjust doses** in late pregnancy (blood volume, CYP changes; lithium and lamotrigine levels).
4. **Drug choices:**
   - **SSRIs** (sertraline) for depression;
   - **antipsychotics** (not major teratogens; screen for **gestational diabetes**);
   - **lithium** with informed consent and fetal echocardiography;
   - **lamotrigine** with levels;
   - **benzodiazepines** only short term (floppy infant);
   - **ECT** for severe or catatonic states.
5. **Liaison:**
   - **obstetrics** (fetal anomaly scans, delivery plan, lithium in labour);
   - **neonatology** (neonatal adaptation and withdrawal monitoring);
   - smoking cessation; addiction services.
6. **Postpartum:**
   - **close monitoring** in the high-risk period;
   - **continue treatment while breastfeeding**, except **lithium and clozapine** (formula feed);
   - **no breastfeeding in bed** on sedatives.
7. **Document every decision.**

**Conclusion.** Psychiatric disorders of pregnancy and the puerperium range from the physiological maternity blues to peripartum depression (about 7–9%) and postpartum psychosis (1–2 per 1000), a bipolar-spectrum emergency with risks of suicide and infanticide. Pre-existing disorders often relapse, especially after delivery and when medication is stopped. Organic causes must be remembered: delirium; Wernicke's encephalopathy from hyperemesis; eclampsia-related encephalopathy; Sheehan's syndrome. Management weighs fetal and neonatal drug risks (mostly small, except valproate, carbamazepine and topiramate) against the substantial harms of untreated illness, through pre-conception planning, continuation of effective treatment, specialist perinatal care and obstetric–neonatal liaison.
