## Exam answer

**Introduction [1].**
- Improved obstetric care has reduced maternal deaths from haemorrhage and medical disease, so **maternal mental health has come to the fore** as a major cause of maternal morbidity and mortality.
- Psychiatrists see perinatal referrals from three places: antenatal and postnatal clinics, obstetric wards and labour rooms, and the obstetric emergency department.

### Conditions by stage (Tables 1 and 2) [1]
**Preconception:**
- infertility and ART: stress, anxiety, depression;
- **tokophobia** (fear of pregnancy and childbirth);
- previous traumatic childbirth (injury, **disrespectful care**, stillbirth, emergency caesarean section), leading to depression, anxiety or PTSD;
- pre-existing SMI planning pregnancy: fear of teratogenicity and **family pressure to stop prophylaxis**.

**Pregnancy:**
- **anxiety 10–15%**, **depression 15–20%**, PTSD;
- **SMI recurrence 60–80% without prophylaxis** vs **20–30% with** it;
- **risk factors:** poor support, **spouse's substance use, domestic violence**, previous female child, marital discord.

**Childbirth:**
- stillbirth, leading to grief;
- infant illness or NICU separation, leading to separation anxiety, guilt and anticipatory grief;
- **stress over the infant's sex**, leading to domestic violence or marital discord.

**Postpartum:**
| Condition | Prevalence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mother–infant bonding disorders | 5% of healthy mothers; **40–60%** of mothers with psychiatric illness | Delay or absence of bonding; **pathological anger**; anxiety about infant care; rejection (rare) |
| Postpartum blues | **40–50%** | Mild, self-limiting; may progress to depression; reassurance and support |
| Postpartum depression | **15–20%** | Within 4 weeks (risk up to 2 years); psychotherapy or antidepressants; suicide and infanticide risk assessment |
| Postpartum psychosis | **12/1000 live births** | Abrupt onset: mania, psychotic depression, acute psychosis, catatonia; in-patient care |
| OCD | — | Contamination or **intrusive harm thoughts about the infant** (without actual harm); CBT, SSRIs |

**Substance use [1]:**
- **No level of alcohol is safe** in pregnancy (growth restriction, facial anomalies, CNS dysfunction).
- **Cannabis:** mood disorders, ASD and ADHD-type problems in offspring.
- **Tobacco:** preterm birth, low birth weight, oral clefts, maternal bleeding.

**Suicide [1]:**
- Risk is **higher in the first year after childbirth than at any other time** in a woman's life.
- **Predictors:** younger age, middle socioeconomic status, poor support, domestic violence, depression, past suicidality.
- **More lethal methods** are used.
- Suicide and infanticide risk may coexist.

### Assessment (Box 1) [1]
- **History:**
  - routine history plus perinatal details: past pregnancies, obstetric violence, abuse;
  - **whether the pregnancy was planned or wanted**;
  - support; substance use in the woman and spouse.
- **Examination:** to exclude anaemia, jaundice, hypertension, thyroid disease, connective tissue disorders and neurological disease.
- **MSE:** attitudes to pregnancy and motherhood, and **infant-related psychopathology**.
- **Risk:** suicide, infanticide, harm to others.
- **Investigations (Table 3):** infection, electrolytes, renal, glucose, haemogram, B12, thyroid and antibodies, LFT, ammonia, drug screen, imaging, EEG, CSF. Exclude **PRES, cortical venous thrombosis, Sheehan syndrome, Wernicke encephalopathy, anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis**.
- **Tools:**
  - screening: Whooley questions, PHQ-9, EPDS, GAD-7;
  - severity: HDRS, HARS, YMRS, PANSS, BPRS;
  - maternal behaviour and bonding: NIMBUS, PBQ;
  - comprehensive: Stafford Interview.

### General management principles [1, 2]
**Preconception [1]:**
- **folic acid 5 mg/day**;
- delay pregnancy until stable (contraception until 6–12 months of remission);
- consider stopping medication only after a **single mild episode**;
- continue prophylaxis in **SMI**;
- taper adjunctive drugs (benzodiazepines, β-blockers, anticholinergics);
- **never stop abruptly**.

**Pregnancy:**
- early, regular antenatal care;
- **anomaly scans at 10–12 and 16–18 weeks**, with **fetal echocardiography** for lithium or SSRIs;
- deliver in a hospital with NICU facilities [1].
- **Mild new illness in the first trimester:** psychotherapy [1].
- Avoid all drugs in the first trimester unless benefits clearly outweigh risks. Use established drugs at the lowest dose [2].

**Risk–benefit analysis**, weighing four things [1]:
1. risks of untreated illness;
2. benefits of avoiding exposure;
3. benefits of controlling illness;
4. risks of medication.
- Consider **confounding by indication**.
- Use the **2015 Pregnancy and Lactation Labelling Rule** rather than FDA categories.
- Present risks as relative and absolute risk, with visual aids, and **document** the discussion (Box 2).

**Pharmacokinetics (Table 6) [1]:**
- **Lithium levels fall** in pregnancy and **return abruptly** after delivery. Check monthly until 34 weeks, then weekly; before and 24 hours after delivery; and often postpartum.
- **Lamotrigine levels can fall by about 50%.** Monitor from preconception until the first postpartum month.

**Drug choices (Tables 8 and 9) [1, 2]:**
- **Antidepressants:** SSRIs, preferably **sertraline**; avoid paroxetine.
- **Antipsychotics:** SGAs as monotherapy. Monitor weight and do an **OGTT** (gestational diabetes). Clozapine carries a floppy infant risk and is contraindicated in breastfeeding.
- **Mood stabilisers:**
  - **avoid valproate** (neural tube defects 6–9%, intellectual disability) and **carbamazepine**;
  - **lithium** (Ebstein's anomaly about 1/1000; smaller than once thought) or **low-dose lamotrigine (under 325 mg/day)** if needed.
- **Benzodiazepines:** taper; use short-acting **lorazepam** if needed (floppy infant if given just before delivery).
- **Alcohol dependence:** lorazepam or diazepam for withdrawal. **Disulfiram is avoided.** Naltrexone and acamprosate only if relapse risk is high.

**Specific disorders (Table 9) [1]:**
- **Adjustment disorder, mild anxiety or mild depression:** psychoeducation and CBT or IPT.
- **Moderate to severe depression:** SSRIs.
- **Recurrent depression (4 or more episodes) or recent severe depression:** antidepressant prophylaxis.
- **OCD:** ERP/CBT; SSRIs for severe OCD.
- **Bipolar disorder:** SGA monotherapy; add lithium if needed. Use antidepressants cautiously.
- **Schizophrenia:** SGA monotherapy. **Do not initiate FGAs or LAIs** in pregnancy.
- **Catatonia:** lorazepam, then ECT.

**ECT in pregnancy (Box 4) [1]:**
- obstetric clearance; antacids; avoid anticholinergics, or use **glycopyrrolate**;
- **right hip elevated after 20 weeks**;
- fetal heart monitoring;
- emergency obstetric and neonatal care available.
- NICE: ECT for severe depression, mixed states, mania or catatonia when the mother's or fetus's health is at serious risk [2].

**Lactation [1]:**
- exclusive on-demand feeding; RID under 10%;
- **clozapine contraindicated**; lithium needs monitoring;
- watch infants for toxicity;
- encourage sensorimotor stimulation (any developmental delay is mild and reversible).

**MHCA 2017 [1]:** least restrictive care; **rooming-in** of children under 3 (Section 21); separation reviewed every 15 days; the MHRB informed if separation lasts beyond 30 days. Mother–baby units are encouraged.

**Conclusion.** The perinatal period brings anxiety, depression, bonding disorders, the blues, PPD, PPP, OCD, substance problems and peak suicide risk. Management rests on:
- careful assessment, including organic causes and risk;
- an individualised risk–benefit analysis (for SMI, the benefits of prophylaxis generally exceed the risks);
- lactation-compatible prescribing;
- MBU-based joint care.

## In depth

### Postpartum psychosis risk [2]
- 0.1–0.25% of births.
- **1 in 5** women with bipolar disorder have a postpartum psychotic relapse.
- Risk up to **50%** with a previous episode, bipolar I or schizoaffective disorder, and family loading.

### Lithium in pregnancy (NICE via Maudsley Box 7.4) [2]
- If she is well, consider stopping lithium **gradually over 4 weeks**.
- If it is continued:
  - levels every 4 weeks, then **weekly from 36 weeks**;
  - deliver in hospital;
  - **stop lithium during labour** and check the level **12 hours after the last dose**;
  - fetal cardiac screening.
