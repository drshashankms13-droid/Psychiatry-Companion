## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 72-year-old man brought to the emergency department with **acute onset** of:
- **irrelevant talk**;
- **disorientation**;
- **disinhibited behaviour**;
- **disturbed sleep**.

### 1. Diagnosis
**Delirium (acute confusional state)** until proven otherwise [1, 2].
- **Features:**
  - acute onset and **fluctuating course**;
  - **inattention** (the **cardinal feature**);
  - disorganised thinking;
  - **altered consciousness**;
  - sleep–wake disturbance;
  - perceptual and affective change.
- **Delirium is a medical emergency.** It is under-diagnosed and raises morbidity and mortality: 22–76% in-hospital mortality in elderly delirious patients [2].
- **Consider delirium superimposed on dementia (DSD):** dementia increases delirium risk **2–5-fold**. Ask about **pre-existing cognitive decline** [1].
- **Differentials:**
  - **dementia** (insidious; consciousness clear);
  - **acute psychosis or mania**;
  - **frontal lesion** (stroke, tumour);
  - **non-convulsive status** [1, 2, 3].
- **Lishman** [3]: acute organic reactions show **positive** emotional disturbance (fear, perplexity, agitation) and **emotional rapport can usually be established**, whereas demented patients may be flat. A prolonged subacute delirium (anoxia, uraemia, hepatic disease) can closely mimic dementia.

### 2. Assessment [1, 2]
- **Confirm delirium with the CAM:** features **1 + 2 plus 3 or 4**:
  1. acute onset and fluctuating course;
  2. inattention;
  3. disorganised thinking;
  4. altered level of consciousness.
- Rate severity with the **MDAS** (cut-off 13) or DRS-R-98 [2].
- **Search for causes.** These are usually multifactorial: **3 or more causes** in many cases [2].
  - **Infection:** UTI, pneumonia, sepsis.
  - **Metabolic:** sodium, glucose, calcium, renal, hepatic, hypoxia.
  - **Drugs:** anticholinergics, opioids, **benzodiazepines**, steroids, sedatives.
  - **Withdrawal:** alcohol or benzodiazepines.
  - **Neurological:** stroke, haemorrhage, seizures, subdural.
  - **Nutritional:** thiamine, B12, folate.
  - **Cardiovascular:** arrhythmia, MI.
  - Pain, urinary retention, constipation [1, 2].
- **Work-up:**
  - full physical examination (sepsis, dehydration, organ failure);
  - medication review;
  - labs: CBC, electrolytes, glucose, renal and liver tests, calcium, urine, cultures;
  - ECG;
  - **brain imaging** and CSF where indicated; **EEG** (generalised slowing; excludes non-convulsive status) [2].
- **Collateral history:** baseline cognition and function; alcohol; recent medication changes.

### 3. Management [1, 2, 4]
**Treat the cause.**

**Non-pharmacological (first line):**
- a calm, well-lit environment; **orientation cues** (clocks, calendars); **familiar faces**;
- **hearing aids and glasses**; early mobilisation; uninterrupted night sleep;
- limit room and staff changes; one-to-one observation if needed;
- fluids and nutrition.

**Pharmacological** (for distressing agitation, psychosis, or risk):
- **low-dose haloperidol**: 0.25–1 mg in the elderly [1]. APA: 0.25–0.5 mg every 4 hours as needed in the elderly [2];
- **atypicals** are comparable (risperidone 0.25–1 mg; olanzapine 2.5–5 mg; quetiapine 25–50 mg) [2];
- **avoid benzodiazepines as first line**: they **worsen confusion** and increase falls. Lorazepam alone was **ineffective and sometimes worsened** delirium (Breitbart). The exception is **alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal** [1, 2];
- **avoid antipsychotics if DLB is suspected** (neuroleptic sensitivity) [1];
- monitor **QTc** (especially with IV haloperidol), and use **low doses for short periods** in the elderly (dementia mortality warning) [2];
- **rapid tranquillisation** in older adults uses lower doses, with physical monitoring [4].

**Follow-up:**
- After resolution, **reassess cognition**, because underlying dementia may be revealed.
- **Delirium recall can be distressing** (54% recall it); explain and reassure.
- Educate the family [1, 2].

**Conclusion.** Acute disorientation, disinhibition and sleep disturbance in a 72-year-old is **delirium** until proven otherwise. Plan:
- confirm with the CAM;
- search systematically for multiple causes;
- manage non-pharmacologically, with low-dose haloperidol or an atypical if needed;
- avoid benzodiazepines (except in withdrawal);
- reassess for underlying dementia.

## In depth

### Delirium vs dementia vs DSD [1]
| Feature | Delirium | Dementia | DSD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onset | Hours to days | Insidious | Acute on chronic |
| Course | Fluctuating | Slowly progressive | Fluctuating, worse than baseline |
| Attention | Severely impaired | Relatively preserved early | Severely impaired |
| Consciousness | Altered | Clear | Altered |
| Precipitant | Usually identifiable | None | Usually identifiable |
