## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Delirium is the **most common severe neuropsychiatric complication** of COVID-19, particularly in older and ICU patients. It can be the **presenting feature** of infection in older adults [1]. Pandemic conditions **increased risk**:
- isolation and PPE-clad staff;
- no family visits;
- sedation;
- prolonged ventilation.

They also **limited standard non-drug measures** [general knowledge]. **All COVID-19 inpatients should be screened for delirium** [1].

### Causes and risk factors in COVID-19
- **Predisposing:**
  - old age;
  - dementia or cognitive impairment;
  - **sensory deprivation**;
  - physical disability;
  - comorbidities;
  - **past delirium**;
  - alcohol use [1].
- **Precipitating:**
  - **hypoxia**; sepsis; fever;
  - **systemic inflammation** (cytokines);
  - **stroke** and microthrombi;
  - encephalitis (rare);
  - electrolyte disturbance (hypo- or hypernatraemia);
  - hypo- or hyperglycaemia (**steroids**, diabetes);
  - renal and hepatic dysfunction;
  - **drugs**: **benzodiazepine and opioid sedation, steroids, HCQ**, anticholinergics;
  - **alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal**;
  - sleep deprivation; immobility; urinary retention; constipation;
  - **isolation and absence of family**; PPE masking faces.

### Assessment [1]
- **Screen daily:** **CAM / CAM-ICU**, **RASS** (sedation and motor activity), 4AT.
- Assess **onset and fluctuating course**; attention; sleep–wake cycle; **hypoactive vs hyperactive vs mixed** subtype.
- Cognition: MMSE/HMSE/MoCA when feasible.
- **Find the cause:**
  - SpO₂ and ABG;
  - glucose, electrolytes, renal and liver function;
  - infection markers;
  - medication review (temporal link to anti-COVID drugs);
  - **withdrawal history** (last drink);
  - CT/MRI for focal signs or stroke;
  - **EEG** (non-convulsive status);
  - LP if encephalitis is suspected.
- **Risk assessment:** pulling out lines or oxygen, falls, aggression.

### Management
**1. Treat the underlying cause (the key step):**
- correct hypoxia;
- treat sepsis and fever;
- correct electrolytes and glucose;
- **reduce deliriogenic drugs** (benzodiazepines, anticholinergics, opioids);
- discuss **steroid dose** with the physicians [1].

**2. Non-pharmacological measures (adapted for COVID wards):**
- **re-orientation cues** [1]: clocks, calendars, windows, staff name badges and **photos on PPE**;
- **family contact by video or phone calls**, which partly substitutes for visits [1];
- hearing aids and spectacles;
- sleep protocol: lights off, noise reduction, cluster care;
- early mobilisation and hydration;
- avoid restraint (MHCA Section 97: only the last resort, documented).

**3. Pharmacological treatment** [1]:
- only for **distressing symptoms or risk** (agitation, psychosis);
- **low dose, short duration**;
- **haloperidol** (0.5–1 mg; IV, IM or oral) **or quetiapine** (12.5–50 mg), **monitoring QTc**: additive risk with HCQ and azithromycin; also hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia;
- **dexmedetomidine** in ICU (sedation without respiratory depression);
- **melatonin** (sleep–wake regulation; some evidence for prevention);
- **lorazepam only for alcohol- or benzodiazepine-withdrawal delirium**, with **monitoring for respiratory depression** given compromised respiratory status [1];
- avoid other benzodiazepines (they worsen delirium and respiratory depression);
- olanzapine (sublingual or IM) and risperidone are alternatives;
- check drug interactions (ritonavir with quetiapine).

**4. Hypoactive delirium:** generally no antipsychotic. Focus on the cause and supportive care.

**5. Special situations:**
- **pre-existing SMI on clozapine:** halve the dose (infection raises levels) [1];
- **Parkinson's disease or DLB:** quetiapine at low dose; avoid haloperidol;
- **elderly:** lowest doses.

**6. High-risk management** [1]:
- 24 × 7 observation if agitated or suicidal;
- safety of lines;
- prevent falls.

**7. Communication:**
- **liaise with the COVID team** about delirium's prognostic significance;
- update and psychoeducate the family (by video);
- **break bad news** sensitively (SPIKES) [1].

**8. Follow-up after recovery:**
- screen for **PTSD** (delirium memories) and **cognitive impairment** (post-delirium cognitive decline);
- **ICU diaries**;
- post-COVID clinic review [1].

**Prevention:**
- ABCDEF bundle in ICU:
  - **A**ssess and manage pain;
  - spontaneous awakening and **B**reathing trials;
  - **C**hoice of sedation (avoid benzodiazepines);
  - **D**elirium monitoring;
  - **E**arly mobility;
  - **F**amily engagement [general knowledge].
- Light sedation, sleep hygiene, sensory aids, family video calls, melatonin.

**Conclusion.** COVID-19 delirium is common, multifactorial (hypoxia, inflammation, drugs, isolation) and prognostically serious. Management rests on **daily screening, aggressive correction of causes, adapted non-pharmacological measures** (video contact with family, orientation), and **cautious low-dose antipsychotics** (haloperidol or quetiapine with QTc monitoring), dexmedetomidine and melatonin. Benzodiazepines are reserved for withdrawal states. Follow-up should address PTSD and cognitive sequelae.

## In depth

### Background
- Delirium is an acute disturbance of attention and awareness with fluctuating course, caused by a medical condition (DSM-5-TR). ICD-11 code 6D70.
- **Pandemic ICU practice** shifted towards deeper sedation and more benzodiazepine use because of ventilator dyssynchrony and staff shortages, which increased delirium (Pun 2021, COVID-D study) [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology
- About 20–30% of hospitalised COVID patients; **up to about 80% in mechanically ventilated** patients [general knowledge].
- **Delirium independently predicts mortality** and longer stay [general knowledge].

### Classification
- **Hyperactive:** agitation, hallucinations.
- **Hypoactive:** withdrawn, drowsy; under-recognised; common in the elderly.
- **Mixed.**

### Mechanism
- Neuroinflammation (cytokines crossing a disrupted BBB).
- Cholinergic deficiency with dopaminergic excess.
- Hypoxia; network disconnectivity.
- Sleep–wake disruption; sensory deprivation.

### Clinical features
- Fluctuating inattention, disorientation, perceptual disturbances (often visual), sleep reversal, emotional lability.
- **In COVID:** agitation pulling at masks and oxygen; fear in an isolated, masked environment.

### Assessment tools
- CAM / CAM-ICU (4 features: acute onset and fluctuation; inattention; disorganised thinking; altered level of consciousness).
- RASS (−5 to +4).
- 4AT; DRS-R-98 (severity).

### Differential diagnosis
- **Steroid-induced psychosis or mania** (clear sensorium).
- **Acute anxiety or panic** with hypoxia.
- **Primary psychosis.**
- **Non-convulsive status epilepticus.**
- **Dementia** (baseline).
- **Wernicke encephalopathy** in alcohol users (give thiamine).

### Management: evidence
- **Antipsychotics do not shorten delirium or reduce mortality** in ICU RCTs (MIND-USA 2018). They are used for distressing symptoms [general knowledge].
- **Dexmedetomidine:** reduces delirium compared with benzodiazepines in ICU sedation (MENDS, SEDCOM) [general knowledge].
- **Melatonin and ramelteon:** mixed prevention evidence [general knowledge].

### Evidence table
| Source | Point |
| --- | --- |
| IPS CPG 2022 | Delirium management in COVID: cause, reorientation, melatonin, haloperidol or quetiapine (QTc), dexmedetomidine, lorazepam for alcohol withdrawal [1] |
| MIND-USA (Girard 2018, *NEJM*) (general knowledge) | Haloperidol and ziprasidone did not alter delirium duration |
| COVID-D (Pun 2021, *Lancet Respir Med*) (general knowledge) | High delirium and coma prevalence in COVID ICU; benzodiazepine use a risk factor |

### Critical appraisal
- Pandemic constraints made best-practice non-pharmacological care difficult. Evidence specific to COVID delirium is observational.
- The QTc risk of antipsychotics combined with HCQ/azithromycin mattered early; it is less relevant now that HCQ is abandoned.

### Indian context
- Few psychiatrists in COVID wards. **Tele-CL** and nurse-led CAM screening are practical [1].
- Alcohol withdrawal delirium was common during lockdown liquor bans [general knowledge].

### Recent advances
- EEG-based delirium monitoring.
- **Family-presence policies** even during infection-control periods.
- Post-ICU cognitive rehabilitation.
