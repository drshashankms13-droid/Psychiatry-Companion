## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A **28-year-old mother of a 3-year-old** has had **2 months** of:
- low mood, worthlessness, **lack of interest in bonding with her child**, disturbed sleep;
- **guilt** ("bad mother");
- the belief that her child **would be better off without her**.

A **similar episode followed childbirth 3 years ago** and resolved **spontaneously**, untreated. Meanwhile the child has **delayed speech, social withdrawal, poor eye contact and hand-flapping**. The **paediatrician suspects ASD**.

### Formulation
- **Mother:** **recurrent depressive disorder, current episode moderate to severe** (ICD-11 6A71). Key points:
  - the **previous episode was postpartum** (peripartum onset);
  - **untreated**;
  - current guilt and the "better off without me" statement mean **passive suicidal ideation**. **Infanticidal or extended-suicide thoughts must be asked about** (risk in maternal depression with guilt about the child) [1].
- **Child:** **probable ASD** (social-communication deficits plus restricted, repetitive behaviour). Needs **formal assessment** (see `autism-spectrum-disorder`).
- **The bidirectional link:**
  - **maternal depression** reduces responsiveness, play and language input, and may delay recognition of developmental problems and the child's engagement with services;
  - **caring for a child with ASD** is a major stressor that raises maternal depression risk;
  - parental guilt is common ("did I cause it?"). Explain that ASD is **neurodevelopmental and largely genetic**, and **not caused by her depression or parenting**.
- **The "lack of bonding" complaint** may reflect depression (anhedonia, guilt) and/or the child's reduced social reciprocity. Assess with the **Postpartum Bonding Questionnaire** principles [1].

### (a) Further assessment
**Mother:**
1. **Risk:**
   - **suicide** (ideation, intent, plans, means, past attempts);
   - **infanticidal ideas or extended suicide**;
   - **neglect** of the child;
   - use IS PATH WARM or C-SSRS [1].
2. **Depression details:**
   - severity (PHQ-9 or HDRS; **EPDS** is validated beyond the postnatal period);
   - **psychotic features**;
   - **bipolarity**: hypomanic episodes, family history, postpartum onset (a bipolar risk marker). **Rule out bipolarity before an antidepressant** [1].
3. **Previous episode:** onset in relation to delivery, duration, severity, any psychotic features, functioning; why it was untreated.
4. **Perinatal and obstetric history:**
   - is she pregnant now or planning pregnancy? (Contraception and preconception counselling are needed);
   - is she breastfeeding?
5. **Psychosocial:**
   - marital relationship; **domestic violence** [1];
   - spouse's substance use [1];
   - family support;
   - gender and family expectations;
   - financial stress;
   - **stigma about the child's condition**; blame from in-laws.
6. **Medical:** **thyroid (TSH)**, anaemia (CBC, ferritin), **B12**, vitamin D, glucose [1].
7. **Family history:** mood disorders, ASD, ID.

**Child:**
- developmental history;
- **hearing assessment** (rule out deafness);
- **INCLEN-ASD / ISAA / M-CHAT-R/F**, then **ADOS-2 / ADI-R** if available, with CARS;
- developmental quotient or IQ (DST, VSMS);
- vision; seizures;
- genetic evaluation (fragile X, chromosomal microarray) if there are dysmorphic features or ID;
- sleep and feeding.

### (b) Management
**1. Safety first:**
- if **active suicidal or infanticidal intent, psychotic depression, or inability to care for herself or the child**: **inpatient care**, ideally in a **mother–baby unit** (NIMHANS). **MHCA Section 21(2)**: no separation from a child under 3;
- otherwise outpatient care with a **safety plan**, means restriction, a family member present, and **Tele-MANAS 14416**.

**2. Treatment of maternal depression** (IPS perinatal CPG principles [1]):
- **Moderate–severe or recurrent depression: antidepressant + psychotherapy:**
  - **SSRI** (sertraline if breastfeeding or planning pregnancy; escitalopram or fluoxetine otherwise);
  - **avoid paroxetine** if pregnancy is likely [1];
  - **CBT or IPT** (IPT suits role transition and the stress of caring for a child with a disability).
- **Psychotic depression:** add an antipsychotic [1]. Consider **ECT** if severe, refusing food, or high risk.
- **Duration:** this is a **second episode**. Continue treatment for at least **12 months** after remission, and consider longer prophylaxis.
- **Future pregnancies** [1]:
  - preconception counselling;
  - **folic acid 5 mg**;
  - plan prophylaxis: a woman with a **previous postpartum depression may be given prophylactic antidepressants after delivery**;
  - monitor closely through pregnancy and postpartum.
- **If bipolarity emerges:** SGA or mood stabiliser; avoid antidepressant monotherapy [1].

**3. Address the mother–child relationship:**
- psychoeducation;
- **mother–child interaction work** (e.g., parent-mediated interventions);
- reduce guilt;
- explain ASD and why early intervention matters.

**4. The child: early intervention for ASD:**
- **parent-mediated interventions** (e.g., **PASS**, the Parent-mediated intervention for Autism Spectrum Disorder in South Asia; JASPER; ESDM principles);
- speech and language therapy; occupational therapy;
- structured teaching (TEACCH);
- inclusive preschool;
- **disability certification** (RPwD Act 2016; ASD is a benchmark disability); UDID; National Trust schemes; DEIC (RBSK).
- **Timing:** start child interventions once the mother's depression is improving. Parent-mediated therapy needs an engaged parent, and her recovery improves the child's outcomes. Share the load with the father and family.

**5. Family and social support:**
- involve the husband (shared caregiving; screen him for distress);
- address domestic violence if present;
- support groups (Action for Autism, parent groups);
- respite.

**6. Follow-up:**
- regular review of mood and risk;
- the child's progress;
- caregiver burden (Zarit);
- maternal relapse warning signs.

**Prognosis.** Good with treatment for her depression. Untreated recurrent episodes predict chronicity and further postpartum recurrences. For the child: early intervention and parental well-being improve outcomes.

## In depth

### Background
- **Perinatal depression:** 15–20% postpartum in Indian data [1]. Onset typically within 4 weeks of delivery, but the risk period extends to 2 years [1].
- **The DSM-5-TR "with peripartum onset" specifier** covers onset in pregnancy or within 4 weeks postpartum. ICD-11 has "mental or behavioural disorders associated with pregnancy, childbirth or the puerperium" (6E20, 6E21).
- **Maternal depression and child development:** associated with insecure attachment, cognitive and language delays, and behavioural problems (Stein 2014, *Lancet*) [general knowledge]. It **does not cause ASD**.
- **Parents of children with ASD** have high rates of depression and stress, higher than parents of children with other disabilities [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology
- Recurrence after a previous PPD: about 25–40% with subsequent deliveries [general knowledge].
- Postpartum blues 40–50%; PPD 15–20%; postpartum psychosis about 1–2 per 1,000 [1].

### Classification and criteria
- ICD-11 recurrent depressive disorder: at least 2 depressive episodes separated by several months without significant mood disturbance.

### Mechanism
- **PPD:**
  - hormonal withdrawal (oestrogen, progesterone; **allopregnanolone** and GABA-A sensitivity, the basis for brexanolone);
  - HPA dysregulation;
  - thyroid;
  - sleep deprivation;
  - psychosocial stress.
- **Recurrence in this case:**
  - kindling;
  - chronic stress of caring for a child with developmental delay;
  - untreated first episode.

### Clinical features
- Maternal depression themes: inadequacy as a mother, guilt, intrusive thoughts.
- **Distinguish obsessional infant-harm thoughts** (ego-dystonic, no intent) from psychotic or depressive infanticidal ideation [1].

### Assessment tools [1]
- **EPDS**, PHQ-9, Whooley's questions; HDRS (severity); PBQ (bonding).
- IS PATH WARM for suicide risk.

### Differential diagnosis
- Bipolar depression.
- Adjustment disorder (to the child's diagnosis).
- Hypothyroidism / postpartum thyroiditis (now later).
- Anaemia.
- GAD.
- Complicated grief.
- Domestic violence-related PTSD.

### Management details
- **Brexanolone** (IV allopregnanolone, 60-hour infusion) for moderate–severe PPD; not available in India [1].
- **Zuranolone** (oral, 14 days; FDA 2023) [general knowledge].
- These are for postpartum onset. **Current depression at 3 years postpartum is treated as standard recurrent depression.**

### Critical appraisal
- The question tests **integration**: two patients, one family. Exam answers often treat them separately.
- Maternal mental health is a modifiable factor in ASD intervention outcomes.

### Indian context
- **Services:**
  - **NIMHANS perinatal psychiatry service and mother–baby unit** [1];
  - **MHCA Section 21(2)** (mother and child under 3 not separated);
  - DEIC under RBSK;
  - ASD disability certification (2024 guidelines; ISAA replaced by INCLEN-based tools in some schemes) [general knowledge; check].
- **Stigma and blame** of mothers for a child's disability is common in Indian families.

### Recent advances
- Neurosteroid antidepressants for PPD.
- Digital parent-mediated ASD interventions.
- **Integrated maternal–child mental health** models.
