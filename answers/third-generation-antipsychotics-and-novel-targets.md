## Exam answer

**Introduction.** "Third-generation antipsychotics" is an informal term. It usually means the **dopamine D2/D3 partial agonists**:
- **aripiprazole** (2002);
- **brexpiprazole** (2015);
- **cariprazine** (2015).

Some authors extend it to drugs acting **beyond dopamine blockade**: lumateperone, pimavanserin, **xanomeline–trospium**. Maudsley notes these newer agents "have antipsychotic activity and do not cause EPS but have almost nothing in common with other atypicals". This is why it recommends **Neuroscience-based Nomenclature** over generational labels [1].

### Part A: dopamine partial agonists ("third generation")
**Mechanism:**
- **Partial agonism at D2/D3** gives "dopamine stabilisation":
  - functional **antagonism** where dopamine is high (mesolimbic, hence antipsychotic);
  - **agonism** where dopamine is low (mesocortical, hence possibly less negative and cognitive impairment; tuberoinfundibular, hence **no hyperprolactinaemia**).
- Plus **5-HT1A partial agonism** and **5-HT2A antagonism** [1].
- **Intrinsic activity:** aripiprazole > cariprazine > brexpiprazole (brexpiprazole has lower intrinsic activity, so **less akathisia**) [general knowledge].

| Feature | Aripiprazole | Brexpiprazole | Cariprazine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distinctive pharmacology | D2 partial agonist; 5-HT1A partial; 5-HT2A antagonist | **Lower D2 intrinsic activity**; stronger 5-HT1A/2A, α1B | **D3-preferring** partial agonist (D3 > D2); **very long-acting metabolite (DDCAR, about 2–3 weeks)** |
| Licensed uses (US/EU) | Schizophrenia; bipolar mania and maintenance; **MDD adjunct**; irritability in ASD; Tourette's | Schizophrenia; **MDD adjunct**; **agitation in Alzheimer's dementia** (FDA 2023) | Schizophrenia (**negative symptoms**: superior to risperidone); bipolar mania; **bipolar depression**; MDD adjunct (2022) |
| Dose | 10–30 mg (minimum effective 10 mg) [1] | 2–4 mg | 1.5–6 mg |
| Adverse effects | **Akathisia**, insomnia, nausea; impulse-control disorders (gambling); **low metabolic and prolactin effects** | Less akathisia; mild weight gain | Akathisia, EPS; low prolactin and metabolic effects |
| Sexual function | Relatively free; can **improve** dysfunction [1] | Negligible prolactin [1] | Low rates [1] |
| NMS | Reported [1] | None reported to mid-2024 [1] | None reported to mid-2024 [1] |
| LAI | Aripiprazole monohydrate (monthly; 2-monthly), lauroxil | — | — |

**Advantages:** metabolically and prolactin-friendly; useful in young women, obesity and diabetes; **add-on aripiprazole normalises antipsychotic-induced prolactin** [1]; antidepressant augmentation [1].

**Limitations:**
- not more effective than other SGAs (except cariprazine for negative symptoms in one RCT);
- akathisia;
- **switching** from a full antagonist can cause **rebound or worsening** (partial agonist displaces a strong antagonist);
- impulse-control problems;
- long half-life (cariprazine): slow steady state, and adverse effects persist after stopping.

### Part B: novel antipsychotic targets (beyond D2 blockade)
**Rationale:**
- all licensed antipsychotics until 2024 worked mainly through **D2 antagonism or partial agonism**;
- about **30% of patients have treatment-resistant schizophrenia (TRS)**;
- **negative and cognitive symptoms** respond poorly;
- **adverse effects** (EPS, metabolic, prolactin) limit adherence.

Non-dopaminergic approaches are "an area of active research" [1].

| Target | Agent(s) | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Muscarinic M1/M4 agonism** | **Xanomeline + trospium (KarXT, Cobenfy)**. Xanomeline is a central M1/M4 agonist; trospium is a peripheral antagonist that limits cholinergic adverse effects | **FDA-approved September 2024**. EMERGENT-2 (*Lancet* 2024) and pooled EMERGENT trials positive. **No EPS, weight gain or prolactin rise**; nausea, constipation, dyspepsia. Max 250/60 mg/day [1] |
| **M4 positive allosteric modulator** | Emraclidine | Phase 2 trials **failed** (2024) [general knowledge] |
| **TAAR1 agonism** | **Ulotaront** (SEP-363856; also 5-HT1A); ralmitaront | Phase 2 positive; **phase 3 (DIAMOND) failed** (high placebo response) [1, general knowledge] |
| **5-HT2A inverse agonism / antagonism** | **Pimavanserin** (no D2 binding; FDA for **Parkinson's disease psychosis**); roluperidone (negative symptoms) | Schizophrenia trials mixed; promising for negative symptoms [1] |
| **Multi-receptor plus glutamate modulation** | **Lumateperone** (5-HT2A >> D2; presynaptic partial agonist / postsynaptic antagonist at D2; SERT inhibition; indirect glutamate (NMDA/AMPA) modulation) | FDA 2019 schizophrenia; 2021 bipolar depression; low metabolic, prolactin and EPS (see `lumateperone`) [1] |
| **Glutamate / NMDA** | Glycine-transporter inhibitor **bitopertin (inactive)**; **iclepertin (BI 425809)** for cognitive impairment (phase 3 failed 2025) [general knowledge]; D-serine; sarcosine; **evenamide** (glutamate release inhibitor; TRS add-on) | Mostly negative or experimental [1] |
| **Inflammation** | Minocycline, NSAIDs, celecoxib, N-acetylcysteine, palmitoylethanolamide | Small or mixed effects [1] |
| **Endocannabinoid** | **Cannabidiol** | Small positive adjunct trial [general knowledge] |
| **Hormonal** | **Raloxifene**, oestrogen (women), pregnenolone | Promising adjuncts [1] |
| **GABA / PV interneurons; Kv3.1 modulators; PDE10A inhibitors** | — | Early or negative |
| **Neuromodulation** | rTMS (negative symptoms, auditory hallucinations), tDCS | Mixed [1] |
| **Immune and antibody** | Treating anti-NMDAR encephalitis-like psychosis; immunotherapy | Subgroup-specific |

**Conclusion.** The **D2/D3 partial agonists** offer **better tolerability** (metabolic, prolactin, sexual) with comparable efficacy, plus useful extra indications (depression, negative symptoms, agitation in dementia). **Muscarinic agonism (xanomeline–trospium, 2024)** is the first genuinely **non-dopaminergic** antipsychotic mechanism to reach the clinic. TAAR1 agonists, glutamatergic and anti-inflammatory approaches have so far been disappointing, and **clozapine remains unmatched for TRS** [1].

## In depth

### Background
- **Dopamine hypothesis:**
  - Carlsson;
  - Seeman (D2 binding correlates with clinical potency);
  - **Howes and Kapur (2009) version III:** presynaptic striatal dopamine synthesis capacity is raised in psychosis.
- **Glutamate (NMDA hypofunction) hypothesis:** PCP and ketamine model positive, negative and cognitive symptoms.
- **Cholinergic hypothesis:** xanomeline's antipsychotic effect was first seen serendipitously in Alzheimer's disease trials (1990s), but it was abandoned for peripheral cholinergic effects. Adding **trospium** made it viable [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology / need
- TRS affects about **30%**; about 40–70% of those respond to clozapine [general knowledge].
- **Negative symptoms** persist in about 40% [general knowledge].

### Classification (NbN examples)
- Aripiprazole: **dopamine and serotonin receptor partial agonist** (DS-RPA).
- Risperidone: **dopamine and serotonin receptor antagonist** (DS-RAn).
- Xanomeline: **acetylcholine receptor agonist**.

### Mechanism in detail
- **M1/M4 agonism:**
  - M4 autoreceptors on striatal cholinergic interneurons and midbrain reduce striatal dopamine release **indirectly**;
  - M1 on cortical neurons may help cognition.
- **TAAR1:**
  - modulates dopamine, serotonin and glutamate firing;
  - reduces presynaptic dopamine synthesis;
  - **no D2 binding**.
- **Partial agonists:** the degree of intrinsic activity determines the balance between akathisia or activation and antipsychotic efficacy.

### Clinical features: switching to partial agonists
- **Cross-taper** gradually.
- Supersensitivity and withdrawal phenomena from a previous antagonist can occur.
- **Akathisia** management: lower the dose, propranolol, mirtazapine.

### Assessment
- For trials of novel agents:
  - confirm TRS by **TRRIP criteria** (2 failed adequate trials, adherence confirmed, ideally by plasma levels);
  - use clozapine first [1].

### Differential: when to choose a partial agonist
- Prolactin-related problems; metabolic risk; sexual dysfunction.
- Bipolar disorder or depressive comorbidity.
- **Avoid** where akathisia is a major problem, or in severe acute agitation (sedation needed).

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| EMERGENT-2 (Kaul 2024, *Lancet*) | Xanomeline–trospium: PANSS reduction about 9.6 points more than placebo at 5 weeks [1; effect size general knowledge] |
| Németh 2017, *Lancet* | Cariprazine better than risperidone for predominant negative symptoms [general knowledge] |
| Koblan 2020, *NEJM* | Ulotaront phase 2 positive; later phase 3 negative [general knowledge] |
| Maudsley "Alternatives to clozapine" (Table 1.53) | Experimental adjuncts listed; TAAR1 and muscarinic agents "may hold some promise" [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- **Placebo response inflation** has derailed several novel-target programmes (ulotaront).
- **Short trials (4–6 weeks)** of acute exacerbations do not establish maintenance efficacy or long-term safety.
- **No head-to-head data** for xanomeline–trospium against established antipsychotics or clozapine.
- The "**third-generation**" label is a marketing construct [1].

### Indian context
- Aripiprazole is widely available and cheap. Cariprazine and brexpiprazole were launched in India [general knowledge; check].
- Lumateperone and xanomeline–trospium are not available in India [Guessing; check].
- Cost limits access to newer agents. **Clozapine** remains the key evidence-based option for TRS.

### Recent advances
- **Cobenfy (2024)**: first new mechanism in decades.
- **ARISE trial** (xanomeline–trospium adjunctive): failed its primary endpoint (2025) [general knowledge; check].
- **Long-acting** aripiprazole 2-monthly (2023).
- Precision approaches: stratifying by dopamine synthesis capacity (PET); glutamatergic biomarkers.
