## Exam answer

### Definition [1]
- **Polysomnography (PSG)** is the simultaneous overnight recording of several physiological variables during sleep, to stage sleep and characterise sleep disorders.
- **Three basic parameters define sleep stage:**
  - **EEG**;
  - **electro-oculogram (EOG)**;
  - **electromyogram (EMG)**.
- Respiratory movements, **airflow** and **oximetry** are added for sleep apnoea; limb EMG for periodic limb movements; ECG.

### Sleep physiology that PSG measures [1]
**EEG bands:** beta (over 12 Hz), **alpha (8–12)**, theta (4–8), **delta (1–3)**.

| Stage | EEG / EOG / EMG |
| --- | --- |
| **Wake (relaxed)** | Alpha; rapid eye movements; high EMG |
| **NREM 1** (30 s to 7 minutes) | Alpha slows to **low-voltage irregular** activity; **slow rolling eye movements** |
| **NREM 2** | **Sleep spindles** (12–15 Hz; about 5/minute), **vertex sharp waves**, **K complexes** (2–3/minute) |
| **NREM 3** | Increasing voltage, slower, synchronised |
| **NREM 4** | **Dominated by delta** slow waves. Abundant in the young; **little or none by 60** |
| **REM** | Desynchronised EEG, **rapid eye movements**, **lowest EMG (active atonia)**; vivid dreams |

**Normal architecture (young adult):**
- sleep onset through stage 1; stages 3–4 reached within **30–45 minutes**;
- **first REM at 75–90 minutes**, then cycles of about **90 minutes**;
- REM periods lengthen (5–10 minutes rising to 20–60 minutes) and intensify towards morning; **slow-wave sleep is concentrated in the first cycles**;
- brief arousals common after REM;
- **REM about 22%** of total sleep across adult life.

**Ageing:**
- total sleep time falls from about 8 hours (young adult) to about **6 hours at 90**;
- more arousals; **stage 4 halves by 30** and is largely gone by 60;
- REM preservation correlates with preserved intelligence; **less REM in dementia**.

### Clinical indications and findings [1]

| Disorder | PSG findings and role |
| --- | --- |
| **Obstructive sleep apnoea** | **Definitive diagnosis and severity**. Overnight EEG, **respiratory movements and airflow**, **oximetry**. Apnoeas (10–20 s) in REM and NREM, worse in REM (second half of the night); reduced REM and stage 3–4. Screening alternatives: pulse oximetry plus clinical indices, 24-hour ECG, lateral neck CT |
| **REM sleep behaviour disorder** | **Loss of REM EMG atonia** (REM sleep without atonia), limb twitching; normal cycling; no seizure activity |
| **Narcolepsy** | **Sleep-onset REM episodes** support the diagnosis (history and HLA DQB1\*0602 are more robust). The **Epworth Sleepiness Scale** correlates well with laboratory measures. Idiopathic hypersomnia: no apnoea, increased stage 2 |
| **Parasomnias vs epilepsy** | NREM parasomnias arise from **stages 3–4**, about 90–120 minutes after sleep onset. Epileptic seizures arise in **stage 2**, within about 30 minutes, with epileptiform discharges |
| **Somnambulism** | Arousal from stage 4; drug provocation (thioridazine) confirmed in the laboratory |
| **Periodic limb movements** | Leg **EMG**: 0.5–5 s jerks every 20–40 s in NREM; arousals |
| **Insomnia** | Laboratory recordings **rarely reveal a cause**; **poor correlation** between complaint and recorded sleep; a careful history and **sleep–wake log** are more useful. Post-traumatic insomnia was validated by PSG (Webb & Kirker) |
| **Psychogenic hypersomnia / dissociative "sleep"** | EEG shows **wakefulness** (fast activity, muscle artefact) |
| **Organic hypersomnia** | Sleep EEG with diffuse slowing; disrupted REM/NREM cycling |
| **Psychiatric disorders** | **OCD and depression:** short sleep, frequent awakenings, **short REM latency**, little stage 4. **Chronic schizophrenia:** usually normal pattern and REM proportion. **Alcohol:** REM suppression then rebound; loss of delta; alcoholics show increased REM EMG |
| **Fatal familial insomnia** | Loss of slow-wave and REM sleep |

### Practical considerations [1]
- Always take a **history from the bed partner** and observe the patient asleep where possible.
- A sleep–wake **log** helps circadian disorders (e.g., delayed sleep phase).
- First-night and laboratory effects: some people sleep very badly in the laboratory yet have no complaint.
- Medication (hypnotics, antidepressants, alcohol) alters sleep architecture; consider this when interpreting results.

**Conclusion.** PSG is the overnight multichannel recording of EEG, EOG and EMG (plus respiration, airflow, oximetry and limb EMG) that stages sleep (NREM 1–4, REM) and defines its architecture: first REM at 75–90 minutes, 90-minute cycles, slow-wave sleep early and REM late, with age-related loss of stage 4. It is **definitive for obstructive sleep apnoea**, essential for **REM sleep behaviour disorder** (REM without atonia), supportive in narcolepsy (sleep-onset REM), and helps separate parasomnias from nocturnal epilepsy and psychogenic states. It is of limited value in insomnia, where the history and sleep diary matter more.
