## Exam answer

### Introduction [1]
- **Origin:** devised by **Folstein, Folstein & McHugh (1975)** as a **simplified form of the routine cognitive status examination**.
- **Strengths:**
  - **brief**: 5–10 minutes;
  - **high test–retest reliability**;
  - discriminates well between patients with **dementia and delirium** (Anthony et al. 1982);
  - widely used; a "**rough and ready index**" of cognitive function that can **track improvement and deterioration**.

### Structure [1]
**Section 1, orientation, memory and attention:**
- **orientation**;
- **registration**: the number of trials needed to learn **three object names**;
- **attention**: **serial sevens** or **spelling a word backwards**;
- **recall** of the three objects later.

**Section 2, language and praxis:**
- **naming** common objects;
- following **verbal and written commands**;
- **writing a sentence** spontaneously;
- **copying** a figure (**overlapping pentagons**).

**Scoring:**
- **maximum 30**;
- **below 24 usually indicates cognitive impairment**.

(Points per item are not given in the source; see the gap note.)

### Uses [1, 2]
1. **Screening** for cognitive impairment in routine psychiatric practice.
2. **Grading severity** in dementia. Lishman: routinely used as a measure of severity, "although it should be remembered that this scale was developed as a **screening** scale".
3. **Monitoring** change over time:
   - in AD, **MMSE at presentation predicts the rate of decline**: the worse the starting score, the faster the decline (O'Hara; Rotterdam);
   - real-world data: MMSE and MoCA decline starts about 2 years before medication is begun [2].
4. **Pattern clues in AD:** early on, the patient can **repeat three items** (working memory) but **fails to recall them** minutes later. **Pentagon copying** taps visuospatial skills [1].
5. **Treatment decisions:**
   - no MMSE cut-off should trigger automatic stopping of cholinesterase inhibitors;
   - patients with MMSE **as low as 3** benefited from continued donepezil [2].
6. **Component of larger instruments:** **CAMCOG** (CAMDEX; out of 107, cut-off 80), the **CERAD** battery, and the **ACE** [1].

### Limitations [1]
- **Relative paucity of memory and general-knowledge items.**
- **Few items tapping frontal-executive function.** The **Frontal Assessment Battery** distinguishes PD, MSA, CBD, PSP and FTD from controls better than the MMSE: FTD patients average MMSE about 21 but FAB about 8/18 (Table 3.3).
- **Normal MMSE despite executive deficits:** a patient with non-cortical vascular disease scored **28/30** on the MMSE but 24/50 on the executive interview (EXIT; above 5 indicates impairment), with abnormal CLOX drawing (Fig. 3.2).
- **Poor predictor of function:** only a **modest correlation** between cognitive and functional loss. The MMSE is not a reliable predictor of an **individual's** functional ability, especially in mild cases; age predicted functional loss better (Teri).
- **Confounds:** abnormalities must be weighed against **low intelligence, education, emotional disturbance or psychotic thought disorder**. Tests must be adapted to intelligence and educational level. Aphasia or poor comprehension affects other items.
- **Screening, not diagnosis:** positive results need fuller cognitive examination and neuropsychology.

### Beyond the MMSE [1]
- **Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination (ACE):**
  - about 20 minutes; **100 points**: orientation 10, attention 8, memory 35, verbal fluency 14, language 28, visuospatial 5;
  - **includes the MMSE items**, plus name-and-address learning, semantic memory, naming of 12 drawings, reading regular and irregular words, a wire cube and a clock;
  - distinguishes "functional" from "organic" disorders reasonably well; **screening cut-off 88**.
- **FAB, EXIT25, CLOX** for executive function.
- **Formal neuropsychology** for early cases, focal vs diffuse disorder, and organic vs non-organic disorder.

**Conclusion.** The MMSE (Folstein 1975) is a brief (5–10 minute), reliable 30-point screen covering orientation, registration, attention (serial sevens or spelling backwards), recall, naming, commands, writing and pentagon copying. Scores below 24 usually indicate impairment. It is widely used to screen, grade and monitor dementia, and is embedded in the CAMCOG, CERAD and ACE. However:
- it is weak on **memory depth and executive function**;
- it is confounded by **education and intelligence**;
- it is a poor predictor of individual **function**;
- it is a screening rather than a diagnostic tool.
