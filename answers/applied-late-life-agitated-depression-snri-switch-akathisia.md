## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 56-year-old married housewife, by nature organised, meticulous and rule-bound, with well-controlled hypertension on losartan.
- **Current episode (2 months, worse for 2 weeks):**
  - low mood and **all-day worry**;
  - **restless wandering** and continuous lamenting;
  - **fear of mishaps** to relatives;
  - **death wishes**;
  - **total insomnia**, no appetite, self-neglect, unable to work.
- **Past:** one episode **30 years ago** that lasted **14–15 months** despite treatment, followed by full recovery off medication.
- **Treatment course:**
  1. **SSRI plus benzodiazepine:** no response at 4 weeks.
  2. **SNRI:** moderate improvement at 3 weeks, then **talkativeness, singing**, wanting to dance, **suspicion of her husband's fidelity**, and **voices** describing his misdeeds.
  3. **Lithium plus olanzapine:** then **extreme restlessness, unable to stand still**, repeatedly asking the same questions and **checking the door**.

### Stage 1: Severe agitated depressive episode
- Recurrent severe depression with **agitation, anxious ruminative worry and death wishes**.
- Assess **suicide risk**, nutrition and hydration.
- The **long previous episode** points to a severe, possibly treatment-resistant course.

### Stage 2: Antidepressant-emergent mania with psychotic features
- Elation, pressured talk and disinhibition, with **delusions of infidelity and auditory hallucinations**, emerging on the SNRI.
- **This reclassifies the illness as bipolar.** The earlier depressive episodes are best regarded as **bipolar depression**.
- **Source points [1]:**
  - "unopposed" antidepressants carry a risk of **switching and rapid cycling** in bipolar depression;
  - **venlafaxine may be more likely to induce a switch** to mania than other antidepressants.
- **Correct steps, which were taken [1, Figure 2.1]:**
  - **stop the antidepressant**;
  - start an **antipsychotic** (symptoms severe and psychotic) with **lithium**: combination treatment is more effective and quicker than either alone;
  - consider a short-term benzodiazepine (lorazepam or clonazepam).

### Stage 3: Extreme restlessness, repetitive questioning, checking
**Most likely: akathisia [1].**
- Core features: **mental unease and dysphoria with restlessness**, a **compulsion to move**, pacing, inability to stay still.
- **Distressing**, with a postulated link to **suicidal ideation**.
- Consider olanzapine (rapid dose increase or a high dose) and the combination. **Olanzapine carries a lower akathisia liability** than haloperidol or lurasidone, but the risk rises with dose.

**Differentials to exclude:**
- **agitation from persisting or mixed affective symptoms**;
- anxiety;
- the **repetitive checking and questioning**: explore whether this reflects the anxious, anankastic premorbid personality under stress, perseveration, or **cognitive impairment or delirium** (check lithium levels);
- **lithium toxicity**: check the plasma level. Maudsley advises **care with losartan** and other angiotensin II receptor antagonists alongside lithium. ACE inhibitors raise lithium levels, and in the elderly they increase the risk of hospitalisation for lithium toxicity **sevenfold**.

### Management of akathisia (Maudsley stepwise programme) [1]
1. **Reduce the antipsychotic dose**, avoid polypharmacy, or **slow the titration**.
2. If ineffective, **switch to a lower-liability antipsychotic** (quetiapine, which has minimal akathisia risk), at the lowest effective dose.
3. **Propranolol 30–80 mg/day** (start 10 mg three times a day). Note contraindications: asthma, bradycardia, **hypotension**. She is on an antihypertensive, so monitor BP.
4. **Mirtazapine 15 mg/day**, trazodone 50 mg or mianserin 30 mg (5-HT2A antagonists).
5. **Antimuscarinic** (benzatropine). Weak support, and **cognitive adverse effects**, which matter at 56.
6. Cyproheptadine 16 mg/day; a benzodiazepine (short-term, dependence risk); clonidine.
- **2024 meta-analysis:** mirtazapine, biperiden and **vitamin B6** were most effective. B6 had the best efficacy–tolerability balance.

### Ongoing bipolar management [1]
- **Maintain an anti-manic agent.** **Quetiapine** is both anti-manic and an established treatment for bipolar depression without switching. It is a sensible switch if olanzapine-related akathisia persists.
- **Lithium:** keep levels therapeutic (1.0–1.2 mmol/L for the acute episode). It reduces suicidality.
- **Avoid unopposed antidepressants** in future depressive episodes. Use olanzapine–fluoxetine, quetiapine, lurasidone or lamotrigine instead.
- **ECT** is an option if depressive or psychotic symptoms prove refractory [1, Psychotic depression].

**Conclusion.**
- Recurrent severe agitated depression, with an **antidepressant (SNRI)-induced switch** to psychotic mania, which **establishes bipolar disorder**.
- The new extreme restlessness on olanzapine and lithium is most likely **akathisia**.
- Manage it by reducing or switching the antipsychotic (quetiapine), then propranolol or mirtazapine.
- Check lithium levels and rule out toxicity or a mixed state.
- Long term: mood stabiliser or antipsychotic prophylaxis, and avoid unopposed antidepressants.

## In depth

### Akathisia risk by antipsychotic (2023 dose–response meta-analysis) [1]
- **Minimal:** sertindole, quetiapine.
- **High:** haloperidol, lurasidone.
- **Partial agonists:** highest with cariprazine, lowest with brexpiprazole.
- **Mitigate by:** avoiding high doses, polypharmacy and rapid dose increases.
