## Exam answer

**Definition.** Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a **multisystem and often long-lasting disorder** that causes substantial morbidity and can severely impair health and well-being [1].

**Burden** [1]:
- An estimated **836,000–2.5 million** people are affected in the USA.
- Patients are typically unable to perform their normal activities. Up to **one-quarter are homebound or bedridden**, sometimes for long periods.
- **Most affected patients are probably never diagnosed.** Those who are diagnosed report long delays.

**The clinical problem** [1]:
- ME/CFS is poorly accepted and poorly understood, and its diagnostic features are contested.
- Patients' concerns are often met with scepticism or dismissal.
- Clinicians face competing research definitions that are complex and hard to use in practice.
- **Diagnosis matters:** effective symptom management is often available, whereas **the wrong interventions can make symptoms worse**.

### IOM 2015 diagnostic criteria: systemic exertion intolerance disease (SEID) [1]
**All three required:**
1. **A substantial reduction or impairment** in pre-illness occupational, educational, social or personal activity. It must:
   - persist for **more than 6 months**;
   - come with fatigue that is often profound;
   - be of **new or definite onset** (not lifelong);
   - **not result from ongoing excessive exertion**;
   - **not be substantially relieved by rest**.
2. **Post-exertional malaise.**
3. **Unrefreshing sleep.**

**Plus at least one of:**
- **cognitive impairment**; or
- **orthostatic intolerance**.

**Qualifier:**
- **Severity:** assess the frequency and severity of symptoms.
- **When to question the diagnosis:** if symptoms are not present at least **half the time** with moderate, substantial or severe intensity.

### Why the new name (SEID)? [1]
- **Core feature:** **any kind of exertion (physical, cognitive or emotional) can adversely affect patients**, often severely and for a long time.
- **"Myalgic encephalomyelitis"** does not describe the disorder.
- **"Chronic fatigue syndrome"** fails to convey its complexity, and patients feel it leads clinicians to belittle or dismiss the illness.
- **New code:** the IOM proposed a new ICD code to distinguish SEID from earlier definitions. Patients meeting the new criteria should be diagnosed with SEID even if they also meet older ME/CFS criteria.
- **Diagnostic support:** the IOM produced a diagnostic algorithm and checklists covering history, examination and supporting tests. It called for efficient, evidence-based tools for clinicians.
- **Comorbidity:** patients often have comorbid conditions that must be identified and treated.

### Place in the functional somatic syndrome spectrum [2]
- **Classification:** CFS is one of the **functional somatic syndromes (FSS)**. It is a *purely descriptive* label, unlike IBS or fibromyalgia, which are attributed to one organ system.
- **Evidence base:** CFS is the **third most studied FSS**, after IBS and fibromyalgia.
- **Shared features:** FSS share psychobehavioural features such as health anxiety and bodily checking, and a wide severity spectrum.

### Differential diagnosis (from the sources)
- **Hypersomnolence disorder** [3]: tiredness in CFS is not necessarily relieved by more sleep and is unrelated to sleep quantity or quality.
- **Adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease)** [4]: depression and CFS are its differentials. A low early-morning cortisol (<3 µg/dL) and a rapid response to steroid replacement distinguish it.
- **Neurasthenia-spectrum conditions** [3]: in the DSM-5-TR glossary entry on *shenjing shuairuo* (neurasthenia), CFS is listed as closely related, with brain fag syndrome and burnout. Related idioms include the Indian *ashaktapanna*. DSM-5-TR lists major depressive, persistent depressive, generalised anxiety and somatic symptom disorders as related DSM conditions.

### Management evidence [2]
- **Few systematic reviews:** surprisingly few since 2007.
- **CBT and exercise therapy:** **low to moderate evidence of efficacy**.
- **Other treatments:** for most, no qualifying reviews exist.
- **General approach for FSS and bodily distress:** reframe the clinical problem **from cure to care and coping**, and from purely biomedical explanations to a broader biopsychosocial view.

### Research gaps [1]
- Most studies compare small patient samples with healthy controls, not with other fatiguing disorders.
- Funding has been limited.
- The cause, course modifiers and effective therapies remain unknown. Identifying subsets may improve diagnosis and treatment.

**Conclusion.** ME/CFS (SEID) is a disabling multisystem disorder defined by IOM 2015 criteria. Its hallmark is **post-exertional malaise**, together with more than 6 months of substantially reduced function, unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive impairment or orthostatic intolerance. It is under-diagnosed, and the wrong interventions can worsen it. Evidence for CBT and exercise therapy is low to moderate.

## In depth

### Critical appraisal (from the sources)
- **Contested definitions:** competing research definitions complicate diagnosis [1].
- **Naming controversy:** the name affects how clinicians and patients view the illness [1].
- **Thin evidence base:** reviews of treatment are scarce, and evidence for CBT and exercise therapy is only low to moderate [2].
