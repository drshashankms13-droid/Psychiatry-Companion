## Exam answer

### Introduction

Gender-affirmation procedures are social, medical and surgical interventions that help align a person's physical characteristics or social presentation with their gender identity. Not every transgender or gender-diverse person wants or requires medical intervention.

### Categories

1.  **Social affirmation:** name, pronouns, clothing, hairstyle and
    social role.
2.  **Hormone therapy:** testosterone for masculinisation; oestrogen
    with or without androgen suppression for feminisation, when
    clinically appropriate.
3.  **Surgical procedures:** chest reconstruction or breast
    augmentation; genital procedures such as metoidioplasty,
    phalloplasty or vaginoplasty; facial and voice-related procedures.
4.  **Other interventions:** hair removal, voice therapy and
    speech/communication support.

### Assessment and informed consent

Discuss goals, expected effects, limitations, risks, fertility, sexual health, medical history and capacity. Use an individualised approach. Gender diversity is not a psychiatric disorder.

### Mental health professional's role

Provide affirming support, assess relevant comorbidity and decision-making capacity, facilitate informed consent and coordinate care. Mental illness alone is not an automatic contraindication.

### Outcomes and risks

Many people experience improved gender congruence and quality of life. Hormones and surgeries have specific medical risks and require monitoring. Surgical complications, fertility effects and need for revision should be discussed.

### Indian context

Care should be consistent with constitutional rights and applicable law, including NALSA 2014 and the Transgender Persons Act 2019 and Rules 2020.

### Conclusion

Gender-affirmation procedures are diverse and individually chosen. Ethical care is based on autonomy, accurate information, safety and respect.
