## Exam answer

**Definition [1].**
- Psycho-oncology is the study of the **psychological aspects of cancer along the continuum from prevention to cure**.
- It covers:
  - the psychological response of **patients, families and health-care providers**;
  - the psychological, behavioural and social factors that influence **cancer risk, detection and survival**.
- It emerged from **1975** as a subspecialty of oncology, psychiatry and psychosomatic medicine.

**Milestones [1]:**
- American Cancer Society 1913; NCI 1937.
- Truth-telling became common by the early 1970s.
- Psycho-oncology units appeared in the 1980s.
- British Psychosocial Oncology Group 1983; **IPOS 1984**; Japanese society 1985.
- Journals: *Journal of Psychosocial Oncology* (1982), *Psycho-Oncology* (1992).
- The **IOM (2008)** stated that quality cancer care requires psycho-oncology integrated into routine practice.
- The American College of Surgeons Commission on Cancer now requires accredited centres to run **psychosocial distress screening**.

### Normal psychological response to the diagnosis [1]
1. **Shock and disbelief:** usually under 1 week.
2. **Turmoil:** mixed anxiety and depression, irritability, insomnia, poor concentration and poor function. Usually 1–2 weeks, and resolves with support and a treatment plan that offers hope.
3. **Adaptation:** the patient returns to previous coping strategies.

- **Core fears:** death, dependency, disfigurement, disability and abandonment, plus disruption of relationships, roles and finances.
- **How the news is broken** shapes the patient's beliefs, emotions and attitude to staff; guidelines exist.
- Recurrence or treatment failure produces a similar upheaval, resolving over **7–10 days**.

**Factors in adaptation [1]:**
- **Society-derived:** stigma; informed-consent burden; monitoring for recurrence.
- **Patient-derived:**
  - intrapersonal: character style, coping, ego strength, life stage and meaning;
  - interpersonal: social support;
  - socioeconomic status.
- **Cancer-derived:** stage, symptoms, site, prognosis, treatment and functional impact.
- **Myth to correct:** well-designed studies do **not** confirm that stress, depression, personality or life events cause cancer.

### Epidemiology of psychiatric disorder [1]
- About **50%** of cancer patients have a psychiatric disorder:
  - **more than two-thirds are adjustment disorders**;
  - **10–15% major depression**;
  - **about 10% delirium**.
- **Inpatients:** depression 20–45%; delirium 15% rising to 75% with advancing disease.
- Major depression and delirium remain **under-diagnosed and under-treated**.
- **Symptom burden in advanced cancer**:
  - fatigue 74%, worry 71%, sadness 66%, pain 63%, drowsiness 61%, insomnia 54%;
  - five of the top seven symptoms are psychological.
- **Screening:** the **NCCN Distress Thermometer**.

### Principles of assessment and management [1]
- **"Therapeutic activism":** several modalities used at once for rapid relief, because cancer is increasingly seen as a **traumatic event**.
- Address the **family and the family–staff interface**, at all stages.
- **Countertransference:** resist distancing and guard against over-involvement. Do not let distress or barriers (for example, a tracheostomy) prevent a full evaluation.
- **Assessment areas:**
  1. Standard psychiatric history plus the **meaning of the illness** and the **understanding of prognosis**: what the patient and family are told and what they absorb (discrepancies cause distress).
  2. The **impact of medical factors** on mental state.
  3. The **environment**: family and its interface with the health system.

**Management modalities:**
- **Psychopharmacology:**
  - start an aetiological work-up (electrolytes, offending drugs) but **give symptomatic relief without waiting**;
  - relieve **pain and insomnia**;
  - avoid habituating hypnotics and antihistamines;
  - use low-dose **mirtazapine or trazodone** in depression, and low-dose olanzapine, risperidone or quetiapine at night for mild confusion or anxiety.
- **Psychotherapy:**
  - CBT, crisis intervention, problem-solving, group and supportive therapy all help. Brief structured interventions are efficient, with benefit at 1 year;
  - dynamic therapy when personal issues overshadow the cancer;
  - episodic use is **not resistance**;
  - face both a shortened life and death itself; accept ambivalence about death;
  - offer bereavement counselling.
- **Meaning-centred psychotherapy (MCP; Breitbart):** based on **Frankl** (and Yalom); individual and group formats. RCTs support it for existential and spiritual distress and depression in **advanced cancer**.
- **Family-focused therapy and caregiver groups.**
- **Patient groups:** education, mutual support and validation. A survival benefit in breast cancer was **not replicated**, but **QoL, coping, self-esteem and pain** benefits are not in question.
- **Behavioural techniques** (hypnosis, relaxation, desensitisation, distraction): for procedural anxiety and **anticipatory nausea and vomiting** (25–30% of chemotherapy patients).
- **Telepsychiatry and the Internet:** explore the patient's online research without giving up the professional stance.
- **Social work** (triage, resources), **chaplaincy** (up to 80% believe in God; faith can be a source of strength or guilt; this is not spiritual counselling), **wellness programmes and volunteers**.

### Major psychiatric syndromes [1]
**Delirium:**
- the **commonest serious** neuropsychiatric complication: **25–85%** depending on stage, and up to **85% in the terminally ill**;
- usually multifactorial (3 or more causes); **irreversible in 30–40%**;
- **two-thirds are hypoactive or mixed**, and are often misdiagnosed as depression;
- **54% recall** the episode; delusions are the main source of distress;
- screen with **MDAS** (cut-off 13; **7/30** in palliative care) or DRS-R-98;
- **treatment:**
  - correct causes; non-drug measures;
  - **haloperidol is the gold standard** (1–3 mg/day; rarely more than 20 mg in 24 hours); chlorpromazine when sedation is needed;
  - atypicals (risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole) are comparable;
  - **lorazepam alone is ineffective and can worsen delirium** (Breitbart);
  - monitor QTc with IV haloperidol.
- **Terminal delirium:** limit investigations to simple, low-burden ones. About **50% of episodes are reversible** (dehydration, drugs).

**Anxiety:**
- the **commonest response** to cancer;
- **phobias** (needles, MRI) and **anticipatory nausea and vomiting**;
- organic causes: PE, hypoxia, hypoglycaemia, hormone-secreting tumours, steroids, bronchodilators, **akathisia** from antiemetics, **nicotine withdrawal**;
- treat with benzodiazepines, antidepressants, atypicals, psychotherapy, MCP and relaxation.

**Trauma and stressor-related disorders:**
- **adjustment disorder is the largest group** (19.4% in oncology settings);
- **PTSD** up to **37%** in non-Hodgkin lymphoma survivors (median 12.9 years); intrusions are future-oriented fears rather than flashbacks; approach posttraumatic denial cautiously;
- **cancer-related PTS** is milder;
- avoidance of clinics can interfere with care.

**Depression:**
- prevalence varies 1–50%; meta-analysis: **25%** depressive or adjustment disorders, 29% all mood disorders;
- **high-risk sites:** **pancreas** (cytokine-serotonergic mechanism), head and neck, breast, lung;
- **drugs:** steroids, interferon, IL-2, vinca alkaloids, procarbazine, asparaginase, tamoxifen, cyproterone;
- **diagnosis:** focus on **dysphoria, anhedonia, hopelessness, worthlessness, guilt and suicidal ideation** rather than vegetative symptoms;
  - four approaches: **inclusive** (best for clinical work), exclusive (research), etiological (DSM-5), substitutive;
  - exclude **delirium** first if psychotic features are present.
- **Management:**
  - alliance (the oncologist is key) and family support;
  - treat reversible causes but **start antidepressants concurrently**;
  - **SSRIs first-line** (sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram have fewer interactions; citalopram max 20 mg if over 60);
  - **SNRIs** also treat neuropathic pain;
  - **mirtazapine** for insomnia, weight loss and nausea (orally disintegrating tablet);
  - **bupropion** for fatigue (caution with CNS tumours);
  - **psychostimulants** (methylphenidate, dextroamphetamine; modafinil) for **rapid onset** when survival is short;
  - TCAs are mainly used as adjuvant analgesics; avoid MAOIs and vortioxetine;
  - psychotherapy: supportive, CBT, **MCP**, supportive-expressive and cognitive-existential groups;
  - **ECT** for refractory depression, severe weight loss, psychosis or high suicide risk (caution with CNS tumours).

**Suicide:**
- **relative risk 12.6 in the first week** after diagnosis and **3.1 in the first year**;
- risk factors:
  - depression, **hopelessness**, **uncontrolled pain**, extreme fatigue, delirium;
  - substance misuse; isolation;
  - **loss of control or dignity**; financial strain;
  - family history; previous attempts;
  - head and neck, lung, breast, urogenital, GI cancers and myeloma; older age; male sex;
- **suicidal ideation in advanced disease should not be viewed as rational**; look for treatable depression;
- **asking does not increase risk**;
- manage with:
  - a supportive relationship and symptom control;
  - family involvement;
  - hospitalisation if needed;
  - respecting a competent patient's right to refuse treatment.

**Bipolar disorder and steroids:**
- steroid-induced mood disorders occur in **28–55%**, usually within 2 weeks, at any dose, even during taper;
- lithium risks: vomiting, hypercalcaemia, cisplatin nephrotoxicity, cranial irradiation, **nephrogenic diabetes insipidus**.

**Cancer-related fatigue:**
- more distressing than pain or nausea;
- **separate it from depression**: hopelessness, guilt and suicidality point to depression;
- screen 0–10 and treat reversible causes (anaemia, hypothyroidism, sleep, drugs);
- exercise and psychosocial interventions;
- **methylphenidate** (with a large placebo effect), modafinil; antidepressants only if depressed; steroids for the terminally ill.

**Others:**
- **Personality disorder:** staff conflict; treat comorbidity; manage counter-transference.
- **Schizophrenia:** delayed presentation; capacity is situation-specific.
- **SUD:**
  - **alcohol and nicotine withdrawal** after admission;
  - smoking cessation alters CYP1A2 levels;
  - long-acting opioids with toxicology and contingency plans.
- **Somatoform disorders:** collaborative limit-setting.

### Psychosocial management issues across the illness [1]
- **Communication:** balance prognosis against **hope**. Patients often ask the psychiatrist to clarify treatment.
- **Sexuality:**
  - an often hidden concern;
  - prostate, bladder and rectal surgery cause erectile dysfunction;
  - treatment-induced menopause; body image in breast cancer.
- **Survivors:**
  - symptom levels about 1 SD above the norm;
  - **43.6%** of childhood cancer survivors report impairment;
  - delayed consequences:
    - infertility;
    - endocrine failure after radiation;
    - fear of second malignancy;
    - **employment and insurance discrimination**.
- **Predictors of poor coping:** previous psychiatric problems, **poor social support**, low education and income, advanced stage.
- **Genetic risk and screening:** distress around testing.
- **Ethics:**
  - the psychiatrist **removes inappropriate psychiatric labels** and separates ethical from psychiatric issues;
  - **truth-telling** varies by culture;
  - informed-consent burden can decompensate vulnerable patients;
  - patients in RCTs need to believe their doctor thinks the trial is good for them.
- **Family:**
  - **reciprocity**: caregiver distress may equal or exceed the patient's and worsen over time;
  - family tasks: support, decisions, care, money, stability, adaptation;
  - 25–30% of caregivers lose jobs;
  - "**conspiracy of silence**" in acute phases; isolation in chronic phases;
  - high-risk families can be identified early and helped.
- **Staff stress and support:**
  - toxic treatments, ambiguity, projected emotions, isolation;
  - organisational support (team approach, realistic leaders), individual support and staff groups;
  - communication-skills workshops.
- **Psychosocial factors and survival:**
  - **social support** and social class affect survival through access, screening and lifestyle;
  - **no good evidence** that attitude ("fighting spirit") changes survival;
  - over-emphasis on it **blames the victim**;
  - interventions are justified by **quality of life**.
- **Complementary and alternative medicine:**
  - 48–66% of breast cancer patients use it;
  - encourage disclosure; support harmless modalities (massage, meditation, yoga);
  - warn about herbs (St John's wort).

**Conclusion.** Psycho-oncology integrates a psychiatrist into the oncology team to:
- screen for distress;
- treat delirium, depression, anxiety and adjustment disorders aggressively;
- use evidence-based psychotherapies such as MCP;
- support families and staff;
- navigate ethical issues.

Its benefits are firmly established for quality of life rather than survival.

## In depth

### Tumour-site and treatment syndromes [1]
- **Breast cancer:**
  - depression twice as common as in women generally, especially in year 1;
  - **hot flushes** in about two-thirds; venlafaxine reduces them in a dose-related way (37.5–150 mg: 37–61% vs 27% on placebo);
  - **tamoxifen:** avoid strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (fluoxetine, paroxetine) and prefer **venlafaxine or citalopram**.
- **Pancreatic cancer:** depression may precede diagnosis (cytokine-mediated).
- **Chemotherapy neurotoxicity:**
  - acute encephalopathy: steroids, methotrexate, cisplatin, vincristine, asparaginase, procarbazine, ifosfamide, interferons, IL-2;
  - chronic encephalopathy: methotrexate, BCNU, Ara-C.
- **Delirium-causing chemotherapy:** most regimens produce CNS effects mainly when dexamethasone or prednisone is included.

### Four perspectives on suicidal thoughts [1]
1. Transient thoughts, common in many patients.
2. Remission with good prognosis. Half of the cancer patients among Finnish suicides were in remission.
3. Poor prognosis or poor symptom control.
4. Terminal stage.

### Cancer in the psychotherapist [1]
- Set a **fixed termination date** to transfer patients while still well.
- Denial may make this hard; colleagues' help is invaluable.
