## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Lithium has a **narrow therapeutic index**. **Both reversible and irreversible neurotoxicity are recognised adverse effects** [1, p279].
- Therapeutic levels: **0.6–0.8 mmol/L** for prophylaxis (0.4–1.0 overall; 0.8–1.0 for mania).
- **Toxic effects reliably occur above 1.5 mmol/L** [1].

### Presentation [1, pp282–283]

| Plasma lithium | Features |
| --- | --- |
| **Therapeutic range** (dose-related adverse effects) | **Fine tremor** (propranolol helps), GI upset, polyuria/polydipsia, metallic taste |
| **>1.5 mmol/L** | **GI:** increasing anorexia, nausea, **diarrhoea**. **CNS:** **muscle weakness, drowsiness, confusion, ataxia, coarse tremor, muscle twitching** |
| **>2 mmol/L** | **Increasing disorientation, seizures**, progressing to **coma and death** |

- **Levels are only a guide:** individual susceptibility varies.
- **Neurotoxicity can occur at "normal" plasma levels**, because **brain lithium may not be reflected by the plasma concentration** [1].
- **Key clinical shift:** from a *fine* to a ***coarse*** tremor, with ataxia, confusion and GI upset, should prompt an urgent level check.

### Precipitants and risk factors [1, 2]

**1. Altered sodium handling** (the main mechanism) [1]:
- **low-salt diet**;
- **dehydration** (vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, heat);
- **hyponatraemia** from any cause, including drug-induced (SSRIs, carbamazepine, antipsychotics) [1];
- **Addison's disease** and other uncommon illnesses.

**2. Pharmacokinetic interactions (Maudsley Table 2.2)** [1]:

| Drug | Effect on lithium level | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ACE inhibitors** (and angiotensin II receptor antagonists) | Unpredictable; **up to fourfold** rise. **Sevenfold rise in hospitalisation for toxicity in the elderly** | Develops over weeks |
| **Thiazide diuretics** | **25–400%** rise | Within **10 days** |
| Loop diuretics | Safer; risk greatest in the first month | — |
| **NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors** (including over-the-counter) | From 10% to more than fourfold | Days to months |

**3. Pharmacodynamic neurotoxicity at normal levels** [1]:
- **Antipsychotics:** neurotoxicity can rarely result from co-prescription.
- **Carbamazepine:** rare reports, mostly with high lithium levels. Carbamazepine-induced hyponatraemia may cause lithium retention.
- **SSRIs:** rare reports of CNS toxicity (they can also cause hyponatraemia).
- **Clozapine:** with lithium, there is an **increased risk of myoclonus, ataxia and seizures** [1, p269].
- **ECT:** lithium may increase the risk of **post-ECT delirium and confusion**. It also potentiates neuromuscular blockers, and thiopentone or propofol with lithium lowers seizure threshold [1, p384].

**4. Patient factors:**
- elderly;
- **established chronic kidney disease**;
- **heart failure** (with ACE inhibitors);
- hypertension and bipolar disorder (more CKD) [1].

**5. Endocrine** [1, 2]:
- **Hypothyroidism:** OH 8–19%; up to 20% in middle-aged women.
- **Hyperparathyroidism and hypercalcaemia:** about 4% [1]; about 10% [2].
- **Why they matter:** both produce neuropsychiatric symptoms that can be confused with toxicity. **Hypercalcaemia** should be considered with atypical psychopathology or non-response [2].

### Management of toxicity [1]
- **Suspected toxicity:** check the lithium level urgently and stop or withhold lithium.
- **More severe symptoms:** **osmotic or forced alkaline diuresis in a medical facility**.
- **Above 3 mmol/L:** **peritoneal or haemodialysis** is often used.

### Prevention [1, 2]
1. **Start only with clear intent** to continue for years and reasonable adherence. Abrupt discontinuation causes rebound mania.
2. **Pre-treatment tests:**
   - **eGFR, U&Es (including calcium), TFTs**;
   - ECG if cardiovascular risk;
   - weight;
   - contraception advice (teratogen).
3. **Prescribing:**
   - start **400 mg at night (200 mg in the elderly)**;
   - level after 7 days and 7 days after every dose change;
   - **sample 12 hours post-dose**;
   - **specify the strength of liquid preparations** (5.4 or 10.8 mmol/5 mL) to avoid toxic or sub-therapeutic dosing.
4. **Target the lowest effective level:** 0.6–0.8 mmol/L. Levels above 0.8 mmol/L raise renal toxicity risk.
5. **Monitoring:**
   - plasma lithium, eGFR, U&Es and TFTs **every 6 months**;
   - **more often** in the elderly, with interacting drugs, or with CKD or other illness;
   - **TSH at baseline and at least 6-monthly**; calcium as well [2].
6. **Educate patients at initiation, and repeatedly,** about:
   - toxicity symptoms;
   - **avoiding dehydration and salt restriction**;
   - **over-the-counter NSAIDs**;
   - telling other prescribers they take lithium.
7. **Avoid or carefully manage interacting drugs** (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, thiazides, NSAIDs). If unavoidable, take extra levels (e.g., within the first month of a diuretic). Prescribe NSAIDs regularly, not intermittently.
8. **With clozapine:** monitor for myoclonus, ataxia and seizures. Use clozapine alone if neurotoxicity is a concern [1].
9. **With ECT:** some suggest stopping lithium **48 hours** before. In the UK, ECT often continues with a low stimulus and close monitoring [1].
10. **Stopping:** taper over at least 1 month (preferably 3), with reductions of no more than 0.2 mmol/L at a time.

**Conclusion.** Lithium neurotoxicity:
- **presents as** GI upset with coarse tremor, ataxia, weakness, twitching, drowsiness and confusion (usually above 1.5 mmol/L), progressing to seizures, coma and death (above 2 mmol/L);
- **can occur at therapeutic plasma levels** and may be irreversible;
- **is mostly precipitated** by dehydration, sodium loss and interacting drugs (ACE inhibitors, thiazides, NSAIDs), and occasionally by antipsychotics, carbamazepine, SSRIs, clozapine or ECT;
- **is prevented by** careful dosing and 12-hour levels, 6-monthly renal, thyroid and calcium monitoring, avoiding interacting drugs, and repeated patient education.

## In depth

### Relationship to the therapeutic benefits [1]
- **Neuroprotection:** lithium promotes hippocampal neurogenesis. A meta-analysis suggests it may prevent transition to dementia, though the largest study found no benefit.
- **The paradox:** the same drug carries recognised reversible and irreversible neurotoxicity.
