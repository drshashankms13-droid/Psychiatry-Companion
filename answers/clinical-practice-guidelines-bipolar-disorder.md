## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) are systematically developed statements that help clinicians and patients decide on appropriate care. For bipolar disorder the major guidelines are **NICE CG185 (UK, 2014, updated 2023)**, **BAP (British Association for Psychopharmacology, 3rd ed. 2016)**, **CANMAT/ISBD (Canada and International Society for Bipolar Disorders, 2018, updated 2023)**, **RANZCP (Australia/New Zealand, 2020)**, **WFSBP** and the **Indian Psychiatric Society (IPS) CPGs** [1]. They largely agree on core principles but differ in how they rank individual drugs — differences that reflect evidence gaps, methods and local context.

### Points of agreement across guidelines [1]

| Phase | Consensus recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Acute mania | **Stop antidepressants**; antipsychotic or mood stabiliser; **combination** (antipsychotic + lithium/valproate) more effective and faster than monotherapy, especially if relapse occurs on a mood stabiliser; short-term benzodiazepine for agitation; lamotrigine has no antimanic effect |
| Bipolar depression | Quetiapine, olanzapine (± fluoxetine), lurasidone, lamotrigine, cariprazine (± lumateperone); **avoid antidepressant monotherapy** |
| Maintenance | **Lithium is first line / gold standard**; continue an effective acute combination as prophylaxis; avoid long-term antidepressants |
| Valproate | **Do not use in women of child-bearing potential** |
| Rapid cycling | Withdraw antidepressants; check thyroid, alcohol; optimise and combine mood stabilisers |
| Physical health | Baseline and regular monitoring (renal, thyroid, metabolic) |
| Psychosocial | Psychoeducation, family therapy, IPSRT, CBT as adjuncts |

### Where guidelines differ

| Issue | NICE | BAP 2016 | CANMAT/ISBD 2018/2023 | RANZCP 2020 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First-line bipolar depression | **Fluoxetine + olanzapine** or **quetiapine** alone; lamotrigine second line [1] | **Lamotrigine** and **lurasidone** first line (lamotrigine needs antimanic cover) [1] | Quetiapine, lithium, lamotrigine, lurasidone, cariprazine; lurasidone first line as **adjunct**; valproate demoted to second line; olanzapine–fluoxetine second line; olanzapine alone third line; lumateperone second line [1] | Lithium, lamotrigine, valproate, quetiapine, lurasidone, cariprazine, alone or in 2–3 drug combinations including antidepressant; olanzapine and carbamazepine second line [1] |
| Maintenance | **Lithium first line**; add valproate if insufficient; if not tolerated, valproate, olanzapine or quetiapine [1] | Lithium; combinations | Lithium, quetiapine, divalproex, asenapine, aripiprazole, lamotrigine (polarity-specific) | Lithium preferred |
| Format | Pathways, cost-effectiveness | Evidence grading with expert consensus | Graded lines (first/second/third) with safety and tolerability | Clinical algorithm (mood disorders together) |

**Maudsley's practical summary of prophylaxis** [1]: first line lithium monotherapy → second line olanzapine, aripiprazole, risperidone or quetiapine with lithium/valproate → third line an alternative antipsychotic (lurasidone, asenapine, ziprasidone) or mood stabiliser (carbamazepine, lamotrigine) in combination → fourth line antipsychotic with two mood stabilisers.

### Critical evaluation

**Strengths**
- Translate a large, complex evidence base into usable steps; reduce unwarranted variation (e.g. unnecessary antidepressants).
- Repeated, systematic updating (CANMAT 2023; NICE update 2023).
- Emphasise safety (valproate in women, lithium monitoring) and physical health.
- Combine efficacy with tolerability and cost (NICE).

**Limitations**
1. **Disagreement on drug rankings** (e.g. lamotrigine, olanzapine–fluoxetine, valproate in depression) reflects reliance on **network meta-analyses**, whose results depend on inclusion criteria and can contradict head-to-head trials — BAP is sceptical of them [1].
2. **Evidence gaps:** few direct comparisons in mania ("no one drug can be recommended over another on efficacy grounds"); early lithium studies are excluded from meta-analyses because of short, crossover designs [1].
3. **Industry-sponsored, short trials** dominate; maintenance trials use enriched designs (responders to the drug), favouring newer antipsychotics.
4. **Under-weighting of lithium** in practice despite guidelines — it is underused in favour of antipsychotics [1].
5. **Limited guidance for real-world complexity:** mixed features, rapid cycling, comorbid substance use, bipolar II, older adults, pregnancy.
6. **Context:** costs, monitoring facilities (lithium levels), drug availability (lumateperone, cariprazine) and regulatory approvals differ by country.
7. **Patient preference and psychosocial care** are acknowledged but given less weight than drug sequencing.
8. **Updating lag:** NICE CG185 is described by Maudsley as "somewhat dated" [1].

### Relevance to India

- **IPS CPG for bipolar disorder (2017)** broadly follows international guidance — lithium and valproate as mood stabilisers, SGAs for mania and depression, ECT for severe or treatment-resistant episodes — while considering cost, availability of lithium monitoring and family involvement (author's knowledge — verify).
- Valproate is still widely prescribed to young women in India; guidelines on teratogenicity need active implementation.
- Lithium is inexpensive but needs reliable level and renal/thyroid monitoring — a limitation in rural settings.

**Conclusion.** Bipolar CPGs agree on the fundamentals — stop antidepressants in mania, use combinations when needed, avoid antidepressant monotherapy in bipolar depression, keep lithium as the maintenance gold standard and avoid valproate in women of child-bearing potential. Their differences reflect weak comparative evidence and methodological choices more than true clinical disagreement. They should be used as frameworks adapted to the individual patient, local resources and patient preference, not as rigid algorithms.

## In depth

### Background — what makes a good guideline

- Developed by multidisciplinary panels, using systematic reviews, explicit grading (e.g. GRADE), external review and regular updating; assessed with the **AGREE II** instrument (author's knowledge).
- Guidelines differ in evidence grading: CANMAT uses levels of evidence plus clinical support (safety, tolerability, switch risk); NICE includes cost-effectiveness; BAP mixes evidence categories with expert consensus.

### Acute mania — detailed recommendations [1]

Stop antidepressant → **if not on antimanic drug:** antipsychotic (severe/disturbed) or valproate (not in women of child-bearing potential) or lithium (if future adherence likely); if response inadequate after 1–2 weeks, combine antipsychotic with lithium or valproate → **if already on antimanic drug:** check adherence and levels; raise lithium to 1.0–1.2 mmol/L or valproate up to 125 mg/L; add an antipsychotic → **all patients:** consider short-term lorazepam or clonazepam.

**Suggested antimanic doses** [1]: lithium 400 mg/day increasing every 3–4 days by level; valproate 250 mg three times daily or 20–30 mg/kg loading; olanzapine 10–20 mg; risperidone 2–6 mg; quetiapine IR up to 800 mg; aripiprazole 15–30 mg; asenapine 5–10 mg twice daily; cariprazine 3–12 mg; haloperidol 5–15 mg; lorazepam up to 4 mg; clonazepam up to 8 mg.

- Network meta-analysis: olanzapine, risperidone, haloperidol and quetiapine have the best combination of efficacy and acceptability; olanzapine more effective than lithium and valproate as monotherapy (Cochrane); a 2024 network meta-analysis ranked tamoxifen most effective [1].
- Other options: clozapine for refractory mania; allopurinol, celecoxib, levetiracetam, endoxifen, oxcarbazepine with limited evidence [1].

### Bipolar depression — evidence notes [1]

- Bipolar depression episodes are more rapid in onset, more frequent, more severe, shorter and more often psychotic or with reverse vegetative features than unipolar depression; they account for most time ill; about 15% of people with bipolar disorder die by suicide.
- Largest network meta-analysis (101 RCTs, 2023): olanzapine + fluoxetine most effective, then quetiapine, olanzapine, lurasidone, lumateperone, cariprazine, lamotrigine.
- Lithium probably effective but weak acute evidence; strong prevention of depression.
- Aripiprazole, risperidone, ziprasidone, most TCAs and MAOIs probably not effective.

### Maintenance — evidence notes [1]

| Drug | Prevents mania | Prevents depression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lithium | Yes | Yes |
| Olanzapine, quetiapine | Yes | Yes |
| Aripiprazole, asenapine, risperidone, paliperidone | Yes | No |
| Lamotrigine | No | Yes |
| Valproate | Yes (?) | Yes |
| Antidepressants | No | No |
| Antipsychotic + mood stabiliser | Yes | Yes |

- Lithium plasma level 0.6–0.8 mmol/L (0.4–0.6 if poorly tolerated; up to 1.0 if insufficient response); elderly 0.4–0.6, not above 0.7 over 80 years (ISBD/IGSLi) [1].
- Combination with an SGA is probably the polypharmacy regimen of choice; naturalistic data favour lithium + valproate + quetiapine [1].
- STEP-BD: no benefit from continuing antidepressants, worse in rapid cyclers [1].

### Critical appraisal in more depth

- **Enriched maintenance designs** select acute responders, inflating maintenance efficacy of newer drugs compared with lithium trials.
- **Polarity index** (CANMAT) helps choose maintenance by predominant polarity, but is based on limited data.
- **Bipolar II** has much less evidence; guidelines extrapolate from bipolar I.
- **Implementation gap:** audits show suboptimal lithium monitoring and high rates of antidepressant and valproate prescribing contrary to guidance [1].

### Indian context

- IPS CPGs (Indian Journal of Psychiatry supplements) cover bipolar disorder, ECT and special populations; they stress family psychoeducation and cost.
- Generic lithium, valproate, olanzapine, quetiapine and risperidone are affordable; lurasidone and cariprazine are available; lumateperone is not widely available (author's knowledge — verify).
- **Mental Healthcare Act 2017** shapes admission and consent during mania; advance directives are useful for patients with recurrent mania.

### Recent advances

- CANMAT/ISBD 2023 evidence update [1].
- Long-acting injectable aripiprazole and risperidone for maintenance in non-adherent patients [1].
- Ketamine/esketamine in bipolar depression — low-certainty evidence [1].
- Digital monitoring and lithium response prediction (genetics, ConLiGen) under study.
