## Exam answer

**Introduction.** **Vortioxetine** (Lu AA21004; Brintellix/Trintellix) is a **"multimodal" serotonergic antidepressant** (a "serotonin modulator and stimulator").
- **Approved for MDD:** FDA 2013, EMA 2013 [general knowledge].
- It combines **serotonin reuptake inhibition** with **direct actions at several 5-HT receptors**.
- It is distinguished by **pro-cognitive effects**, a **low sexual-dysfunction burden** and **cardiac and metabolic neutrality** [1].

### Mechanism of action
**Targets** [general knowledge; affinities approximate]:
| Target | Action | Proposed consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SERT** | Inhibition (about 50% occupancy at 5 mg, above 80% at 20 mg) | ↑ synaptic 5-HT |
| **5-HT3** | **Antagonist** (highest affinity) | Disinhibits glutamate and other neurotransmitter release (via GABA interneurons); pro-cognitive. Theoretically antiemetic, yet nausea is common (attributed to SERT inhibition) |
| **5-HT7** | Antagonist | Pro-cognitive, circadian, antidepressant |
| **5-HT1D** | Antagonist | ↑ 5-HT release |
| **5-HT1B** | Partial agonist | ↑ ACh, DA, NA release; cognition |
| **5-HT1A** | **Agonist** | Antidepressant, anxiolytic; desensitisation |

**Net effects:**
- **↑ serotonin, noradrenaline, dopamine, acetylcholine, histamine and glutamate** in the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus;
- **↓ GABA inhibition**;
- enhanced **neuroplasticity** (BDNF, dendritic spine density).

These are proposed to underlie the effects on **cognition independent of mood**. Maudsley describes "potential effects on cognitive function through its mechanisms on glutamate neurotransmission and neuroplasticity in the prefrontal cortex" [1].

**Pharmacokinetics [1, general knowledge]:**
- **long half-life (about 66 hours)**, steady state in about 2 weeks;
- metabolised mainly by **CYP2D6** (also 3A4/5, 2C9 and others);
- **reduce the dose with strong 2D6 inhibitors** (bupropion, fluoxetine, paroxetine) and in poor metabolisers;
- **little inhibition** of CYP enzymes itself, so it is among the **safest for interactions** (alongside citalopram and escitalopram) [1];
- **food-independent**.

**Dose:**
- **minimum effective dose 10 mg/day** [1];
- range 5–20 mg;
- **older adults:** start at 5 mg, with caution above 10 mg (limited data) [1];
- no adjustment in renal impairment;
- little hepatic adjustment needed (limited experience) [1].

### Clinical applications
1. **Major depressive disorder** (licensed):
   - efficacy comparable to other antidepressants;
   - **the strongest evidence for switching after one failed treatment** is probably for vortioxetine (REVIVE vs agomelatine) [1];
   - **Maudsley** lists it among **third-choice options** in its depression algorithm (with mirtazapine and agomelatine) [1];
   - a 2018 network meta-analysis found **few clear advantages over older drugs** [1];
   - **maintenance**: relapse prevention shown [general knowledge].
2. **Cognitive dysfunction in depression:**
   - improvement in processing speed, executive function and attention (**DSST**), **partly independent of mood improvement** (FOCUS, CONNECT trials) [general knowledge];
   - **FDA rejected a cognitive label** (2016) [general knowledge].
3. **Depression in older people:**
   - among the most effective in a network meta-analysis (with quetiapine, duloxetine, agomelatine, imipramine);
   - effective and reasonably well tolerated [1];
   - **interaction-safe** [1].
4. **Depression with medical comorbidity:**
   - **post-stroke depression:** cognitive benefits; no cardiovascular effects; no interaction with warfarin or aspirin [1];
   - **Parkinson's disease:** improved depression, cognition, apathy, fatigue (open-label) [1];
   - **dementia/AD with depression:** open-label benefit; one RCT negative [1];
   - **diabetes:** weight- and glucose-neutral [1];
   - **epilepsy:** not proconvulsive [1];
   - **cardiac:** **no effect on QTc** [1].
5. **Sexual dysfunction-sensitive patients:**
   - lower rates than SSRIs, SNRIs, duloxetine and paroxetine; **placebo-like at 10 mg or below** [1];
   - one of the **best-evidenced switch options** for antidepressant-induced sexual dysfunction [1];
   - used in PSSD (anecdotal) [1].
6. **GAD:** licensed in some countries, but **evidence weak**. Maudsley: no clear effect over placebo, and it "may not be effective" in panic [1].
7. **Negative and cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia:** adjunctive use with small benefits; case series with high-dose lurasidone in TRS [1].
8. **Children and adolescents:** **no better than placebo** in a large paediatric trial (12–17 years). Not recommended [1].

### Adverse effects [1]
- **Nausea** (most common, dose-related, about 20–30%; usually transient) [general knowledge for %];
- vomiting, constipation, dizziness, pruritus;
- **sexual dysfunction low**;
- **no sedation, weight gain or QT effect**;
- **hyponatraemia** possible (as with all antidepressants);
- **bleeding** (intermediate SERT inhibition);
- galactorrhoea (one review suggests a probable relation);
- **no withdrawal symptoms reported** so far (long half-life), but they cannot be excluded [1];
- **safe in overdose**: 250 mg caused no symptoms [1];
- **no effect on driving** [1].

### Switching considerations [1]
- Cross-taper cautiously; limited experience.
- **Wait 3 weeks after stopping vortioxetine before starting an MAOI.**
- Take care with CYP2D6 inhibitors (bupropion, fluoxetine, paroxetine).
- Its long half-life makes it a probable exception to fluoxetine overlap issues.

### Pregnancy and breastfeeding [1]
- Limited data.
- **RID about 1.1–1.8%**; one case of infant lethargy and jitteriness after in-utero exposure.

**Conclusion.** Vortioxetine is a **multimodal antidepressant**: SERT inhibition plus 5-HT3, 5-HT7 and 5-HT1D antagonism, 5-HT1B partial agonism and 5-HT1A agonism. Its antidepressant efficacy is comparable to other drugs. Its niche is **cognitive symptoms**, **older and medically ill patients** (no QTc, weight or interaction problems), and patients troubled by **SSRI-induced sexual dysfunction**. Nausea is the main adverse effect. It is not effective in children and adolescents.

## In depth

### Background
- Developed by **Lundbeck and Takeda**.
- The "multimodal" concept: combining receptor actions with reuptake inhibition (like vilazodone, a "SPARI").

### Epidemiology / use
- Increasingly prescribed in India (generic vortioxetine available) [general knowledge].

### Classification
- **NbN:** serotonin multimodal (SERT inhibitor, 5-HT3/5-HT7/5-HT1D antagonist, 5-HT1B partial agonist, 5-HT1A agonist).
- **Group:** "serotonin modulators" with vilazodone [1].

### Mechanism in detail
- **5-HT3 antagonism** on GABA interneurons disinhibits pyramidal glutamate neurons. This increases cortical glutamate and **theta oscillations** linked to memory.
- **5-HT7 antagonism** promotes neurogenesis and regulates sleep and circadian rhythm.
- Animal models show reversal of memory deficits (object recognition) that SSRIs do not produce.

### Clinical features: patient selection
- Depression with prominent cognitive complaints ("brain fog"), in older adults, post-stroke, in Parkinson's disease, or with sexual dysfunction concerns. Also patients on multiple drugs.

### Assessment
- Cognitive measures in practice: **DSST**, Trail Making Test, PDQ-D (perceived deficits).

### Differential: comparison with other antidepressants
| Feature | Vortioxetine | SSRI | Agomelatine | Bupropion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sexual dysfunction | Low | High | Low | Low |
| Weight | Neutral | Neutral or ↑ (paroxetine) | Neutral | ↓ |
| Cognition | ↑ (evidence) | Neutral | Possible | Possible |
| Nausea | **High** | Moderate | Low | Low |
| QTc | None | Citalopram ↑ | None | None |
| Interactions | Few (substrate of 2D6) | Fluoxetine, paroxetine potent | 1A2 (fluvoxamine) | Potent 2D6 inhibitor |

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Cipriani 2018, *Lancet* NMA (cited in [1]) | Vortioxetine efficacy similar to others; good acceptability |
| REVIVE (Montgomery 2014) (general knowledge) | Switch after SSRI/SNRI failure: vortioxetine better than agomelatine |
| FOCUS (McIntyre 2014); CONNECT (Mahableshwarkar 2015) (general knowledge) | DSST improvement partly independent of mood |
| Paediatric RCT (cited in [1]) | Not better than placebo in 12–17-year-olds |
| Maudsley sexual dysfunction table | Low rates at 10 mg or below [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- **Cognitive benefit** is statistically significant but of **uncertain clinical magnitude**. Path analyses (direct vs indirect effect) are model-dependent.
- **Industry-funded** trials.
- **Cost** is higher than generic SSRIs.

### Indian context
- Available as generics. Useful in elderly and post-stroke populations given interaction safety [general knowledge].
- **TOG 2020:** vortioxetine is a **List B** drug (follow-up teleconsultation only).

### Recent advances
- Trials in **post-COVID cognitive impairment** (mixed) [general knowledge].
- **Long-term cognitive outcomes** studies.
- Combination with lurasidone in TRS (case series) [1].
