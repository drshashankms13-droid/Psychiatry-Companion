## Exam answer

**Definition.**
- In DSM-IV, BED sat under "disorders for further research" (within EDNOS). DSM-5 made it a **formal diagnosis** [1, 3].
- ICD-11 code: **6B82** [3].

**DSM-5 criteria (Table 27.4–3) [1]:**
- recurrent binge eating (a larger than normal amount, with **loss of control**) **at least once a week for 3 months**;
- with **three or more** of:
  - eating very rapidly;
  - eating until uncomfortably full;
  - eating when not hungry;
  - eating alone or hiding eating;
  - guilt or disgust afterwards;
- **no recurrent compensatory** purging, exercise or fasting;
- marked distress.

**Epidemiology [3]:**
- lifetime prevalence **1.9%**; female-to-male ratio **3.5:1**; median onset at age **23**;
- comorbid **affective and anxiety disorders**.

**Associations [1]:**
- obesity; eating in response to negative emotions (stress, boredom, anxiety, depression);
- **food addiction** is very common in disordered-eating samples (57.6% vs 16.2%);
- binge eating raises the likelihood of depression in obesity;
- night eating syndrome is a related pattern.

### Aetiology [3]
- **Heritability 39–45%.**
- **Reward circuitry:** binges are linked to excessive dopamine release and D2 downregulation (shown for BN, the binge-type disorder); craving for food stimulation.
- **Brain systems for eating** (Himmerich):
  - **self-regulatory** (PFC; 5-HT, NE, ACh);
  - **hedonic** (basal ganglia; DA, opioids, cannabinoids);
  - **homeostatic** (hypothalamus; leptin, ghrelin, insulin, GLP-1, NPY, histamine).

### Assessment [3]
- SCOFF, EDE-Q, EDI-3, BITE; screen for depression and anxiety.
- **Obesity class and recommended weight treatment** (Table 27.4–2) [1]:
  - overweight: behavioural weight management (BWM);
  - class I: BWM plus medical weight management;
  - class II: BWM plus medical management, or BWM plus weight-loss surgery;
  - class III: BWM plus weight-loss surgery.

### Management
**Psychological (first line) [1, 2]:**
- **NICE:** an evidence-based **CBT self-help programme** first, then **CBT** [2].
- **CBT is the gold standard** [1]:
  - 12–19 weekly group sessions;
  - marked improvement in binges, psychopathology and function, with modest weight loss;
  - response may appear **by the 4th session**.
- **IPT** [1]:
  - similar outcomes, but slower;
  - four focus areas: grief, role conflicts, role transitions, interpersonal deficits;
  - at **4 years**, **64.4%** remained in full remission across CBT and IPT.
- **DBT-BED** [1]:
  - targets **emotion dysregulation**: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, mindful eating, diary cards;
  - higher baseline psychopathology predicted better results;
  - avoidant PD and early-onset dieting moderated the effect.
- **ACT and mindfulness** [1]: reductions in BMI were mediated by fewer binges in one study.
- **Motivational interviewing** for ambivalence (stages of change) [1].
- **Internet and app-based self-help** works better for **BED than BN**, especially with less comorbidity [3].

**Pharmacological [2, 3]:**
- **Never the sole treatment** [2].
- **SSRIs:** a trial may be combined with other treatments [2]:
  - **fluoxetine** (effective dose **60 mg**);
  - sertraline reduces binges in BN and BED; citalopram improves BED.
- **Lisdexamfetamine:**
  - **approved for BED in the USA**; one high-quality RCT [2];
  - fewer binge days, less obsessive-compulsive binge symptoms, lower weight, higher 4-week cessation [3].
- **Topiramate:** reduces binge frequency. **Contraindicated in pregnancy** and in women of child-bearing potential without highly effective contraception [2].
- **Naltrexone plus bupropion:** better weight outcomes [3].
- **Limited evidence:** aripiprazole, duloxetine, lamotrigine, liraglutide, methylphenidate, zonisamide, acamprosate [2].
- **Phentermine–topiramate:** refused marketing authorisation. **Dasotraline:** development stopped [2].
- **Comorbid depression:** avoid or use **mirtazapine** with caution (weight gain) [2].

**Conclusion.** BED is binge eating without compensatory behaviour. Treatment:
- CBT (self-help, then therapist-led), IPT or DBT first;
- add an SSRI or lisdexamfetamine;
- manage obesity and comorbidity alongside.

## In depth

### Drugs under investigation for BED [3]
- Reboxetine, tesofensine, taranabant, peptide YY3-36, cetilistat, intranasal insulin, lorcaserin.

### Case lesson (K&S "Robin") [1]
- A 24-year-old woman with BMI 40 and occasional binges did not complete monitoring forms.
- The clinician **shifted from action to exploring ambivalence** with MI (discrepancies; pros and cons). This prevented drop-out.
