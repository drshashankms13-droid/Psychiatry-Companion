## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- Migraine is common: about **12%** of adults have an attack in a year, with a lifetime prevalence of about **16%** and a male:female ratio of about **1:3** [1]. K&S gives a lifetime prevalence of **18% in women** and **6% in men**, peaking in the 3rd–6th decades [2].
- Advances in management have followed a **new understanding of pathophysiology**: the old vascular theory (vasoconstriction causing aura, vasodilation causing headache) has been **discarded** [1].

### 1. Advances in understanding that guide treatment [1]
- **Trigeminovascular system:** meningeal nociceptors, trigeminal sensory fibres to the brainstem, and parasympathetic efferents to meningeal vessels.
- **Cortical spreading depression** (a wave of depolarisation spreading at about 2–3 mm/min) is very probably the cause of **aura**. It is shown as a wave of oligaemia (20–30% reduced flow) moving posterior to anterior over 15–45 minutes.
- **CGRP** released from trigeminal terminals causes vasodilation and **neurogenic inflammation** (plasma extravasation, dural mast-cell degranulation).
- **5HT:** ergotamine and triptans act on 5HT receptors on meningeal vessels and trigeminal endings.
- **Brainstem generator:** PET shows dorsolateral pontine activation during attacks, **persisting after sumatriptan relief**.
- **Genetics of familial hemiplegic migraine:** mutations in ion-translocation genes (a voltage-gated calcium channel on chromosome 19, the Na+/K+ pump, a neuronal sodium channel). The final common pathway is thought to be excess potassium or glutamate facilitating spreading depression. This supports a **neuronal hyperexcitability** model.
- **Central sensitisation** explains cutaneous **allodynia** in the trigeminal distribution.

### 2. Acute treatment advances
- **Triptans** (selective **5HT1B/1D agonists**) "have markedly improved control of migraine" [1]:
  - work even when given **several hours after onset**;
  - also relieve **nausea and photophobia**;
  - **non-oral routes** (nasal spray, suppository) for patients who are vomiting;
  - **sumatriptan has been followed by a range of newer agents**;
  - contraindicated in ischaemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension and pregnancy.
- **CGRP antagonists:** "an exciting new class of drugs, developed from translation of laboratory work into clinical practice". One trial showed efficacy in acute migraine (Olesen 2004) [1].
- **Evidence-based alternatives** (Tfelt-Hansen 2006): **aspirin plus metoclopramide**; **ergotamine** by nasal spray or rectally [1].
- **NSAIDs** have demonstrated efficacy in acute attacks [2].
- **Low-dose IV prochlorperazine** has been used in migraine. **Buccal prochlorperazine** is indicated in the UK for migraine-associated nausea and vomiting [3].

### 3. Prophylaxis advances
- **Indication:** 2 or more attacks a month, regularly (Bigal and Lipton 2006) [1].
- **Evidence-based, well tolerated** (Tfelt-Hansen 2006): **valproate, amitriptyline, propranolol, timolol**. **Propranolol** is the first choice [1].
- **Effective but poorly tolerated:** methysergide, pizotifen [1].
- **Best prophylactic agents in K&S:** β-blockers, **flunarizine**, valproate, **topiramate**, and **CBT** [2].
- **Buspirone** (5HT1A agonist) may reduce headache in **migraineurs with anxiety** (Lee 2005) [1].

### 4. Neuromodulation [1]
- **Occipital nerve stimulation** may help **chronic migraine** (Matharu 2004).

### 5. Psychological and integrated care [1, 2]
- **Relaxation training:** enduring improvement [1].
- **Hypnosis** to reduce tension and apprehension [1].
- **Behavioural techniques** reduce frequency and severity [1].
- **Detect and treat comorbid depression and anxiety** (Griffith and Razavi 2006) [1].
- **Chronic daily headache:** **medication plus CBT** is better than either alone [2].
- **Lifestyle:** regular sleep and meals, managing stress, dietary triggers [1].

### 6. Treating chronic or transformed migraine [1, 2]
- Recognise the **migraine–depression vicious circle** and **over-medication**.
- Treat the depression.
- Avoid **analgesic overuse and rebound headache** [2].

**Conclusion.** The move from a vascular to a **neuronal–trigeminovascular** model has produced targeted drugs: **triptans**, the emerging **CGRP antagonists**, and **neuromodulation**. Evidence-based prophylaxis (propranolol, valproate, topiramate, amitriptyline, flunarizine) and **CBT and relaxation**, with treatment of psychiatric comorbidity, complete modern management.

## In depth

### Why psychiatrists treat migraine [1]
- Depression or anxiety is **2–3 times commoner** in migraine. About **half** of patients presenting with major depression have migraine.
- **Depression at baseline** triples the risk of new-onset migraine over 2 years (Breslau 2003).
- **Sumatriptan non-responders** have higher anxiety and neuroticism (Meckling 2001).
- Interictal cognitive testing shows only subtle changes, mainly **psychomotor slowing**. There is no evidence of greater age-related decline or of AD risk (O'Bryant 2005).
