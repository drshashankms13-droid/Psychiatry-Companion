## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 72-year-old retired engineer, brought by his daughter.
- **6 months** of **inappropriate sexual gestures** towards his daughter-in-law, and **aggressive behaviour**.
- **Withdrawal:** socially active until 2 years ago, then gradually withdrew. His **wife died 2 years ago**.
- **Cognition and safety:** **lost his way** once; **fell from his scooter** 2 months ago (minor bruises).
- **Driving:** he wants to drive, but his son forbids it.

### 1. Diagnosis
- **Major neurocognitive disorder (dementia) with behavioural and psychological symptoms.** Leading possibilities:
  1. **Behavioural-variant FTD** [1]:
     - **disinhibition** (socially inappropriate behaviour), **apathy and withdrawal**;
     - possible bvFTD needs **3 of 6** features: disinhibition; apathy or inertia; loss of empathy; perseverative or compulsive behaviour; hyperorality; an executive profile with relative sparing of memory and visuospatial function;
     - **sexual symptoms are more prevalent in frontal lobe disorders** (most commonly **stroke and bvFTD**) [2];
     - against bvFTD: his **age (72)**, and **topographical disorientation** (getting lost), which suggests visuospatial or memory involvement. **Typical bvFTD onset is 45–65** [1].
  2. **Alzheimer's disease with BPSD** [1]: getting lost (visuospatial or topographical), with disinhibition and aggression as BPSD. Sexually inappropriate behaviour occurs in **2–17%** of people with dementia, across subtypes [2].
  3. **Vascular dementia** with frontal involvement [1, 2].
- **Differentials:**
  - **depression after bereavement**, with withdrawal (pseudodementia; depression as a dementia prodrome) [3];
  - **delirium** (which can cause sexual disinhibition) [2];
  - **mania**;
  - **medication causes** (benzodiazepines, dopamine agonists, L-dopa) [2].

### 2. Assessment
- **History** from several informants:
  - onset and order of the behavioural vs memory changes;
  - other frontal features (loss of empathy, compulsions, food changes, hyperorality);
  - memory and language;
  - mood and grief;
  - the **specifics of the sexual behaviour** (precipitants, frequency, where and with whom) [2].
- **Medical and medication review:** **urinary or genital conditions** (discomfort can lead to genital touching), **delirium**, drugs [2].
- **Cognitive testing:** MMSE, **MoCA, ACE-III** (more sensitive to executive and language deficits), frontal tests [1].
- **Imaging:** MRI for **frontal or anterior temporal atrophy** (probable bvFTD); infarcts; hippocampal atrophy [1].
- **Risk:** to the daughter-in-law (sexual behaviour, aggression); **driving**; falls; wandering.

### 3. Management
**Inappropriate sexual behaviour [2]:**
- **Non-pharmacological first** (Box 6.3):
  - treat medical causes; treat psychiatric disorder (mania, depression); stop causative drugs;
  - meet the need for intimacy or connection in other ways (group meals, conversation, walking);
  - **discussion with the family** about the behaviour and attitudes;
  - **distraction or redirection**, hands-on activities;
  - sensory stimulation (music, aromatherapy, pets);
  - behavioural or CBT approaches.
- **Avoid pathologising normal sexual behaviour.** For example, private masturbation can be managed with privacy plans.
- **Pharmacological:**
  - **SSRIs first line**;
  - **antipsychotics or antiandrogens** (cyproterone, medroxyprogesterone) second line;
  - LHRH agonists and oestrogens third line.
  - **No drug is licensed.** All are potentially harmful.
  - **Cyproterone** carries a **meningioma** risk.

**bvFTD, if confirmed [1]:**
- **SSRIs first line** for disinhibition, compulsions and hyperorality;
- low-dose atypicals only for severe disturbance;
- **avoid cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine** (no evidence; may worsen behaviour);
- genetic counselling for the family;
- caregiver education about **impaired empathy**.

**If AD:** cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine per AD management. BPSD as above [1].

**Driving [1]:**
- **Any stage of dementia raises concern.** Mild dementia needs **individual assessment** (on-road test, neuropsychology). Moderate to severe dementia is generally unsafe.
- **Getting lost and a recent fall from his scooter argue strongly against driving.**
- **India has no mandatory reporting law**, so the clinician must **advise strongly** and involve the family.

**Grief and depression:** assess and treat depression after his wife's death [3].

**Family:** caregiver burden (Zarit); support for the daughter-in-law; safety planning [1].

**Capacity:** decision-specific. Plan advance directives and nominated representatives under the **MHCA 2017** while he has capacity [1].

**Conclusion.** Late-onset frontal-type behavioural change (sexual disinhibition, aggression, apathy) with topographical disorientation suggests **dementia with BPSD**: bvFTD or AD (or vascular). Management:
- exclude delirium, depression and medical or drug causes;
- treat inappropriate sexual behaviour non-pharmacologically first, then with an SSRI;
- stop driving after structured advice;
- support the family.

## In depth

### Pharmacological ladder for inappropriate sexual behaviour (Maudsley, systematic reviews) [2]
| Line | Options |
|---|---|
| First | SSRIs (also TCAs) |
| Second | Antiandrogens (cyproterone 10–100 mg/day; MPA) or antipsychotics |
| Third | LHRH agonists, oestrogens |
