## Exam answer

### Definition

Technostress is stress arising from using, adapting to, or being continually exposed to digital technologies. Technology can improve flexibility and productivity, but poorly designed systems and unrealistic expectations increase psychological burden.

**Common dimensions (Tarafdar framework):**
- **Techno-overload:** More tasks and faster work.
- **Techno-invasion:** Work intrudes into private time through messages and notifications.
- **Techno-complexity:** Systems feel difficult to learn or use.
- **Techno-insecurity:** Fear of job loss or replacement.
- **Techno-uncertainty:** Frequent upgrades and changing procedures.

### Mental-health effects

Possible outcomes include stress, fatigue, sleep disturbance, irritability, anxiety, poor concentration, burnout, work–family conflict, and reduced job satisfaction. Effects depend on autonomy, digital literacy, usability, leadership, and support. Technostress should not be framed solely as individual weakness when organizational design contributes.

### Assessment

Explore symptoms, duration, impairment, workload, after-hours contact, technology usability, role clarity, organizational support, sleep, and coping. Screen for depression, anxiety, burnout, and suicidality where indicated. Organizational surveys can supplement but not replace clinical assessment.

### Management

Individual measures include training, task prioritization, notification control, breaks, sleep hygiene, boundaries, relaxation, and help-seeking. Organizational measures are essential: involve workers in technology selection; provide training and technical support; reduce redundant platforms; clarify response expectations; protect non-work hours; audit surveillance; ensure reasonable workloads; and design accessible systems. Managers should model healthy boundaries. Occupational-health policies can include psychosocial risk assessment and confidential support.

### Indian/workplace relevance

Remote and hybrid work, uneven digital skills, long working hours, and high-connectivity expectations make organizational policies and access to support important.

### Conclusion

Technostress is both an individual experience and a work-design risk. Prevention requires personal coping plus organizational reduction of overload, invasion, insecurity, and uncertainty.
