## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 12-year-old boy has a **6-month history** of:
- marked **irritability and aggression**;
- **expansive beliefs**, **disinhibited behaviour**, **excessive talk**;
- **sleeplessness**;
- **frequent low mood and suicidal ideas**.

**Risperidone 3 mg for 2 months** has not helped. He has had **ADHD since early childhood**, which improved considerably on **methylphenidate for 5 years**. His **father has unipolar depression**.

### Provisional diagnosis
**Bipolar affective disorder (paediatric-onset), current episode manic or hypomanic with mixed features (concurrent depressive symptoms and suicidality), not responding to risperidone, with comorbid ADHD (combined type, previously stimulant-responsive).**

**Supporting points:**
- **New, distinct symptoms** on top of a long-standing ADHD baseline:
  - **expansive beliefs or grandiosity**; **decreased sleep**; pressured speech; disinhibition.
  - These are not ADHD symptoms. Distinguishing features of mania: **recent onset, a definite change from the premorbid state, goal-directed activity, grandiosity** [1].
- **Mixed features:** low mood and **suicidal ideas** alongside manic symptoms. This carries a **high suicide risk**.
- **Family history of mood disorder.** Unipolar depression in a parent still raises the risk of bipolar disorder in offspring.
- **Duration** (6 months) suggests a persistent episode, or **rapid or ultra-rapid cycling**, which is common in youth.

**Differentials:**
| Diagnosis | Discriminating points |
| --- | --- |
| **Stimulant-induced manic or psychotic symptoms** | Temporal link to dose increase or a new formulation. He has been on methylphenidate 5 years with benefit, so this is less likely but possible (a dose increase, abuse, or an interaction). Test by withholding the stimulant [1, 2] |
| **ADHD with severe emotional dysregulation** | Irritability without grandiosity or decreased need for sleep; non-episodic |
| **DMDD** | Severe, frequent temper outbursts with chronic irritability, onset before 10. **Not** expansive mood, grandiosity or decreased sleep. It was created to curb over-diagnosis of paediatric bipolar disorder in the US [2] |
| **Conduct disorder / ODD** | Aggression is goal-directed or defiant; no mood episode |
| **Major depression with agitation / irritability** | Low mood and suicidality; but grandiosity is absent |
| **Substance-induced** (cannabis, inhalants, stimulant misuse) | Urine drug screen |
| **Antidepressant- or steroid-induced** | Medication history |
| **Organic** | Hyperthyroidism, epilepsy (frontal lobe), head injury, encephalitis (anti-NMDAR), SLE, brain tumour |
| **Schizophrenia / schizoaffective (early onset)** | Delusions or hallucinations outside mood episodes |
| **Sleep disorder** | Primary insomnia worsening ADHD symptoms |
| **Akathisia from risperidone** | Restlessness; check for it |
| **Abuse or trauma (PTSD)** | Hyperarousal, irritability |

### (a) Evaluation
**1. History (child, parents and teachers) [2]:**
- **Episodicity:** a **distinct change** from the ADHD baseline. Map a **life chart** of mood episodes, onset and triggers.
- Manic symptoms: elation vs irritability; grandiosity (expansive beliefs, unrealistic plans); decreased **need** for sleep (not tired); pressured speech; racing thoughts; increased goal-directed activity; risk-taking; **hypersexuality** (age-inappropriate sexual behaviour is a specific sign in children [general knowledge]).
- **Depressive symptoms and suicidality:** ideation, plans, attempts, access to means.
- **Psychotic symptoms.**
- **Methylphenidate:** dose, formulation, recent changes, adherence, **misuse or diversion**. Was the onset temporally linked?
- **Risperidone:** dose, adherence, side effects (akathisia, prolactin, weight).
- Other drugs: antidepressants (SSRIs), steroids, antihistamines, **substances**.
- **Family history:** depression in the father; ask about **bipolar disorder, suicide, substance use, response to lithium** in second-degree relatives [2].
- Psychosocial stressors; abuse; school functioning.

**2. Mental state examination:**
- mood, affect, speech, thought form (flight of ideas);
- grandiosity; perceptual disturbances;
- suicidality; insight.
- **Use structured instruments where possible** [2].

**3. Rating scales [2]:**
- **YMRS** and **parent YMRS** (mania);
- **CDRS-R** (depression);
- **CGI-BP** (impairment);
- K-SADS-PL (structured diagnosis);
- C-SSRS;
- Conners/Vanderbilt (ADHD baseline).
- **Mood and sleep diaries** kept prospectively [2].
- **Avoid symptom checklists alone** [2].

**4. Physical baseline [2]:**
- height, weight, **waist and hip circumference**, pulse, BP, **ECG**;
- fasting glucose, **HbA1c**, fasting lipids, FBC, U&E, **creatine kinase**, LFTs, **prolactin**, **thyroid function** (also needed before lithium);
- **urine drug screen**;
- **EEG or MRI** if there are atypical or neurological features.

### (b) Management
**Setting:**
- Consider **inpatient care** because of the **mixed state with suicidal ideation**, aggression and treatment failure.
- **MHCA 2017, s87** (admission of minors on application by the nominated representative; MHRB informed within 72 hours) [general knowledge].

**Step 1: Review existing medicines:**
- **Withhold or reduce methylphenidate** during acute mania, then reassess. It **can be reintroduced after mood stabilisation**: stimulants "may be safe and effective to use after mood stabilisation" [2].
- **Stop any antidepressant.**
- **Risperidone 3 mg for 2 months without response:**
  - check adherence and akathisia;
  - in youth, the duration of most trials is **3–5 weeks**; "complete absence of response at 2 weeks should prompt consideration of a switch to another SGA" [2];
  - two months is an adequate trial. **Switch.**

**Step 2: Acute treatment of mania or mixed state [2]:**
- **SGAs are first line** in children and adolescents (effect size about 0.65 vs mood stabilisers about 0.20) [2].
- **Switch to another SGA:**
| Drug | Dose | Notes [2] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aripiprazole | 10 mg/day | EPS; weight gain over 30 weeks |
| Quetiapine | Up to 400 mg/day | Sedation |
| Olanzapine | 5–20 mg/day | Most weight gain (3.7 kg in 3 weeks); add-on topiramate reduces gain |
| Asenapine | 2.5–10 mg twice daily | Metabolic effects; oral hypoaesthesia |
- **If two SGAs fail, add a mood stabiliser** (NICE) [2]. **Combining an SGA with a mood stabiliser** is reasonable for non-response [2].
- **Lithium:**
  - in youth it is less effective than risperidone or quetiapine acutely, but superior to placebo (8-week RCT) [2];
  - recommended for **maintenance in preference to valproate** [2];
  - also **anti-suicidal** [general knowledge];
  - **twice-daily dosing** (faster clearance in children); monitor levels, thyroid and renal function;
  - **use only if adherence is assured** [2].
- **Valproate: avoid** in all peri- and post-pubertal young people [2].
- Oxcarbazepine: ineffective [2].
- **ECT:** for severe, treatment-resistant mania or catatonia. Requires MHRB permission in minors [general knowledge].

**Step 3: If depressive features dominate later [2]:**
- **lurasidone** 20–80 mg (optimal in a 2022 NMA for youth bipolar depression);
- or **olanzapine–fluoxetine** combination;
- antidepressants only rarely, with an antimanic agent;
- **quetiapine and lamotrigine are not effective** in youth bipolar depression.

**Step 4: Maintenance [2]:**
- continue the effective acute regimen;
- **consider lithium early**;
- follow adult guidelines for duration.
- NICE suggests SGAs should not routinely be continued **beyond 12 weeks** in youth without review [2].

**Monitoring [2]:**
- weight, height, waist and hip at each visit;
- fasting bloods at **3 months**, then every 6 months;
- prolactin; EPS;
- lifestyle advice.

**ADHD after stabilisation:**
- reintroduce **methylphenidate cautiously** [2], or atomoxetine (watch for activation);
- clonidine is an option.

**Psychosocial [2]:**
- **psychoeducation** (relapse signatures, adherence);
- **family-focused therapy (FFT-A)**;
- CBT; IPSRT (sleep and routine regularity);
- a school plan;
- a **safety plan** (lethal means locked; parents hold medicines);
- treatment of the **father's depression**.

**Prognosis:**
- Paediatric-onset bipolar disorder has a **chronic, relapsing course** with high comorbidity and **suicide risk**.
- **Early onset, mixed features and comorbid ADHD** predict a poorer outcome.
- Adherence and family support improve outcomes [general knowledge].

**Conclusion.** The appearance of **grandiosity, decreased sleep, pressured speech and disinhibition** with depressive symptoms and suicidality, in a boy with long-standing stimulant-responsive ADHD and a family history of mood disorder, suggests **paediatric bipolar disorder with mixed features**. Stimulant-induced mania, DMDD, substances and organic causes need to be excluded. Management:
- ensure safety;
- withhold the stimulant temporarily;
- switch to another SGA after the failed risperidone trial, adding lithium if needed (avoid valproate);
- monitor metabolic effects;
- reintroduce ADHD treatment after stabilisation;
- provide family-focused psychosocial care [1, 2].

## In depth

### Background
- **The paediatric bipolar debate:** US diagnoses increased about 40-fold from 1994 to 2003, driven by broad "chronic irritability" definitions [general knowledge]. This led to the introduction of **DMDD** in DSM-5 [2].
- The **TEAM trial** used a broader definition of mania than other countries do, so caution is needed in interpretation [2].

### Epidemiology [general knowledge]
- About **1.8%** worldwide meta-analytic prevalence of the bipolar spectrum in youth (Van Meter).
- Adult bipolar disorder: about **50–65% report onset before 18** (US data); lower in Europe and India.
- **ADHD comorbidity** in paediatric bipolar disorder: about 60–90% (prepubertal) and about 30% (adolescent).

### Classification
- **DSM-5-TR:** bipolar I, bipolar II, cyclothymia, other specified (short-duration hypomania); **mixed-features specifier**.
- **ICD-11:** bipolar type I (6A60) and type II (6A61); mixed episode retained as a current-episode descriptor.

### Mechanism [general knowledge]
- High heritability (60–85%).
- **Offspring of parents with depression** carry increased risk of bipolar disorder, especially with early-onset or psychotic depression and **antidepressant-induced switching**.
- **Stimulants:** dopaminergic potentiation; may unmask mania in predisposed individuals. Large cohort data show an increased risk of mania after methylphenidate **only in patients not on mood stabilisers** (Viktorin 2017).

### Clinical features: mania in children vs adults
| Feature | Children | Adults |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mood | Irritability more than euphoria; "affective storms" | Euphoria more common |
| Course | Mixed, rapid cycling, chronic | Discrete episodes |
| Comorbidity | ADHD, ODD, anxiety | Substance use |
| Specific signs | **Grandiosity, hypersexuality, decreased need for sleep, elation** (Geller's "FIRST" symptoms) | |

### Assessment: distinguishing mania from ADHD [1]
| Feature | Mania | ADHD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Recent; episodic | Early childhood; chronic |
| Activity | Goal-directed | Not goal-directed |
| Self-esteem | Inflated, grandiose | Often low |
| Sleep | Decreased need | Difficulty settling but tired |
| Mood | Elated or irritable episodes | Labile but brief |

### Differential: DMDD [2]
- Severe recurrent temper outbursts (at least 3 times a week) plus persistently irritable mood, for 12 months or more, in 2 or more settings.
- Onset before 10; diagnose between 6 and 18 years.
- **No established treatment.** **Lithium is ineffective.** SSRIs and parenting interventions may be considered [2].

### Management details: paediatric trials [2]
| Drug | Trial | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risperidone | N = 169, 3 weeks | Superior to placebo; lower dose (0.5–2.5 mg) equally effective with fewer adverse effects |
| Risperidone vs lithium vs divalproex (TEAM) | | Risperidone had the best response but more metabolic effects |
| Quetiapine | N = 277 | 400–600 mg superior to placebo |
| Aripiprazole | N = 296 (4 weeks), N = 210 (30 weeks) | Superior; EPS; weight gain |
| Olanzapine | N = 161 | Superior; 3.7 kg weight gain |
| Asenapine | N = 350 | Superior from day 4; weight gain |
| Ziprasidone | N = 237 | Superior; metabolically neutral |
| Divalproex ER | N = 150 | No better than placebo |
| Oxcarbazepine | N = 116 | No better than placebo |
| Lithium | N = 81, 8 weeks | Superior on YMRS; TSH rise |
| Lurasidone (bipolar depression, 10–17 years) | | Effective acutely and over 2 years |
| Omega-3 plus inositol (5–12 years) | | Emerging evidence; confirmatory trials needed |

### Critical appraisal
- Short trials; heterogeneous diagnostic definitions [2].
- Metabolic risk is greater in youth than in adults [2].
- Stimulant–mania links come mostly from observational data.
- There is limited Indian data on paediatric bipolar disorder.

### Indian context
- Methylphenidate is the only stimulant available (Schedule X) [1].
- **Lurasidone** and **aripiprazole** are available as generics. **Asenapine** is available [general knowledge].
- Lithium monitoring is challenging (phlebotomy in children; cost).
- **MHCA s87** for minors [general knowledge].

### Recent advances
- **Lurasidone** is the preferred agent for youth bipolar depression (2022 NMA) [2].
- **Cariprazine** may be effective in young people (limited evidence) [2].
- **rTMS** is experimental; it was ineffective as an add-on in youth mania [2].
- **Melatonin** adjunct in adult mania (insufficient evidence for children) [2].
- **Digital mood monitoring.**
