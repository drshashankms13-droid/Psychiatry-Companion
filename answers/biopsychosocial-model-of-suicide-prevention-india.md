## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- "Suicidal behaviour is a **complex phenomenon** influenced by several interacting factors, including **personal, social, psychological, cultural, biological and environmental** factors" [1].
- The NSPS groups risk factors as **bio-psychosocial, environmental and sociocultural** [1].
- **The biopsychosocial model** therefore treats suicide prevention as acting on all three levels together, not on mental illness alone.

**The Indian burden [1, 3]:**
- More than **1 lakh** suicide deaths a year; the rate rose from **10.2 to 11.3 per 100,000** in 3 years.
- Suicide is the **leading cause of death at 15–29 years**.
- India accounts for **36.6%** of global female suicides and **24.3%** of male suicides (2016).
- **65%** of suicides are at 18–45 years.
- About **25 attempts per death** [3].

### The model as applied in the sources
**1. Biological / individual level** [1, 2]:
- **Psychiatric illness:** depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, anxiety disorders, certain personality disorders.
- **Substance use:** "a common but **modifiable**" risk factor (NSPS Exhibit B).
- **Family history** and previous attempts; **major physical illness**.
- Aggressive and impulsive tendencies.
- **Lifetime suicide rates by disorder** (Harris and Barraclough) [2]: bipolar 15.5%, mixed drug misuse 14.7%, MDD 14.6%, anxiety and panic 7.2%, schizophrenia 6.0%, personality disorder 5.1%.
- **Interventions:**
  - early identification and management;
  - **lithium and clozapine** (major reductions in suicide and attempts);
  - **ECT** for acute suicidality;
  - ketamine;
  - antidepressants;
  - palliative care and pain relief [1, 2].

**2. Psychological level** [2]:
- **Theories:**
  - Baumeister: escape from the self;
  - **Shneidman: psychache** from frustrated needs;
  - Williams: Cry of Pain;
  - **Joiner: interpersonal theory** (thwarted belongingness, perceived burdensomeness, acquired capability);
  - **O'Connor: integrated motivational–volitional model** (defeat, entrapment);
  - **Klonsky: three-step theory**;
  - **Beck: cognitive model** (hopelessness, schemas, inability to recall reasons for living);
  - Apter and Plutchik: two-stage aggression model.
- **Risk:** **hopelessness**, impulsivity, poor coping.
- **Interventions:** psychotherapies; **life skills for the young** (WHO LIVE LIFE "Form the young"); resilience-building (problem solving, conflict resolution) [1].

**3. Social / environmental / cultural level** [1]:
- **Environmental:** job or financial loss, relationship loss, violence, child abuse, **easy access to lethal means** (pesticides, hanging), local clusters.
- **Sociocultural:** lack of support and isolation, **stigma about help-seeking**, barriers to care, cultural beliefs (for example, suicide as a "noble resolution"), **media sensationalisation**.
- **Indian reasons (NCRB):** **family problems 34%, illness 18%**, marital issues, love affairs, debt, substance use. Plus **family suicide pacts** (poverty, debt, humiliation, superstition).
- **Vulnerable groups:** **daily-wage earners**, income under Rs 1 lakh a year, **low education** (60% had not completed school; graduates only about 4%), housewives, students, farmers, transgender persons.
- **Protective factors:** connections to family and community; **access to clinical care**; **restricted access to lethal means**; cultural and religious beliefs that discourage suicide; a healthy lifestyle.
- **Interventions:**
  - **means restriction** (pesticides);
  - **responsible media reporting**;
  - helplines and crisis centres;
  - **training community stakeholders** (teachers, police, faith and political leaders, PRI members, youth volunteers);
  - postvention for the bereaved;
  - **decriminalisation** under MHCA s115 [1, 3].

### Strengths
- **Matches the evidence of multiple causation.** Indian suicides are driven largely by family, illness, financial and marital stressors, not psychiatric illness alone [1].
- **Supports multisectoral action.** NSPS involves health, education, home affairs, information and broadcasting, social justice, Panchayati Raj, NGOs and media [1].
- **Integrates the four WHO effective interventions** (means restriction, media, life skills, early identification and follow-up) into one framework [1].
- **Reduces stigma.** MHCA chose "**severe stress**" rather than "mental illness" for suicide attempters, partly because of stigma, yet still obliges care, treatment and rehabilitation [3].
- **Directs attention to modifiable risks:** substance use, means, and access to care [1].

### Limitations
- **Breadth may dilute action.** Many risk factors are hard to target. Implementing the REDS path needs coordination across many ministries [1].
- **Poor surveillance:** method is undocumented in about **5%**, cause in about **10%**; data come from police (NCRB); attempts are under-registered. Hard to measure which component works [1].
- **Limited prediction:** "standardised risk scales are **not predictive** of risk", with low PPV and sensitivity, so individual-level application relies on clinical judgement [2].
- **Workforce and infrastructure gaps:** limited capacity for substance-use treatment, and poor integration with suicide prevention [1].
- **Legal tension:** **IPC 309 still exists**; it is unclear whether attempts must be reported to police; abetment provisions (306, 109, 116) remain [1, 3].
- **Cultural factors cut both ways:** family is protective, yet family problems are the commonest reason, and family suicides are rising [1].

### Real-world applicability in India
- **NSPS goals:** cut suicide mortality by **10% by 2030** through **REDS** [1]:
  - **R**einforce leadership;
  - **E**nhance health-service capacity;
  - **D**evelop community resilience;
  - **S**trengthen surveillance.
- **Objectives** [1]:
  - effective **surveillance within 3 years**;
  - **psychiatric OPDs offering suicide prevention through DMHP in all districts within 5 years**;
  - a **mental-well-being curriculum in all educational institutions within 8 years**.
- **Platforms:** DMHP, Ayushman Bharat HWCs (MNS disorders), **PMJAY** (17 mental-health packages including ECT and TMS), RKSK, School Health Ambassadors, Nasha Mukt Bharat and its helpline, the palliative care programme [1].
- **Clinical pathway after an attempt (MHCA)** [3]:
  1. triage;
  2. stabilise;
  3. register as a medico-legal case;
  4. **mandatory psychiatric referral**;
  5. inform the patient of s115;
  6. enquiry about abetment;
  7. discharge with follow-up.

**Conclusion.** The biopsychosocial model fits India's suicide profile, where social and economic stressors and substance use weigh as heavily as psychiatric illness, and it underpins the NSPS's multisectoral REDS strategy. Its practical success depends on:
- better surveillance;
- means restriction;
- stigma reduction;
- district-level services;
- resolving legal ambiguities.

## In depth

### WHO LIVE LIFE framework (cited in NSPS) [1]
- **LIVE:** Leadership; Interventions; Vision (innovation, financing); Evaluation.
- **LIFE:** **L**ess means; **I**nteraction with media; **F**orm the young (life skills); **E**arly identification, management and follow-up.

### Durkheim's sociological types (seminar) [2]
| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Egoistic | Poor integration into society |
| Altruistic | Over-integration |
| Anomic | Weakened social cohesion; not belonging |
| Fatalistic | No control over one's life |
