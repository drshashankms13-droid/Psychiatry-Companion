## Exam answer

**Introduction.** The cerebellum was long regarded as a purely motor structure. Anatomical, imaging, lesion and stimulation studies now show that **most of the cerebellum maps to cerebral association areas**: "one half of the cerebellum", with non-motor greater than motor [1].

### Anatomical basis [1]
- **Functional divisions of the deep nuclei:** a **limbic**, a **motor** and a **cognitive** cerebellum.
- **Connections:**
  - no monosynaptic links to the cortex. Transneuronal tracing (HSV-1, rabies) shows **closed loops with prefrontal and parietal association cortex** (Kelly & Strick 2003);
  - a **cingulo-cerebellar circuit**.
- **Evolution:**
  - **Crus I and II** (the "cognitive cerebellum" or "silent frontal lobe") have **no connections to motor cortex**;
  - they are enlarged in humans and chimpanzees, in parallel with prefrontal expansion (grey matter in crus I/II vs white matter in PFC: co-evolution);
  - lobule V (motor) is relatively reduced in humans.
- **Network mapping (Buckner 2011):**
  - lateral cerebellum couples with the **default mode network**; medial cerebellum with the **frontoparietal network**;
  - primary visual and auditory cortex have **no cerebellar representation**;
  - **language is right-lateralised and spatial function left-lateralised** in the cerebellum.

**Topographic organisation (Stoodley & Schmahmann 2009):**

| Axis | Region | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anterior–posterior | **Anterior lobe (I–V)** and secondary representation in VIII/IX | Sensorimotor |
| | **Neocerebellum (VI, VII: vermis and hemispheres)** | Cognitive and affective |
| Medial–lateral | **Vermis and fastigial nucleus** | **Autonomic regulation, affect, emotionally important memory** ("limbic cerebellum"; vermis as "emotional pacemaker", Heath 1977) |
| | **Hemispheres and dentate nucleus** | **Executive, visuospatial, linguistic, learning and memory** |

### Physiological concept [1]
- **Universal cerebellar transform:** a context-dependent, automatic "**general-purpose modulator**". It detects patterns, pattern changes and errors in **both movement and thought**, and gives adaptive feedback to the cortex.
- **"Oscillation dampener":** just as the cerebellum regulates the **rate, force, rhythm and accuracy** of movement, it regulates the **speed, capacity, consistency and appropriateness** of cognition and emotion.
- **Purkinje output** is inhibitory (GABAergic) to the deep nuclei, filtering output to the cortex.
- **Dysmetria of thought ("cognitive dysmetria"):**
  - **overshoot:** exaggerated, positive, released, hypermetric responses;
  - **undershoot:** diminished, negative, restricted, hypometric responses.

### Evidence for cerebellar regulation of mood [1]
- **Animal studies:** stimulating Purkinje cells or the dentate nucleus in mice increases **dopamine efflux in prefrontal cortex**.
- **Human stimulation:**
  - cerebellar stimulation changes prefrontal EEG; single-pulse TMS increases frontal theta;
  - **medial cerebellar high-frequency rTMS elevated mood and alertness**, with a shift in frontal fast-EEG asymmetry;
  - **low-frequency rTMS worsened negative mood** (Schutter 2005–2009).
- **Lesion data:**
  - emotional disorders arise mainly from **vermian and paravermian** lesions, with autonomic dysfunction;
  - **cerebellar degeneration** carries **78% psychiatric comorbidity** (Leroi 2002).

### Cerebellar cognitive affective syndrome (CCAS; Schmahmann syndrome) [1]

| Domain | Features |
| --- | --- |
| **Executive** | Impaired planning, set-shifting, verbal fluency, abstract reasoning, working memory |
| **Spatial cognition** | Impaired visuospatial organisation and memory |
| **Personality** | **Blunted affect, or disinhibited and inappropriate behaviour** |
| **Language** | Agrammatism, aprosodia, anomia |

- **Lesion location:** **anterior lobe (I–V) strokes cause motor deficits; posterior lobe (VII–X) lesions cause cognitive and behavioural deficits.**
- **Most pronounced with:** bilateral or large unilateral **PICA** infarcts, and subacute pancerebellar disorders (e.g., post-infectious cerebellitis).

**Neuropsychiatric spectrum (Schmahmann 2007): "overshoot" vs "undershoot":**

| Domain | Positive (hypermetric) | Negative (hypometric) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Inattention, distractibility, hyperactivity, compulsive and ritualistic behaviour | Rumination, perseveration, difficulty shifting, obsessional thoughts |
| **Emotion** | Impulsivity, disinhibition, **lability**, pathological laughing or crying, **anxiety, agitation, panic** | **Anergy, anhedonia, sadness, hopelessness, dysphoria, depression** |
| Autism spectrum | Stereotypies, self-stimulation | Avoidance, tactile defensiveness, sensory overload |
| **Psychosis** | Illogical thought, **paranoia, hallucinations** | Lack of empathy, muted affect, blunting, apathy |
| Social | Anger, aggression, irritability, oppositional behaviour | Passivity, immaturity, poor social cues, over-trusting |

**Clinical reports** (Schmahmann 2007): delayed psychosis after cerebellar tumour resection; aggression and behavioural dyscontrol in childhood cerebellar disease; panic disorder; emotional blunting; pathological laughing and crying; OCD-like and autistic behaviours.

### Relevance to psychiatric disorders [1]
- **Schizophrenia (Andreasen's cognitive dysmetria):** aberrant cerebellar–cortical connections produce **misconnections between percepts and their meanings**:
  - **positive symptoms** (perceptual binding errors leading to delusions and hallucinations; disorganised thought from poor coordination of language);
  - **negative symptoms** (information flow "paralysed", causing alogia and blunting);
  - **cognitive impairment** (inefficient processing).
- **Mood, OCD and autism:** these are represented in the positive and negative symptom domains above.

### Future directions [1]
- **"Bottom-up" neuromodulation:** cerebellar stimulation may access networks unreachable by cortical stimulation. Its mechanisms should be studied with fcMRI, task fMRI and MRS.
- **Animal work:** optogenetic network mapping.
- **Trials needed:**
  - double-blind RCTs vs placebo and vs standard protocols (e.g., DLPFC rTMS);
  - in schizophrenia, depression and OCD;
  - with long-term follow-up, standard protocols and systematic adverse-effect monitoring.

**Conclusion.** The cerebellum, especially the **posterior lobe (crus I/II, lobules VI–VII) and vermis**, forms closed loops with prefrontal, parietal and limbic cortex. It acts as a universal modulator that "smooths" thought and emotion as it does movement. Damage produces **CCAS**: executive, visuospatial, linguistic and affective change. Disinhibition and lability ("overshoot") or blunting and depression ("undershoot") reflect **dysmetria of thought**. This underlies cerebellar models of schizophrenia and the emerging use of **cerebellar rTMS** to modulate mood.

## In depth

### Critical appraisal (from the source)
- **Small studies:** evidence for cerebellar stimulation in mood comes mainly from small experimental studies.
- **Unknown mechanisms:** they need imaging and neurochemical study.
- **Trials needed:** large, controlled clinical trials with standardised protocols are required before clinical use [1].
