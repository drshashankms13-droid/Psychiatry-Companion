## Exam answer

**Definition.** Erectile dysfunction (ED) is the persistent or recurrent inability to attain or maintain an erection sufficient for satisfactory sexual performance. DSM-5-TR **erectile disorder** requires that on almost all (about 75–100%) occasions of partnered activity there is marked difficulty obtaining or maintaining an erection, or decreased rigidity, for **≥6 months**, causing distress. ICD-11 classifies **male erectile dysfunction (HA01.1)** under conditions related to sexual health, with aetiological qualifiers (psychological/behavioural, medical, medication, lack of knowledge, relationship, cultural) [1].

### Mechanism
Sexual stimulation → parasympathetic (S2–S4) and NANC nerves release **nitric oxide** → guanylate cyclase → **cGMP** → smooth-muscle relaxation in corpora cavernosa → arterial inflow, veno-occlusion → erection. PDE-5 breaks down cGMP, so **PDE-5 inhibitors** prolong cGMP action (they need sexual stimulation and androgens). Psychogenic ED: anxiety → sympathetic overactivity (noradrenaline) → vasoconstriction; "spectatoring" and performance anxiety maintain it [1].

### Psychogenic vs organic ED

| Feature | Psychogenic | Organic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Sudden, often with a stressor | Gradual |
| Situational | Yes (partner-specific; normal with masturbation) | Consistent across situations |
| Nocturnal/morning erections | Present | Absent or reduced |
| Course | Episodic | Persistent, progressive |
| Ejaculation | Premature ejaculation, intermittent | Retrograde or absent |
| Libido | Often normal | Reduced in hypogonadism |
| NPT | >90–180 min/night | <60 min/night |
| Age | Younger | Older, with vascular risk factors |

Source: Indian CPG [1].

### Assessment
- **History:** sexual history (onset, situational, morning erections, desire, ejaculation), partner factors, relationship, performance anxiety, myths (e.g., dhat), depression and anxiety, substances (alcohol, tobacco, cannabis), drugs (SSRIs, antipsychotics, beta-blockers, thiazides, finasteride, opioids).
- **Medical:** diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, CVD (ED is an early marker of cardiovascular disease), obesity, hypogonadism, neurological disease, pelvic surgery.
- **Examination:** BMI, BP, secondary sexual characteristics, genitalia (Peyronie's), peripheral pulses, neurological examination (bulbocavernosus reflex).
- **Investigations:** fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids, morning testosterone (repeat if low), prolactin if libido is low, TSH. Specialist tests: NPT (RigiScan), penile Doppler, intracavernosal injection test.
- **Scales:** IIEF-5 (SHIM; ≤21 suggests ED).

### Management (IPS CPG algorithm)

**First line**
- **Sex education** and correcting myths; **relaxation**; **lifestyle** changes (weight loss, exercise, stopping smoking, limiting alcohol, controlling diabetes, BP and lipids) [1].
- **Sex therapy** (Masters & Johnson): ban on intercourse → **non-genital sensate focus** → **genital sensate focus** → vaginal containment → intercourse. Addresses performance anxiety and spectatoring; "teasing" technique (allowing erection to subside and return) [1].
- **Oral PDE-5 inhibitors:** **sildenafil** 25–100 mg (onset ~60 minutes, window 6–8 hours; delayed by fatty meals); **vardenafil** 5–20 mg; **tadalafil** 10–20 mg on demand (window ~36 hours) or 2.5–5 mg daily; avanafil. Contraindicated with nitrates and recent MI or stroke; caution with alpha-blockers [1].
- Treat psychiatric comorbidity; adjust offending drugs (e.g., switch an SSRI to bupropion; trazodone for depression with ED) [1].

**If no response:** check correct use (sexual stimulation, timing, food, adequate dose, at least 6–8 attempts), adherence, hypogonadism (add testosterone), missed comorbidity → retry → switch PDE-5 inhibitor or use daily tadalafil [1].

**Second line:** intracavernosal injection (alprostadil/PGE1, papaverine, phentolamine — "trimix"); intraurethral alprostadil (MUSE); vacuum constriction device [1].

**Third line:** penile prosthesis (inflatable is better cosmetically; infection is the main complication) [1].

### Key evidence
- **Goldstein 1998 (NEJM, n=532):** sildenafil improved erections (IIEF) vs placebo; about 70% vs 20% improved.
- **PDE-5 inhibitors are effective** in ED with diabetes, spinal cord injury, CKD, Parkinson's disease, antidepressant use, and after radical prostatectomy [1].
- **Psychosexual therapy + PDE-5 inhibitor** is better than either alone for psychogenic or mixed ED (Melnik, Cochrane 2007).
- **Daily tadalafil** improves endothelial function [1].

**Conclusion.** ED is common, often multifactorial, and can be the first sign of cardiovascular disease. Management combines evaluation for organic and psychological causes, education, lifestyle change and sex therapy with PDE-5 inhibitors as first-line drugs, escalating to injections, devices or prostheses when needed. Psychiatric comorbidity and medication effects must be addressed.

## In depth

### Background
Masters & Johnson (1970), *Human Sexual Inadequacy*, introduced sensate focus. Kaplan (1974) added psychodynamic understanding (the "new sex therapy"). Intracavernosal papaverine (Virag 1982; Brindley's dramatic 1983 demonstration) followed. Sildenafil (Viagra, 1998), developed for angina, transformed management. Vacuum devices and penile implants date from the 1970s–80s.

### Epidemiology
- **Massachusetts Male Aging Study:** 52% of men aged 40–70 have some degree of ED; complete ED rises from 5% at 40 to 15% at 70.
- Indian studies: high prevalence among diabetic men (50–70%); psychogenic ED is common in young men, often with performance anxiety and dhat-related beliefs [1].
- ED shares risk factors with CVD; it predicts cardiac events by about 2–5 years.

### Classification and criteria
- **DSM-5-TR:** erectile disorder: lifelong vs acquired; generalised vs situational; mild, moderate, severe; ≥6 months; ~75–100% of occasions.
- **ICD-11 HA01.1** male erectile dysfunction: lifelong/acquired; generalised/situational; aetiological qualifiers [1].
- **ICD-10:** F52.2 failure of genital response (non-organic).
- **Medication-induced** sexual dysfunction is a separate category.

### Mechanism in detail
- **Central:** dopamine (medial preoptic area, paraventricular nucleus) is pro-erectile (apomorphine); serotonin is mostly inhibitory; oxytocin; testosterone supports libido and NOS expression.
- **Peripheral:** endothelial NO, cGMP, PDE-5; veno-occlusive mechanism.
- **Psychological:** performance anxiety, spectatoring (Masters & Johnson), cognitive distraction (Barlow's model), relationship conflict, sexual myths, depression (anhedonia, low libido).
- **Organic:** vasculogenic (most common: atherosclerosis, diabetes), neurogenic, hormonal, drug-induced, structural (Peyronie's).

### Clinical features in detail
Situational ED (with partner but not with masturbation); anxiety about failure; avoidance of sex; low self-esteem; partner distress. Comorbid premature ejaculation is common. In India, presentations often include dhat syndrome beliefs (semen loss causing weakness), guilt about masturbation and anxiety before marriage [1].

### Assessment in detail

| Test | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| IIEF-5 / SHIM | Severity screening |
| NPT (RigiScan) | Organic vs psychogenic [1] |
| Penile Doppler ultrasound | Arterial or venous insufficiency |
| Intracavernosal injection test | Vascular integrity |
| Bulbocavernosus reflex latency | Neurogenic [1] |
| Penile–brachial index | Vascular [1] |
| Testosterone, prolactin, LH | Endocrine |
| Lipids, glucose, ECG | Cardiovascular risk |

### Differential diagnosis
- ED vs **low desire** (hypoactive sexual desire disorder).
- ED secondary to **premature ejaculation** (loss of erection after early ejaculation).
- ED due to **depression** or **anxiety**.
- **Medication-induced** ED (SSRIs, antipsychotics with high prolactin, beta-blockers, thiazides, 5-ARIs, opioids).
- **Substance-induced** (alcohol, cannabis, opioids).
- Organic ED (diabetes, vascular, endocrine, neurological, post-surgery).
- **Relationship problems:** situational, partner-specific.

### Management details
- **PDE-5 education:** take on an empty stomach (sildenafil, vardenafil); sexual stimulation is required; do not expect an immediate erection; try at least 6–8 times; daily tadalafil for frequent intercourse or non-responders [1].
- **Side effects:** headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, visual disturbance (sildenafil: blue tinge), back pain (tadalafil), hypotension with alpha-blockers; NAION (rare).
- **Absolute contraindications:** nitrates, riociguat. Assess cardiovascular fitness for sexual activity (Princeton consensus).
- **Testosterone:** only if hypogonadal; monitor PSA, haematocrit, prostate [1].
- **Other oral agents:** trazodone (psychogenic ED with depression), yohimbine and apomorphine (limited) [1].
- **Psychosexual therapy:** individual or couple; sensate focus; CBT for performance anxiety; homework for single men (graded fantasy, masturbation exercises without performance demand) [1].
- **Psychiatric patients:** antipsychotic-induced ED, so switch to prolactin-sparing drugs (aripiprazole) or add PDE-5 inhibitor; SSRI-induced, see the SSRI sexual dysfunction answer.
- **Special populations:** diabetes (higher doses, lower response); post-prostatectomy (penile rehabilitation); CVD (cardiology clearance).

### Critical appraisal
- **"Psychogenic vs organic"** is a false dichotomy; most ED is mixed [1].
- Easy access to PDE-5 inhibitors may lead to under-assessment of cardiovascular disease or psychological factors.
- Sex therapy evidence comes mostly from older, small trials; its effect is larger when combined with medication.
- In India, over-the-counter and online purchase of PDE-5 inhibitors and "herbal" products (some adulterated) is common.

### Indian context
- **IPS CPG for sexual dysfunction (Avasthi, Grover, Rao 2017)** gives a detailed algorithm, sensate focus protocols and homework tasks [1].
- Cultural factors: dhat syndrome; myths about masturbation; arranged marriages with limited sex education; joint families limiting privacy; reluctance to involve partners [1].
- Quackery: "sex clinics" and roadside "specialists" are widely used; psychiatrists should correct misinformation.
- Generic sildenafil and tadalafil are inexpensive.
- High diabetes prevalence means organic ED is common in middle age.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** daily tadalafil; avanafil (rapid onset).
- **Emerging:** low-intensity shockwave therapy (Li-ESWT) for vasculogenic ED (mixed evidence); orodispersible PDE-5 formulations; digital CBT for sexual dysfunction.
- **Investigational:** platelet-rich plasma and stem cell therapies (experimental); gene therapy.
