## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Treatment-resistant depression (TRD)** is most often defined as **failure to respond to at least 2 adequate trials** (adequate dose for at least 4–6 weeks, with adherence) of antidepressants in the current episode.
- TRD is "**not a uniform entity but a complex spectrum of severity which can be graded**". Outcome is linked to the grade [1].
- **Staging systems:** Thase–Rush, **Maudsley Staging Method (MSM)**, Massachusetts General Hospital staging [general knowledge for names].
- There is a move towards "**difficult-to-treat depression**" (DTD). The TRD label implies non-response is abnormal and pushes ever more complex polypharmacy, whereas DTD emphasises **realistic goals** (function, quality of life) [1].
- About **30%** of people with depression do not remit after several treatments (STAR*D was the classic estimate) [general knowledge].

### Step 1: reassess "pseudo-resistance" (the first advance is better assessment)
- **Bipolar depression:** a significant minority of apparent unipolar TRD is bipolar, and responds poorly to antidepressants [1].
- **Inadequate dose or duration; non-adherence**; drug interactions; rapid metabolisers (CYP2D6/2C19, where **pharmacogenomic testing** may help).
- Comorbidity: **substance use**, anxiety, personality disorder, **hypothyroidism**, anaemia, B12 deficiency, sleep apnoea, chronic pain, neurological disease.
- Psychosocial maintaining factors.
- **Psychotic depression** (needs an antipsychotic or ECT).
- Measurement-based care (PHQ-9, HAM-D, MADRS).

### Step 2: established pharmacological strategies (Maudsley Tables 3.3–3.4) [1]
| Strategy | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Optimise dose / switch** (within or between classes) | Including higher-dose venlafaxine and TCAs |
| **Augment with an SGA:**<br>• **aripiprazole 2–20 mg** (low doses of 2–10 mg often enough);<br>• **brexpiprazole**;<br>• **cariprazine**;<br>• **quetiapine XR**;<br>• risperidone | Best-evidenced augmentation. Watch for akathisia (aripiprazole) and metabolic effects (quetiapine) |
| **Augment with lithium** (0.4–0.8 mmol/L, up to 1.0) | NICE-recommended; may reduce suicide; level monitoring |
| **Olanzapine + fluoxetine** | US-licensed for TRD; weight gain |
| **Combine:** SSRI + **bupropion**; SSRI/SNRI + **mirtazapine or mianserin** | Mirtazapine add-on was negative in one large RCT |
| **T3** 20–50 µg/day | Thyroid monitoring |
| **Lamotrigine** | Bipolar-spectrum cases; slow titration |
| **ECT** | Most effective; for severe, psychotic, suicidal or catatonic depression, or when a rapid response is needed |

### Step 3: recent advances
**1. Glutamatergic, rapid-acting antidepressants:**
- **Ketamine** (NMDA antagonist) [1]:
  - **IV 0.5 mg/kg over 40 minutes** (Berman 2000): effect within hours, peaking around 3 days; reduces **suicidal ideation**;
  - effective in unipolar and bipolar TRD; **broadly as effective as ECT** (ELEKT-D, 2023);
  - **mechanism:** blocks NMDA receptors on GABA interneurons, causing a glutamate surge, **AMPA activation**, BDNF/mTOR signalling and **synaptogenesis**;
  - other routes (oral, sublingual, IM, SC) are less established;
  - **adverse effects:** **dissociation**, transient BP and HR rises, laryngospasm (rare), **cystitis** with chronic use, misuse potential;
  - **observe for at least 1 hour**; pre-treatment ECG, BP, bloods.
- **Esketamine nasal spray** (S-enantiomer; Spravato, 28–84 mg) with an oral antidepressant. Licensed in the USA and Europe for TRD (2019) and for depression with acute suicidal ideation [1, general knowledge].
  - **ESCAPE-TRD (2023):** **esketamine superior to quetiapine XR augmentation**. Benefits kept building over 32 weeks, which challenges the "rapid-acting" label [1].
- **Dextromethorphan–bupropion** (AXS-05; NMDA antagonist plus sigma-1; **FDA 2022** for MDD). Rapid onset. Maudsley lists dextromethorphan + quinidine as a promising option [1, general knowledge].
- **Nitrous oxide:** short-lived effect [1].

**2. Neurosteroids:**
- **Brexanolone** (IV) and **zuranolone** (oral, 14 days; **FDA 2023** for postpartum depression): GABA-A positive allosteric modulators [general knowledge].

**3. Psychedelic-assisted therapy:**
- **Psilocybin** (COMP360) 10 or 25 mg. **Goodwin 2022 (*NEJM*):** 25 mg single dose superior to 1 mg at 3 weeks in TRD. Specialist use only [1, general knowledge].
- **Ayahuasca** [1]. See `psychedelic-assisted-therapies`.

**4. Neuromodulation:**
- **rTMS / theta-burst stimulation (iTBS)** to the left DLPFC (NICE-approved).
- **Accelerated, fMRI-guided iTBS (SAINT / SNT):** FDA-cleared in 2022. About 79% remission in a small RCT [general knowledge].
- **ECT** remains the gold standard. **Ketamine anaesthesia** may enhance ECT [1].
- **Vagus nerve stimulation** (FDA, chronic TRD).
- **Deep brain stimulation** (subcallosal cingulate, ventral capsule/ventral striatum; investigational).
- **tDCS** (home-based trials).
- Ablative psychosurgery for extreme cases.

  See `neuromodulation-in-psychiatry`, `deep-brain-stimulation-in-psychiatry`.

**5. Other pharmacological options with emerging or limited support (Maudsley Table 3.5) [1]:**
- **pramipexole** (probably effective);
- **hyoscine (scopolamine)** IV (rapid);
- **buprenorphine** low dose;
- **minocycline** (meta-analyses positive, recent RCTs negative);
- anti-inflammatory strategies;
- **testosterone** (if low);
- **methylfolate**;
- modafinil and stimulants;
- **pindolol** (acceleration);
- EPA omega-3 (many failed trials);
- **MAOIs** (phenelzine, tranylcypromine) are under-used and effective.

**6. Psychological treatments:**
- **CBT, MBCT, CBASP** (chronic depression);
- **psychodynamic** therapy (Tavistock adult depression study);
- CBT augmentation of pharmacotherapy in TRD (**CoBalT trial**: CBT added to usual care roughly halved non-response, with long-term benefit) [general knowledge].

**7. Precision psychiatry:**
- **pharmacogenomic-guided prescribing** (CYP2D6, CYP2C19; modest benefit in GUIDED, PRIME Care);
- **inflammatory markers (CRP)** to select anti-inflammatory augmentation;
- EEG and fMRI biomarkers (EMBARC); none are routine yet [general knowledge].

**8. Service-level:**
- **measurement-based care**;
- specialist **mood disorder clinics**;
- **realistic goals** (DTD model);
- treat comorbidity;
- psychoeducation.

### Critique of the evidence base [1]
- **STAR*D** (the basis of TRD algorithms) has been "somewhat discredited":
  - high dropout;
  - no placebo; open treatment;
  - post hoc outcome switching;
  - payments to subjects;
  - **93% of remitters had relapsed or dropped out by 12 months**;
  - reanalysis found cumulative remission of **35%, not 67%**.
- Augmentation evidence often comes from **short, industry-funded trials**. Long-term data are sparse.
- **Ketamine and esketamine:** limited long-term safety data; functional unblinding (dissociation); cost; misuse.

**Conclusion.** Managing TRD begins with **re-diagnosis** (bipolarity, comorbidity, adherence) and **measurement-based optimisation**. Established augmentation (aripiprazole and other SGAs, lithium, combinations) and **ECT** remain the backbone. Recent advances add:
- **glutamatergic rapid-acting agents** (ketamine, **esketamine**, dextromethorphan–bupropion);
- **neurosteroids**;
- **psilocybin**;
- **accelerated iTBS**;
- **precision tools**.

All are within a **difficult-to-treat depression** framework that prioritises function and realistic goals.

## In depth

### Background
- TRD carries:
  - **higher suicide risk**;
  - **disability**;
  - **2× healthcare costs**;
  - **higher mortality** [general knowledge].
- The DTD concept comes from an international consensus (McAllister-Williams 2020) [1, general knowledge].

### Epidemiology
- About **20–30%** of MDD meets TRD criteria [general knowledge].
- India: few data. Probably under-recognised because of inadequate trials and non-adherence [Guessing].

### Classification (staging)
| Model | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| **Thase–Rush** | Stages I–V by class failures (I: 1 adequate trial … V: ECT failure) |
| **European Staging** | Non-responder; TRD (2 or more trials); chronic resistant depression (more than 12 months) |
| **Maudsley Staging Method** | Duration + severity + number of treatment failures (including augmentation and ECT); scored 3–15; predicts outcome |
| **MGH-S** | Points per trial; optimisation and augmentation counted |

### Mechanism: why monoaminergic drugs fail
- Glutamatergic, synaptic-plasticity, inflammatory and HPA-axis dysfunction.
- **Network hypotheses:** DLPFC–subgenual cingulate dysconnectivity, which is the basis for rTMS targeting.
- **Inflammation** (CRP above 3 mg/L in about a quarter of patients) predicts poorer SSRI response [general knowledge].

### Clinical features associated with TRD
- Comorbid anxiety, personality disorder, substance use.
- Chronicity; early onset.
- Melancholic or atypical features.
- **Childhood adversity.**

### Assessment
- Antidepressant Treatment History Form (ATHF) for trial adequacy.
- **Rating scales:** HAM-D, MADRS, QIDS.
- **Bipolarity screens:** MDQ, HCL-32.
- Investigations: TFT, B12, folate, CRP, sleep study if indicated, drug levels.

### Differential diagnosis
- Bipolar depression.
- Organic causes (hypothyroidism, Cushing's, Parkinson's disease, dementia).
- Substance-induced depression.
- Personality disorder.
- Chronic PTSD.
- Demoralisation.

### Management: ketamine practicalities (Maudsley Table 3.6) [1]
- **IV:**
  - 0.5 mg/kg (up to 1.0 mg/kg; start at 0.25 mg/kg in older people) over 40 minutes;
  - **induction:** once or twice weekly;
  - **maintenance:** weekly, then fortnightly or monthly.
- **Monitoring:** BP and HR; observe for 1 hour; no driving that day.
- **Contraindications:** uncontrolled hypertension, psychosis, substance misuse (caution), pregnancy.

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| STAR*D critique (Pigott 2023, cited in [1]) | Real remission about 35%; 93% of remitters relapsed or dropped out by 12 months |
| Berman 2000 | Single IV ketamine produced rapid antidepressant effect [1] |
| ESCAPE-TRD (Reif 2023, *NEJM*) | Esketamine better than quetiapine XR for remission at week 8; benefits grew to 32 weeks [1] |
| ELEKT-D (Anand 2023, *NEJM*) (general knowledge) | Ketamine non-inferior to ECT in non-psychotic TRD |
| Goodwin 2022, *NEJM* (general knowledge) | Psilocybin 25 mg better than 1 mg at 3 weeks |
| CoBalT (Wiles 2013, *Lancet*) (general knowledge) | CBT augmentation effective, sustained |
| Cole 2020, SAINT (general knowledge) | Accelerated fMRI-guided iTBS: high remission |

### Critical appraisal
- **Placebo response** is high in augmentation trials. Effect sizes are modest (SMD about 0.3).
- **Esketamine** licensing trials were mixed (TRANSFORM-1 and -3 missed their primary endpoints) [general knowledge].
- **Psychedelic trials** have unblinding and expectancy problems.
- **Generalisability to India:** cost and availability (esketamine is expensive); ECT is accessible and under-used.

### Indian context
- **Availability:**
  - ketamine is used off-label in some tertiary centres;
  - esketamine was approved in India [general knowledge; check status];
  - **rTMS** is widely available in private practice;
  - **ECT** is widely available and cheap.
- **IPS Clinical Practice Guidelines** for depression (Gautam 2017; TRD sections) [general knowledge].
- **MHCA 2017:** ECT consent and prohibitions; research ethics for novel treatments.
