## Exam answer

### Introduction

Mixed presentations involve clinically significant depressive and manic/hypomanic symptoms occurring together or in close temporal association. They are important because they are associated with diagnostic complexity, greater impairment and potentially increased suicide risk.

### Evolution of classification

DSM-IV used a distinct mixed episode requiring full criteria for both mania and major depression simultaneously. DSM-5 removed this category and introduced the **with mixed features** specifier for manic, hypomanic and depressive episodes. ICD-11 also recognises mixed presentations within mood disorders.

### DSM-5-TR mixed-features specifier

During a manic or hypomanic episode, at least three depressive symptoms may be present, such as depressed mood, anhedonia, psychomotor retardation, fatigue, worthlessness/guilt or recurrent thoughts of death.

During a depressive episode, at least three manic/hypomanic symptoms may be present, such as elevated mood, grandiosity, pressured speech, racing thoughts, increased energy or decreased need for sleep.

Symptoms should be distinguished from overlapping features such as irritability, distractibility and psychomotor agitation.

### Clinical features

Patients may report sadness, hopelessness or suicidal thoughts alongside increased energy, racing thoughts, agitation, impulsivity and reduced sleep. This combination may increase the ability to act on suicidal ideas.

### Aetiology and neurobiology

The condition is associated with complex interactions among genetic vulnerability, circadian dysregulation, neurotransmitter systems and frontolimbic networks. No single biomarker is established.

### Assessment and differential diagnosis

Assess longitudinal mood history, sleep, energy, psychomotor activity, suicidality, substance use, medications and collateral history. Consider ADHD, substance-induced states, personality pathology, agitation in depression and medical causes.

### Management

Treatment is guided by predominant polarity, severity and risk. Mood stabilisers and selected antipsychotics are commonly used. Antidepressants require caution, particularly in bipolar I disorder. Treat substance use and sleep disruption; consider hospitalisation for severe risk. ECT may be considered in severe or refractory presentations.

### Conclusion

Mixed features are clinically important mood presentations requiring careful assessment and risk management. The DSM-5 mixed-features specifier captures clinically relevant symptoms but does not fully resolve the complexity of mixed states.
