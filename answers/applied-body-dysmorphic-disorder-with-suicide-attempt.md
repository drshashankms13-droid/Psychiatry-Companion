## Exam answer

**Case summary.**
- **Patient:** a 25-year-old doctor.
- **Belief:** for **5 years** she has been **convinced she looks ugly**, that her **nose is large and crooked**, and that it **needs surgery**.
- **Insight:** **unshakeable** despite reassurance from family and many doctors.
- **Referential ideas:** she believes others notice and talk about her nose.
- **Behaviour:** **mirror checking**; **housebound**.
- **Consequences:** **clinical depression**, and a **suicide attempt by hanging last month**.

### 1. Diagnosis
- **Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD), with absent insight / delusional intensity beliefs**:
  - BDD is classed with the **obsessive–compulsive disorders** in DSM-5 and ICD-11 [1];
  - **up to 50%** of BDD cases hold appearance beliefs of **delusional intensity** [1].
- **Comorbid depressive episode:** **80–90%** of people with BDD have comorbid depression [1].
- **High suicide risk:** about **one in three** people with BDD attempt suicide [1]. **She has just made a near-lethal attempt (hanging).**
- **Differential:** a primary delusional disorder (somatic type). However, **delusional-intensity beliefs remain BDD**, and treatment follows BDD lines (below).

### 2. Immediate priorities
- **Safety:** assess suicide risk in detail (intent, plans, access to means, hopelessness). Consider **in-patient care** after a recent hanging attempt.
- **Avoid cosmetic surgery.** Her belief is not open to reassurance, and she is pressing for surgical "correction". The source contains no data on surgery outcomes; the clinical stance is to engage her in psychiatric treatment first.

### 3. Treatment [1]
**Psychological:**
- **CBT** is the initial treatment for BDD, and the first choice in young people (NICE).
- **Poor insight reduces motivation** to engage. Keep reviewing engagement if medication is the only treatment at first.

**Pharmacological:**
- **SSRIs** are the drug treatment. No drug is licensed for BDD, but SSRIs significantly improve **BDD symptoms, suicidality and comorbid depression**.
- **Moderate to severe BDD (as here):** add an SSRI to CBT, which may improve outcome.
- **Delusional intensity does not predict poorer response.** In adult research, patients with delusional appearance beliefs respond to **SSRI monotherapy** as well as non-delusional patients.
- **Antipsychotics are not effective and are not recommended** for BDD, despite the delusional intensity. **Antipsychotic augmentation is not recommended in BDD**, unlike in OCD.
- **Buspirone** may usefully augment the SSRI (no RCT).
- **Dosing:**
  - use OCD-type SSRI regimens: titrate towards the **maximum tolerated dose**;
  - allow **up to 12 weeks** per trial, with **two SSRI trials** before calling it refractory (NICE);
  - **about 40% respond to a second SSRI** in BDD;
  - monitor with the **BDD-YBOCS**.
- **SSRI safety in a young adult:** monitor for **akathisia, increased anxiety and emergent suicidal ideation**. The risk is greatest **under 30, with comorbid depression**, which applies to her [1, ch 3].
- **Refractory BDD:** specialist referral. Clomipramine has OCD evidence; for BDD the source lists no augmentation strategy beyond buspirone.

**Continuation:**
- Continue **at least 6 months after remission** (NICE). Many patients are counselled about **long-term SSRI** use because relapse is common.
- If relapse occurs, see her **urgently** [1].

### 4. Other measures
- Treat **comorbid depression** (the SSRI covers both).
- Psychoeducation for the family; avoid repeated reassurance-seeking.
- Graded return to social and professional activity as the CBT proceeds.

**Conclusion.** This is BDD with delusional-intensity conviction, comorbid depression and a recent near-lethal suicide attempt. Plan:
- prioritise safety (consider admission);
- discourage surgery;
- start an SSRI titrated to a high dose (not an antipsychotic, despite the conviction);
- add CBT as insight allows;
- monitor suicidality closely;
- continue long term.

## In depth

### BDD vs OCD in the source [1]
| Feature | OCD | BDD |
|---|---|---|
| Insight | Variable | Characteristically **poorer**; up to 50% delusional |
| Comorbid depression | Common | **80–90%** |
| Suicide | ×10 completed suicide (untreated OCD) | About **1 in 3 attempt** |
| Antipsychotic augmentation | Low-dose aripiprazole or risperidone | **Not recommended** |
| SSRI response with poor insight | — | Same as with good insight |
