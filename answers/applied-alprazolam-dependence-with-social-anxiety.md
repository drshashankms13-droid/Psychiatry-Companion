## Exam answer

**Case summary.**
- **Patient:** a 35-year-old unmarried engineer.
- **Alprazolam:** started **10 years ago for anxiety**; used **almost daily for 5 years**. It now gives **little relief**.
- **Anxiety:**
  - **panic attacks when performing** in front of others (for example, presentations);
  - **marked discomfort with unfamiliar people**;
  - **restricted social life**; **work severely affected**; he plans to quit his job.
- **Background:** shy and inhibited since childhood; **punitive upbringing**.

### 1. Diagnosis
1. **Social anxiety disorder (social phobia)**, with performance-related panic attacks and avoidance [1, Box 3.10].
2. **Benzodiazepine (alprazolam) dependence / misuse:**
   - **tolerance**: it no longer relieves him, and tolerance can develop after 2–4 weeks;
   - long-term use beyond the recommended **2–4 weeks**;
   - probably **non-iatrogenic or escalating** use; quantify dose and source [1].
3. **Explore:** lifelong **shyness and inhibition** with a punitive upbringing, which suggests enduring avoidant traits. **Depression** (consider the risk around his plan to resign). Other substances.
4. **Benzodiazepine-related symptoms that can mimic psychiatric disorder** (Box 3.11) [1]:
   - **anxiety, phobias, panic, agitation, depression**;
   - **inter-dose withdrawal** (especially with short-acting drugs such as alprazolam);
   - **avoidance and agoraphobia**;
   - cognitive deficits.
   - Part of his worsening anxiety may therefore be **drug-induced or withdrawal-related**.

### 2. Assessment
- **Benzodiazepine history:** daily dose, timing, **inter-dose withdrawal**, escalation, source (prescribed, diverted, illicit or Internet), previous attempts to stop, **withdrawal seizures**.
- **Other substances:** alcohol, opioids, gabapentinoids. Illicit benzodiazepines are often used alongside a primary drug [1].
- **Anxiety:** severity and functional impact; depressive comorbidity; **suicidality**.
- **Consequences:** memory and attention deficits, accidents, **driving** [1].

### 3. Management
**A. Benzodiazepine withdrawal [1]:**
- **Shared decision-making** (NICE). **Do not impose a taper** against his will. Use educational interventions and continuing support.
- **Never stop abruptly**: seizures, possibly fatal, and severe, prolonged withdrawal.
- **Switch to diazepam:**
  - alprazolam is short-acting, and short-acting drugs cause more severe withdrawal;
  - convert to an **equivalent dose of diazepam**, **stepwise**;
  - Cochrane is lukewarm, but switching is commonplace.
- **Hyperbolic tapering:**
  - reduce by a **proportion of the most recent dose** (for example, **5–10% a month** for high-risk patients: use over 6 months, a short half-life drug, past withdrawal);
  - steps get smaller as the dose falls; final doses may be under 1 mg diazepam equivalent;
  - monitor symptoms for 2–4 weeks after each step;
  - if symptoms are significant, **hold the dose or go back** to the last tolerable dose, then taper more slowly.
- **Diazepam guide:** reduce by 5–10 mg/day every 2–4 weeks to 50 mg; by 2–5 mg to 20 mg; by 1–2 mg to 10 mg; then smaller steps.
- **Typical iatrogenic schedule:** 30 mg, then 25, 20, 18, 16, 14, 12, 10 mg every 2 weeks; then 2 mg every 2 weeks.
- **Supports:**
  - **CBT alongside dose reduction** (Cochrane);
  - contingency management and education.
  - **No drug add-on** helps (Cochrane 2018).
  - Do **not** treat withdrawal with another dependence-forming drug, **valproate or buspirone**.
- **Avoid prescribing over 30 mg diazepam-equivalent a day**. Substitute prescribing does not reduce misuse.
- **Watch for withdrawal effects** (Box 3.12):
  - anxiety, **panic**, insomnia, depersonalisation;
  - **akathisia, new suicidality**;
  - seizures (with rapid withdrawal);
  - protracted withdrawal lasting more than a year in some people.

**B. Treat the social anxiety disorder [1, Box 3.10]:**
- **Psychological therapy:** good evidence; **exposure therapy** in social phobia. NICE supports psychological therapy as first line for many patients.
- **First-line drugs:**
  - **SSRIs** up to the maximum licensed dose; if the first fails, **try another SSRI** (meta-analyses support fluvoxamine and citalopram);
  - **venlafaxine MR 75–225 mg/day**.
- **Second line:**
  - **moclobemide** (licensed in the UK; NICE);
  - **phenelzine** (NICE; tyramine restriction);
  - pregabalin 600 mg; olanzapine; gabapentin.
  - **Atenolol** for performance autonomic symptoms is probably not effective in social phobia.
- **Benzodiazepines** (clonazepam) are an option for social phobia on a **prn** basis, but they are **inappropriate here** given his dependence.

**C. Occupational and psychosocial:**
- Discourage **quitting his job** until treatment has had a chance.
- Graded exposure to presentations; support at work.

**Prognosis:** many long-term users need **months or years** to taper. Mental state often **improves after withdrawal settles** [1].

## In depth

### Why alprazolam has failed him [1]
- **Tolerance** to anxiolytic effects develops within weeks.
- Benzodiazepines are somewhat **ineffective long-term** for anxiety. Symptoms on stopping may be withdrawal as much as relapse.
- **Inter-dose withdrawal** with short-acting drugs adds rebound anxiety between doses.
- Adverse effects include **avoidance and agoraphobia**, which may worsen his social restriction.

### Iatrogenic vs non-iatrogenic dependence [1]
| Feature | Iatrogenic | Non-iatrogenic |
|---|---|---|
| Dose | Low daily doses for years | High (often over 100 mg diazepam-equivalent) |
| Source | Prescription | Illicit, diverted, Internet |
| Pattern | Regular | Often intermittent; polysubstance |
| Addiction (craving, compulsion) | Uncommon (about 2% of users) | More likely |
