## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Bipolar disorder has the **highest perinatal relapse risk** of any psychiatric illness:
- about **1 in 5 women** relapse with postpartum psychosis;
- the risk reaches **50%** with prior postpartum episodes, bipolar I or schizoaffective disorder, and family history;
- **after stopping lithium before conception**, postpartum relapse can reach **70%** (Viguera 2000) [1].

At the same time, mood stabilisers include the **most teratogenic drugs in psychiatry** (valproate, carbamazepine, topiramate). The task is to balance **teratogenicity against relapse**, ideally **before conception**.

### General principles [1]
- In **every woman of childbearing potential**:
  - discuss pregnancy, since half are unplanned;
  - **avoid valproate, carbamazepine and topiramate**;
  - if they are unavoidable, ensure **effective long-term contraception** and documented counselling.
- **Plan pre-conception** with perinatal psychiatry.
- **Never stop abruptly** after conception, because abrupt discontinuation sharply increases relapse risk. **Valproate is the exception: stop immediately.**
- **No mood stabiliser is clearly safe.** NICE recommends **mood-stabilising antipsychotics** as the preferred alternative.
- Use the **lowest effective dose and monotherapy**, folic acid, fetal anomaly scanning, and plan delivery and the postpartum period.

### Drug-by-drug

| Drug | Teratogenic risk | Other pregnancy issues | Recommendation [1] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lithium** | **Ebstein's anomaly** and septal defects. Risk is **much smaller than once thought**: about 1 in 1,000 vs 1 in 20,000 in the general population is the classic figure. A 5.6-million-birth study linked Ebstein's to maternal mental illness generally. Peak risk is **2–6 weeks after conception**. Risk is dose-related | Completely crosses the placenta. **Dose needs rise** as total body water increases, then **fall abruptly at delivery**. Possible preterm birth and LGA. **Brain development probably unaffected** | Avoid if possible. **If lithium is what keeps her well, support her to stay on it.** If she is well, consider a gradual stop over 4 weeks (NICE). | **Valproate** | **About 10% major malformations**: neural tube defects (spina bifida), ASD, cleft palate, hypospadias, polydactyly, craniosynostosis, small head. **Dose-related** | **Up to 40% developmental delay** in preschool children; **lower IQ, ASD**, poorer language, memory and executive function and school performance. Pre-eclampsia | **Do not use in women of childbearing potential.** Stop before conception. UK: no initiation under 55 without **two specialists** agreeing. Folic acid does not reverse the risk during pregnancy |
| **Carbamazepine** | Neural tube defects, other malformations (lower than valproate). Dose-related | Neonatal vitamin K deficiency (bleeding) | NICE: do not offer for mental illness in pregnancy. If continued, **low-dose monotherapy** and **vitamin K** for mother and neonate |

| **Oxcarbazepine, pregabalin** | Unclear; small signals | — | Avoid where possible (MHRA pregabalin warning) |

### Treatment by situation (Maudsley Box 7.4; NICE) [1]
- **Long remission, planning pregnancy:**
  - switch to an **antipsychotic**, or taper all treatment before conception and through the first trimester;
  - restart early postpartum if relapse risk is high.
- **Severe illness or rapid relapse after discontinuation:** continue the mood stabiliser after discussion. This does not apply to valproate.
- **Pregnant on lithium:**
  - **well:** taper over 4 weeks;
  - **unwell or high risk:**
    - switch gradually to an antipsychotic; or
    - stop lithium and restart it in the second trimester (if she will not breastfeed and responds best to lithium); or
    - continue lithium if an antipsychotic is unlikely to work.
- **Lithium monitoring:**
  - **levels every 4 weeks, then weekly from 36 weeks**;
  - maintain fluids;
  - **deliver in hospital**;
  - **stop at onset of labour** and check a level 12 hours after the last dose;
  - restart postpartum at the **pre-pregnancy dose**;
  - **second-trimester detailed scan and fetal echo** (ACOG).
- **Valproate:**
  - **taper over 2–4 weeks** once the decision to conceive is made;
  - add **folic acid 5 mg/day**, continued through the first trimester (COPE);
  - if valproate is the only option, obtain **written informed consent**. Maudsley: "difficult to imagine" a case where benefit outweighs risk.
- **Acute mania in pregnancy:** **antipsychotic**; if ineffective, **ECT** (safe in pregnancy; NICE).
- **Bipolar depression in pregnancy:**
  - **CBT** for moderate depression;
  - **SSRI** for more severe depression (with antimanic cover);
  - **lamotrigine** as an option.

### Postpartum
- This is the **highest-risk period**. Consider **prophylaxis immediately after delivery**: lithium or antipsychotic.
- Protect sleep. Use a mother–baby unit if needed.
- **Breastfeeding:**
  - **lithium: advise against** (RID 12–30%; infant levels up to 57% of maternal);
  - **valproate, carbamazepine, lamotrigine:** compatible with infant monitoring (lamotrigine RID 9–18%; watch for rash and apnoea);
  - **olanzapine and quetiapine:** preferred when starting treatment postpartum.

**Conclusion.**
- **Valproate** has no place in women who may conceive.
- **Carbamazepine and topiramate** should be avoided.
- **Lithium** is a **moderate, manageable** cardiac risk, outweighed in many women by its unique protection against relapse.
- **Lamotrigine and antipsychotics** are the safer options.

Each decision should be individualised, made before conception where possible, and documented, with intensive monitoring through pregnancy and the postpartum period.

## In depth

### Background
- The "lithium baby register" (Schou, 1970s) produced the historic estimate of **Ebstein's anomaly risk** (a 400-fold increase), which made lithium feared in pregnancy.
- This was **retrospective, voluntary reporting** with marked selection bias. Later cohorts reduced the estimate drastically.
- Valproate's teratogenicity came mainly from **epilepsy registries** (UK and Ireland, EURAP, NAAED, NEAD). Its neurodevelopmental toxicity was established later. Some data come from women with epilepsy, whose illness carries extra malformation risk, so they may not transfer exactly to psychiatric populations [1].

### Epidemiology of risk
- **Bipolar disorder:** postpartum relapse in about 20% overall (Wesseloo 2016 meta-analysis); about 37% in untreated women [general knowledge].
- **Viguera 2000:** about 70% relapse in women who stopped lithium before conception [1]. Rapid discontinuation (over less than 2 weeks) roughly doubled the risk.
- **Malformation rates by anticonvulsant** (EURAP 2018, general knowledge; check):
  - valproate about 10%;
  - phenobarbital about 6.5%;
  - carbamazepine about 5.5%;
  - lamotrigine about 2.9%;
  - levetiracetam about 2.8%.

### Classification of mood stabilisers in pregnancy
| Risk tier | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Avoid absolutely | Valproate, topiramate |
| Avoid; use with safeguards if essential | Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine |
| Use if clearly needed, with monitoring | Lithium |
| Preferred alternatives | Antipsychotics (olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole), lamotrigine (bipolar depression), ECT |

### Mechanism of teratogenicity
- **Valproate:**
  - **histone deacetylase inhibition** (epigenetic dysregulation);
  - folate antagonism;
  - oxidative stress.

  These explain neural tube defects and neurodevelopmental effects [general knowledge].
- **Carbamazepine:** reactive epoxide metabolite; folate interference.
- **Lithium:** affects cardiac morphogenesis through GSK-3β/Wnt signalling during **weeks 2–6**, when the heart forms [general knowledge].
- **Neural tube** closure is complete by about **day 28 after conception**, and organogenesis by about week 8, often before the pregnancy is known. **Pre-conception** folate and drug choice are therefore what matter [1].

### Clinical features in the neonate
- **Lithium:**
  - floppy infant: hypotonia, lethargy, poor suck, cyanosis;
  - hypothyroidism or goitre;
  - nephrogenic diabetes insipidus;
  - arrhythmias;
  - raised urea and creatinine (when breastfed).
- **Fetal valproate syndrome:**
  - facial dysmorphism (epicanthic folds, flat nasal bridge, long philtrum, thin upper lip);
  - neural tube defects;
  - developmental delay;
  - ASD.
- **Carbamazepine:** neonatal haemorrhage from vitamin K deficiency.

### Assessment before conception
- Illness history:
  - number and severity of episodes;
  - polarity;
  - postpartum episodes;
  - time to relapse after previous discontinuation.
- Response to each drug.
- Family history of postpartum psychosis.
- Contraception; folate status; thyroid and renal function (lithium); levels (lamotrigine baseline).
- Partner involvement; capacity; informed choice; documented risk–benefit discussion.

### Differential: options if she wants to conceive on valproate
1. Taper valproate over 2–4 weeks with **folic acid 5 mg**.
2. Choose a replacement:
   - an antipsychotic with mood-stabilising evidence (quetiapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole); or
   - lithium if she has been lithium-responsive; or
   - lamotrigine if her illness is depression-predominant.
3. Allow **3–6 months of stability** on the new regimen before conception.

### Management of lithium across pregnancy [1]
| Phase | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-conception | Discuss risks; decide continue, taper or switch; check thyroid, renal and calcium |
| First trimester | If continued, use the lowest effective level; consider avoiding high-dose peaks (divided doses); scan and **fetal echo at 18–22 weeks** |
| Second and third trimesters | Levels every 4 weeks, **weekly from 36 weeks**; dose often needs to rise as GFR and body water increase; avoid NSAIDs and dehydration (hyperemesis) |
| Labour | Deliver in hospital; **stop lithium at onset**; IV fluids; check a level 12 hours after the last dose |
| Postpartum | Restart at the **pre-pregnancy dose** within 24–48 hours, as clearance falls abruptly; check level within a week; **avoid breastfeeding**; neonatal check of thyroid, renal and lithium level |

| Study | Finding |

### Critical appraisal
- Lithium risk has been **overestimated historically** because of reporting bias. Modern cohorts suggest an absolute excess of about **1 in 100 cardiac malformations**, many minor.
  - Counselling should give **absolute** numbers, not just relative risks.
- **Confounding:** bipolar illness itself, smoking, alcohol, obesity.
- **Valproate evidence is robust:** dose-response, biological plausibility and replication. It meets Bradford Hill criteria for causation.
- **Lamotrigine:** reassuring, but its antimanic weakness means relapse risk may increase if it replaces lithium in mania-prone women.
- **Antipsychotic alternatives** carry GDM risk and limited prophylactic evidence in pregnancy.
- **Gaps:**
  - few RCTs (unethical);
  - little Indian data;
  - long-term outcomes of lithium exposure (MRI study: no effect on brain development).

### Indian context
- **Valproate prescribing to young women remains common in India**, often without contraception counselling [Guessing; no Indian source in this batch]. Every psychiatrist should screen for this.
- Supports and constraints:
  - lithium-level monitoring is limited outside cities;
  - fetal echocardiography is not universally available;
  - the **MHCA 2017** right to informed consent and to stay with a child under 3;
  - **PCPNDT** and termination law (MTP Amendment Act 2021) for counselling about fetal anomaly [general knowledge].
- **IPS Clinical Practice Guidelines (2022)** [6]:
  - **SGA monotherapy is first-line** in bipolar disorder in pregnancy;
  - add **lithium** if the SGA alone fails, or if she is already well on lithium;
  - **low-dose lamotrigine (below 325 mg/day)** may be continued for bipolar depression;
  - **valproate and carbamazepine are avoided**;
  - **lithium monitoring:** levels **monthly until 34 weeks, then weekly** until delivery; repeat before and 24 hours after delivery; check frequently in the early postpartum weeks;
  - **lamotrigine levels may fall about 50%**; monitor from preconception to 1 month postpartum;
  - relapse is **about 66% if prophylaxis is stopped vs about 25% if continued**;
  - **lithium during breastfeeding** needs infant monitoring and investigations (RID 12–30%).

### Recent advances
- **UK MHRA 2023–24:** valproate not to be started in anyone under 55 (male or female) unless two specialists agree. There is also a possible paternal-exposure signal (neurodevelopmental disorders in children of fathers on valproate), with a precautionary MHRA warning in 2024 [general knowledge; check].
- **Lamotrigine dosing algorithms** using levels across pregnancy (Clark 2013).
- **Pregnancy registries** (NAAED, EURAP) continue to update drug-specific risks.
