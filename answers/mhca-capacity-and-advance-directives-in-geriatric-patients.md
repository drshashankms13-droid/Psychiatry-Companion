## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- MHCA 2017 is built on **autonomy**. Every person, including a person with mental illness, is **presumed to have capacity**. **Advance directives (AD)** and **nominated representatives (NR)** let people direct their care if they lose capacity [1, 2].
- In older adults, **dementia, delirium and late-life depression or psychosis** make questions of capacity, AD and NR especially common [3]. The MHCA definition of mental illness includes "a substantial disorder of thinking, mood, perception, **orientation or memory**" [1], so the Act applies (application).

### Capacity under MHCA (Section 4) [1]
- **Presumption:** every person "shall be deemed to have capacity" if able to:
  - **(a) understand** the information relevant to a decision on treatment, admission or personal assistance; **or**
  - **(b) appreciate** any reasonably foreseeable consequence of a decision or lack of decision; **or**
  - **(c) communicate** the decision by speech, expression, gesture or any other means.
- **s4(2):** information must be given in **simple language**, sign language, **visual aids** or other means the person understands.
- **s4(3):** a decision that others think **inappropriate or wrong** does not by itself mean lack of capacity.
- **s14(8):** having an NR, or being unable to appoint one, does **not** mean lack of capacity.
- **s14(9):** all persons with mental illness have capacity but **may need varying support**.

**Clinical capacity assessment in dementia [3]:**
- **Decision-specific and time-specific.** A patient may decide what to eat for lunch but not complex finances.
- **Four components (MacCAT-T, "UARC"):** **U**nderstanding, **A**ppreciation, **R**easoning, **C**ommunicating a consistent choice.
- "**Diagnosis of dementia does NOT automatically mean lack of capacity.**" Assess each decision separately.
- **Note:** MHCA's three abilities are joined by "**or**", so any one is enough. Math et al. argue this lets many patients refuse treatment, and call for "**and**" plus a capacity-assessment guidance document [2].

**Application to older adults:**
- **Fluctuation** (delirium, Lewy body dementia) means capacity should be reassessed over time. This follows from the Act's **time-specific** framing and from s89(8), which requires capacity review **every 7 days** when the NR consents.
- **Sensory impairment:** use s4(2) adaptations such as visual aids and simple language.
- **Communication** by gesture counts under s4(1)(c).

### Advance directives (Chapter III, ss 5–13) [1]
| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| **5(1)** | Any adult (not a minor) may write an AD stating (a) how they wish to be cared for and treated; (b) how they wish **not** to be treated; (c) NR(s) in order of precedence |
| **5(2)** | May be made **irrespective of past mental illness or treatment** |
| **5(3)** | Invoked **only when capacity is lost**; effective until capacity is regained |
| **5(4)** | A **current capacitous decision overrides** the earlier AD |
| **5(5)** | An AD contrary to law is **void ab initio** |
| **6** | Made in the manner specified by **Central Authority regulations** |
| **7** | Each **Board** keeps an **online register** and makes ADs available to MHPs |
| **8** | Can be **revoked, amended or cancelled** at any time, by the same procedure |
| **9** | Does **not apply to emergency treatment**. The source copy cross-refers "section 103"; emergency treatment itself is **s94** |
| **10** | **Duty** of the medical officer in charge and the treating psychiatrist to treat according to a valid AD, subject to s11 |
| **11** | An MHP, relative or carer who wishes not to follow it must apply to the **Board** |
| **12** | Central Authority reviews the use and procedure of ADs |
| **13** | No liability for **unforeseen consequences** of following a valid AD, or for not following one if **no copy was given** |

**Grounds on which the Board may uphold, modify or cancel an AD (s11(2)) [1]:**
1. Was it made of **free will**, without force, **undue influence or coercion**?
2. Did the person intend it to apply to the **present circumstances**?
3. Was the person **sufficiently well informed**?
4. Did the person **have capacity** when making it?
5. Is it **contrary to other laws** or the Constitution?
- **s11(3):** the maker and the NR must ensure the AD is **accessible** to clinicians.

**Application to older adults:**
- **Make the AD early.** An AD must be made **while capacity is intact**. In a progressive dementia, that is the window after diagnosis. The notebook stresses **advance care planning**, which "reduces emergency decision-making burden", and urgent **financial power of attorney** while capacity is partly present [3].
- **Undue influence:** the s11(2)(a) test matters where an older adult depends on family or carers [1] (application).
- **Changed circumstances:** s11(2)(b) covers an AD written for one illness when a different condition or physical comorbidity now applies. Math et al. ask "which mental illness? … if it becomes comorbid with a physical illness? What severity?" [2]
- **Scope:** the MHCA AD covers **mental healthcare** only. Financial and estate decisions need separate instruments: **lasting power of attorney**, **guardianship**, **testamentary capacity** [3].

### Nominated representative (ss 14–17) [1]
- Any adult may appoint an NR in **writing on plain paper**, signed or thumb-printed. The NR must be an adult, competent, and give **written consent** (s14(1)–(3)).
- **Default order if none appointed (s14(4)):**
  1. the NR named in the AD;
  2. a **relative**;
  3. a **care-giver**;
  4. a suitable person appointed by the Board;
  5. the **Director, Department of Social Welfare**.
  - A registered NGO may act **temporarily**.
- **NR duties (s17):**
  - consider **current and past wishes, life history, values, cultural background** and **best interests**;
  - give credence to the person's views as far as they understand;
  - **support** decisions under ss 89–90;
  - seek information; join discharge planning; apply to the Board on rights violations.

**Application:** for an older person with no relative, the **care-giver** or the Board's nominee steps in. This is relevant for isolated elderly people.

### When capacity is lost: treatment pathways [1]
- **Supported admission (s89, up to 30 days):** high support needs; the person cannot decide independently. Treatment follows the **AD if any**, or informed consent with NR support. If nearly 100% support is needed, the **NR may consent temporarily**; **capacity is reviewed every 7 days** (s89(6)–(8)). Admission must be the **least restrictive option**, taking the AD into account (s89(1)(b)).
- **Emergency treatment (s94):**
  - any registered medical practitioner, with **NR consent where available**;
  - to prevent death or irreversible harm, serious harm to self or others, or serious property damage;
  - limited to **72 hours** (7 days in a declared disaster);
  - **no ECT**;
  - the AD **does not apply**.
- **Community care (s18):** services close to home; no person, "including children and **older persons**", should have to travel long distances.

### Telepsychiatry and dementia [4]
- Follow-up consultations in the patient's absence normally need a **formal authorisation letter** from the patient.
- **Exception:** if the psychiatrist has documented **dementia**, its severity as **moderate or severe**, and **loss of capacity to consent**, and can recognise the family member, the follow-up may proceed **without the letter**.
- Clinicians must **check for an AD and NR** and follow them.

### Criticisms relevant to geriatric practice [2]
- **"Or" in s4** sets a low threshold for capacity.
- **Repeated revocation:** a person may revoke or amend an AD many times. Only the **Board** can override, and this must happen within **24–48 hours** to allow treatment.
- **Resources:** there are too few medical and judicial resources. A Cochrane review found **too few data** to recommend ADs.
- **Family:** in India the family is the natural carer. ADs and NRs may cause **patient–family disharmony** or even abandonment.

**Conclusion.**
- In older patients, MHCA requires clinicians to:
  - **presume capacity**;
  - assess it **decision by decision** and repeatedly;
  - give information in an accessible way;
  - honour a **valid AD** (except in emergencies), or go to the Board if they wish not to follow it.
- Early diagnosis of dementia should prompt **timely AD and NR appointment** and separate financial and legal planning.
- Implementation problems remain: the low capacity threshold, Board delays, and family dynamics.

## In depth

### Legal instruments for older adults with cognitive impairment (NB-16, Table 14.2) [3]
| Instrument | Use |
|---|---|
| Advance directive / living will | Instructions for future care when capacity is lost |
| Lasting power of attorney | Proxy decision-maker for finances and/or health |
| Guardianship | Court-appointed when capacity is lost and there is no LPA |
| Testamentary capacity | Valid will: knows the estate, knows the heirs, understands making a will, no delusions affecting the will |
