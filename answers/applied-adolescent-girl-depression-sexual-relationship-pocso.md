## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A **16-year-old girl** is brought by her mother.
- For a few months: **irritability**, **declining school performance**, **loss of interest** in activities she used to enjoy.
- Recently: **wishes to die**.
- She privately tells the doctor she is **romantically involved with a 20-year-old neighbour** and they have had **physical intimacy on a few occasions**.

### Clinical formulation
- **Probable moderate depressive episode** (ICD-11 6A70). In adolescents depression often presents with **irritability** rather than sadness.
  - Features: anhedonia, functional decline, death wishes.
  - **Suicide risk must be assessed urgently**.
- **Possible contributors:**
  - relationship stress (secrecy, conflict, coercion, breakup);
  - guilt or fear of discovery;
  - academic pressure;
  - family conflict;
  - **possible exploitation or abuse** (a 4-year age gap; neighbour; power imbalance);
  - pregnancy fears; STI;
  - substance use; bullying; online exposure.
- **Legal dimension:**
  - under the **POCSO Act 2012** the **age of consent is 18**. Any sexual act with her is an offence **regardless of her consent**;
  - **penetrative** contact would be "penetrative sexual assault" (Section 3; for a child aged 16–18, minimum 10 years);
  - **Section 19 makes reporting mandatory** for anyone with knowledge, including doctors;
  - **failure to report is punishable (Section 21)** [1, 2].

### (a) Further assessment
1. **Suicide risk assessment (priority):**
   - ideation, intent, plans, means (pesticides, medicines, height);
   - previous attempts; self-harm;
   - hopelessness;
   - triggers (relationship conflict, fear of exposure);
   - protective factors;
   - **C-SSRS**.
2. **Depression:**
   - onset, duration, severity;
   - biological symptoms;
   - psychotic features;
   - **bipolarity** (family history, hypomanic episodes);
   - substance use;
   - **PHQ-A / CDRS-R**.
3. **The relationship (sensitively, alone with her; HEEADSSS framework):**
   - nature and timeline;
   - who initiated;
   - **coercion, grooming, threats, blackmail, image sharing, alcohol or drug use**;
   - whether the intimacy was penetrative;
   - contraception;
   - **pregnancy risk** (last menstrual period);
   - STI symptoms;
   - whether he has other relationships with minors;
   - her feelings about him (attachment vs fear).
4. **Other trauma:** previous abuse; domestic violence; online exploitation.
5. **Family:** relationship with her mother and father; how they would react (risk of honour-based violence, forced marriage, school withdrawal); family psychiatric history.
6. **School:** academic decline; bullying; peer relationships.
7. **Physical:** examination as appropriate; **pregnancy test**; STI screening (with consent); thyroid, CBC (rule out medical causes of fatigue).
8. **Capacity and maturity:** her understanding of the situation and preferences (MHCA; Gillick-type maturity is not a legal standard in India, but her views matter).

### (b) The ethical–legal dilemma
| Value | Tension |
| --- | --- |
| **Confidentiality and trust** | She disclosed in confidence; reporting may rupture the alliance and increase suicide risk |
| **Mandatory reporting (Section 19)** | Doctors have no exemption; failure to report is punishable (Section 21) |
| **Autonomy and adolescent sexuality** | She may see the relationship as consensual. About 1 in 4 POCSO cases are "romantic" [1] |
| **Protection** | A 20-year-old adult and a 16-year-old: possible exploitation; she is in a vulnerable mental state |
| **Beneficence / non-maleficence** | Reporting may bring family conflict, police involvement, stigma and secondary victimisation; not reporting may leave abuse unaddressed |

**Recommended approach:**
1. **Prioritise safety:** treat suicide risk first. If risk is high, admit (MHCA Section 87 for minors, with the nominated representative/guardian).
2. **Be transparent with her:**
   - explain the **limits of confidentiality** early;
   - say that the law requires reporting sexual activity involving minors;
   - say what will happen next, and that you will support her through it.
   - **Do not promise secrecy.**
3. **Report to the SJPU or local police** as Section 19 requires. Document the disclosure verbatim, the reasons and the actions taken. The police inform the **CWC within 24 hours** (JJ Act) [1, 3].
4. **Involve her mother** in a planned way, with the girl's participation, to prevent an explosive reaction. Psychoeducation: blame and punishment worsen depression and suicide risk.
5. **Request a support person** from the CWC (Rule 4), and ask the police for sensitive handling: a woman officer, not in uniform, statement at a place of her choice (Sections 24–26) [2].
6. **Medical care:**
   - emergency contraception if within the window;
   - pregnancy testing;
   - STI prophylaxis or testing;
   - if pregnant: **MTP Act** options (the provider must report under POCSO, but the Supreme Court has allowed the minor's identity to be withheld in the report) [1; general knowledge].
7. **Continue therapeutic engagement:** the same clinician, regular reviews through the legal process.

### (c) Treatment of depression
- **Psychological:** **CBT** or **IPT-A** (interpersonal therapy for adolescents fits relationship stressors well); family therapy; problem-solving; safety planning.
- **Pharmacological** (moderate to severe, or not responding to therapy): **fluoxetine** first-line (10 mg, then 20 mg); escitalopram as second-line.
  - Monitor for **suicidality in the early weeks** (black-box warning).
  - Weekly review initially.
- **Safety plan:**
  - restrict means (medicines locked; pesticides);
  - crisis contacts (**Tele-MANAS 14416**);
  - family supervision.
- **School:** coordinate support; exam accommodations.
- **Follow-up:** relapse prevention; bipolar switch monitoring; the impact of court processes.

**Conclusion.** This is **adolescent depression with suicidal ideation** in the context of a **sexual relationship that is legally an offence under POCSO**. The psychiatrist must:
- **secure safety first**;
- **report as required**, transparently and supportively;
- coordinate with the CWC, police, family and gynaecology;
- treat the depression with psychotherapy, with or without fluoxetine.

The case shows the **tension between mandatory reporting and adolescent confidentiality and autonomy**, a recognised criticism of POCSO.

## In depth

### Background
- **Adolescent depression:** about 8% prevalence. Irritability may replace sad mood (DSM). Suicide is a leading cause of death among Indian adolescents and young adults [general knowledge].
- **POCSO "romantic cases":** about a quarter of cases. In nearly half, the girl was 16–18. Families often file complaints against boys [1].

### Epidemiology
- NFHS-4: 11% of girls had intercourse before 15, and 39% before 18 [1].
- NMHS 13–17-year-olds: depressive disorder about 0.8%; any mental disorder 7.3% [general knowledge; check].

### Classification and criteria
- ICD-11 depressive episode: at least 2 weeks of depressed mood or diminished interest, plus other symptoms. In adolescents, irritable mood can count (DSM-5-TR).
- Severity by symptoms and impairment.

### Mechanism
- Stress–diathesis:
  - interpersonal stressors (romantic relationships are a leading trigger in adolescent depression);
  - genetic vulnerability;
  - pubertal hormones;
  - sleep disruption.

### Clinical features in detail
- Adolescent presentation: irritability, academic decline, social withdrawal, somatic complaints, risky behaviour, substance use.

### Assessment in detail: HEEADSSS
- Home, Education/employment, Eating, Activities, Drugs, Sexuality, Suicide/depression, Safety. Interview the adolescent **alone** for part of the session.

### Differential diagnosis
- Adjustment disorder.
- **Bipolar depression** (screen; family history).
- PTSD (if coercion or abuse).
- Substance-induced mood disorder.
- Hypothyroidism, anaemia.
- Early psychosis.

### Management details: evidence
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| TADS (2004) (general knowledge) | Fluoxetine + CBT best at 12 weeks; CBT reduced suicidal events |
| IPT-A trials (Mufson) (general knowledge) | Effective for adolescent depression |
| POCSO seminar | Romantic-case data; reporting dilemmas [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- **Mandatory reporting without discretion** may deter adolescents from seeking health care, including sexual and mental health care [1].
- **Law Commission (2023)** recommended guided judicial discretion for 16–18-year-olds [general knowledge; check]. Several High Courts have acquitted in consensual cases.
- The clinician cannot change the law, but should **minimise harm** within it.

### Indian context
- **MHCA 2017:** minors' admission (Section 87) requires the nominated representative and a Board review. Consent for treatment comes from the guardian, with the adolescent's views considered.
- **JJ Act:** the CWC may intervene if the child is in need of care and protection (risk at home) [3].
- **Tele-MANAS** and school counselling.

### Recent advances
- **Digital safety planning apps.**
- **Brief contact interventions** after suicidal crises.
- Advocacy for adolescent-sensitive amendments to POCSO.
