## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- **Chlorpromazine (1952)** began the antipsychotic era. **Clozapine's** remarketing in **1990** launched the "atypical" or **second-generation antipsychotics (SGAs)**:
  - risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, ziprasidone, amisulpride, aripiprazole;
  - later lurasidone, asenapine, iloperidone, brexpiprazole, cariprazine, lumateperone.
- They were marketed with claims of **greater efficacy (especially for negative and cognitive symptoms), fewer EPS and better adherence** [1].
- **Maudsley:** the typical/atypical (and FGA/SGA) split is "defined by nothing other than time of introduction". Clozapine was synthesised in 1959 and olanzapine patented in 1971. **Neuroscience-based Nomenclature (NbN)** is recommended instead [1].

### What "atypical" was supposed to mean [1]
- **Low EPS liability:** really true only of **clozapine and possibly quetiapine**. For others, EPS is **dose-related**. The real difference is "the ease with which a dose can be chosen … that is effective but does not cause EPS" (compare haloperidol with olanzapine). **Risperidone at 16 mg is as "typical" as a drug can be.**
- **No common pharmacology or chemistry**, except a general (not universal) preference for D2 receptors outside the striatum and **5-HT2A > D2 antagonism** (Meltzer).
- **Not free of hyperprolactinaemia:** risperidone, paliperidone and amisulpride are worse than many FGAs.

### Comparison
| Domain | FGAs (conventional) | SGAs (newer) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Efficacy (positive symptoms)** | Effective | **As a class, no consistent advantage**, except **clozapine** (refractory illness) and small advantages for **olanzapine, amisulpride, risperidone** [1] |
| **Negative and cognitive symptoms** | Little effect; may worsen secondary negative symptoms (EPS, dysphoria) | Modest benefit, much of it via **less secondary negative symptoms**; clozapine best for negative symptoms (Huhn 2019) [1] |
| **Depressive symptoms** | "Neuroleptic dysphoria" | Better: sulpiride, clozapine, amisulpride, olanzapine, partial agonists [1] |
| **Refractory schizophrenia** | — | **Clozapine is the drug of choice** (30–60% of resistant patients respond) [1] |
| **Suicide** | — | Clozapine reduces suicidal behaviour (InterSePT) [general knowledge] |
| **EPS and tardive dyskinesia** | **High** (especially high-potency drugs) | **Lower** as a class; TD risk about a third (annual about 3% vs 5–8%) [general knowledge] |
| **NMS** | Higher incidence and mortality | Lower; atypical presentations [1] |
| **Metabolic** (weight, diabetes, dyslipidaemia) | Low–moderate (low-potency phenothiazines higher) | **High with clozapine and olanzapine**; moderate with quetiapine and risperidone; low with aripiprazole, lurasidone, ziprasidone [1] |
| **Prolactin** | Raised | Varies: **risperidone, paliperidone and amisulpride highest**; aripiprazole lowers it [1] |
| **QTc** | Thioridazine, pimozide, IV haloperidol | Ziprasidone, sertindole |
| **Sedation and anticholinergic** | Low-potency drugs | Clozapine, olanzapine, quetiapine |
| **Cost** | Cheap | More expensive (now mostly generic) |
| **LAIs** | Fluphenazine, haloperidol, flupentixol, zuclopenthixol decanoates | Paliperidone (1-, 3-, 6-monthly), aripiprazole, risperidone, olanzapine pamoate; **better-defined dose ranges** [1] |

### Have atypicals made a difference?
**The case that they have made less difference than claimed [1]:**
1. **CATIE (2005):**
   - **74% discontinued** within 18 months;
   - **perphenazine (an FGA) was as effective** as risperidone, quetiapine and ziprasidone;
   - olanzapine had the longest time to discontinuation but the most metabolic harm.
2. **CUtLASS-1 (2006):** **no quality-of-life advantage** for SGAs over FGAs over a year.
3. **WPA review (Tandon 2008, 51 FGAs vs 11 SGAs):** if EPS are minimised by careful dosing, **no convincing advantage** for SGAs.
4. **EUFEST (2008)**, first-episode: haloperidol had the highest discontinuation, but efficacy was **similar** [general knowledge].
5. **Leucht 2009 (Lancet) meta-analysis:** only **clozapine, amisulpride, olanzapine and risperidone** beat FGAs. **SGAs are not a homogeneous class.**
6. **Metabolic syndrome** became a major cause of morbidity and mortality: the **trade-off** of EPS for metabolic harm.
7. **Placebo response has risen** in trials since 1970, and newer drugs show lower apparent efficacy [1].
8. **Mortality gap** (15–20 years) has **not narrowed** [general knowledge].

**The case that they have made a real difference:**
1. **Clozapine** transformed treatment-resistant schizophrenia and suicide prevention [1].
2. **Fewer EPS and much less tardive dyskinesia** improved tolerability, dignity and stigma (no "neuroleptic look").
3. **Better subjective well-being** and adherence for many patients. Less neuroleptic dysphoria [1].
4. **Choice:** a range of profiles allows **individualised prescribing**. Aripiprazole, for example, is prolactin-sparing for young women; lurasidone is metabolically neutral.
5. **Extended indications:** bipolar mania, depression and maintenance; TRD augmentation; PTSD and other conditions.
6. **Long-term data** may favour olanzapine (Leucht 2023 NMA) [1].
7. **SGA LAIs** changed the "punitive" image of depots. A logical case can be made for LAIs as **first-line** [1].

### Choosing an antipsychotic today [1]
- **Patient choice** and **individual properties**, not class (NICE CG178).
- Consider:
  - previous response;
  - **adverse-effect priorities** (metabolic, prolactin, EPS, sedation, QTc);
  - comorbidity;
  - adherence (**LAI**);
  - cost.
- Early use of **clozapine** after 2 failed trials (olanzapine should probably be one of them).

**Conclusion.** As a class, **SGAs are not more effective than FGAs** (with the exceptions of **clozapine** and small advantages for olanzapine, amisulpride and risperidone). Their main gain is **less EPS and tardive dyskinesia**, at the price of **metabolic and prolactin harms** in some drugs. The FGA/SGA dichotomy is scientifically weak. What has really "made a difference" is **clozapine**, **individualised drug choice**, better-tolerated **LAIs**, and attention to physical health, not "atypicality" itself.

## In depth

### Background
- **Timeline:**
  - chlorpromazine (1952), haloperidol (1958);
  - **clozapine** (1959 synthesis; withdrawn after agranulocytosis deaths in Finland in 1975; **Kane 1988** RCT; reintroduced 1990);
  - risperidone (1993), olanzapine (1996), quetiapine (1997), ziprasidone (2001), aripiprazole (2002);
  - "third-generation" partial agonists (see `third-generation-antipsychotics-and-novel-targets`).
- **Typicality** was originally defined by catalepsy in rodents and EPS at therapeutic doses [1].

### Epidemiology / use
- SGAs account for the vast majority of prescriptions worldwide. FGAs remain important in India for cost (haloperidol, trifluoperazine, chlorpromazine; depot fluphenazine) [general knowledge].

### Classification
| System | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Chemical | Phenothiazines (aliphatic, piperidine, piperazine), thioxanthenes, butyrophenones, diphenylbutylpiperidines, benzamides [1] |
| Typical/atypical; FGA/SGA | EPS liability; date [1] |
| Receptor-based | D2 antagonists; SDAs (serotonin–dopamine antagonists); **D2/D3 partial agonists**; MARTAs |
| **NbN** | Pharmacological domain and mode of action [1] |
| **Binding-profile clusters (2023)** | Muscarinic-high (olanzapine, quetiapine); low D2 antagonism (partial agonists, lurasidone); serotonergic (risperidone); pure D2 (amisulpride). These map to adverse effects best [1] |

### Mechanism
- **D2 occupancy:** antipsychotic effect at 65–80%; EPS above 80%; prolactin above 72% [general knowledge].
- **5-HT2A antagonism** (Meltzer ratio) releases striatal dopamine, which reduces EPS.
- **Fast-off-D2 hypothesis** (Kapur & Seeman): loose binding (clozapine, quetiapine) means less EPS.
- **Partial agonism:** functional stabilisation.
- **Clozapine's superiority** is unexplained [1].

### Clinical features: adverse-effect profiles at a glance [1, Maudsley "Relative adverse effects"]
- **Highest weight gain:** clozapine, olanzapine.
- **Prolactin:** amisulpride, risperidone, paliperidone.
- **EPS:** haloperidol and high-potency FGAs.
- **Sedation:** clozapine, chlorpromazine, quetiapine.
- **QTc:** sertindole, ziprasidone, IV haloperidol, pimozide.

### Assessment
- **Physical monitoring** before and during treatment: weight, BMI, waist, BP, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, prolactin, ECG [1].
- **GASS** (patient-rated) and ANNSERS (clinician-rated) for adverse effects [1].

### Differential: choosing between drugs (examples)
| Patient | Reasonable choice |
| --- | --- |
| Young woman, prolactin concerns | Aripiprazole, quetiapine, lurasidone |
| Obese, diabetic | Aripiprazole, lurasidone, ziprasidone, amisulpride |
| Non-adherent | LAI (paliperidone, aripiprazole, or FGA depot) |
| Treatment-resistant | Clozapine |
| Prominent EPS with FGA | Quetiapine, clozapine |
| Cost-limited setting (India) | Haloperidol/trifluoperazine at low dose, olanzapine, risperidone (generics) |

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| CATIE (Lieberman 2005, *NEJM*) | 74% discontinuation; perphenazine comparable; olanzapine marginally better but metabolic harm [1] |
| CUtLASS-1 (Jones 2006) | No QoL advantage of SGAs [1] |
| Tandon 2008 (WPA) | No convincing SGA advantage if EPS minimised [1] |
| Leucht 2009, *Lancet* | Only 4 SGAs beat FGAs [1] |
| Leucht 2013, *Lancet* (15 drugs) | Clozapine, amisulpride, olanzapine ranked highest [1] |
| Huhn 2019, *Lancet* (32 drugs) | Amisulpride best for positive symptoms; clozapine best overall and for negative symptoms [1] |
| Zhu 2017 (first episode) | Small advantages for olanzapine and amisulpride; haloperidol poor [1] |
| Siskind 2016 | Clozapine better than other antipsychotics in TRS [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- **Trial design bias:** early SGA trials compared against **high-dose haloperidol** without anticholinergics, which inflated SGA advantages.
- **Industry funding** and **publication bias**.
- **Short trials** (6–8 weeks) miss long-term metabolic harm and TD.
- **Heterogeneity within classes** makes class comparisons misleading [1].

### Indian context
- **Cost and access:** FGAs and depot fluphenazine are cheap and on the DMHP essential list. Generic SGAs (olanzapine, risperidone) are now inexpensive [general knowledge].
- **Metabolic vulnerability of South Asians** (central obesity, diabetes) makes metabolically neutral choices important.
- **Clozapine under-use** in India because of blood monitoring and cost [general knowledge].
- **Telepsychiatry Operational Guidelines 2020:** List A (first consult) includes haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine; most other SGAs are List B.

### Recent advances
- **Non-D2 mechanisms:**
  - **xanomeline–trospium (Cobenfy; FDA 2024)**: muscarinic M1/M4 agonist;
  - pimavanserin (5-HT2A inverse agonist);
  - TAAR1 agonists (ulotaront; phase 3 negative) [1, general knowledge].
- **Long-acting injectables** at 6-monthly intervals (paliperidone).
- **Clozapine pharmacogenetics.**
