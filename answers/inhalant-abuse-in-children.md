## Exam answer

### Definition/substances

Inhalant use is intentional inhalation of volatile substances or gases for psychoactive effects. Agents include solvents (glues, paints, thinners, petrol), aerosols, gases, and nitrites. Low cost and accessibility increase risk among vulnerable children and adolescents.

### Risk factors

Poverty, street-connectedness, family conflict, neglect, school dropout, peer use, trauma, easy access, and co-occurring psychiatric or substance-use problems. Avoid stereotyping: use occurs in diverse settings.

### Clinical features

Acute intoxication may cause euphoria, disinhibition, dizziness, slurred speech, ataxia, nausea, confusion, hallucinations, or reduced consciousness. Complications include hypoxia, aspiration, arrhythmias, seizures, trauma, and sudden death ("sudden sniffing death"). Chronic effects vary by agent and may include cognitive impairment, memory problems, peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar dysfunction, hearing loss, renal/hepatic injury, and white-matter damage.

### Assessment

Stabilize first: airway, breathing, circulation, oxygenation, temperature, glucose, mental state, trauma, and ECG when indicated. Ask nonjudgmentally about product, route, frequency, last use, and co-ingestants. Chemical odour, residue, and perioral dermatitis may be clues but are neither sensitive nor specific. Investigations depend on presentation (electrolytes, renal/liver tests, blood gas, ECG, toxicology advice). Routine urine screens often do not detect inhalants.

### Management

- Emergency care is supportive: oxygen/ventilation, treatment of arrhythmias or seizures, and management of trauma/metabolic complications. Seek toxicology advice.
- Assess SUD severity, suicidality, cognition, trauma, exploitation, family safety, and comorbidity.
- Use motivational interviewing, developmentally appropriate CBT, family work, school liaison, and case management. There is no specific antidote for most inhalants and no established medication specifically for inhalant use disorder.
- Address safeguarding, homelessness, neglect, and access to safe activities; coordinate child-protection and community services.

### Conclusion

Inhalants can cause fatal acute toxicity and lasting neurological injury. Early recognition, stabilization, compassionate treatment, and social protection are essential.
