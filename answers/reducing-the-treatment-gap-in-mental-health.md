## Exam answer

**Definition.** The **treatment gap** is the **proportion of people with a disorder who need care but do not receive it**. It was popularised by Kohn et al. (WHO Bulletin, 2004) [general knowledge]. Some writers add:
- a **quality or "effective coverage" gap:** people receive care, but not minimally adequate care;
- a **"functional treatment gap" in primary care:** patients with psychiatric disorders presenting with physical symptoms receive inadequate or inappropriate treatment [2].

### Magnitude
- **Global (mhGAP 2008):** above **75%** in many low- and middle-income countries; **35–50%** in high-income countries [1].
- **India (NMHS 2015–16):**
  - **any mental disorder 84.5%**;
  - **common mental disorders 85%**;
  - **severe mental disorders 73.6%**;
  - **substance use disorders 91%** (tobacco 91.8%; alcohol 86.3%);
  - epilepsy 31% [2; `national-mental-health-survey-india`].
- **Burden:** mental disorders are **5 of the 10 leading causes of disability** and nearly **a third of years lived with disability** worldwide [1].

### Causes of the gap (Tanahashi framework: availability → accessibility → acceptability → contact → effectiveness)
| Level | Barriers |
| --- | --- |
| **Supply / health system** | Few psychiatrists (about 0.3 per 100,000 in India), psychologists and PSWs; services concentrated in cities and tertiary centres; poor integration with primary care; drug stock-outs; low budget (about 1% of health budget); **mental health absent from the public-health agenda**; weak public mental health leadership [1] |
| **Demand / community** | Low mental health literacy; **stigma**; explanatory models (supernatural, karma); preference for faith healers; mental illness **not a "felt need"** until people are aware and see treatment working [1]; cost of travel and lost wages |
| **Quality** | Short consultations; inappropriate prescribing; no psychosocial care; poor follow-up and continuity |
| **Social determinants** | Poverty, gender, homelessness, conflict, disability |

### Strategies to reduce the gap
**1. Policy, law and financing:**
- **National Mental Health Policy 2014**; **MHCA 2017**:
  - **right to access** mental health care (Section 18) at all government facilities, with **basic and emergency care at CHCs and upwards**;
  - **free care** for people below the poverty line and the homeless;
  - **community living** (Section 19) [2].
- **Ring-fenced budgets** [1]; insurance parity (MHCA Section 21(4)); **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY** packages.

**2. Integrate mental health into primary and general health care:**
- **DMHP** (Bellary model, 1985–90): training PHC staff, **6 essential drugs at PHCs**, simple records, monthly reporting, district team supervision [2]. Now in about 700+ districts [general knowledge; check].
- **Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** (Health and Wellness Centres) include mental, neurological and substance-use (MNS) care in the comprehensive primary health care package.
- **Primary Care Psychiatry Programme (NIMHANS):**
  - **Diploma in Primary Care Psychiatry** (1-year, digitally driven);
  - **Clinical Schedule for Primary Care Psychiatry**;
  - tele-on-consultation training;
  - outcome measures: Translational Quotient and PCP quotient [2].
- **mhGAP Intervention Guide** for non-specialists.

**3. Task-sharing:**
- Lay health workers, ASHAs, ANMs, counsellors.
- Evidence from India:
  - **MANAS** (Goa; lay counsellors for CMDs; Patel 2010);
  - **Healthy Activity Program** and **Counselling for Alcohol Problems (PREMIUM; Patel 2017)**;
  - **COPSI** (community care for schizophrenia: modest reduction in disability) [1];
  - **Thinking Healthy Programme** (perinatal depression).

**4. Community-based care and rehabilitation:**
- **CBR** (WHO 2005/2010 guidelines; matrix of health, education, livelihood, social and empowerment) [1].
- **Barwani CBR** (Chatterjee 2003, 2009): CBR reduced disability more than outpatient care [1].
- **Thirthahalli experience:** 60–80% returned to optimal functioning with adequate treatment; 20–30% needed community-supported rehabilitation; 1–10% needed institutional rehabilitation [1].
- **Jagalur (Davangere) taluk project** (NIMHANS with partners):
  - about 300 patients identified and treated;
  - disability certification and benefits;
  - weak multisectoral links [1].
- **Karnataka DMHP innovations** [2]:
  - **Manochaitanya ("Super Tuesdays")** taluk clinics;
  - **Maanasadhara** day care;
  - **Manasakendras** half-way homes;
  - **Assisted Home Care**;
  - e-monitoring.
- **Lesson:** community initiatives succeed only when **families and the community are involved** (Thara 2008) [1].

**5. Digital and tele-mental health:**
- **Tele-MANAS (14416)**, launched in 2022: 24 × 7, in 20 languages, linked to DMHP.
- **e-Sanjeevani.**
- Earlier models [2]:
  - **SCARF STEP** (Pudukkottai);
  - **PGI "Psychiatrists on web"**;
  - **Ganiyari** (Jan Swasthya Sahyog, Chhattisgarh);
  - **NIMHANS hub-and-spoke**.
- **Telepsychiatry Practice Guidelines 2020.**

**6. Human resources:**
- more postgraduate seats;
- **Centres of Excellence** (NMHP Scheme A) and PG department strengthening (Scheme B);
- **NIMHANS Digital Academy**;
- training of general physicians;
- nursing and psychology workforce.

**7. Reduce stigma and raise awareness:**
- mass media campaigns;
- contact-based interventions;
- school mental health (**RKSK, School Health and Wellness Programme**);
- **involve users, families and communities** [1].

**8. Target high-gap groups:**
- **Substance use:** NAPDDR, Drug Treatment Clinics, tobacco cessation centres.
- **Perinatal:** screening in antenatal care.
- **Children:** RBSK, DEIC.
- **Elderly:** NPHCE.
- **Homeless people with mental illness:**
  - MHCA Section 100(7) (police FIR and tracing family);
  - free care;
  - home-again models (Banyan) [2].
- **Suicide prevention:** **National Suicide Prevention Strategy 2022**; **MHCA Section 115** decriminalisation [2].

**9. Monitor and research:**
- repeat NMHS;
- **effective coverage indicators**;
- implementation research [1].

**WHO's ten recommendations (World Health Report 2001):**
1. Provide treatment in primary care.
2. Make psychotropic drugs available.
3. Give care in the community.
4. Educate the public.
5. Involve communities, families and consumers.
6. Establish national policies, programmes and legislation.
7. Develop human resources.
8. Link with other sectors.
9. Monitor community mental health.
10. Support more research [1].

**Conclusion.** India's treatment gap of 70–90% stems from **scarce, centralised services, stigma, low awareness and weak integration**. Closing it requires:
- **integration into primary care** (DMHP, Ayushman Arogya Mandirs), **task-sharing**, **community-based care and rehabilitation**, and **digital outreach** (Tele-MANAS);
- **rights-based law** (MHCA 2017) with **adequate, ring-fenced financing**;
- **community and family participation**;
- measurement of **effective coverage**, not just contact.

## In depth

### Background
- **Kohn et al. (2004)** synthesised community surveys and estimated median treatment gaps:
  - schizophrenia 32%;
  - depression 56%;
  - alcohol use disorders 78% [general knowledge].
- **WHO mhGAP (2008)** responded with scalable packages for priority conditions [1].
- **Lancet Global Mental Health series (2007, 2011)** and the **Lancet Commission (2018)**: "Scale up services for mental disorders" [general knowledge].
- **India's community psychiatry history** [2]:
  - Bhore Committee (1946);
  - Mudaliar Committee (1962);
  - **Sakalawara (1975)**;
  - **Raipur Rani (1976–81, PGIMER)**;
  - ICMR–DST severe mental morbidity project;
  - **Alma-Ata (1978)**;
  - NMHP (1982);
  - Bellary pilot (1985);
  - DMHP (1996).

### Epidemiology
- **NMHS 2015–16:**
  - lifetime prevalence 13.7%; current prevalence 10.6%;
  - substance use disorders 22% (mainly tobacco);
  - schizophrenia and other psychoses: lifetime 1.41%, current 0.42%;
  - mood disorders: lifetime 5.6%, current 2.8%;
  - neurotic and stress-related disorders: lifetime 3.7%, current 3.5% [2].
- **GBD 2017 India:** depressive disorders account for 33.8% of mental disorder DALYs; anxiety 19%; idiopathic ID 10.8%; schizophrenia 9.8% [2].

### Classification of the gap
- **Prevalence-based gap** (any contact).
- **Minimally adequate treatment gap** (WMH Surveys: only about 1 in 27 people with depression in LMICs received minimally adequate treatment; Thornicroft 2017) [general knowledge].
- **Effective coverage:** the proportion who gain health benefit (Tanahashi 1978 cascade).

### Mechanism: why integration and task-sharing work
- They increase **availability** (more providers), **accessibility** (proximity, cost) and **acceptability** (familiar providers, less stigma).
- **Collaborative care** (case manager, supervising psychiatrist, measurement-based care) improves outcomes (IMPACT).

### Clinical features of successful programmes (common ingredients)
- Clear care pathways.
- Training plus **supervision**.
- Reliable drug supply.
- Simple records and monitoring.
- Community engagement.
- Referral linkages.
- Rehabilitation and livelihood components.

### Assessment: evaluating district mental health services [2]
1. Monthly programme reports.
2. Field evaluation of a random 20% of PHCs (staff ability, community knowledge).
3. Half-yearly district review meetings (medical officers, DMHP team, primary care staff).
4. Meetings with NGOs and agencies (school and college promotion activities).
5. Annual state-level review (DHS, SMHA, DMHP team).

### Differential: competing models
| Model | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Institution expansion | Specialist care | Unrealistic; inaccessible [1] |
| Primary care integration (DMHP) | Scale, proximity | Variable quality; staff overload |
| Task-sharing / lay counsellors | Cost-effective; strong RCT evidence | Supervision needs; sustainability |
| Tele-mental health | Reach, speed | Digital divide; continuity |
| CBR | Disability and inclusion | Multisectoral coordination [1] |

### Management: barriers to implementation [1]
- Mental health absent from the public-health agenda (funding).
- Service organisation (centralised).
- Lack of primary care integration.
- Inadequate human resources.
- Lack of public mental health leadership.

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Chatterjee 2003, 2009 (Barwani) | CBR reduced disability more than outpatient care; feasible and acceptable [1] |
| COPSI (Chatterjee 2014, *Lancet*) | Collaborative community plus facility care modestly better than facility care alone for disability [1] |
| MANAS (Patel 2010, *Lancet*) (general knowledge) | Lay counsellors in primary care improved CMD recovery in public facilities |
| PREMIUM (Patel 2017, *Lancet*) (general knowledge) | HAP for depression and CAP for harmful drinking effective and cost-effective |
| Bellary DMHP pilot (1985–90) | Feasibility of primary care delivery [2] |
| Thirthahalli (unpublished) | 60–80% recover with treatment alone [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- **"Treatment gap" can be misleading:**
  - not all cases need specialist care (mild, self-limiting);
  - closing contact gaps without quality may medicalise distress;
  - **effective coverage** is the better metric.
- **DMHP evaluations** show patchy implementation: staff vacancies, under-utilised funds and weak monitoring [general knowledge; see `district-mental-health-programme`].
- **Tele-MANAS** volume (over 25 lakh calls) shows demand; outcome data are pending [general knowledge; check figure].
- **Task-sharing RCTs** are strong, but scale-up and sustainability evidence is weaker.

### Indian context
- **NMHP / DMHP restructuring under NHM.**
- **Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** (over 1.7 lakh) with mental health packages.
- **Tele-MANAS** (53 cells).
- **MHCA 2017** rights framework.
- **National Suicide Prevention Strategy 2022.**
- **Mental health in the National Education Policy 2020.**
- **Karnataka** as a model state (NIMHANS–NHM MOU).

### Recent advances
- **Digital phenotyping and AI triage** (experimental).
- **WHO Special Initiative for Mental Health (2019–2025)** and the **World Mental Health Report 2022** ("transform mental health for all").
- **Lancet Commission on ending stigma (2022).**
- **Community-based crisis services** (Section 18 MHCA).
