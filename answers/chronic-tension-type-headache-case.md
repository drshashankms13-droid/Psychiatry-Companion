## Exam answer

### Summary of the case
A 33-year-old woman has had **bilateral, pressing (non-pulsating), mild** headache over the scalp for **2 years**, on **20–25 days a month**. She has **no photophobia, phonophobia or vomiting**, only **occasional mild nausea**. ENT and eye examinations are normal.

### Provisional diagnosis
**Chronic daily headache, most consistent with chronic tension-type headache (TTH)** [1, 2]:
- the headache is global or bilateral, **mild**, and **pressing or band-like**, with few associated symptoms [1];
- **more than 15 headache days a month** defines **chronic TTH**, a subtype of chronic daily headache [1];
- it is long-standing (2 years) and unchanged, with no red flags given in the vignette.

### Points against, or to be excluded
| Differential | Why it must be considered | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chronic migraine** | **Most CDH patients have underlying migraine** [1]. Chronic migraine = 15 or more headache days a month, 8 or more of them migrainous [4]. Her **nausea** is a migrainous feature [6]. Migraine and TTH may coexist [1] | Past history of discrete throbbing, unilateral attacks; aura; photophobia during worse days; a headache diary |
| **Medication-overuse (analgesic-overuse) headache** | **Any** analgesic can cause it, not only opioids [1]. Overuse = simple analgesics on **15 or more days a month**, or triptans, combinations or opioids on 10 or more days [4] | **Count analgesic days a month**; over-the-counter combinations; caffeine |
| **Raised intracranial pressure / IIH** | IIH can produce a **TTH-like** headache [5]. Raised ICP headache is worse lying down, present in the morning, disturbs sleep, and later causes vomiting [1] | **Fundi for papilloedema**, pulse-synchronous tinnitus, transient visual obscurations, weight; LP opening pressure if suspected [5] |
| **Low CSF pressure (spontaneous intracranial hypotension)** | Features are the inverse of raised ICP: comes on after getting up, relieved by lying down [1]. Consider in refractory "chronic" headache [5] | Orthostatic pattern |
| **Other secondary causes** (SNOOP) [3] | Required before labelling a primary headache | Systemic symptoms, neurological signs, sudden onset, onset over 50, pattern change (progressive, Valsalva or posture), pregnancy |
| **Headache with psychiatric disorder** | CDH has a **bidirectional** link with **depression and anxiety** [1, 2]. "Headache attributed to psychiatric disorder" is an ICHD-3 secondary group [3] | Mood, anxiety, stress-related worsening, sleep |

### Evaluation
1. **History:**
   - **headache diary** (days, severity, features, triggers);
   - **analgesic and caffeine intake per month**;
   - pattern over the day (TTH often **worsens as the day goes on or after stress**) [1];
   - sleep; stressors; disability.
2. **Examination:** full neurological examination and **fundi**. Symptoms alone cannot separate primary from secondary headache [3].
3. **Investigations:** **only if red flags** are present [3]. The vignette gives none.
4. **Psychiatric assessment:**
   - **depression and anxiety** (common, and they worsen outcome) [1, 2];
   - catastrophizing; stress;
   - analgesic dependence.

### Management [1, 2, 4, 7]
1. **Explain the diagnosis.** Make it clear the headache is real, not imaginary, and that the aim is fewer and milder headaches.
2. **Analgesic overuse:**
   - if present, **withdraw the overused analgesics**, which usually brings improvement;
   - this needs **careful explanation** [1];
   - afterwards, **limit acute analgesics** to below the overuse thresholds [4].
3. **Acute treatment of episodes:** simple analgesics (**ibuprofen, paracetamol**, ketoprofen, dipyrone) have **low-level** evidence. Aspirin has none [7].
4. **Preventive drug treatment (chronic TTH):**
   - a **TCA (amitriptyline)** is supported for chronic daily headache [2];
   - TCAs are effective in headache syndromes and **better than SSRIs for TTH** [2];
   - **mirtazapine** reduced the duration and intensity of chronic TTH [2];
   - ⚠️ Henningsen grades TCAs only "+ / o" and SNRIs "o" for TTH with neck pain [7]. This conflicts with Levenson [2] and is not reconciled here;
   - doses are not given in the sources.
5. **Non-drug treatment:**
   - **CBT** (combined with medication it **beats either alone** for CDH [2]); relaxation and biofeedback;
   - **exercise**; **multimodal manual therapy** (++ in Henningsen); acupuncture (+) [7].
6. **Treat comorbid depression or anxiety** with antidepressants and CBT [1]. A **TCA** can serve both purposes.
7. **Follow-up** with the diary. **Reconsider the diagnosis** (migraine, overuse, CSF pressure) if she does not respond [5].

**Conclusion.** [Likely, given the gaps above] This is **chronic daily headache of the chronic tension-type pattern**: bilateral, pressing and mild, on more than 15 days a month for 2 years. Her occasional nausea means **coexisting chronic migraine must be actively excluded**. Management is to:
- exclude red flags and **medication overuse**;
- start a **TCA (or mirtazapine)** for prevention;
- add **CBT and exercise**;
- treat any comorbid depression or anxiety.
