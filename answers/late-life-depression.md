## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Late-life depression (LLD) is depression in adults **aged 60 or over** (Indian convention; 65 elsewhere). It is **not a normal part of ageing**. It differs from depression in younger adults in several ways:
- phenomenology (less sadness, more somatic, cognitive and anxiety symptoms);
- aetiology (vascular and neurodegenerative contributions);
- outcomes (higher suicide, disability, dementia risk and mortality);
- treatment (pharmacokinetic vulnerability, ECT especially effective) [1].

A key clinical distinction [1]:
- **Early-onset depression recurring in late life:** stronger family and genetic loading.
- **Late-onset depression (first episode after 60–65):** more cerebrovascular disease, neurodegeneration and medical comorbidity. Organic causes must be **actively** excluded.

### Epidemiology [1]
- **WHO:** about **7%** of older adults have major depression.
- **India:**
  - **LASI (Longitudinal Ageing Study in India):** **8.3%** probable major depression; about **30%** with depressive symptoms (including subsyndromal);
  - **NMHS** older-adult figure about **6.9%**;
  - **WHO-SAGE India:** only about **4.1% treated**, a huge treatment gap.
- **Subsyndromal depression** (2 or more symptoms for at least 2 weeks without meeting MDD criteria) is the most common form. It causes disability and carries an increased risk of progression to MDD [1].

### Clinical features: how LLD differs [1]
| Domain | Late-life presentation |
| --- | --- |
| Mood | Often **denies sadness** ("depression without sadness"); irritability, anxiety, hopelessness; **anhedonia** a more reliable marker |
| Somatic | Fatigue, insomnia, pain, GI complaints, **hypochondriasis**; overlap with physical illness |
| Cognitive | **Executive dysfunction** (planning, initiation, working memory, flexibility); slowing; **pseudodementia** |
| Psychotic | **Nihilistic and somatic delusions** (Cotard), delusions of poverty or guilt; psychotic depression more common, with poorer prognosis |
| Behaviour | Apathy, withdrawal, self-neglect, food refusal |
| Suicide | **Attempt-to-completion ratio about 4:1** (vs about 200:1 in adolescents): high intent, lethal methods, frailty, isolation. Risk factors: male sex, widowhood, bereavement, chronic painful illness, isolation, access to means [1] |

### Aetiology
- **Biological:**
  - **Vascular hypothesis:** small-vessel disease and white-matter hyperintensities disrupt **fronto-striatal circuits**. This causes the **depression–executive dysfunction syndrome**: apathy, slowing, poor SSRI response [1]. See `vascular-depression`.
  - **HPA-axis hyperactivity:** glucocorticoid neurotoxicity in the hippocampus.
  - **Inflammation** (cytokines in CKD, COPD, cardiovascular disease).
  - **Neurodegeneration:** depression as a **prodrome** of Alzheimer's disease (early locus coeruleus involvement) or Parkinson's disease.
  - **Genetic vulnerability:** 5-HTTLPR short allele, BDNF Met allele (white-matter disease), AGTR polymorphisms (vascular link) [1].
- **Medical illness (bidirectional):**
  - stroke, myocardial infarction, heart failure;
  - diabetes, **hypothyroidism**, COPD, CKD, cancer, Parkinson's disease;
  - **B12 and folate deficiency** [1];
  - medications: beta-blockers (debated), steroids, levodopa, benzodiazepines, interferon.
- **Psychosocial:**
  - bereavement, loss of role and income (retirement);
  - **loneliness**, migration of children, elder abuse;
  - disability, caregiving burden;
  - poverty.

### Assessment [1]
- **History:** onset (early vs late), episodes, suicide risk, psychotic features, cognitive change, collateral from family.
- **Comprehensive geriatric assessment:**
  - **ADLs and IADLs** (IADL decline is an early sign of both depression and dementia);
  - falls, sensory deficits, nutrition, polypharmacy review.
- **Investigations:**
  - CBC, **B12, folate**, **TFT**, electrolytes (Na), glucose, renal and liver function, calcium, ECG;
  - **MRI** for late onset, treatment resistance or atypical neurological features (white-matter hyperintensities, infarcts, tumour, NPH).
- **Scales:**
  - **Geriatric Depression Scale** (GDS-30/15/5; focuses on psychological symptoms, avoiding somatic confounds);
  - **Cornell Scale for Depression in Dementia** (caregiver and observer based);
  - MADRS (tracks change);
  - **MMSE/MoCA** (cognition).

### Differential diagnosis
- **Dementia vs depressive pseudodementia** [1]:
  - pseudodementia: acute onset, complains about memory, "don't know" answers (poor effort), improves with treatment;
  - but pseudodementia predicts later dementia in many patients.
- **Apathy syndrome** (frontal or vascular), delirium (hypoactive), hypothyroidism, Parkinson's disease, medication effects, bereavement, adjustment disorder, bipolar depression, alcohol use.

### Management
**Principles:** "**start low, go slow — but go**". Reduced hepatic metabolism and renal clearance, more body fat and less water raise drug levels and prolong half-lives [1].

1. **Psychological and social:**
   - **psychotherapy as first-line monotherapy for mild–moderate depression** [1];
   - **Problem-Solving Therapy** (targets executive dysfunction);
   - **PATH** (Problem Adaptation Therapy: calendars, checklists for cognitively impaired patients);
   - **Engage therapy** (reward exposure);
   - CBT, IPT, behavioural activation, reminiscence therapy;
   - social prescribing, day care, addressing loneliness;
   - exercise.
2. **Pharmacotherapy:**
   - **SSRIs first-line: sertraline, escitalopram** (citalopram up to 20 mg in over-65s, because of QTc);
   - **mirtazapine** (sleep, appetite; falls risk);
   - **vortioxetine** (pro-cognitive signal; possibly useful in executive dysfunction) [1];
   - **duloxetine** (pain);
   - **bupropion**;
   - **avoid TCAs** (anticholinergic, cardiac, falls; nortriptyline is the exception if needed);
   - **monitor sodium** (SIADH: female sex, low weight, diuretics are risk factors) [1], falls, GI bleeding (with NSAIDs or anticoagulants) and QTc;
   - adequate trial: **4–6 weeks at an adequate dose before deciding non-response; older adults may need 8–12 weeks**;
   - **maintenance:** continue for at least 12 months after a first episode, 2 years or more after recurrent episodes, and **lifelong** in many cases.
3. **Treatment-resistant depression** [1]:
   - optimise the dose; **switch** (e.g., to venlafaxine);
   - **augment**: **lithium** (lower levels, 0.4–0.6 mmol/L), **aripiprazole** (OPTIMUM trial supports it), T3;
   - neuromodulation.
4. **ECT:**
   - **highly effective in the elderly**, often more so than in younger adults;
   - **first-line** for psychotic depression, food refusal, high suicide risk, catatonia;
   - **RUL ultrabrief pulse** reduces cognitive effects;
   - **PRIDE**: continuation ECT plus medication prevents relapse [1]. See `ect-for-depression-in-the-elderly`.
5. **rTMS:** after one failed antidepressant; well tolerated; less effective in psychotic depression [1].
6. **Psychotic depression:** antidepressant + antipsychotic (STOP-PD: sertraline + olanzapine), or ECT.
7. **Treat comorbidities:** vascular risk factors (BP, diabetes, statins), pain, sensory aids.

### Course and prognosis [1]
- **Relapse and chronicity are common**, driven by progressive vascular and neurodegenerative change.
- Outcomes:
  - increased mortality (suicide, cardiovascular);
  - **increased dementia risk** (about 2× for LLD);
  - disability.
- **Long-term maintenance is often needed.**

**Conclusion.** LLD is common, under-recognised and under-treated (about 4% treated in India). It often presents **without overt sadness**, with somatic, cognitive and anxious features, and carries **high suicide lethality**. **Late-onset cases** demand a search for vascular, neurodegenerative and medical causes. Management is **biopsychosocial**: careful antidepressant use, psychotherapy adapted to executive deficits, **early use of ECT** for severe or psychotic illness, social reconnection, and long-term maintenance.

## In depth

### Background
- **Alexopoulos's** concepts:
  - "vascular depression" (1997);
  - "depression–executive dysfunction syndrome";
  - "depression of Alzheimer's disease" (Olin criteria, NIMH 2002).
- **Kraepelin's involutional melancholia** (now abandoned).

### Epidemiology (expanded)
- **LASI Wave 1 (2017–18):** about 72,000 adults aged 45+ [general knowledge]. Depression was higher in women, rural residents, widowed people and lower-income groups [Likely].
- **Western community MDD in older adults:** 1–5%. Higher in medical inpatients (about 10–20%) and care homes (up to 30%).

### Classification
- ICD-11 and DSM-5-TR use the same criteria at all ages; there is no separate late-life category [1].
- **Subtypes:**
  - early vs late onset;
  - **vascular depression**;
  - **psychotic depression**;
  - depression of Alzheimer's disease;
  - subsyndromal / minor depression;
  - bereavement-related.

### Mechanism: circuit model [1]
- **Dorsal cognitive-control system** (DLPFC, dorsal ACC): underactive, causing slowing and poor regulation.
- **Ventral affective system** (amygdala, subgenual ACC, OFC): overactive, causing negative affect.
- **Network model:**
  - salience network dysregulation;
  - **DMN hyperconnectivity** (rumination);
  - weak CEN engagement.
- **Reward circuit** (mesolimbic dopamine) dysfunction causes anhedonia and apathy.
- **Structural findings:**
  - **hippocampal and prefrontal atrophy**;
  - **white-matter hyperintensities** (deep and periventricular; Fazekas grading) predicting poor response.

### Clinical features: psychotic depression in the elderly
- Somatic delusions; Cotard syndrome; delusions of poverty; persecutory delusions.
- High suicide risk; food refusal and dehydration. This is a medical emergency, so consider ECT.

### Assessment: pseudodementia vs dementia
| Feature | Depressive pseudodementia | Dementia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Relatively acute, dateable | Insidious |
| Complaint | Emphasises cognitive loss | Minimises or unaware |
| Responses | "Don't know" | Near-miss, confabulation |
| Mood | Depressed first | Labile, apathetic |
| Course | Improves with treatment | Progressive |

### Management: evidence on drugs in older adults
- **Antidepressant efficacy in over-65s** is smaller than in younger adults (NNT about 8–14). Placebo response is lower in late-onset illness [general knowledge].
- **Sertraline, escitalopram and duloxetine** have RCT support. **Vortioxetine** improved cognition (Katona 2012 elderly RCT) [general knowledge].
- **OPTIMUM (Lenze 2023, *NEJM*):** in older adults with TRD, **aripiprazole augmentation** gave more remission than switching to bupropion, and fewer falls than bupropion augmentation [general knowledge; check].
- **IRL-GRey (Lenze 2015, *Lancet*):** aripiprazole augmentation of venlafaxine in older TRD: remission 44% vs 29% [general knowledge].
- **Lithium:** older patients need lower levels; risk of neurotoxicity and renal impairment.

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| LASI / NMHS / SAGE | India prevalence 6.9–8.3%; about 4% treated [1] |
| PRIDE (Kellner 2016, *Am J Psychiatry*) | RUL ultrabrief ECT: 62% remission. Continuation ECT plus venlafaxine and lithium better than medication alone [1; general knowledge for numbers] |
| IRL-GRey (Lenze 2015) | Aripiprazole augmentation effective in older TRD |
| STOP-PD (Meyers 2009) | Sertraline + olanzapine better than olanzapine alone for psychotic depression |
| PROSPECT (Bruce 2004) | Care-management model in primary care reduced suicidal ideation |
| IMPACT (Unützer 2002) | Collaborative care doubled effectiveness of usual care |

### Critical appraisal
- Trials exclude the frail, demented and multimorbid, who are the patients clinicians actually see.
- Antidepressant benefit is modest. **Psychosocial and collaborative care** models may matter more.
- **Vascular depression** is a useful construct but lacks specific validated treatment.

### Indian context
- **Policy:**
  - **NPHCE (National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly, 2010)** provides geriatric clinics and wards in district hospitals; mental health is integrated weakly [Likely];
  - **Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007**;
  - **IPS Clinical Practice Guidelines for late-life depression** (Indian J Psychiatry 2018/2022 supplement) [Likely; check].
- **Social change:** loneliness is rising with urbanisation and migration of children; high rates of widowhood among women.
- **Services:** a few geriatric psychiatry units (NIMHANS, AIIMS, CMC Vellore). Tele-MANAS.
- **Barriers:** stigma; attributing symptoms to "old age"; multiple physicians and polypharmacy.

### Recent advances
- **Ketamine/esketamine** in older adults (TRANSFORM-3: modest effect in over-65s) [general knowledge].
- **Theta-burst rTMS** (shorter sessions).
- **Digital and tele-delivered PST.**
- **Precision approaches:** white-matter burden and inflammatory markers to guide treatment choice.
