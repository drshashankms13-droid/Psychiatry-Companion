## Exam answer

### Definition and classification [1]
- **Tremor:** an **involuntary, rhythmic, oscillatory** movement of a body part.
- The **MDS consensus (2018)** classifies tremor on **two axes**:
  - **Axis 1, clinical**: age at onset, sex, body distribution, temporal evolution, activation condition;
  - **Axis 2, aetiological**.
- **Activation conditions:**
  - **rest**;
  - **action**: **postural**, **kinetic**, **intention** (worse on goal-directed precision tasks);
  - **task- or position-specific**.

### Clinical approach: history [1]
- **Onset and course:** age (ET peaks in the third and seventh decades), insidious vs **abrupt** (functional), progression (ET about **2% worse per year**).
- **Distribution:** arms, head, voice, jaw, legs; symmetry.
- **Functional impact:** eating, drinking, pouring, writing, keys, phone/keyboard.
- **Modifiers:** **alcohol improves** ET (and sometimes dystonic tremor); **caffeine worsens** it; **sensory trick** suggests dystonia.
- **Family history:** ET heritability **30–70%**; over **80%** of early-onset ET have an affected first-degree relative.
- **Drug history** (key for psychiatrists):
  - **lithium, valproate, SSRIs, TCAs, antipsychotics**;
  - β-agonists, immunosuppressants, hormones.
- **Medical:** stroke, trauma, MS (Holmes tremor), neuropathy, thyroid, hypoglycaemia, anxiety.
- **Associated features:**
  - **REM sleep behaviour disorder, anosmia**, slowness, gait change (PD);
  - neck pain or pulling (dystonia);
  - cognitive change or ataxia (FXTAS, Wilson).

### Examination [1]
- **Rest:** arms fully supported on the lap or armrest (or lying down). Add **walking or mental arithmetic**: PD rest tremor **increases**, ET does not.
- **Posture:** arms outstretched (ET: distal **flexion–extension** at wrist and MCP joints); **wing-beating** position (proximal; **Wilson disease** classically).
- **Kinetic and intention:** pouring, writing, **spiral drawing** (ET spirals show a consistent **axis**: right hand toward the upper right, left hand toward the upper left; **no axis in dystonic tremor**), finger–nose–finger, dot approximation.
- **Head and voice:** ET head tremor **disappears lying down**; dystonic head tremor **persists supine** and has a **null point**.
- **PD clues:** **re-emergent** postural tremor after a latency; **jaw tremor at rest with mouth closed** (ET jaw tremor appears when speaking); asymmetry; bradykinesia, rigidity.
- **Functional clues:** **entrainment, distractibility, suggestibility**; variable frequency and amplitude.
- **Gait and cognition:** tandem missteps (**30–50%** of ET); attention, executive and memory testing.

### Differential diagnosis (Figure 5-1) [1]

| Tremor | Key history | Key examination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Essential tremor** | Familial, bilateral arm action tremor at least 3 years, alcohol-responsive | Postural and kinetic, symmetric, spiral axis; head tremor resolves supine |
| **Parkinson disease** | Unilateral/asymmetric; slowness, stiffness, gait change; RBD, anosmia | Rest tremor (arm/leg) increasing with walking or counting; re-emergent postural tremor; jaw/lip tremor at rest |
| **Dystonic tremor** | Pain, pulling, restricted movement; **sensory trick**; typically a woman of 45–50 with isolated head tremor | Jerky, irregular; muscle hypertrophy; **null point**; persists supine; asymmetric arm tremor; no spiral axis; voice breaks |
| **Enhanced physiologic** | Linked to a physiological factor (hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, **hyperthyroidism, anxiety**); stops when removed | Fast, fine, symmetric arms; **head spared**; no rest or intention component |
| **Drug-induced** | **Temporal link and dose–response** (e.g., **lithium**, valproate, SSRIs, TCAs, neuroleptics, β-agonists) | Usually fine and fast postural; **valproate may be coarse**; psychotropics can add rest and intention components; head usually spared |
| **Holmes tremor** | Stroke, trauma, MS (cerebello-rubro-thalamic circuit) | Slow, unilateral, proximal; **kinetic > postural > rest** |
| **FXTAS / Wilson disease** | Older men with ataxia ± parkinsonism and cognitive change (FXTAS); autosomal recessive copper deposition (Wilson) | FXTAS: intention tremor; Wilson: **wing-beating** tremor |
| **Neuropathic** | Tremor follows the neuropathy | Mild to moderate postural and kinetic tremor |
| **Functional** | **Abrupt onset**, spontaneous remissions | Variable frequency; **entrainment, distractibility, suggestibility** |

- **Overdiagnosis:** **30–50%** of "essential tremor" cases turn out to be something else, mostly **dystonia or PD** [1].
- **ET diagnostic requirement:**
  - **isolated bilateral upper-limb action tremor for at least 3 years**, with or without head, voice or leg tremor;
  - **no** dystonia, ataxia or parkinsonism;
  - **isolated head or voice tremor** and **task- or position-specific tremor exclude ET**;
  - under 3 years = "indeterminate".
- **ET plus:** ET with **soft signs** (impaired tandem gait, questionable dystonic posturing, memory impairment, **rest component**). It is controversial and not proven biologically distinct [1].

### Investigations [1, 3]
Diagnosis is **clinical**; tests are targeted:
1. **Drug review and levels:** e.g., **serum lithium**. In the Continuum case, a lithium level of 1.5 mEq/L (therapeutic range 0.6–1.2) explained tremor with parkinsonism unresponsive to levodopa [1].
2. **Metabolic triggers** of enhanced physiological tremor: **thyroid function, glucose** (the source names hyperthyroidism and hypoglycaemia as causes) [1].
3. **Dopamine transporter (DAT) imaging:** shows presynaptic nigrostriatal loss in **PD**; **normal in ET and drug-induced tremor** (unremarkable in the lithium case) [1].
4. **Clinical electrophysiology (EMG/accelerometry):**
   - enhanced physiological tremor has **higher frequency and lower amplitude**;
   - **inertial (weight) loading** separates a **mechanical peak** from a central peak (two peaks), unlike ET [1].
5. **Phosphorylated α-synuclein** in CSF or **skin biopsy**: may distinguish PD from ET in future. Accuracy is not yet established [1].
6. **MRI brain** when Holmes tremor is suspected (stroke, demyelination); **genetic and copper studies** for FXTAS or Wilson disease *(the source lists these as causes; specific test protocols are not given)* [1].
7. **Neuropathy assessment** when tremor follows neuropathy [1].
8. **Cognitive, gait and mood assessment** in ET (non-motor features) [1].

### Management

**A. Treat the cause or remove the trigger** [1, 3]:
- **Drug-induced:**
  - reduce or stop the offending drug where possible; correct **toxic lithium levels**;
  - **propranolol can help lithium-induced tremor** [3];
  - **valproate** causes dose-related tremor in **up to a quarter** of patients, mostly postural or intention, rarely parkinsonism with cognitive decline, **reversible on stopping** [3];
  - β-agonist tremor in PD or ET can lead to **misdiagnosis and over-treatment** [3].
- **Enhanced physiological tremor:** correct hyperthyroidism, hypoglycaemia or anxiety [1].
- **Dystonic tremor:** **botulinum toxin** to the dystonic muscles (Continuum Case 5-2: injections every 12 weeks) [1].
- **Functional tremor:** see the FND answers.

**B. Essential tremor: stepwise, shared decision-making (Figure 5-4)** [1]:
- **Multidisciplinary team:** neurology, neurosurgery, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, neuropsychology, **psychiatry**. Treat the patient, not the disease.
- **Step 1, non-pharmacological (especially mild ET):**
  - occupational therapy; adaptive aids (**weighted utensils, pens, rocker knives**, modified mouse and keyboard);
  - **orthotic devices** (wrist weights, tuned-mass dampers, gyroscopes);
  - **peripheral limb cooling**;
  - an FDA-cleared (2022) **wrist device stimulating the median and radial nerves**.
- **Step 1, first-line drugs** (when daily activities are affected):
  - **propranolol**: **60–200 mg/day** reduces tremor by **50–70% in 50%** of patients. Side effects: dizziness, fatigue, erectile dysfunction, sedation. **Avoid in asthma**; use **atenolol or metoprolol** instead;
  - **primidone**: effective at **250 mg/day** (acts on sodium channels; metabolites phenobarbital and PEMA). Confusion, ataxia and nausea, especially in the elderly at high doses, so **start low, go slow**. Phenobarbital pre-treatment for 3 days reduced early side effects in one study;
  - **propranolol plus primidone** together gives better outcomes;
  - drugs help **arm tremor more than head tremor**.
- **Step 2:** **topiramate, gabapentin, alprazolam or clonazepam** (less effective or weaker evidence; choose for comorbidity, e.g., a **benzodiazepine with psychiatric comorbidity**).
- **Step 3:** **botulinum toxin A** injections.
- **Step 4, surgery** for disabling, socially embarrassing, **medication-refractory** tremor:
  - **DBS** (most used):
    - **non-destructive and reversible**; controls **arm and head** tremor; can be **bilateral** without the dysarthria, dysphagia and ataxia of bilateral thalamotomy;
    - drawbacks: cost, infection, seizure, stroke, hardware problems, battery replacement;
  - **MRI-guided focused ultrasound (MRgFUS) thalamotomy** (FDA 2016):
    - **incisionless lesion**; efficacy similar to DBS (no head-to-head trial);
    - needs full scalp shaving and 3–4 hours in the scanner; no long-term data;
    - **unilateral only** (FDA-approved for unilateral tremor);
    - RCT: **47%** reduction in contralateral hand tremor; gait disturbance 36% and sensory disturbance 38%, persisting at 1 year in 9% and 14% [2];
  - **stereotactic radiosurgery (VIM thalamus)**: no burr hole, but **delayed, unpredictable** benefit.

**C. Parkinson disease tremor** [2]:
- levodopa, dopamine agonists, anticholinergics;
- for refractory, tremor-predominant disease: **VIM** thalamotomy by **MRgFUS or radiosurgery** (unilateral), or **DBS** (bilateral or axial tremor).

**Conclusion.** Tremor is evaluated clinically: activation condition, distribution, symmetry, modifiers (alcohol, caffeine, sensory tricks), family and **drug history**, and bedside tests (spirals, re-emergent tremor, supine head tremor, null point, entrainment). ET is the commonest adult tremor (about 1%, 5% over 60), but 30–50% of "ET" is really dystonic or parkinsonian tremor. Investigations are targeted:
- drug levels (lithium), thyroid and glucose;
- DAT imaging for PD;
- electrophysiology with inertial loading;
- MRI or genetic tests for secondary tremors.

Management:
- remove causes (e.g., lithium toxicity; propranolol for lithium tremor);
- botulinum toxin for dystonic tremor;
- for ET, a stepladder: aids, orthoses and peripheral stimulation → **propranolol or primidone** (or both) → topiramate, gabapentin or benzodiazepines → botulinum toxin → **DBS or focused-ultrasound thalamotomy**, within a multidisciplinary team that includes psychiatry.
