## Exam answer

**Introduction.** About 1 in 10 pregnant women have depression, and many women with SMI conceive while on medication. Neonatal outcomes after antenatal psychotropic exposure fall into five groups:
1. **Structural teratogenicity** (first trimester);
2. **Obstetric and growth outcomes** (preterm birth, birth weight, GDM-related macrosomia);
3. **Neonatal adaptation, toxicity and withdrawal** (late pregnancy);
4. **Specific neonatal syndromes** (PPHN, floppy infant, EPS);
5. **Long-term neurodevelopment** (behavioural teratogenicity).

The key principle throughout is that **maternal illness itself** independently raises the risk of malformations, stillbirth, preterm birth, low birth weight and adverse child development. Many drug associations **attenuate or disappear when maternal illness is controlled for** [1]. Background major malformation risk is **2–3%** in any pregnancy [1].

### Timing matters
| Period | Vulnerability |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 0–2 after conception | "All or none" |
| **Weeks 3–8 (organogenesis)** | Structural malformations. Neural tube closes by about day 28; heart at 2–6 weeks |
| Second and third trimester | Growth, CNS maturation, **obstetric complications** |
| **Last weeks before delivery** | **Neonatal adaptation, withdrawal, toxicity, PPHN, bleeding** |

### Drug class summary [1]

| Class | Malformations | Obstetric / growth | Neonatal (late exposure) | Neurodevelopment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SSRIs** | **Not major teratogens.** Paroxetine and fluoxetine cardiac signals inconsistent, probably confounded | Small ↑ preterm birth, LBW, low Apgar, NICU admission; **postpartum haemorrhage** (exposure in month before delivery; MHRA); gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia (small) | **Poor neonatal adaptation syndrome (PNAS)** in up to about 30%: jitteriness, irritability, poor feeding, respiratory distress, hypoglycaemia. **PPHN:** small absolute risk, late exposure, possibly lower with sertraline | ASD and ADHD associations **disappear after controlling for maternal illness** (sibling and propensity studies; Suarez 2022) |
| **SNRIs** (venlafaxine, duloxetine) | Not major teratogens (2022 meta-analysis) | PPH; SGA (venlafaxine) | Neonatal withdrawal; PPHN | Limited data |
| **TCAs** | Not major; clomipramine cardiac signal | **Preterm delivery** | **Withdrawal:** agitation, seizures, respiratory distress; anticholinergic effects (urinary retention, ileus, tachycardia) | No consistent effect |
| **Mirtazapine, bupropion, trazodone** | Not associated (bupropion: possible VSD) | Miscarriage (probably illness-related) | Breastfeeding reduced PNAS with mirtazapine | Bupropion: ADHD signal |
| **Antipsychotics** | **Not major teratogens.** Risperidone, olanzapine (clefts) and SGA gastroschisis signals | **GDM → macrosomia and LGA** (olanzapine, clozapine, quetiapine); FGAs → preterm birth and LBW; caesarean section | **Withdrawal and EPS** (15–20%): crying, agitation, tremor, increased suckling; somnolence; **clozapine:** neonatal seizures, floppy infant | No causal link with ADHD or ASD |
| **Lithium** | **Ebstein's anomaly** and septal defects (smaller risk than thought; weeks 2–6) | Possible preterm birth, LGA | **Floppy infant**, lethargy, cyanosis, **hypothyroidism and goitre**, nephrogenic DI, arrhythmia, low Apgar | Probably unaffected |
| **Valproate** | **About 10%:** neural tube defects, cardiac defects, clefts, hypospadias, craniosynostosis, small head | Pre-eclampsia | Hepatotoxicity, coagulopathy, hypoglycaemia (rare); withdrawal | **Up to 40% developmental delay; ↓ IQ; ASD** |
| **Carbamazepine** | Neural tube defects, others | — | **Vitamin K-deficient bleeding** | Less than valproate |
| **Lamotrigine** | Similar to unexposed | — | — | Probably unaffected |
| **Topiramate** | **Oral clefts** | SGA | — | Neurodevelopmental disorders |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Oral cleft signal inconsistent | Miscarriage, preterm birth, LBW, SGA, small head; caesarean section | **Floppy infant syndrome** (hypotonia, hypothermia, apnoea, poor suck); **withdrawal** (tremor, irritability, hypertonia) | No consistent ADHD or ASD effect |
| **Z-drugs** | Not associated | Preterm birth, LBW, SGA; zolpidem: caesarean section | Sedation | No association |
| **Stimulants** | Methylphenidate: small cardiac signal; amfetamines: none | Amfetamines: preterm birth, LBW | Withdrawal (rare) | No association |
| **Modafinil** | **Malformations** (cardiac, hypospadias, clefts) | — | — | — |

### Neonatal management
- **Before birth:**
  - inform the paediatric and neonatal team;
  - deliver where **neonatal intensive care** is available (antipsychotics, lithium, high-dose SSRIs);
  - give **vitamin K** with carbamazepine.
- **After birth:**
  - observe for **at least 48–72 hours** (NICE: extra monitoring after SSRI or SNRI exposure);
  - check glucose, temperature, tone, feeding, respiratory status;
  - **lithium:** level, TFT, renal function, ECG;
  - **valproate:** clotting, glucose, LFT.
- **Supportive care** is usually enough, because PNAS is **self-limiting** within days to 2 weeks.
- **Do not stop maternal medication in late pregnancy** to prevent PNAS. This raises relapse risk without proven neonatal benefit.
- **Breastfeeding** may soften withdrawal (mirtazapine, venlafaxine data) [1].
- **Long-term:** developmental follow-up for valproate exposure and for preterm or low-birth-weight infants.

**Conclusion.**
- Most psychotropics (SSRIs, antipsychotics, lamotrigine) carry **small absolute risks**, mainly **transient neonatal adaptation problems** and metabolic or obstetric effects.
- **Valproate, carbamazepine, topiramate and modafinil** are the clear exceptions, and **lithium** needs cardiac screening.
- Because untreated maternal illness worsens the same outcomes, the decision should rest on **individual risk–benefit**, **monotherapy at the lowest effective dose**, and **planned neonatal surveillance**.

## In depth

### Background
- **Thalidomide (1960s)** created the concept of drug teratogenicity and the "avoid everything" reflex.
- In psychiatry, early alarms changed practice dramatically. Some were later shown to be largely confounded:
  - paroxetine cardiac defects (2005);
  - SSRI-related PPHN (Chambers 2006);
  - SSRI and autism (Croen 2011).
- **Valproate** is the opposite story: an early modest signal grew into a major neurodevelopmental hazard [1].

### Epidemiology
- About **10%** of pregnant women have depression, and up to a third of postpartum depression begins before birth [1].
- Antidepressant exposure: about 3–8% of pregnancies in high-income countries [general knowledge].
- **Baseline risks:**
  - major malformation 2–3%;
  - PPHN about 1–2 per 1,000 (general population); SSRI late exposure adds about 1–2 per 1,000 at most (Huybrechts 2015: adjusted OR about 1.1–1.3) [general knowledge; check];
  - PNAS after SSRI exposure about 20–30% [general knowledge].

### Classification of outcome types (Schaefer framework, general knowledge)
1. Structural (morphological) teratogenicity.
2. Fetotoxicity (growth, organ function).
3. Neonatal syndromes (toxicity vs withdrawal; often indistinguishable).
4. Behavioural teratogenicity (long-term cognition and behaviour).

### Mechanism
- **SSRIs:**
  - serotonin regulates cardiac and craniofacial development and pulmonary vascular tone, hence the plausibility of septal defects and PPHN;
  - **PNAS** may reflect **serotonergic toxicity** (hyperthermia, myoclonus) or **discontinuation**. Clinically they overlap.
  - **Sertraline** has the lowest placental passage [1].
- **Antipsychotics:** D2 blockade causes EPS and dyskinesia; metabolic effects cause GDM, fetal hyperinsulinaemia and macrosomia.
- **Benzodiazepines:** GABA-A agonism in an immature neonate with slow glucuronidation causes accumulation, leading to hypotonia, apnoea and hypothermia (floppy infant).
- **Lithium:** free placental equilibration; neonatal renal immaturity causes accumulation and thyroid and renal toxicity.
- **Valproate:** HDAC inhibition, folate antagonism, oxidative stress.

### Clinical features: recognising PNAS
- **CNS:** jitteriness, tremor, irritability, high-pitched cry, hypertonia or hypotonia, seizures (rare).
- **Respiratory:** tachypnoea, respiratory distress.
- **Gastrointestinal:** poor feeding, vomiting.
- **Autonomic:** temperature instability, hypoglycaemia.
- **Onset** within hours to 3 days. Usually resolves within 2 weeks.
- The **Finnegan score** (designed for opioid withdrawal) is sometimes adapted.

### Assessment: interpreting a study for a patient
- Ask:
  - Was the **comparison group** women with the same illness who were unexposed?
  - Was **dose** recorded?
  - What was the **absolute risk** difference?
  - Were siblings compared?
  - Was **confounding** by smoking, alcohol, BMI and polypharmacy controlled?
- Express risks per 1,000 births when counselling.

### Differential: drug vs illness vs other causes of adverse neonatal outcome
- Maternal depression and anxiety (cortisol and HPA programming).
- Smoking (the largest single contributor in women with schizophrenia).
- Alcohol: fetal alcohol spectrum disorder was 10 times more common in one SSRI-exposed cohort, perhaps from self-medication.
- Illicit drugs: opioids cause neonatal abstinence syndrome.
- Obesity and diabetes.
- Prematurity itself.
- Infection.
- Genetic syndromes.

### Management: framework for counselling (BAP 2017, NICE)
1. Risk of the illness untreated (relapse rates; harm to mother and fetus).
2. Risk of each drug (absolute numbers).
3. Risk of switching (relapse; exposure to two drugs).
4. Patient preference and values.
5. Plan:
   - scans;
   - monitoring;
   - delivery setting;
   - neonatal observation;
   - breastfeeding;
   - documentation.

### Evidence table
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Huybrechts 2014, *NEJM* (general knowledge) | 950,000 pregnancies: no significant cardiac malformation increase with SSRIs after adjustment for depression |
| Furu 2015, *BMJ* (sibling design) | SSRI and venlafaxine cardiac association attenuated in sibling analysis [1] |
| Huybrechts 2015, *JAMA* | Late SSRI exposure and PPHN: modest increase, small absolute risk [1] |
| Brown 2017, *JAMA*; Sujan 2017, *JAMA* | Autism association with SSRIs not significant after controlling for confounders; sibling comparison null [1] |
| Suarez 2022, *JAMA Intern Med* | Antidepressants in pregnancy do not increase neurodevelopmental disorders [1] |
| Cohen 2006, *JAMA* | 68% relapse after stopping antidepressants vs 26% continuing [1] |
| Straub 2022; Wang 2021 | Antipsychotics not causally linked with ADHD or ASD [1] |
| Meng 2023, *Lancet Psychiatry* | Late-pregnancy benzodiazepines: stillbirth and preterm birth; early: SGA only [1] |
| Jentink 2010, *NEJM*; NEAD | Valproate malformations and IQ deficits [1] |
| Koc 2023, *JAMA Psychiatry* | SSRI exposure: brain morphology changes into adolescence; clinical significance unclear [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- **Confounding by indication** dominates. Active-comparator and sibling designs are the strongest available methods.
- **Detection bias:** exposed infants are examined more (echocardiography finds septal defects).
- **Multiple comparisons** generate isolated signals.
- **Exposure misclassification:** prescriptions filled are not the same as tablets taken.
- **Publication bias** toward positive signals.
- **Long-term outcomes** need decades of follow-up; studies so far reach adolescence at most.
- The **Indian evidence base is minimal**, so extrapolation from Nordic registries is unavoidable.

### Indian context
- Antenatal mental health screening is not routine in **Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan** check-ups [Guessing].
- Neonatal intensive care availability varies by district.
- Many women discontinue on their own because of family pressure.
- Psychiatrists should liaise with obstetricians (Janani Suraksha Yojana institutional delivery) [general knowledge].
- NIMHANS perinatal service data (Chandra and colleagues) show high rates of medication discontinuation in pregnancy among Indian women [Likely; check].

### Recent advances
- **Brexanolone and zuranolone** (neurosteroid GABA-A modulators) for postpartum depression [1]. These are postnatal treatments, so they avoid fetal exposure.
- **Large multinational registries and target-trial emulation** methods.
- **Pharmacogenomic and therapeutic drug monitoring** approaches to dosing across pregnancy.
