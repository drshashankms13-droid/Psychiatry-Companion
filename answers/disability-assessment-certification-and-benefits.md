## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Psychiatric (psychosocial) disability** is the long-term limitation in activities and participation (self-care, relationships, communication, work) that results from mental illness, in interaction with environmental barriers. The **Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act 2016** recognises **21 specified disabilities**. Those relevant to psychiatry are:
- **mental illness**;
- **intellectual disability**;
- **specific learning disability (SLD)**;
- **autism spectrum disorder (ASD)**;
- chronic neurological conditions, including dementia;
- multiple disabilities.

A **person with benchmark disability** has **at least 40%** of a specified disability, certified by a medical authority. Most benefits require benchmark disability [1, 2].

### Legal framework
- **RPwD Act 2016**, replacing the PwD Act 1995, aligned with UNCRPD. Certification is under **Sections 56–59**.
- **Guidelines for assessment and certification:**
  - first notified **2018**;
  - **superseded by the Gazette notification of 12 March 2024** (Section 56; G.S.R.) [1].
- **UDID** (Unique Disability ID) portal and card: a single national certificate and ID [1].

### Certification of mental illness (2024 guidelines) [1]
- **Definition (as in MHCA):** a substantial disorder of thinking, mood, perception, orientation or memory that grossly impairs judgement, behaviour, capacity to recognise reality, or ability to meet the ordinary demands of life. **Excludes intellectual disability.**
- **Examination:** clinical assessment, **IDEAS** and/or IQ assessment.
  - IDEAS alone suffices where only the behavioural condition is involved.
  - An IQ test is added if intellectual deficit is suspected.
  - **Co-existing ID and mental illness** is certified as **multiple disability**.
- **Duration:**
  - **Permanent certificate** requires at least **2 years** of illness from onset.
  - Under 2 years: a **time-limited certificate** (permanent may be considered if severe or profound).
- **Treatment requirement:**
  - **Treatment-naïve** persons or those **without adequate treatment**: certificate valid up to **2 years** only, with advice to seek treatment.
  - **Permanent certificate** only for **residual disability despite adequate evidence-based treatment**, documented in medical records.
  - Treated persons **without records**: time-limited certificate; preserve records and reassess after 2 years.
- **Medical Board:** headed by the **Medical Superintendent/CMO/Civil Surgeon**, plus a **psychiatrist**, and a psychiatrist/physician or **RCI-registered clinical or rehabilitation psychologist / PSW** (a psychological report within 3 months where required). **Private specialists may be co-opted** because of backlogs.

### IDEAS: Indian Disability Evaluation and Assessment Scale (Indian Psychiatric Society, 2002) [3]
- **Four domains:**
  1. Self-care.
  2. Interpersonal activities (social relationships).
  3. Communication and understanding.
  4. Work (employment, housework or education).
- **Scoring:**
  - Each domain is scored **0–4** (0 none, 1 mild, 2 moderate, 3 severe, 4 profound).
  - Domain total: 0–16.
  - Plus a **duration of illness (MI duration) score of 1–4**.
  - Gives the **global IDEAS score (0–20)**.
- **Conversion to percentage** (verify bands against the notified table):

| Global IDEAS score | Disability |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No disability (0%) |
| 1–6 | Mild (<40%) |
| **7–13** | **Moderate (40–70%)**, i.e., benchmark |
| 14–19 | Severe (71–99%) |
| 20 | Profound (100%) |

A **global score of 7 or more corresponds to ≥40% (benchmark) disability** [3]. For **multiple disability** calculations, the 2024 guidelines use the **midpoint** of the band: moderate = 55%, severe = 85% [1].

### Other psychiatric-relevant certifications (2024) [1]
| Condition | Tool | Key rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intellectual disability** | **VSMS** for disability %; IQ by BKT, WISC-IV, MISIC, NIEPID Indian Test of Intelligence | VSMS score → disability: 0–20 profound (100%); 21–35 severe (90%); 36–54 moderate (75%); 55–69 mild (50%); 70–84 borderline (25%). Minimum age 1 year. Under 5: temporary **Global Developmental Delay** certificate. Renew at 10 and 18; lifelong after 18 |
| **ASD** | Diagnosis by **INDT-ASD** (AIIMS-modified INCLEN, DSM-5 based); severity by **ISAA** | ISAA 70–106 mild (40–59%); 107–153 moderate (60–79%); >153 severe. Under 6: temporary (60–79%), reassess at 6–7. Lifelong after 18 |
| **SLD** | Paediatric/neurological + vision and hearing → **psychiatrist** (rule out mimics) → IQ ≥85 → **NIMHANS battery or GLAD** | Functioning **3 SD below current class**; age 8 or above; repeat in Class X and XII if needed; lifelong after 18 |
| **Dementia** | **IDEAS** | Under chronic neurological conditions guidelines |

### Process for the patient
Apply on the **UDID portal** (or through the district hospital) → Medical Board assessment → certificate with percentage, permanent or temporary status and validity → UDID card. **Appeal** under Section 59 to the appellate authority [1, 2].

### Benefits (with ≥40% benchmark disability, unless stated)
| Domain | Benefit |
| --- | --- |
| **Employment** | **4% reservation** in government posts (Section 34). **1%** is shared by **ASD, ID, SLD and mental illness** (with acid-attack victims and multiple disabilities, per the clause). Age relaxation; reasonable accommodation; **non-discrimination and no demotion or dismissal** for acquired disability (Section 20) [2, 4] |
| **Education** | **5% reservation** in government and aided higher education (Section 32). Free education for children 6–18 with benchmark disability (Section 31). **Exam accommodations**: extra time, scribe, exemption from a language (SLD) [2] |
| **Social security** | State **disability pensions** (amounts vary by state); Indira Gandhi National Disability Pension Scheme for **BPL** persons with severe disability (≥80%); unemployment allowance in some states [4] |
| **Health and insurance** | **Niramaya** health insurance (National Trust) for **ASD, CP, ID and multiple disabilities** (mental illness is **not** covered); Ayushman Bharat |
| **Tax** | **Section 80U** (self) and **80DD** (dependant), with higher deductions for severe disability (≥80%) |
| **Guardianship** | National Trust Act 1999 **local level committees** for **ASD, ID, CP, multiple disability** (not mental illness) |
| **Travel and concessions** | Railway concessions (for intellectual disability with escort, and some categories; check whether mental illness qualifies); state bus passes |
| **Housing, poverty alleviation** | Priority in schemes such as PMAY; 5% reservation in poverty alleviation schemes (Section 37) [2] |
| **Assistive / rehabilitation** | ADIP scheme; **DDRS** (Deendayal Disabled Rehabilitation Scheme) grants to NGOs for day care and halfway homes |
| **High support needs** | Section 38 assessment for high support |

**Conclusion.** Disability certification turns psychiatric illness into **legal entitlements**: employment reservation, education support, pensions and tax relief. The **2024 guidelines** strengthened the process by tying permanent certification to **at least 2 years of illness and adequate treatment**, standardising tools (IDEAS, VSMS, ISAA, NIMHANS battery or GLAD) and using UDID. Psychiatrists should **proactively offer certification** to people with severe mental illness, document treatment carefully, and help families use benefits. Uptake is still poor because of stigma, bureaucracy and low awareness.

## In depth

### Background
- **PwD Act 1995** included "mental illness" and "mental retardation" with 3% reservation (1% each for three categories, none for mental illness in practice).
- **IDEAS** was developed by an IPS task force (2002) and adopted by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (MSJE) guidelines (2002) for certifying mental illness [3].
- **UNCRPD (2007 ratification)** → **RPwD Act 2016** (7 → 21 disabilities; 4% reservation) → 2018 assessment guidelines → **2024 revised guidelines** (Gazette, 12 and 14 March 2024) [1].

### Epidemiology
- **Census 2011:** 2.68 crore persons with disabilities (2.21%). "Mental illness" and "mental retardation" were each about 0.7–1.5 million (check Census tables).
- **NMHS 2015–16:** at least half of those with depression, bipolar disorder or epilepsy reported disability. Extreme disability in schizophrenia was 20.5–28.2% [5].
- **Low uptake:** in a JIPMER general hospital psychiatry unit, a few hundred certificates were issued over 5 years. Most were for schizophrenia. **The validity period was missing in 81.4% of certificates** [3].

### Classification and criteria
| RPwD category (Schedule) | Psychiatric relevance |
| --- | --- |
| Intellectual disability: SLD, ASD | Child psychiatry |
| Mental behaviour (mental illness) | Adult psychiatry |
| Chronic neurological conditions (MS, Parkinson's; dementia assessed by IDEAS) | Neuropsychiatry [1] |
| Multiple disabilities | Combined (combining formula) [1] |

**Combining formula for multiple disabilities** [1]: total = a + b(100 − a)/100, where a is the higher value. Apply successively, highest first. Include only components of 25% or more. Cap at 100%.

### Mechanism in detail: how disability arises
Symptoms (positive, negative, cognitive), course (chronic, relapsing), treatment side effects and stigma lead to functional limitation in self-care, relationships, communication and work. The **ICF (WHO) framework** treats disability as impairment × activity limitation × participation restriction × environmental factors.

### Clinical features in detail: who to certify
- Typical candidates: **chronic schizophrenia**, bipolar disorder with residual impairment, chronic severe OCD, recurrent depression with impairment, dementia, ID and ASD.
- **Substance use disorders:** usually not certified [3]. Personality disorders are contested.

### Assessment in detail: administering IDEAS [3]
- **Information sources:** patient, informant and records. Rate the **past 2 weeks** (or the predominant level over the past 2 years for chronic illness).
- **Self-care:** hygiene, grooming, eating, dressing.
- **Interpersonal:** initiating and responding; family and friends.
- **Communication and understanding:** conversation, understanding instructions.
- **Work:** job, housework or studies.
- **Duration of illness score** (verify): under 2 years = 1; 2–5 years = 2; 6–10 years = 3; over 10 years = 4.
- **Who scores:** senior resident, PSW or clinical psychologist, with **independent review by a consultant psychiatrist** [3].

### Differential: pitfalls in certification
| Pitfall | Correct approach |
| --- | --- |
| Certifying during an acute episode | Assess residual disability after adequate treatment [1] |
| Ignoring the 2-year rule | Time-limited certificate if under 2 years [1] |
| Missing ID comorbidity | IQ test; certify as multiple disability [1] |
| No validity mentioned | Always specify permanent/temporary and the review date [3] |
| Malingering or exaggeration (benefit-seeking) | Collateral history, records, consistency |
| Treatment-naïve applicants | 2-year certificate plus a referral for treatment [1] |

### Management details: the psychiatrist's role
1. Identify eligible patients and **discuss benefits** with them and their families.
2. **Document treatment** (dates, doses, adherence) to support permanent certification [1].
3. Provide disability, rehabilitation and **employment support** (supported employment, IPS).
4. Write certificates **only through the notified Medical Board**. Individual psychiatrist certificates are not valid for benefits.
5. Support appeals; help with UDID enrolment.

### Critical appraisal
- **IDEAS strengths:** simple; quick; Indian; validated for chronic mental illness; easy to train.
- **IDEAS limitations:**
  - Crude 4-domain scale; subjective; weighting of duration is debatable.
  - Not sensitive to cognitive or episodic disability.
  - Poor fit for anxiety, OCD or personality disorders.
  - Little inter-rater reliability data at scale.
- **2024 rule debates:**
  - The 2-year minimum and "adequate treatment" requirement guard against premature labelling.
  - But they may **delay benefits** for severely ill or treatment-refractory people, and for those who lacked access to treatment.
- **Systemic barriers:**
  - Stigma (families hide illness).
  - **Low awareness**; travel to district boards; bureaucratic delays.
  - Boards lack psychiatrists; benefits are fragmented across ministries.
  - **Mental illness is excluded from National Trust schemes** (Niramaya, guardianship).
  - The 1% reservation is shared by four categories.
- **Rights view:** certification should enable participation (reasonable accommodation), not just welfare.

### Indian context
- **Implementing bodies:** Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (DEPwD, MSJE); state disability commissioners; **UDID portal** (swavlambancard.gov.in).
- **Related laws:**
  - MHCA 2017 (rights, community living).
  - National Trust Act 1999 (ASD, ID, CP, multiple disability).
  - **RCI Act 1992** (registers rehabilitation professionals).
- **State variation:** pension amounts and travel concessions differ. Check the current state schemes.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** 2024 notification (revised tools, treatment-linked permanence, private specialists on boards); UDID cards [1].
- **Emerging:** digital applications and tele-assessment; proposals for function-based ICF-aligned tools; inclusion of mental illness in more welfare schemes (advocacy).
