## Exam answer

### Introduction

Psychotherapy is an important adjunct to pharmacotherapy in bipolar disorder. It improves illness understanding, medication adherence, early detection of relapse, coping and psychosocial functioning. It does not generally replace mood-stabilising medication.

### Major evidence-based interventions

1.  **Psychoeducation:** teaches illness course, symptoms, treatment
    adherence, sleep regularity, substance avoidance and early warning
    signs. It may reduce recurrence and hospitalisation.
2.  **Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT):** targets maladaptive
    beliefs, depressive cognitions, behavioural activation, coping and
    adherence. It is generally used as an adjunct to medication.
3.  **Family-Focused Therapy (FFT):** includes psychoeducation,
    communication enhancement and problem-solving. It aims to reduce
    expressed emotion and improve family support.
4.  **Interpersonal and Social Rhythm Therapy (IPSRT):** stabilises
    daily routines and sleep–wake rhythms while addressing
    interpersonal stressors.
5.  **Mindfulness-based approaches:** may improve awareness, emotional
    regulation and residual depressive symptoms; evidence is less
    established than for core bipolar psychotherapies.
6.  **Peer support and self-management:** can reduce isolation and
    improve empowerment when integrated with clinical care.

### Indications and clinical use

Psychotherapy is useful during maintenance and recovery phases, and as an adjunct during acute episodes when clinically appropriate. Choice depends on patient preference, phase of illness, cognitive capacity, family context and availability of trained therapists.

### Limitations

Access to trained therapists is limited; treatment requires time and engagement. Evidence varies by intervention and illness phase. Psychotherapy should not delay urgent treatment of mania, severe depression or high suicide risk.

### Conclusion

Psychoeducation, CBT, FFT and IPSRT have the strongest established roles as adjunctive treatments. Combining medication with structured psychotherapy offers a comprehensive approach to bipolar disorder.
