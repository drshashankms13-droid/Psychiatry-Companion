## Exam answer

**Definition.**
- **Somatic symptom disorder (SSD; DSM-5, 2013)** requires one or more distressing or disruptive somatic symptoms **plus** excessive thoughts, feelings or behaviours about them (any one of: disproportionate and persistent thoughts about seriousness; persistently high health anxiety; excessive time and energy devoted to symptoms or health concerns), with symptoms persisting **>6 months**. Specifiers: with predominant pain, persistent, and mild/moderate/severe.
- **Bodily distress disorder (BDD; ICD-11 6C20, 2018):** distressing bodily symptoms with **excessive attention** directed toward them, shown by repeated contact with health-care providers. Medical reassurance and examination do not relieve this. Symptoms persist on most days for at least several months. Severity: mild, moderate, severe.
- Both replaced the older **"somatoform disorders"** (somatisation, undifferentiated somatoform, somatoform pain, hypochondriasis). They drop the negative "medically unexplained" criterion in favour of **positive psychobehavioural features** [1].

### Mechanism (biopsychosocial; perception model)
**Predisposing:**
- Genetics (up to ~30% of variance; partly distinct from anxiety and depression).
- Adverse childhood experiences; insecure attachment; poor emotion regulation (alexithymia).
- Cultural tendency to express distress somatically [1].

**Triggering:** infection, injury, life stress, illness in others [1].

**Maintaining (most relevant clinically):**
- Somatosensory amplification and **predictive-processing errors**: the brain's top-down expectations generate or amplify interoceptive signals.
- Health anxiety and illness conviction; catastrophising; symptom-focused attention.
- Avoidance → deconditioning.
- Iatrogenic factors: repeated tests, conflicting messages, inappropriate treatments.
- Operant reinforcement (secondary gain) [1].

### Assessment
- **Collaborative stance:** symptoms are real. Do not equate them with malingering [1].
- Thorough but proportionate medical evaluation; review previous investigations; avoid unnecessary tests.
- **Severity:** mild (one functional somatic syndrome, little impairment), moderate, or severe (multiple organ systems, high health anxiety, disability, high health-care use) [1].
- **Psychiatric comorbidity:** depression, anxiety, panic, PTSD, personality disorder (especially in severe cases) [1].
- **Scales:** PHQ-15 (somatic symptom burden), Whiteley Index-7 (health anxiety), SSD-12 (psychobehavioural features), PHQ-9, GAD-7.

### Management — principles and stepped care

| Level | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| **All patients (primary care / treating physician)** | Therapeutic alliance; legitimise symptoms; **regular scheduled visits not contingent on symptoms**; one coordinating doctor; treat comorbid medical disease; limit tests and referrals; reassure that grave disease has been ruled out; **explain how the body can generate symptoms** (stress, nervous system sensitivity, vicious circles); taper unnecessary drugs (opioids, benzodiazepines); explore psychosocial cues; **goal = coping and function, not cure** ("care, not cure") [2] |
| **Moderate** | Graded activity/exercise, relaxation, problem-solving; brief CBT by trained GP/nurse; consider antidepressant if pain or depression predominates [1,2] |
| **Severe / complex** | Specialist **CBT** (individual > group > usual care); psychodynamic (brief psychodynamic interpersonal therapy), mindfulness, ACT; multidisciplinary rehabilitation; liaise with specialists; treat comorbidities [1,2] |

**Pharmacotherapy** [2,3]
- SSRIs, SNRIs and TCAs improve symptoms in meta-analyses, especially pain (amitriptyline 10–75 mg, duloxetine 60 mg) and comorbid depression or anxiety.
- Use low starting doses (patients are sensitive to side effects).
- Avoid opioids and long-term benzodiazepines.
- Pregabalin or gabapentin for fibromyalgia-type pain (misuse risk).

**CBT model and techniques**
- Psychoeducation on the vicious circle: symptom → catastrophic interpretation → anxiety/attention → more symptoms → avoidance or checking.
- Reattribution; attention retraining; reducing checking, reassurance-seeking and doctor shopping; graded activity; activity pacing; addressing illness beliefs; stress management; relapse prevention.

### Key evidence
- **van Dessel 2014 (Cochrane, 21 RCTs):** psychological therapies (mainly CBT) modestly reduce somatic symptoms vs usual care (SMD ~−0.34).
- **Kleinstäuber 2014 (Cochrane):** new-generation antidepressants and natural products have low-quality evidence of benefit; TCAs are effective.
- **Henningsen 2018:** multidisciplinary stepped care; moderate benefit from various treatments [1].
- **Reattribution training** for GPs (Morriss 2007) improved communication but had a small effect on outcome.

**Conclusion.** Bodily distress and somatic symptom disorders are common, disabling and costly. Management rests on a sound doctor–patient relationship, coordinated and limited medical care, explanation, and the goal of improved function. CBT is the best-evidenced psychological treatment, with antidepressants for pain or comorbid mood and anxiety disorders, in a stepped-care model matched to severity.

## In depth

### Background
- Briquet (1859) described hysteria with multiple bodily complaints. **Briquet's syndrome** (Guze, Perley 1960s; St Louis criteria) required many symptoms.
- DSM-III (1980) "somatoform disorders": somatisation disorder (DSM-IV: 8 symptoms in 4 groups).
- Criticisms of mind–body dualism, poor reliability and the stigmatising "medically unexplained" label led to DSM-5 SSD (2013) and ICD-11 BDD (2018). Fink and Schröder's "bodily distress syndrome" (2010) informed ICD-11 [1].
- Hypochondriasis became **illness anxiety disorder** (DSM-5) and moved to OCD-related disorders in ICD-11 (6B23 hypochondriasis) [1].

### Epidemiology
- Bodily distress or functional somatic symptoms account for about 20–25% of primary care consultations and up to 30–50% in specialist clinics [1].
- SSD is about 5–7% in the general population.
- Female preponderance (2:1 to 5:1).
- Comorbid depression or anxiety in up to 50%. Personality disorder is common in severe cases [1].
- Long-term outcome is poor for multiple-symptom presentations: disability, sick leave, high costs [1].
- Low back and neck pain, which overlap heavily with bodily distress, are top-5 causes of DALYs globally [1].
- Indian studies: high rates of somatic presentations of depression and anxiety in primary care ("weakness", "gas", burning sensations).

### Classification and criteria

| System | Category | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DSM-IV | Somatisation disorder | ≥8 unexplained symptoms (4 pain, 2 GI, 1 sexual, 1 pseudoneurological) before age 30 |
| DSM-5-TR | **Somatic symptom disorder** | ≥1 distressing symptom + excessive thoughts/feelings/behaviours; >6 months; explained or not |
| DSM-5-TR | Illness anxiety disorder | Health anxiety with minimal somatic symptoms |
| DSM-5-TR | Functional neurological symptom disorder | Neurological symptoms incompatible with disease |
| ICD-11 | **6C20 Bodily distress disorder** (mild, moderate, severe) | Distressing symptoms + excessive attention; repeated contact; reassurance fails |
| ICD-11 | 6B23 Hypochondriasis (OCRD chapter) | Health anxiety |
| ICD-11 | 6B60 Dissociative neurological symptom disorder | Conversion |
| ICD-11 Primary care | Bodily stress syndrome | ≥3 distressing symptoms [1] |
| Specialty | Functional somatic syndromes (IBS, fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue) | Rome IV, ACR criteria [1] |

**Criticisms of DSM-5 SSD:** over-inclusive (it could label patients with cancer or heart disease plus distress), with arbitrary positive criteria [1].

### Mechanism in detail
- **Predictive processing:** perception = prior expectation + sensory evidence. Strong priors (illness beliefs, attention) override weak interoceptive signals and create real symptoms. Patients show worse interoceptive accuracy [1].
- **Central sensitisation:** in pain syndromes (fibromyalgia); reduced descending inhibition.
- **HPA axis and immune:** altered cortisol, low-grade inflammation in some.
- **Psychological:** somatosensory amplification (Barsky), alexithymia, catastrophising, illness behaviour (Pilowsky's "abnormal illness behaviour"), attachment insecurity [1].
- **Social and iatrogenic:** a fragmented health system, defensive investigations, and secondary gain [1].

### Clinical features in detail
Multiple symptoms (pain, fatigue, GI, cardiopulmonary, neurological); frequent consultations and investigations; doctor shopping; polypharmacy; disability; strained relationships with doctors; comorbid depression and anxiety. **Indian idioms:** "body ache all over", "weakness" (kamzori), "heat in the body", burning feet, "gas", dhat (semen loss) anxiety.

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
| --- | --- |
| Undiagnosed medical disease | Red flags: weight loss, fever, objective signs, age >50 with new symptoms (MS, SLE, hyperparathyroidism, porphyria, endocrine) |
| Depression / anxiety / panic | Somatic symptoms secondary to the primary syndrome |
| Illness anxiety disorder | Health fear with minimal symptoms |
| Functional neurological disorder | Positive neurological signs (Hoover's) |
| Factitious disorder | Intentional production, no external incentive |
| Malingering | Intentional, external incentive |
| Delusional disorder, somatic type | Fixed false belief, no insight |
| Body dysmorphic disorder | Appearance-focused |
| Personality disorder | Pervasive patterns |

### Management details
- **Communication:** give a positive diagnosis ("your symptoms are real and common, and we understand how they arise"). Avoid "nothing is wrong" and "it's all in your mind". Use explanatory metaphors (a sensitive alarm system; a volume knob) [1,2].
- **Consultation structure:** regular, time-limited appointments (every 2–6 weeks); brief physical examination at each visit (reassures); avoid symptom-contingent visits [2].
- **Medication rationalisation:** taper benzodiazepines, opioids and unnecessary supplements [2].
- **Antidepressants:** low-dose TCA (amitriptyline 10–25 mg at night) for pain and sleep; SNRIs for pain with depression; SSRIs for anxiety and depression. Warn about initial side effects [2,3].
- **CBT:** 8–16 sessions; individual is superior to group [2].
- **Other therapies:** brief psychodynamic interpersonal therapy (Hamilton; Sattel 2012 RCT for multisomatoform), mindfulness-based approaches, ACT, hypnotherapy (IBS), graded exercise (chronic fatigue; controversial).
- **Collaborative care:** psychiatric consultation-liaison with primary care; a "consultation letter" to the GP (Smith 1986) reduced costs.
- **Special groups:** children (family-based CBT); elderly (comorbid disease); cultural adaptation (Indian somatic idioms).

### Critical appraisal
- **Nosology:** SSD may over-diagnose (patients with medical illness) and BDD may under-capture. Unifying functional somatic syndromes (IBS, fibromyalgia) with psychiatric categories is unresolved [1].
- **Patient acceptability:** "distress" implies a psychological cause that some patients reject. Communication matters [1].
- **Effect sizes are modest** across treatments; there are many unmet needs and structural barriers (reimbursement, fragmentation) [1].
- **Dualism:** the move away from "medically unexplained" is welcome but implementation is inconsistent.

### Indian context
- Somatisation is the commonest presentation of common mental disorders in Indian primary care. Many patients see multiple doctors and receive vitamin injections, analgesics and benzodiazepines.
- Culture-bound and idiom-specific presentations: dhat syndrome, "gas", "brain fag", burning feet, *kamzori*.
- Faith healers and AYUSH practitioners are often consulted. Integrated care through Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, DMHP and Tele-MANAS can help.
- MHCA 2017 rights; avoid stigmatising labels.
- Indian research: the Bradford Somatic Inventory; somatic symptom studies at PGIMER, NIMHANS and CMC Vellore.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** ICD-11 BDD with severity grading [1].
- **Emerging:** predictive-processing-based interventions (expectation-focused psychotherapy); internet CBT for somatic symptoms; ACT; transdiagnostic "PRESS" models [1].
- **Investigational:** interoceptive training; neuromodulation (rTMS for fibromyalgia: HF motor cortex has evidence in the IPS CPG) [4]; digital therapeutics.
