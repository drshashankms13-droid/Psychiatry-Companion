## Exam answer

**What it is.**
- Lemborexant is an **orexin receptor antagonist**, in the same class as suvorexant and daridorexant [1, 2].
- It is used for **insomnia** [1]. Brand: **Dayvigo** (Eisai) [1].

**Why orexin is a target [2].**
- Orexin (hypocretin) neurons of the **lateral hypothalamus** reinforce monoaminergic wake-promoting tone.
- They **stabilise the VLPO–monoaminergic "flip-flop" switch** between sleep and wakefulness. The VLPO neurons themselves lack orexin receptors.
- In insomnia (hyperarousal), wake-promoting transmitters such as **orexin**, histamine, dopamine and cortisol are raised.
- **Blocking orexin receptors** therefore promotes sleep.

**Clinical use [1, 2].**
- **Dose:** **5 mg at night**, taken no more than once a night, **immediately before bed** [1].
- **Effect:** orexin receptor antagonists **reduce sleep-onset latency and increase total sleep time** [2].
- **Dementia:** a Cochrane review (2020) found some beneficial effects on sleep from trazodone and orexin antagonists (**suvorexant and lemborexant; two studies, n = 323**), with no harm, though larger trials are needed [1].
- **Driving:** orexin antagonists (suvorexant, lemborexant) **appear not to impair driving the next day**. By contrast, daridorexant may impair driving in the first few days, and zolpidem impairs it [1].

**Special populations [1].**
- **Hepatic impairment:**
  - mild: no adjustment (risk of more somnolence);
  - **moderate: starting and maximum dose 5 mg**;
  - severe: **not recommended**;
  - hepatotoxicity has not been reported.
- **Renal impairment:** less than 1% is excreted unchanged, and no adjustment is needed. Exposure may rise in **severe** renal impairment, with more somnolence.
- **Overdose:** none reported; 75 mg was the maximum trial dose. Expect increased somnolence.

**Place in therapy.** It is one of the newer hypnotic options, alongside z-drugs, melatonin agonists and sedating antidepressants. It is particularly relevant where next-day impairment matters [1, 2].
