## Exam answer

### Definition and diagnostic features

Illness Anxiety Disorder (IAD), introduced in DSM-5, is characterised by preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness, with absent or mild somatic symptoms. The individual has high health anxiety and performs excessive health-related behaviours or maladaptive avoidance. The preoccupation persists for at least six months, although the feared illness may change.

DSM-5 describes care-seeking and care-avoidant types.

### Clinical features

Patients may repeatedly check their body, seek medical consultations and reassurance, or avoid hospitals and health information. Minor sensations may be interpreted catastrophically. Functional and interpersonal impairment can occur.

### Aetiology

Predisposing factors include childhood illness experiences, family modelling, anxiety sensitivity, intolerance of uncertainty and cognitive biases. A cognitive-behavioural model describes bodily sensations being misinterpreted as evidence of serious illness, leading to anxiety, checking and reassurance-seeking that maintain the cycle.

### Assessment and differential diagnosis

Assess symptom preoccupation, duration, impairment, healthcare utilisation, avoidance, medical history and comorbid anxiety/depression. Exclude medical conditions and consider somatic symptom disorder, OCD, panic disorder, delusional disorder, depression and factitious disorder.

Possible measures include the Health Anxiety Inventory, Short Health Anxiety Inventory and Whiteley Index.

### Management

1.  Establish a collaborative, validating relationship.
2.  Arrange planned follow-up and coordinated medical care.
3.  Provide clear explanations without repeated unnecessary
    investigations.
4.  Use CBT targeting catastrophic interpretations, checking, avoidance
    and reassurance-seeking.
5.  Consider SSRIs when anxiety is severe or comorbid depression/anxiety
    is present.
6.  Address functional impairment and family accommodation.

### Conclusion

IAD is characterised by excessive fear of serious illness despite absent or mild somatic symptoms. CBT, continuity of care and appropriate management of comorbidity are central to treatment.
