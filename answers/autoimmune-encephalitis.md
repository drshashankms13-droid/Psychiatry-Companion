## Exam answer

### Introduction [1, 2]
- **Concept:** immune-mediated inflammation of the brain, particularly the **limbic system**, producing memory loss, psychiatric disturbance and seizures. It may be **paraneoplastic** (remote effect of cancer) or **non-paraneoplastic** [1].
- **Why psychiatrists must know it:**
  - patients frequently present **first to psychiatry** with psychosis, catatonia, mood or personality change, often with **no neurological signs early**;
  - encephalitis presenting with **delusions (54%), auditory hallucinations (44%)** and depression has been described [1];
  - it is a recognised cause of **catatonia** [2].
- **Reversibility:** potentially **treatable with immunotherapy** and tumour removal [1].

### Types described in the source [1]
**1. Paraneoplastic limbic encephalitis (limbic encephalopathy)** (Corsellis 1968):
- **tumours:** usually **small-cell lung carcinoma** (often hilar nodes only); also testis, lymphoma, breast, thymus, bladder. The tumour may be found only at post-mortem;
- **symptoms** may **precede the cancer diagnosis** (in about one-third), progress despite treatment of the tumour, or appear after its removal;
- **clinical:**
  - **marked recent-memory impairment**, later more general intellectual impairment;
  - **early affective disturbance** (severe anxiety or depression);
  - hallucinations, **seizures**;
  - consciousness usually preserved;
  - sometimes carcinomatous neuropathy;
- **frequency:** cognitive impairment 84–92%; **psychiatric features 42–50%**; EEG abnormal 82–100%; CSF abnormal 78–80%; serum antineuronal antibodies 60–64%; **MRI temporal signal change 57–83%**; seizures 50–58%; tumour lung 50–54%, testis 8–20%;
- **pathology:** inflammation and neuronal loss in the **hippocampus, amygdala, uncus, cingulate, insula and orbital cortex** (sometimes the fornices and mamillary bodies). No tumour cells and no inclusion bodies; can resemble viral encephalitis. **Immune damage** is the accepted mechanism;
- **course:** weeks to 5 years.

**2. Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis** (Dalmau 2007):
- 12 young and middle-aged **women with ovarian teratoma**;
- **florid psychopathology:** **psychotic or catatonic** symptoms, "**acute personality change**", **panic**;
- most had **seizures** and **generalised EEG slowing**;
- **tumour removal plus immunotherapy** usually, not always, cured.
- **Lishman's case:** a 21-year-old student; acute depression over 24–48 hours with **overwhelming fear**, **mutism, perplexity**, disturbed behaviour (incontinence, faecal smearing); first admitted to psychiatry. **EEG** pointed to encephalitis; **MRI normal**; no tumour found. **Steroids and plasmapheresis**; recovered over 2 months.

**3. Voltage-gated potassium channel (VGKC) antibody limbic encephalitis (non-paraneoplastic):**
- **profound amnesia**;
- **seizures common** (6 of 7);
- **bilateral medial temporal** MRI signal change or atrophy (8 of 10);
- improves with **steroids, plasma exchange or IVIG**;
- **spectrum:** peripheral nerve hyperexcitability (neuromyotonia) through **Morvan's syndrome** (myokymia, autonomic features, **confusion, hallucinations, severe insomnia**) to pure limbic encephalitis; may be paraneoplastic (thymoma).

**4. Other autoimmune neuropsychiatric conditions:**
- Hashimoto's encephalopathy (listed among causes of rapidly progressive and atypical presentations; not detailed) [1];
- **anti-NMDAR antibodies in SLE** (associated with depression and cognitive impairment) [1];
- **SLE** and **antiphospholipid syndrome** as causes of catatonia [2].

### Clinical clues to an autoimmune cause in a psychiatric presentation [1, 2]
*(Assembled from the source's descriptions; formal "red flag" criteria are not in the sources.)*
- **Acute or subacute onset** of psychosis, catatonia, mood change, fear or personality change in someone without a psychiatric history.
- **Prominent recent-memory loss.**
- **Seizures**, decreased consciousness, mutism or **catatonia**.
- **Female with ovarian teratoma**; smoker or known cancer (small-cell lung).
- **Neuromyotonia, autonomic disturbance, severe insomnia** (VGKC/Morvan).
- **Abnormal EEG** (generalised slowing or temporal abnormality) or **CSF** (lymphocytosis, raised Ig, oligoclonal bands), or **MRI medial temporal T2/FLAIR change**, even if one of these is normal.

### Investigations [1]
1. **EEG:** generalised slowing (anti-NMDAR); temporal non-specific changes in paraneoplastic limbic encephalitis (unlike the periodic pattern of HSV).
2. **MRI with FLAIR:** medial temporal signal change; may be **normal**.
3. **CSF:** lymphocytosis, raised immunoglobulin, oligoclonal bands; **exclude HSV** (PCR).
4. **Serum and CSF antineuronal antibodies** (anti-NMDAR, VGKC; Purkinje-cell antibodies in cerebellar syndromes).
5. **Tumour search:** chest imaging (lung), testis, **ovarian imaging (teratoma)**, thymus.
6. Basic **organic screen** for catatonia and psychosis (metabolic, endocrine, drugs, NMS) [2].

### Differential diagnosis [1]
- **HSV encephalitis:** necrotising; PCR positive; temporal PLEDs.
- Other viral encephalitides.
- Primary psychosis, catatonic schizophrenia, mania; NMS.
- Korsakoff and other amnesic syndromes; CJD and other rapidly progressive dementias.
- Metabolic encephalopathy in cancer (fluctuating consciousness, liver metastases).

### Management [1, 2]
1. **Treat the tumour:** for example **removal of ovarian teratoma** or treatment of the lung primary. Paraneoplastic cerebellar degeneration does **not** respond to tumour treatment [1].
2. **Immunotherapy:** **corticosteroids, plasma exchange (plasmapheresis), IVIG**, as used in the anti-NMDAR and VGKC cases [1]. *(Second-line agents and treatment algorithms are not in the source.)*
3. **Catatonia:** **lorazepam**; **ECT** (responsive catatonia described in limbic encephalitis). **Treat the underlying condition** [2].
4. **Psychosis and agitation:** symptomatic treatment. *(Antipsychotic sensitivity and NMS risk in anti-NMDAR encephalitis are not discussed in the sources.)*
5. **Seizures:** anticonvulsants.
6. **Rehabilitation** of residual memory and executive deficits; family support.

**Conclusion.** Autoimmune (limbic) encephalitis causes subacute memory loss, psychiatric disturbance (anxiety, depression, psychosis, catatonia, fear, personality change) and seizures, often before neurological signs, so patients may first reach psychiatry. The sources describe:
- **paraneoplastic limbic encephalitis** (small-cell lung, testis);
- **anti-NMDAR encephalitis** (young women, ovarian teratoma, psychosis or catatonia, seizures, EEG slowing);
- **VGKC encephalitis** (amnesia, seizures, Morvan's).

Investigation is EEG, MRI-FLAIR, CSF, antibodies and a tumour search. Treatment is tumour removal plus immunotherapy (steroids, plasmapheresis, IVIG), with benzodiazepines or ECT for catatonia. Recovery is possible, so the diagnosis must not be missed. **Modern criteria and second-line therapy require an updated source (see the gap note).**
