## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 35-year-old man working in IT at a multinational company has felt **weak and easily tired for 5 years**.
- **Function:** he cannot manage day-to-day activities, partly because of **poor concentration**.
- **Lethargy:** present throughout the day, although his **mood is fine**.
- **Sleep:** good, with occasional disturbance. **Appetite:** normal. **Social functioning:** good.
- **Pain:** **generalised pain** at times for 2 years.
- **Substances:** he uses **tobacco**.
- **Investigations:** normal medical evaluation.

### 1. Differential diagnosis
1. **Chronic fatigue syndrome:**
   - K&S notes that, with fibromyalgia, it shows **CNS dysfunction and possible genetic contributions** on functional brain research;
   - although some still see these syndromes as somatisation variants, they are **established medical diagnoses** [1].
2. **Fibromyalgia** (the generalised pain adds this) [2]:
   - widespread pain with **fatigue, poor sleep, cognitive difficulties**, and often depression;
   - reflects **central sensitisation**;
   - suspect it in multifocal pain without a definable cause.
3. **Somatic symptom disorder** [2]:
   - DSM-5 removed the "medically unexplained" requirement;
   - the diagnosis rests on **distress and excessive thoughts, feelings or behaviours** about the symptoms;
   - somatisation is seen as **dimensional** (somatic distress and care-seeking).
4. **Masked or atypical depression** [2, 3, 4]:
   - his mood is reported as "fine", but presentation bias means somatic presenters are often missed (22% vs 77% correctly diagnosed) [3];
   - to separate fatigue from depression, **hopelessness, worthlessness, guilt, suicidal ideation and family history** point to depression [4].
5. **"Neurasthenia":** the historical term (George Beard, late 19th century; "tired nerves") covered depression, anxiety, somatisation and **chronic fatigue** [1].
6. **Tobacco use:** quantify it, and consider withdrawal-related symptoms and its contribution to poor health [4].

### 2. Assessment [2, 3]
- **Symptom history:**
  - onset (for example, a preceding infection or stressor), course and diurnal pattern;
  - what worsens or relieves the fatigue;
  - post-exertional worsening;
  - sleep quality versus quantity.
- **Functional impact:** work and home. Rate fatigue and pain on 0–10 scales.
- **Psychiatric screen:**
  - depression (core cognitive symptoms), anxiety, somatic preoccupation;
  - **illness beliefs and catastrophising**;
  - fear-avoidance;
  - **work stress**;
  - substance use;
  - personality.
- **Collateral** from family, and review of previous investigations. Avoid unnecessary repeat tests [3].
- **Symptom diary** of activity, rest, sleep and stressors [3].

### 3. Management [2, 3]
- **Validation and an explanatory model:** the symptoms are real. Physical and psychological factors interrelate; it is not "either/or" [2].
- **Focus on function**; limit investigations; reassure [3].
- **CBT-based rehabilitation:**
  - graded increase in activity;
  - address fear-avoidance and catastrophising;
  - build self-efficacy and coping skills;
  - relaxation [2].
- **For the pain component** (if fibromyalgia): SNRIs (duloxetine, milnacipran), gabapentin or pregabalin [2].
- **Treat any comorbid depression or anxiety** concurrently [2].
- **Tobacco cessation** support.
- **Avoid benzodiazepines** for chronic symptoms [2].
- **Workplace:** a graded return and attention to work stress.

## In depth

### Historical shift in somatisation [1]
- In the 20th century, somatisation symptoms moved from **neurological** presentations (hysterical paralysis) to **fatigue and chronic pain**.
- Shorter gives three reasons:
  1. better diagnostics ruled out neurological disease;
  2. the CNS paradigm faded;
  3. social roles changed.
- CNS explanations of chronic pain and fatigue are now gaining ground again.

### Cognitive targets in chronic symptoms [2]
- **Self-efficacy** and an internal **locus of control** are linked to lower pain and better coping.
- **Acceptance** predicts adjustment, independently of catastrophising.
- Passive or maladaptive coping goes with higher disability.
