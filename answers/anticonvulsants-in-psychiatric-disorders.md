## Exam answer

**Introduction.** "Virtually all antiseizure medications are known to have psychotropic effects", which can be **helpful or harmful** [1, p812]. Several are established in mood disorders; others are adjuncts in anxiety, aggression, substance use, schizophrenia and drug side-effects.

### Psychiatric benefits vs harms (Maudsley Table 10.4) [1, 2]

| Drug | Psychiatric benefit | Psychiatric adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Valproate** | Mood stabilising, **anti-manic**, anti-panic | Behavioural disturbance (high doses in children) |
| **Carbamazepine / oxcarbazepine / eslicarbazepine** | Mood stabilising, anti-manic | Insomnia |
| **Lamotrigine** | **Antidepressant**, mood stabilising | Anxiogenic in some; behavioural disturbance with cognitive impairment |
| **Gabapentin / pregabalin** | **Anxiolytic** | Depression and anxiety on cessation |
| Barbiturates/primidone, benzodiazepines | Anxiolytic, hypnotic | Behavioural disturbance/ADHD symptoms, depression, cognitive impairment |
| Tiagabine | Anxiolytic | Behavioural disturbance, depression |
| Phenytoin | Anti-manic | Behavioural disturbance, depression |
| Topiramate | Unclear (possibly anti-manic or antipsychotic) | Anxiety, behavioural disturbance, depression, psychosis |
| **Levetiracetam** | None confirmed | Anxiety, behavioural disturbance, depression, **suicidality** |
| Brivaracetam | None reported | Depression, aggression, rage, suicidality |
| Perampanel, ethosuximide | None reported | Behavioural disturbance, depression, **psychosis** |
| **Vigabatrin** | None reported | Behavioural disturbance/ADHD symptoms, depression, **psychosis** |
| Zonisamide | None confirmed | Behavioural disturbance, depression |
| Felbamate; rufinamide | None reported | Anxiety, depression; anxiety, insomnia |
| Lacosamide | None reported | None reported |

The seminar groups **lamotrigine, valproate, carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine** as having positive psychiatric effects [2].

### 1. Valproate [1, pp289–292]
- **Mechanism:** inhibits GABA catabolism; ERK pathway and synaptic plasticity; BDNF; reduces PKC; inositol depletion; Na⁺-channel effects.
- **Mania:**
  - response about 50% (**NNT 2–4**); may work in lithium non-responders;
  - less effective than lithium and some antipsychotics overall, but better tolerated;
  - **serum level above 94 mg/L gives the most robust response; below 55 mg/L is no better than placebo**;
  - loading doses are well tolerated.
- **Bipolar depression:** small-to-medium effect.
- **Prophylaxis:** limited RCT data. In BALANCE, lithium + valproate beat valproate alone. Adjunctive valproate adds protection. Maintenance level probably at least 50 mg/L.
- **Other:** aggression; agitation in secure settings; GAD (one small RCT).
- **Adverse effects:**
  - nausea (gastric irritation, **hyperammonaemia**);
  - lethargy and confusion at starting doses above 750 mg;
  - **weight gain** (especially with clozapine);
  - **tremor** in up to 25%; rare reversible parkinsonism with cognitive decline;
  - hair loss with curly regrowth;
  - thrombocytopenia, pancreatitis;
  - hyperandrogenism/PCOS;
  - **rare fulminant hepatic failure**.
  - Adverse effects rise sharply above 100 mg/L.
- **Teratogen:** **major human teratogen**, with impaired cognition after in-utero exposure. **Not to be used in women of child-bearing potential**.
- **Monitoring:** baseline FBC, LFT and weight; repeat at 6 months.
- **Interactions:** raises lamotrigine, TCA (clomipramine), quetiapine and warfarin levels; aspirin displaces it.

### 2. Carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine [1, pp295–298]
- **Mechanism:** Na⁺-channel blockade; reduces glutamate release and DA/NA turnover.
- **Mania:** effective (extended-release; about twice placebo response) but poorly tolerated, so **not first line**. Oxcarbazepine has useful efficacy.
- **Prophylaxis:** **third line** (NICE). It may be similar to lithium in efficacy, but more patients drop out because of adverse effects. Licensed in the UK for bipolar disorder not responding to lithium.
- **Other:** bipolar and unipolar depression (weak evidence); **alcohol withdrawal** (high doses poorly tolerated; Cochrane not supportive); aggression in schizophrenia; case reports in panic disorder, BPD and episodic dyscontrol.
- **Dose and levels:** at least 600 mg/day and a level of at least 7 mg/L may be needed in affective illness (anticonvulsant range 4–12 mg/L). Trough sampling. **Auto-induction**: recheck levels 2–4 weeks after changes.
- **Adverse effects:**
  - dizziness, diplopia, ataxia, drowsiness;
  - **hyponatraemia**;
  - rash in 3%; **SJS/TEN with HLA-B*15:02** (test South-East Asians);
  - low WCC; agranulocytosis or aplastic anaemia in 1 in 20,000;
  - hypersensitivity;
  - teratogen.
- **Interactions:** **potent enzyme inducer**. It lowers levels of antidepressants, antipsychotics, oral contraceptives and others. **Avoid with clozapine** (marrow suppression).

### 3. Lamotrigine [1]
- **Bipolar depression (established):** effective for acute depression **and prophylaxis of depressive relapse**. Does not induce switching or rapid cycling; as effective as citalopram; less weight gain than lithium; useful with quetiapine or lithium, and as a lithium alternative in pregnancy. Small anti-manic effect [p320].
- **Maintenance:** bipolar II; **prevents depressive, not manic, recurrence** [1].
- **Treatment-resistant depression:** augmentation at **100–400 mg** is "probably the best-tolerated augmentation strategy"; slow titration; rash risk [p351].
- **Rapid cycling:** step-4 adjunct up to 225 mg/day [p317].
- **Schizophrenia:** olanzapine + lamotrigine has unconvincing evidence. For **clozapine augmentation** the meta-analytic findings are inconsistent; Maudsley suggests it may help with poor clozapine response or persistent negative symptoms. In people with epilepsy who need clozapine, lamotrigine is the **antiseizure medication of choice**, and carbamazepine should not be used [1, p815].
- **BPD:** small studies were positive, but **a large RCT showed no effect** [1].

### 4. Gabapentinoids [1]
- **Pregabalin:** **licensed for GAD**, with onset as fast as benzodiazepines. Start 150 mg, up to 600 mg/day in divided doses. **Misused**, especially with opioids, and worsens opioid overdose toxicity. Taper; do not stop abruptly [p447].
- **Gabapentin:**
  - mania: probably effective only through anxiolysis;
  - BPD: anxiety and lability;
  - TD: 900–1,200 mg/day [1].
- **In epilepsy:** pregabalin for social phobia and GAD, gabapentin for social anxiety, lamotrigine for PTSD [2].

### 5. Topiramate and others [1]
- **Topiramate:**
  - **antipsychotic-induced weight gain:** reliably reduces weight, but causes sedation and cognitive impairment;
  - schizophrenia: small effect, with some negative-symptom benefit;
  - **mania: probably not effective**;
  - BPD: improves anger;
  - clozapine seizure prophylaxis.
- **Levetiracetam:** adjunct in mania (2 RCTs; one case of causing mania); rapid cycling; TD.
- **Phenytoin** (mania; rarely used); zonisamide (weight; not recommended).

### 6. Anticonvulsants as adjuncts for drug adverse effects [1]
- **Clozapine-induced seizures:** **valproate, lamotrigine, topiramate or gabapentin** as prophylaxis with risk factors or plasma level ≥600 µg/L. Some argue that a level below 1,300 µg/L does not justify primary prophylaxis. After a seizure: withhold clozapine 1 day, restart at half dose, add an ASM.
- **Akathisia:** pregabalin investigated; clonazepam.
- **TD:** levetiracetam, gabapentin, clonazepam.

### 7. Borderline personality disorder [1, p787]
- **Valproate and topiramate:** may improve **anger**; valproate also aggression and irritability.
- **Gabapentin:** anxiety and lability.
- **Lamotrigine, carbamazepine:** ineffective in a large RCT (lamotrigine).
- **No drug is licensed for BPD.**

### Cautions common to the class [1]
- **Suicidality:** valproate and other ASMs have been linked to suicidal behaviour, though inconsistently; risk may be higher in depressed patients. **Carbamazepine has not been implicated.**
- **Teratogenicity:** valproate and carbamazepine. Ensure contraception (CBZ needs ≥50 µg oestrogen or non-hormonal methods) and folate.
- **Withdrawal:** taper slowly (at least 1 month). Valproate withdrawal in epilepsy is linked to depression and falls.
- **Pharmacokinetic interactions:** enzyme induction (carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital) vs inhibition (valproate).

**Conclusion.** In psychiatry:
- **valproate:** mania and prophylaxis (but not in women of child-bearing potential);
- **carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine:** mania and third-line prophylaxis;
- **lamotrigine:** bipolar depression and depressive prophylaxis; well-tolerated TRD augmentation;
- **pregabalin:** licensed for GAD; gabapentin as an adjunct;
- **topiramate and others:** mainly adjunctive (weight gain, BPD anger, clozapine seizure prophylaxis, TD).

Choice depends on efficacy, adverse-effect profile, teratogenicity, interactions and each drug's own psychotropic liabilities.

## In depth

### Comparative positioning (Maudsley)
- **Mania:** lithium and antipsychotics have stronger efficacy data than valproate. Carbamazepine is not first line.
- **Maintenance:** lithium plus an SGA is probably the polypharmacy regimen of choice; carbamazepine is third line.
- **Rapid cycling:** combinations such as lithium + valproate, lithium + lamotrigine or valproate + carbamazepine.
