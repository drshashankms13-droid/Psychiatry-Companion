## Exam answer

**Introduction [1].**
- Treatment of OCD in children and young people **largely follows adult principles**.
- **Untreated OCD runs a chronic course.** It does not remit spontaneously and carries great morbidity.
- Untreated OCD has a **10-fold** higher risk of completed suicide than the general population.
- **Comorbidities:** depression, autism spectrum disorder (poor insight), **ADHD and tic disorders**.

### Assessment [1, 2]
- **CY-BOCS** at baseline and for monitoring. An observer-rated measure helps, because **informants may notice improvement first**.
- **Response definitions** [2]:
  - March and Mulle: **response = 35–50% fall in CY-BOCS; remission = CY-BOCS under 10**;
  - Storch: response ≥25%; remission ≥45% fall or score ≤14;
  - Nord LOTS: response ≥35%; remission ≥55% fall or score ≤11.
- Look for **comorbid ASD, ADHD, tics and depression**. Untreated ADHD impairs engagement with CBT [1].
- **PANDAS:** if OCD is secondary to PANDAS, **continue antibiotic prophylaxis** [2].

### Management: stepped care (NICE, Figure 5.1) [1]
| Severity | Intervention |
|---|---|
| **Mild** impairment | Guided self-help; information for family and carers |
| **Moderate or severe**, or mild if self-help fails | **CBT with ERP**, involving family (individual or group) |
| CBT ineffective or refused | **Consider an SSRI** with careful monitoring, after multidisciplinary review |
| Continuing | **SSRI plus ongoing CBT/ERP**: *consider* at 8–11 years; *offer* at 12–18 years |

**IPS CPG algorithm** [2]:
- **Mild to moderate OCD:** **CBT first** if feasible; otherwise an SSRI (fluoxetine or sertraline) for 12 weeks.
- **Severe OCD:** **combination of CBT plus an SSRI** (fluoxetine or sertraline) for 12 weeks.
- **Partial or no response:**
  - CBT alone: continue CBT and add an SSRI;
  - SSRI alone: try another SSRI or clomipramine, or replace with CBT;
  - combination: continue CBT and change the SSRI or use clomipramine.
- **Remission:** continue for **12 months**, then taper. Continue longer if illness is very severe or relapses are multiple.

### Pharmacotherapy [1, 2]
- **SSRIs** have a **medium to large effect size**; medication consistently beats placebo (meta-analysis of 12 RCTs).
- **UK-licensed:** **sertraline (from 6 years)** and **fluvoxamine (from 8 years)**.
- **Fluoxetine** is useful with **comorbid depression** or **poor adherence** (long half-life). A 2020 meta-analysis found fluoxetine and sertraline more effective than fluvoxamine.
- **Paroxetine is not recommended.** **SNRIs are not recommended.**
- **Clomipramine** is debatably more efficacious, but its side-effects (sedation, dry mouth, cardiac effects) mean SSRIs stay first line.
- **Doses** [2]:

  | Drug | FDA minimum age | Starting dose (preadolescent / adolescent) | Maximum |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Clomipramine | Approved (the age is illegible in the source copy) | 6.25–25 / 25 mg | 50–200 mg |
  | Fluoxetine | 8 years | 2.5–10 / 10–20 mg | 10–80 mg |
  | Sertraline | 6 years | 12.5–25 / 25–50 mg | 50–200 mg |
  | Fluvoxamine | 8 years | 12.5–25 / 25–50 mg | 50–300 mg |

- **Trial length:**
  - effects begin at **1–2 weeks** and continue for at least 24 weeks; mood may improve first;
  - NICE recommends **two SSRI trials of 3 months**, working towards the maximum tolerated dose;
  - **most young people need higher doses** [1].
- **Safety:**
  - MHRA (2004) warned about suicidal ideation;
  - in paediatric OCD the pooled risk of suicidal ideation or attempts was **under 1%**, and SSRIs are clearly **more efficacious in OCD than in moderate depression**;
  - explain and monitor, especially early;
  - slower titration in **developmental disabilities** (activation) [1].

### Treatment-refractory OCD [1, 2]
- About **a quarter** fail an initial SSRI (12 weeks at the maximum dose) plus adequate CBT/ERP.
- **Reassess:** adherence, missed comorbidity.
- **A second SSRI** helps about **40%**. Then refer to a specialist centre.
- **Clomipramine**, or **low-dose antipsychotic augmentation**:
  - **aripiprazole 2.5–5 mg** (more evidence) or **risperidone 0.5 mg**;
  - a 6–8-week trial; stop if there is no response;
  - antipsychotics **alone** do not work;
  - patients **with tics** may benefit more.
- **Fluvoxamine plus clomipramine** (pharmacological synergy) is for specialist centres only, because of the **serotonin syndrome** risk [1, 2].
- **IPS** [2]: in-patient intensive CBT, motivation enhancement, and **clonazepam to facilitate CBT** when severe anxiety blocks it.
- **ADHD:** treating it can improve CBT engagement. **Caution with stimulants**: compulsions may increase as concentration improves [1].

### Continuation and relapse [1]
- Continue for **at least 6 months after remission** (NICE; consensus). IPS suggests 12 months [2].
- Stopping often brings **relapse**. Discuss long-term SSRI use honestly; taper slowly and transparently.
- **Relapse should be seen urgently**, not placed on a routine waiting list.
- **Support families.**

**Conclusion.** Childhood OCD is chronic and impairing. Management:
- CBT with ERP first for mild to moderate OCD, with family involvement;
- CBT plus an SSRI for severe OCD (sertraline, fluvoxamine, fluoxetine);
- two SSRI trials, then clomipramine or low-dose aripiprazole or risperidone augmentation;
- at least 6–12 months' continuation, with relapse prevention.

## In depth

### Alternative and experimental treatments [1]
- **Aripiprazole augmentation:** evidence in children with OCD; none in BDD.
- **Risperidone augmentation:** fewer paediatric studies.
