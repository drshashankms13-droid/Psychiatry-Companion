## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- Anorexia nervosa (AN) is characterised by **significantly low body weight**, an **intense fear of weight gain** and **disturbed body perception** [2].
- **Lifetime prevalence** is about **0.6%**, with a female-to-male ratio of 10–20:1 and median onset at age 18 [2].
- Eating disorders have the **highest mortality among psychiatric disorders** [2].
- **Comorbidities:** depression, OCD, autism spectrum disorder [2].
- **ICD-11 6B80** subtypes [2]:
  - with significantly low body weight (6B80.0);
  - with dangerously low body weight (6B80.1);
  - in recovery with normal body weight (6B80.2).

### Principles [1]
- **Prompt weight restoration to a safe weight, family therapy and structured psychotherapy are the main interventions.**
- Physical treatment aims to improve nutritional health through **re-feeding**.
- **Medicines have limited efficacy; none is licensed for AN.** Do **not** use medication as the sole treatment (NICE).
- Treat comorbid conditions as usual. Sometimes they need treating **before** AN is addressed, depending on severity.
- **Psychological interventions are the treatment of choice**, with psychoeducation and monitoring of weight, mental and physical health, and risk (NICE, Box 9.1).

### Assessment and monitoring tools [2]
- SCOFF, EDE-Q, Eating Disorders Inventory-3, Eating Attitudes Test, Morgan–Russell Average Outcome Scale.

### Psychological treatment [2]
1. **MANTRA** (Maudsley Anorexia Treatment for Adults):
   - targets inflexible thinking, social-emotional impairment, the **perceived positive value of AN**, and unhelpful responses from the support network;
   - delivered in a **motivational interviewing** style.
2. **Radically open DBT:** a skills-based adaptation of DBT for **over-control** in AN. Targets inhibited emotional expression, over-caution, rigid rule-governed behaviour and high social comparison, to improve social functioning and reduce loneliness.
3. **REaCH** (Regulating Emotions and Changing Habits): habit-reversal techniques covering cue awareness, new routines, suppression of maladaptive habits, and emotion regulation.
4. **Third-wave and mindfulness approaches:** compassion-focused therapy, schema therapy, mindfulness-based interventions.
5. **Telemedicine and Internet self-help:** CBT-based, cost-effective, usable in stepped care or while waiting. Less effective in restrictive presentations than in binge-type disorders.

### Pharmacological treatment [1, 2]
**For weight restoration:**
- **Olanzapine:**
  - positive effect on weight in **seven RCTs**;
  - in one RCT, **87.5% achieved weight restoration vs 55.6% on placebo**;
  - limited by poor tolerance and acceptability [1].
- **Aripiprazole:** some non-RCT evidence [1]. It may reduce eating-related preoccupations and rituals more than olanzapine [2].
- **Not effective:** risperidone (one negative RCT); quetiapine (later RCT negative) [1].
- **Not recommended for weight:** **amitriptyline, clomipramine, fluoxetine, citalopram, sertraline**, benzodiazepines and antihistamines [1].
- **Others:**
  - mirtazapine: case reports only;
  - **metreleptin** (leptin analogue): 5 cases with weight gain;
  - **dronabinol**: weight gain without psychological improvement, and dysphoria [1];
  - D-cycloserine: greater BMI gain vs placebo [2].

**For psychological symptoms:**
- **Antidepressants:** a Cochrane review found **no benefit** for eating disorder or associated psychopathology, possibly because of starvation-related neurochemistry. There is a **risk of switch to mania** [1].
- **Olanzapine** may reduce agitation, **pre-meal anxiety** and obsessional beliefs [1].
- **Use only prolactin-sparing antipsychotics** (avoid risperidone, amisulpride and sulpiride) because of the **osteoporosis** risk [1].
- **Experimental:** ketamine (case studies: lower depression and suicidality); psilocybin trials are under way [1].

**Comorbidity [1]:**
- Second-generation antidepressants for comorbid depression, anxiety and OCD. Depression caused by starvation improves only with weight restoration.
- **Bupropion is contraindicated** (it causes weight loss).
- **Mania or psychosis:** olanzapine. **Bipolar depression:** olanzapine plus fluoxetine.

**Relapse prevention:** very weak evidence for SSRIs or mirtazapine. An RCT of fluoxetine showed **no effect** [1].

### Physical treatment [1]
- **Oral multivitamin and multimineral** supplements during weight restoration; vitamin D may be needed.
- **Electrolytes:**
  - hypokalaemia may be silent;
  - **rapid correction is hazardous** and risks **re-feeding hypophosphataemia**;
  - supplement orally to prevent sequelae;
  - monitor U&Es, bicarbonate, calcium, phosphate, magnesium and ECG.
- **QT and arrhythmia:** put an **alert on the prescribing record**; ECG if any cardiotoxic drug is essential.
- **Bone:**
  - for long-standing low weight with low bone mineral density, **17β-oestradiol with cyclic progesterone** (or oestrogen at 13–17 years);
  - **bisphosphonates** for women over 18;
  - avoid prolactin-raising drugs.
- **Endocrine** [2]: pulsatile GnRH for hypothalamic amenorrhoea; oestrogen (reduces trait anxiety). Growth hormone is inconclusive.

### Neuromodulation [2]
- **Non-invasive brain stimulation** (dlPFC, dmPFC): improves the urge to restrict and feelings of fatness.
- **DBS** (nucleus accumbens, subcallosal cingulate): for **severe or enduring AN** with comorbid depression or OCD.
- **Neurofeedback.**

**Conclusion.** AN is treated mainly with:
- weight restoration;
- family and structured psychotherapy (MANTRA, RO-DBT);
- careful physical monitoring (re-feeding, electrolytes, QT, bone).

Medication is adjunctive: olanzapine has the best evidence for weight and anxiety. Antidepressants do not help core AN.

## In depth

### Neurobiology that informs treatment [2]
- **Reward:** hyporesponsiveness to reward. The insula and cingulate are hyperactive when anticipating losses.
- **Cognitive control:** the dorsal striatum is active when choosing low-calorie foods, so **habitual** restriction (the target of REaCH).
- **Dopamine:** lower D2/D3 binding in the striatum. Aripiprazole's D2 partial agonism may desensitise receptors, which is linked to greater weight gain.
- **5-HT2A binding** is reduced in both ill and recovered AN.
- **Genetics:**
  - heritability 48–74%;
  - female relatives have **11 times** the risk;
  - GWAS locus on chromosome 12 (metabolic overlap), so AN is reconceptualised as **metabolic plus psychiatric**.
- **Endophenotypes:** cognitive rigidity, impaired set-shifting, weak central coherence, anhedonia.

### NICE summary (Box 9.1) [1]
- **AN:** psychological treatment of choice, plus monitoring. **No medication as sole treatment.**
