## Exam answer

**Case summary.**
- **Patient:** a 40-year-old man with **6 months of severe depression**.
- **Current features:** **severe agitation, suspiciousness, auditory hallucinations, intense suicidal ideas**.
- **Treatment so far:** no response to **sertraline 150 mg for 2 months**, **escitalopram 20 mg for 6 weeks**, or **rTMS**.
- **Past history:**
  - **three similar episodes** in 10 years, each with **high suicide risk and psychotic features needing admission**;
  - **one episode was followed by hypomania**;
  - **full inter-episode recovery**.

### 1. Diagnosis
- **Current episode:** a **severe depressive episode with psychotic features**, recurrent.
- **The past antidepressant-associated hypomania** places him on the **bipolar spectrum**. Treat the episode as **bipolar depression with psychotic features** unless the history disproves it.
- **Why this matters [1]:**
  - psychotic depression **can occur in both MDD and bipolar disorder**;
  - it **needs a different treatment approach**, yet is often under-diagnosed;
  - bipolar depression is more often **delusional**, more frequent and more severe than unipolar depression.
- **Prognosis [1]:**
  - once psychotic symptoms have occurred, they tend to **recur in future episodes** (as here);
  - long-term outcome is poorer than for non-psychotic depression;
  - **suicide attempts and completions are much more likely**;
  - about **15%** of people with bipolar disorder die by suicide.
- **Treatment resistance:** two failed SSRIs plus rTMS. These were monotherapies, which are expected to work poorly in psychotic depression [1].

### 2. Immediate management
- **Admit**, given intense suicidal ideas and psychosis, and his history of admission in every episode.
- **ECT is the treatment of choice here [1]:**
  - effective in psychotic depression, especially when a **rapid response is required**;
  - some studies show **greater efficacy with psychotic features**;
  - **more protective against relapse** in psychotic than non-psychotic depression;
  - maintenance ECT plus nortriptyline beat nortriptyline alone at 2 years.

### 3. Pharmacological management [1]
**Psychotic depression principles:**
- **An antidepressant plus an antipsychotic beats either alone.** The strongest evidence is for **fluoxetine plus olanzapine** (2024 network meta-analysis; NICE 2022).
- If monotherapy is unavoidable, **TCAs** are the drugs of choice. They are more effective than venlafaxine or mirtazapine. **But in bipolar depression, TCAs are best avoided.**

**Bipolar considerations:**
- **Avoid "unopposed" antidepressants** (switching, rapid cycling), especially in bipolar I. Venlafaxine is more likely to induce switching.
- **Olanzapine plus fluoxetine** is recommended **first line by NICE** for bipolar depression. It is the strongest evidence for an antidepressant in bipolar depression, and fits both his psychotic and bipolar features.
- **Alternatives:**
  - **quetiapine** 300–600 mg (no switching; prevents relapse into depression and mania);
  - **lurasidone** (BAP first line);
  - lamotrigine (second line for NICE).
- **Lithium:** fairly strong support for **reducing suicidality** in bipolar disorder, and protection against relapse. Strongly consider it given his repeated high-risk episodes.

**Proposed plan:**
1. ECT course.
2. Start **olanzapine plus fluoxetine** (for example, 5/20 mg, going to 10/40 mg as tolerated) or **quetiapine**.
3. Add **lithium** for anti-suicidal and prophylactic effect.
4. Stop the ineffective SSRI (escitalopram).

**Refractory options [1]:**
- **ketamine** (0.5 mg/kg IV): standard for refractory **bipolar** depression, and possibly effective in psychotic depression. Not first line;
- **mifepristone** (glucocorticoid receptor blockade; reduces psychotic symptoms);
- pramipexole (adjunct in bipolar depression).

### 4. Continuation and maintenance [1]
- **Continue the antidepressant–antipsychotic combination for at least 4–6 months after remission.**
- In STOP-PD II, stopping olanzapine from sertraline–olanzapine **more than doubled relapse** (55% vs 20%), mostly in the **first 2 months**.
- Then consider **slowly tapering the antipsychotic**, weighing metabolic risk against his **history of psychotic relapse**. Reintroduce it if psychosis returns.
- **Long-term bipolar prophylaxis:** lithium or quetiapine. Do not continue an unopposed antidepressant long term.
- Consider **maintenance ECT** with relapse-prone, high-risk episodes.

**Conclusion.** This is recurrent psychotic depression with high suicide risk, a probable bipolar diathesis, and failure of SSRI monotherapy and rTMS. Plan:
- admit;
- give ECT;
- treat with olanzapine plus fluoxetine (or quetiapine), with lithium;
- avoid unopposed antidepressants;
- continue the combination at least 4–6 months after remission, within long-term bipolar prophylaxis.

## In depth

### Evidence ladder for psychotic depression [1]
| Option | Evidence |
|---|---|
| SSRI plus SGA (fluoxetine plus olanzapine) | Highest response in network meta-analysis; best efficacy–tolerability balance |
| Combination vs monotherapy | Combination more effective |
| TCA monotherapy | Better than SNRI or mirtazapine; drug of choice if monotherapy |
| ECT | Rapid; possibly more effective with psychosis; protects against relapse |
| Ketamine and esketamine | Small series; reduces depression and sometimes psychosis; not first line |
| Mifepristone | Pooled data from 5 RCTs: reduces psychotic symptoms |
