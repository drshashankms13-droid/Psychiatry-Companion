## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- The **RPwD Act 2016** follows the **UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD, 2006)**. It aims at **equal participation** of persons with disabilities in society "on an equal basis with others" [3].
- **Mental illness** is one of the disabilities it recognises. SLD is also a benchmark disability under it [4, 5].
- **Reasonable accommodation** means the adjustments and supports that let a person with disability use services, education and work on an equal footing. The sources list it as:
  - a duty to employees with disability: "providing **reasonable accommodation** and appropriate **barrier-free and conducive environment**" [1];
  - a key element of the **livelihood** component of CBR [2];
  - a purpose of the disability certificate, as an "authentic document" for reasonable accommodation in **public places** [3].

### Elements of reasonable accommodation found in the sources
**1. Employment [1]:**
- **Reservation** in government jobs increased **from 3% to 4%**.
- Vocational training; **non-discrimination** in employment; equal opportunity policy.
- **Reasonable accommodation** and a **barrier-free, conducive environment**.
- **Incentives to private employers.**
- **No denial of promotion** on grounds of disability.
- **No dismissal or reduction in rank** for an employee who acquires a disability. If they are unsuitable for their post, shift them to another post with the **same pay and benefits**, or keep them on a **supernumerary post** until a suitable post is available or until superannuation.
- **Posting and transfer policies** for employees with disabilities.

**2. Education [5, 6]:**
- **Accommodations** that help a student access material:
  - larger pens or grippers, tactile paper;
  - spell-checkers, audiobooks, voice recognition, calculators;
  - **individualised assessment** (time, length, breaks).
- **Exam concessions** by boards (CBSE, NIOS) when an approved authority certifies the disability [6].
- **Higher education:** at least **5% of seats** reserved for benchmark disabilities, with **5 years' upper age relaxation** [5].
- **Free education** in an appropriate environment until 18 [5].

**3. Community and livelihood (CBR) [2]:** environmental accessibility; reasonable accommodation; personal choice and the local context; formal and informal economy.

**4. Public places [3]:** the disability certificate helps access reasonable accommodation at public places and **state benefits**.

### Application to persons with psychiatric (psychosocial) disability

**A. Getting recognised: certification [4]:**
- Mental illness is assessed by **clinical assessment plus IDEAS** (Indian Disability Evaluation and Assessment Scale), with an IQ test only if intellectual deficit is suspected.
- **Permanent certificate:** needs at least **2 years of illness**, assessed on **residual disability despite adequate evidence-based treatment**.
- **Time-limited certificate (up to 2 years):** for illness of under 2 years, untreated persons, or those without records. Severe or profound disability may be certified permanently.
- **Co-existing ID and mental illness:** certify as **multiple disability**.
- **Dementia:** disability assessed with **IDEAS**.
- The certificate is the gateway to accommodations and benefits [3].

**B. The employment barrier [1]:**
- **87.3%** of persons with mental illness in India were **out of the labour force**.
- Disability employment rates are very low: 0.28% in the private sector, 0.54% in the public sector. Only **3.54%** of identified posts were filled by people with disabilities (NCPEDP 2009).
- **Emerging issues:**
  - job identification and the reservation clause;
  - **repeated medical examinations** of disabled employees;
  - accessibility;
  - **disability discrimination**.

**C. Workplace accommodations for psychosocial disability (application of [1]):**
- **Retention after illness:** shift to another post on the same pay, or a supernumerary post, rather than dismissal, when an employee develops a disabling mental illness.
- **Protected promotion.**
- **Posting and transfer policies**, for example near treatment services.
- **Supported employment** as the vehicle for accommodation:
  - **Individual Placement and Support (IPS):** zero exclusion; integration with clinical services; benefits counselling; client preference; **rapid job search**; **follow-along support** after employment; team-based services;
  - **Choose–Get–Keep(–Leave)** approach;
  - **augmented supported employment** with social-skills training;
  - delivery through **ACT**, residential programmes and peer services.

**D. Education (from SLD practice) [5]:** a student with a mental disorder may benefit from similar accommodations: extra time, breaks, modified assessments. The psychiatrist's role is to **advocate** with the school, **psychoeducate** teachers and **certify**.

**E. Healthcare: parity as accommodation (MHCA) [7]:**
- **s21:** persons with mental illness must be treated **as equal to persons with physical illness** in all healthcare, with **no discrimination** (including on disability). Emergency services, ambulances, living conditions and other services must be of the **same quality**.
- **s18(4)(d):** services close to home.
- **s19:** right to **community living**.

### Challenges [1, 4, 6]
- **Episodic, invisible disability:** fluctuating illness fits poorly with fixed certification. Hence time-limited certificates and repeat assessment [4] (application).
- **Untreated illness:** permanent certificates need adequate prior treatment [4].
- **Discrimination and repeated medical examinations** at work [1].
- **Rigid certification rules** can deny certificates despite a clinical diagnosis (shown for SLD) [6].
- **Language:** no vernacular assessment methods (SLD) [6].

**Conclusion.**
- Under the RPwD Act, reasonable accommodation means:
  - a **barrier-free, conducive environment**;
  - **job protection, promotion and transfer** safeguards;
  - **reservations**;
  - **educational and exam concessions**;
  - access to public places and benefits.
- For psychiatric disability, these depend on **IDEAS-based certification** and are best delivered through **supported employment (IPS)**, school advocacy and **parity of healthcare** under MHCA.
- Stigma, episodic illness and rigid certification remain the main obstacles.

## In depth

### IDEAS-based certification rules for mental illness (Gazette 2024) [4]
| Situation | Certificate |
|---|---|
| Illness of at least 2 years, adequately treated | Permanent, based on residual disability |
| Illness of under 2 years | Time-limited (permanent if severe or profound, at the Board's discretion) |
| Not received standard treatment | Time-limited, up to 2 years; advise treatment |
| Treated but no records | Time-limited; advise keeping records; reassess after 2 years |
| ID plus mental illness | Multiple disability |
