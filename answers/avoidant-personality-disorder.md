## Exam answer

**Definition.** Avoidant personality disorder (AvPD) is a Cluster C ("anxious–fearful") personality disorder marked by a pervasive pattern of **social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy and hypersensitivity to negative evaluation**, beginning by early adulthood and present across contexts [1]. ICD-10 called it **anxious (avoidant) personality disorder** (F60.6); ICD-11 describes it as a personality disorder of a given severity with prominent **negative affectivity** and **detachment** traits [2].

### DSM-5 criteria (at least 4 of 7) [1]

1. Avoids occupational activities with significant interpersonal contact for fear of criticism, disapproval or rejection.
2. Unwilling to get involved with people unless certain of being liked.
3. Restraint in intimate relationships for fear of being shamed or ridiculed.
4. Preoccupied with being criticised or rejected in social situations.
5. Inhibited in new interpersonal situations because of feelings of inadequacy.
6. Views self as socially inept, personally unappealing or inferior.
7. Unusually reluctant to take personal risks or engage in new activities because they may be embarrassing.

**Associated features:** fearful, tense demeanour; fear of blushing or crying when criticised; **social isolation with a longing for relationships** and fantasies of ideal relationships [1].

### Mechanism

- **Temperament:** high **harm avoidance** (anxious, shy, fatigable) with low self-directedness in Cloninger's model; Cluster C as a whole maps onto fearfulness [1].
- **Development:** childhood shyness and fear of strangers and new situations; disfiguring illness in childhood predisposes [1]; behavioural inhibition in infancy (Kagan) and parental rejection or criticism.
- **Cognitive model:** core beliefs of being defective and unlikeable → expectation of rejection → avoidance → no disconfirming experience → beliefs maintained.

### Epidemiology [1]

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| General population | 0.5–2% |
| Psychiatric outpatients | About 10% |
| Sex ratio | Equal |
| Course | Begins in childhood shyness; chronic but may soften with age |

### Comorbidity [1]

- Mood disorders and anxiety disorders, especially **generalised social anxiety disorder**.
- Co-occurring personality disorders: schizotypal, schizoid, paranoid, dependent, borderline.

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing feature [1] |
| --- | --- |
| **Social anxiety disorder** | Fear of specific performance or social situations rather than interpersonal contact itself; many regard AvPD as the severe end of the same spectrum |
| Agoraphobia / panic disorder | Avoidance follows panic attacks |
| **Schizoid PD** | Isolation from **indifference**; no wish for relationships |
| Schizotypal PD | Oddities, magical thinking, suspicious social anxiety |
| Dependent PD | Seeks to be cared for; clinging rather than withdrawal |
| Paranoid PD | Guardedness, conspiratorial explanations |
| Autism spectrum disorder | Lifelong social communication deficits, restricted interests |
| Depression | Episodic withdrawal with low mood |

### Assessment

- Longitudinal history from early adolescence with collateral information.
- SCID-5-PD or IPDE; Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale for comorbid social anxiety.
- Screen for depression, substance use (often used to cope socially) and suicidality.

### Management

**Psychological (first line)**
- **CBT:** psychoeducation → cognitive restructuring of beliefs about defectiveness and rejection → **graded exposure** to social situations → behavioural experiments.
- **Social skills training**, often in groups.
- **Group therapy:** a safe setting to rehearse interaction and receive feedback.
- **Schema therapy** for long-standing core beliefs.
- Psychodynamic psychotherapy focusing on shame and early rejection.
- MBT is being studied for AvPD [3].

**Pharmacological (adjunct)**
- **SSRIs** (e.g. sertraline, escitalopram, paroxetine) or **SNRIs** (venlafaxine), especially with comorbid social anxiety or depression [1].
- **MAOIs** (phenelzine) are effective for avoidant traits but limited by dietary restrictions [1].
- Beta-blockers for performance-related somatic symptoms [1].
- Avoid long-term benzodiazepines (dependence).

**Principles:** a warm, non-judgemental alliance; gradual pace; anticipate early dropout due to fear of evaluation by the therapist.

**Conclusion.** Avoidant personality disorder combines a longing for relationships with a fear of rejection that drives pervasive avoidance. It overlaps closely with severe social anxiety disorder, responds to CBT with graded exposure, social skills and group work, and benefits from SSRIs or MAOIs when anxiety or depression coexist.

## In depth

### Background

- Millon (1969) described the "active-detached" personality, distinguishing avoidant people (who want relationships but fear rejection) from schizoid people (who are indifferent). DSM-III (1980) introduced AvPD.
- Kretschmer's spectrum view and later research propose that AvPD and social phobia lie on a continuum of the same liability [1].
- ICD-11 removed specific personality disorder types; an AvPD presentation would be coded as personality disorder (mild, moderate or severe) with negative affectivity and detachment [2].

### Classification and criteria in detail

| System | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| DSM-5 Section II | 4 of 7 criteria [1] |
| DSM-5 AMPD | Impairment in identity and intimacy + anxiousness, withdrawal, anhedonia, intimacy avoidance |
| ICD-10 | Anxious (avoidant) PD: tension, apprehension, inferiority, restricted lifestyle for security |
| ICD-11 | Severity + traits; **detachment** (social and emotional distance) and **negative affectivity** (anxiety, shame, low self-worth) [2] |

### Mechanism in detail

- **Harm avoidance** is linked to serotonergic and GABAergic systems, amygdala reactivity and the serotonin transporter polymorphism in Cloninger's psychobiological model [1].
- **Behavioural inhibition** in infancy predicts later social anxiety and avoidance.
- **Learning:** criticism, rejection or teasing (including about appearance) and overprotective or critical parenting reinforce avoidance; avoidance is maintained by negative reinforcement (relief).
- **Schema model:** defectiveness/shame and social isolation/alienation schemas.

### Clinical features in detail

- Chooses jobs with minimal contact; turns down promotions involving people.
- Relationships restricted to a few people where acceptance is guaranteed; may live with parents.
- Hypervigilant for cues of disapproval; interprets neutral responses as rejection.
- Desires affection and acceptance, which distinguishes AvPD from schizoid detachment [1].

### Management details

- **Evidence:** RCTs show CBT and brief dynamic therapy reduce avoidant symptoms; group CBT for social anxiety benefits patients with comorbid AvPD, though they start and end more impaired.
- **Pharmacotherapy evidence** comes mainly from social anxiety trials in which many participants also met AvPD criteria.
- Combination of SSRI and CBT may help severe cases.
- **Cloninger's symptom-domain approach:** SSRIs or MAOIs for avoidant traits and chronic anxiety; psychotherapy as primary treatment [1].

### Critical appraisal

- **Overlap with generalised social anxiety disorder** is so high that many authors regard them as the same condition at different severities [1].
- The ICD-11 dimensional model removes the category, raising questions about loss of a clinically recognisable pattern.
- Few treatment trials target AvPD specifically.

### Indian context

- Shyness and deference may be culturally sanctioned, especially in women and young people; diagnosis requires impairment and distress beyond cultural norms [1].
- Arranged marriage and joint family settings may mask or intensify avoidance.
- Group therapy and social skills training are feasible in Indian outpatient services; stigma limits help seeking.

### Recent advances

- MBT and schema therapy trials for Cluster C disorders [3].
- Internet-delivered CBT for social anxiety with avoidant features.
