## Exam answer

**Definition.** Avoidant personality disorder is a pervasive pattern of **social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy and hypersensitivity to negative evaluation**, beginning by early adulthood and present across contexts [1]. It belongs to DSM-5 **Cluster C** (anxious–fearful) with dependent and obsessive-compulsive personality disorders; ICD-10 calls it **anxious (avoidant) personality disorder** (F60.6). In **ICD-11** it would be recorded as a personality disorder of given severity with prominent **negative affectivity** and **detachment** traits [1, 2].

### Diagnostic features (DSM-5: at least 4 of 7) [1]

1. Avoids occupational activities involving significant interpersonal contact, for fear of criticism, rejection or disapproval.
2. Unwilling to get involved with people unless certain of being liked.
3. Restraint in intimate relationships for fear of being shamed or ridiculed.
4. Preoccupied with being criticised or rejected in social situations.
5. Inhibited in new interpersonal situations because of feelings of inadequacy.
6. Sees self as socially inept, unappealing or inferior.
7. Unusually reluctant to take risks or try new activities because they may be embarrassing.

**Associated features:** fearful, tense demeanour; fear of blushing or crying when criticised; social isolation **with craving for relationships** and fantasies of ideal relationships [1].

### Epidemiology and course [1]

- Prevalence **0.5–2%** in the general population, about **10%** of psychiatric outpatients; **equal sex ratio**.
- Often begins in childhood with **shyness and fear of strangers**; disfiguring illness in childhood predisposes.

### Mechanism

High **harm avoidance** temperament (anxious, inhibited) + low **self-directedness** → fearful expectation of rejection → avoidance → lack of corrective social experience and skills → reinforced low self-esteem (author's synthesis of K&S psychobiological model and cognitive model) [1].

### Comorbidity and complications [1]

Mood and anxiety disorders — especially **social anxiety disorder (generalised)**; co-occurring schizotypal, schizoid, paranoid, dependent and borderline personality disorders.

### Differential diagnosis [1]

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
| --- | --- |
| **Social anxiety disorder** | Very hard to separate — many regard them as the same condition on a severity continuum; social anxiety focuses on specific situations, avoidant PD on interpersonal contact generally |
| Panic disorder with agoraphobia | Avoidance follows panic attacks |
| Schizoid / schizotypal PD | Do **not** desire relationships (avoidant patients do) |
| Paranoid PD | Guardedness and conspiratorial interpretations |
| Dependent PD | Focus on being cared for rather than fear of evaluation |
| Depression | Episodic low mood, anhedonia |
| Autism spectrum disorder | Social communication deficits from early childhood, restricted interests |

### Management

**Psychotherapy (first line):**
- **CBT** — graded exposure to feared social situations, cognitive restructuring of beliefs of inadequacy and rejection, **social skills training**; often delivered in groups.
- **Schema therapy** and psychodynamic therapy have trial support for Cluster C personality disorders (author's knowledge).
- Group therapy provides a safe setting for exposure and feedback.
- MBT is being studied for avoidant PD [3].

**Pharmacotherapy (adjunctive):**
- Target the anxiety and mood domain: **SSRIs/SNRIs** (as for social anxiety disorder), or MAOIs; avoid benzodiazepines because of abuse risk [1].
- Treat comorbid depression and social anxiety disorder.

**Therapeutic stance:** patients are highly sensitive to criticism — a warm, non-judgemental, patient therapeutic relationship and gradual pacing reduce dropout; avoid direct advice and premature confrontation [1].

**Conclusion.** Avoidant personality disorder is a common, under-recognised Cluster C disorder of fear of rejection and low self-worth that overlaps heavily with social anxiety disorder. It responds to CBT-based exposure and social skills work, often in groups, supported by SSRIs for anxiety and comorbid depression, within a gentle, validating therapeutic relationship.

## In depth

### Background

- Millon (1969) introduced "avoidant personality" as an active detached pattern; DSM-III (1980) included avoidant personality disorder.
- The overlap with social phobia has been debated since DSM-III-R broadened social phobia to a generalised form (author's knowledge).

### Classification

- **DSM-5** clusters (A odd, B dramatic, C anxious) correspond to temperament dimensions: low reward dependence, high novelty seeking and high **harm avoidance** respectively [1].
- **ICD-11** abolishes named types; avoidant features map onto **negative affectivity** (anxiety, low self-worth) and **detachment** (social withdrawal) trait domains [2].
- **Dimensional view:** avoidant PD may represent the severe end of the social anxiety spectrum.

### Clinical features in detail

- Patients want relationships but avoid them; they test others for acceptance and withdraw at minimal signs of rejection.
- Occupational under-achievement through avoiding promotion, presentations or teamwork [1].
- Pervasive self-criticism; shame rather than guilt predominates.

### Management details

| Approach | Content |
| --- | --- |
| CBT | Graded in vivo exposure; behavioural experiments; challenging "I am inferior/unlikeable" beliefs; reducing safety behaviours |
| Social skills training | Conversation, assertiveness, role-play |
| Group CBT | Combines exposure, modelling and peer support |
| Schema therapy | Targets defectiveness/shame and social isolation schemas |
| Pharmacotherapy | SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine, escitalopram), SNRIs (venlafaxine); MAOIs (phenelzine) in resistant cases [1] |

### Critical appraisal

- Few RCTs specifically for avoidant PD; most evidence is extrapolated from social anxiety disorder trials.
- The distinction from generalised social anxiety disorder has limited validity, supporting dimensional models such as ICD-11 [1, 2].
- Diagnostic reliability of individual personality disorders is modest [1].

### Indian context

- Shyness and deference may be culturally sanctioned, especially in women and young people; the diagnosis must account for cultural norms of social behaviour [1].
- Patients often present with depression, exam-related anxiety or "lack of confidence" rather than personality concerns.
- Group-based CBT and college counselling services are practical options.

### Recent advances

- ICD-11 trait-based recording replaces the avoidant category [2].
- MBT and internet-delivered CBT are being studied for avoidant personality (author's knowledge for internet CBT) [3].
