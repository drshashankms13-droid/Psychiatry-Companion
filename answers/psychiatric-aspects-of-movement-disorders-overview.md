## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Movement disorders matter to psychiatrists for five reasons [1]:
1. **High psychiatric comorbidity**, e.g., depression in PD and behavioural change in early Wilson's disease.
2. **Psychiatric presentation before the motor signs**: HD, Wilson's, PD (depression and anxiety as a prodrome).
3. **Psychotropic drugs cause movement disorders** (neuroleptic EPS and TD), and **antiparkinsonian drugs cause psychiatric disorders** (psychosis, ICDs).
4. **Marked "psychosensitivity"**: dystonia, tics and PD bradykinesia worsen with emotion, so organic disorders are **misdiagnosed as hysterical**, and vice versa.
5. **Shared neurobiology**: basal ganglia–frontal circuits and dopamine link motor and mental symptoms.

Lishman warns that **marked psychosensitivity need carry no implication of psychogenesis** [1].

### Framework: mechanisms of psychiatric symptoms in movement disorders [1, 2]

| Mechanism | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Intrinsic to the neurodegeneration** (frontostriatal, monoaminergic, cholinergic loss) | Depression preceding PD motor onset; apathy; subcortical dementia (PD, PSP, HD, Wilson's); HD psychosis; DYT1 depression in non-manifesting carriers |
| **Iatrogenic** | Levodopa/agonist psychosis, ICDs, dopamine dysregulation, hypersexuality; anticholinergic delirium; penicillamine psychosis; tetrabenazine depression; neuroleptic akathisia (suicidality, non-adherence), TD |
| **Psychological reaction** | Disability, stigma, social handicap (depression tracks *handicap* more than impairment in PD); family burden in HD |
| **Psychiatric illness presenting as a movement disorder** | Functional (psychogenic) movement disorders; catatonia; stereotypies and mannerisms in schizophrenia |

### Disorder-by-disorder summary [1, 2]

| Disorder | Key psychiatric features | Management pointers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Parkinson's disease** | Depression (about 40%; prodromal), anxiety, **apathy**, dementia (2–6×), **visual hallucinations in clear consciousness** (drug-related; agonists 2–3× levodopa), personality change, DDS, ICDs, emotionalism | Exclude organic causes; **sertraline**/SSRI, agonist, ECT. Psychosis: reduce anticholinergics, then agonists, then COMT inhibitors; quetiapine trial, then **clozapine 6.25–35 mg** or pimavanserin; cholinesterase inhibitors |
| **DLB** (Lewy body spectrum) | Fluctuating cognition, complex visual hallucinations, parkinsonism; **severe neuroleptic sensitivity** (death within 1 year after reactions) | Avoid typical antipsychotics |
| **PSP / CBD / MSA** | PSP: subcortical dementia, **apathy and disinhibition**, pseudobulbar affect. CBD: depression, alien limb. MSA: depression, RBD, no dementia | Supportive; treat depression |
| **Huntington's disease** | Personality change, irritability/aggression, **depression 30–70% (often years before chorea)**, **suicide**, apathy, OCB, paranoid psychosis, subcortical dementia; misdiagnosed as schizophrenia or TD | Tetrabenazine (not if depressed) or SGA for chorea; SSRIs (avoid TCAs); SGA/clozapine for psychosis; genetic counselling |
| **Wilson's disease** | **20% present psychiatrically**; personality change, disinhibited/reckless behaviour (25%), irritability, depression (21–27%), mild cognitive impairment; psychosis rare | **Screen (caeruloplasmin, slit lamp)** for new behavioural change under 40, unexplained EPS, liver disease. Chelation/zinc; beware neuroleptics |
| **Dystonias** | Psychosensitive and bizarre, so **misdiagnosed as hysteria**; depression and anxiety (cervical 15–42%; blepharospasm 37%); DYT1 depression 4× | Botulinum toxin, anticholinergics; treat comorbidity; psychological support |
| **Tourette's syndrome** | **ADHD, OCD/OCB**, self-injury, depression and anxiety, attentional deficits; coprolalia about 10% | Psychoeducation and behavioural therapy; treat OCD and ADHD first (stimulants don't worsen tics); clonidine/guanfacine, antipsychotics (aripiprazole, risperidone, haloperidol, pimozide) |
| **Neuroleptic-induced** | Akathisia (dysphoria, agitation, suicidality, non-adherence), parkinsonism mistaken for negative symptoms or depression, catatonia-like states, NMS, TD (stigma) | Prevent: atypicals, lowest dose; propranolol for akathisia; anticholinergics for acute dystonia; for TD, stop anticholinergics, switch or clozapine, VMAT2 inhibitors |
| **Functional (psychogenic)** | Comorbid depression 43%, anxiety 38%, personality disorder 45%; conversion vs factitious vs malingering | Explain the diagnosis; physiotherapy; CBT; antidepressants; multidisciplinary care |

### General principles of management [1, 2]
1. **Diagnose accurately.**
   - Remember that psychiatric features may come first (HD, Wilson's).
   - Do not attribute bizarre movements to hysteria or schizophrenic mannerisms without neurological assessment.
   - Do not call new movements "TD" without considering HD or Wilson's.
2. **Joint care with neurology.** Psychiatric and motor treatments interact; there is a trade-off between mobility and psychosis.
3. **Review medication first.**
   - Many symptoms are drug-induced: dopaminergic psychosis; anticholinergic delirium; antipsychotic EPS and akathisia; tetrabenazine depression.
4. **Choose psychotropics with motor effects in mind.**
   - **Avoid** typical antipsychotics and risperidone in PD/DLB.
   - **Clozapine or quetiapine** for psychosis in PD.
   - **SSRIs over TCAs**.
   - Interactions: SSRIs with MAO-B inhibitors (serotonin syndrome); CYP2D6 inhibitors with VMAT2 inhibitors.
   - Start low and go slow.
5. **ECT** is effective for depression, and sometimes motor symptoms, in PD and HD, but carries a delirium risk.
6. **Psychological and social care:**
   - CBT, supportive therapy and adjustment work;
   - **carer support** (PD carers have 5× psychiatric morbidity; HD families heavily burdened);
   - genetic counselling (HD, Wilson's, DYT1);
   - patient societies.
7. **Screen for suicide** (HD early stages; depression in all disorders; akathisia).
8. **Cognitive impairment:**
   - cholinesterase inhibitors in PD dementia and DLB (not established in HD);
   - stop anticholinergics.

**Conclusion.** Psychiatric disorders in movement disorders are common, often **precede** the motor syndrome, and arise from the disease, its treatment and the psychological reaction to it. Good management rests on:
- accurate diagnosis (avoiding both "hysteria" and "TD" mislabels);
- medication review;
- motor-safe psychotropic choices;
- liaison with neurology;
- psychosocial and family care.

## In depth

### "Subcortical" psychiatry [1]
PD, PSP, HD and Wilson's share a profile:
- **apathy**, slowing (bradyphrenia), executive and retrieval memory deficits;
- **no aphasia, apraxia or agnosia**;
- depression and irritability.

This reflects frontostriatal circuit disruption, a model for understanding psychiatric symptoms arising from basal ganglia disease.

### Not covered by the sources (deliberately left blank)
- **Essential tremor** psychiatric comorbidity (Lishman notes only similar personality traits to PD in Poewe's study).
- **Restless legs syndrome** psychiatric aspects (Lishman covers RLS only as an akathisia differential).
- **Catatonia** as a movement disorder (Maudsley covers it in a separate section, not used here).
- **Deep brain stimulation** psychiatric effects.
