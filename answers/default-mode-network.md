## Exam answer

### Introduction

The Default Mode Network is a set of functionally connected brain regions that show relatively greater activity during internally directed thought and reduced activity during many externally focused tasks. It was initially identified through functional neuroimaging studies of the resting brain.

### Neuroanatomy

Major components include:
- Medial prefrontal cortex.
- Posterior cingulate cortex and precuneus.
- Angular gyrus and lateral parietal cortex.
- Medial temporal structures, including hippocampal regions.

The posterior cingulate/precuneus is an important hub.

### Functions

The DMN contributes to:
- Self-referential processing and autobiographical memory.
- Mind-wandering and spontaneous thought.
- Future thinking and mental simulation.
- Social cognition and understanding others' perspectives.
- Integration of internally generated information.

It interacts dynamically with the central executive network, involved in goal-directed cognition, and the salience network, which helps identify behaviourally relevant stimuli and coordinate network switching.

### Relevance to psychiatric disorders

-   **Depression:** altered DMN connectivity and excessive self-focused
    rumination have been reported.
-   **Schizophrenia:** abnormal DMN connectivity and failure to
    appropriately suppress internally directed activity during tasks may
    contribute to cognitive and self-processing disturbances.
-   **ADHD:** altered network maturation and DMN interference with
    task-positive networks have been studied.
-   **Autism spectrum disorder:** atypical connectivity may relate to
    self-referential and social-cognitive processing.
-   **Anxiety and PTSD:** altered internally directed attention,
    autobiographical memory and threat-related processing may involve
    DMN circuitry.
-   **Neurodegenerative disorders:** DMN hubs are vulnerable in
    Alzheimer's disease and are studied as potential imaging markers.

### Clinical significance and limitations

DMN research helps explain network-level dysfunction rather than isolated regional abnormalities. However, findings are heterogeneous, group-level and not sufficiently specific for individual diagnosis or treatment selection. Resting-state results are influenced by motion, analytic methods and clinical heterogeneity.

### Conclusion

The DMN is central to self-referential thought, memory and social cognition. Its altered connectivity across psychiatric disorders provides a useful research framework, but it is not currently a stand-alone diagnostic biomarker.
