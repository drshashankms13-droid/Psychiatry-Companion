## Exam answer

**Introduction.** SARS-CoV-2 primarily causes respiratory disease, but it affects the brain **directly and indirectly**. Neuropsychiatric manifestations occur:
- in the **acute phase** (delirium, anxiety, psychosis);
- in the **post-acute phase** (depression, PTSD, cognitive impairment, fatigue: "long COVID");
- **iatrogenically** (steroids, antivirals, ICU care);
- **psychosocially** (isolation, bereavement, stigma) [1].

**Scale:** in about 236,000 survivors, the **6-month incidence of a neurological or psychiatric diagnosis was about 34%** (about 13% first-ever), rising to **about 46% after ICU admission** (Taquet 2021) [1].

### Mechanisms
1. **Direct viral effects:**
   - possible neuroinvasion via the olfactory route (anosmia) or haematogenous spread;
   - ACE2 expression on endothelium and some neural cells.

   Direct CNS infection appears **uncommon**. Viral RNA in the CSF is rarely found [general knowledge].
2. **Systemic inflammation:**
   - **cytokine storm** (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β);
   - **blood–brain barrier disruption**;
   - **microglial activation** and neuroinflammation.
3. **Hypoxia** (respiratory failure) causes hypoxic-ischaemic injury.
4. **Endotheliitis and coagulopathy:**
   - microthrombi, **stroke**, microbleeds;
   - immunothrombosis [2].
5. **Autoimmunity:** autoantibodies, molecular mimicry. Can cause GBS and autoimmune encephalitis.
6. **Iatrogenic:**
   - **corticosteroids** (dexamethasone: mania, psychosis, depression, insomnia);
   - hydroxychloroquine (psychosis, depression);
   - interferons;
   - ritonavir interactions;
   - ICU sedation (benzodiazepines);
   - **QTc** interactions [1].
7. **Psychosocial:**
   - **isolation, fear of death**;
   - witnessing deaths;
   - stigma; grief;
   - financial loss [1].

### Clinical syndromes
**A. Acute phase [1]:**
- **Delirium:** the most common severe syndrome, especially in older and ICU patients. It may be the **presenting feature** in older adults (hypoactive common). See `covid-19-delirium-management`.
- **Acute stress reaction, panic attacks:** related to breathlessness, masks, BiPAP and isolation.
- **Depression and suicidal ideation/attempts** in wards and home isolation.
- **New-onset psychosis:** "COVID psychosis" case series; often steroid-related or delirium-related.
- **Mania:** steroid-induced.
- **Insomnia.**
- **Substance withdrawal** (alcohol, opioids, tobacco) in COVID wards.
- **Exacerbation of pre-existing disorders:** OCD, psychosis, anxiety, depression.

**B. Neurological (relevant for differentials) [general knowledge]:**
- anosmia/ageusia;
- headache; encephalopathy; encephalitis (including **ADEM**, autoimmune);
- **stroke** (large-vessel in young patients);
- seizures;
- **Guillain–Barré syndrome**;
- myopathy;
- critical illness neuropathy.

**C. Post-acute ("long COVID") [1, 2]:**
- **depression, anxiety, PTSD** (ICU survivors, bereaved);
- **cognitive impairment** ("brain fog"): attention, executive function, memory; MCI; increased dementia diagnoses in older people;
- **fatigue**, sleep disturbance;
- psychosis; substance use disorders;
- autonomic dysfunction (POTS).

**D. Special groups [1]:**
- **Elderly:** delirium, cognitive decline, unmasking of dementia.
- **Children:** **MIS-C** with encephalopathy; anxiety; school-related problems.
- **Peripartum women:** anxiety about transmission and breastfeeding; domestic violence.
- **People with SMI:** higher infection risk and **higher mortality (OR 1.67)**; clozapine toxicity during infection (**halve the dose**) [1].

### Assessment [1]
- **Baseline:**
  - pre-existing illness; psychotropics; substance use;
  - cognition; sleep; suicide risk;
  - PHQ and GAD-7.
- **In ward:**
  - **CAM/CAM-ICU**, RASS;
  - MMSE/HMSE/MoCA;
  - pain; sleep;
  - **temporal association with anti-COVID drugs** (HCQ, remdesivir, favipiravir, dexamethasone, tocilizumab, plasma).
- **Investigations:**
  - electrolytes (Na), glucose, oxygen saturation, inflammatory markers;
  - **ECG (QTc)**;
  - neuroimaging for focal signs or stroke;
  - **EEG** (non-convulsive status);
  - CSF and autoimmune panels if encephalitis is suspected.
- **Post-COVID:**
  - PHQ-9, GAD-7, PTSD screen, MoCA/IQCODE, fatigue and sleep scales, C-SSRS;
  - functioning and coping [1].

### Management principles [1]
- **Psychological first aid for all** infected patients.
- **Don't rush medication**; many symptoms settle as respiratory illness improves.
- **Start low, go slow.**
- **Monitor:**
  - **QTc** (antipsychotics + HCQ/azithromycin);
  - **respiratory depression** with benzodiazepines;
  - hyponatraemia;
  - CYP interactions (ritonavir, a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, raises quetiapine, lurasidone, pimozide and others).
- **Disorder-specific management:**
  - anxiety: benzodiazepines short-term, SSRIs;
  - depression: SSRIs or SNRIs, CBT;
  - psychosis: SGAs;
  - delirium: treat the cause; haloperidol or quetiapine; melatonin; dexmedetomidine;
  - withdrawal: lorazepam, OST, NRT.
- **Steroid-induced syndromes:** discuss dose reduction with physicians; antipsychotics for mania or psychosis.
- **Post-COVID:**
  - CBT and SSRIs for depression, anxiety and PTSD;
  - **cognitive rehabilitation**;
  - **donepezil** (or memantine with vascular risk) for dementia-range deficits;
  - grief work [1].
- **Liaison:** with the COVID team and families; breaking bad news by video [1].

**Conclusion.** COVID-19 produces a wide neuropsychiatric spectrum:
- **delirium** and acute stress, mood and psychotic syndromes during infection;
- **depression, anxiety, PTSD, cognitive impairment and fatigue** afterwards.

These arise from inflammation, hypoxia, vascular injury, drugs and psychosocial stress more than from direct viral invasion. Consultation-liaison psychiatry with careful attention to **drug interactions and QTc**, psychological first aid, and structured post-COVID follow-up form the core response.

## In depth

### Background
- **Earlier coronaviruses** (SARS, MERS) were associated with delirium acutely and with depression, anxiety, PTSD and fatigue in survivors (Rogers 2020 meta-analysis) [general knowledge].
- **"Spanish flu" 1918** and encephalitis lethargica are a historical reminder of post-viral neuropsychiatric sequelae.

### Epidemiology
- **Delirium in hospitalised COVID-19:** about 20–30% overall; up to 70–80% in ICU [general knowledge].
- **Psychosis:** rare (under 1%) [general knowledge].
- **Taquet 2021:** anxiety 17%, mood disorders 14%, dementia 0.7% (2% after ICU), psychotic disorder 1.4% after ICU [general knowledge; check exact figures].

### Classification
| Timing | Syndromes |
| --- | --- |
| Acute | Delirium, encephalopathy, acute stress, panic, depression, psychosis, mania (steroids), withdrawal |
| Subacute / post | Depression, anxiety, PTSD, cognitive impairment, fatigue, insomnia, OCD exacerbation |
| Long-term | Dementia risk (older adults), persistent long COVID |

### Mechanism (expanded)
- **Neuroimaging:**
  - UK Biobank (Douaud 2022): **reduced orbitofrontal and parahippocampal grey matter** and global brain volume after infection, with cognitive decline [general knowledge].
- **Neuropathology:** microglial activation, perivascular inflammation, microinfarcts. Little evidence of widespread viral encephalitis [general knowledge].

### Clinical features: steroid-induced psychiatric syndromes
- Dose-related (above 40 mg prednisolone-equivalent).
- Hypomania or mania most common; depression with longer use; psychosis; delirium.
- Usually within the first 1–2 weeks.
- **Management:** reduce the steroid if possible; antipsychotic (olanzapine or quetiapine); avoid lithium in unstable fluid status.

### Assessment: drug interaction checklist
| COVID drug | Psychiatric relevance |
| --- | --- |
| Dexamethasone / methylprednisolone | Mania, psychosis, insomnia, depression |
| Hydroxychloroquine | QTc; psychosis; depression |
| Azithromycin | QTc |
| Remdesivir | Few interactions |
| Favipiravir | Hyperuricaemia; few CNS effects |
| Lopinavir/ritonavir; nirmatrelvir/ritonavir | **CYP3A4 inhibition**: raises quetiapine, lurasidone, pimozide, midazolam, alprazolam; dose adjustment |
| Tocilizumab | Restores CYP activity (IL-6 suppression), so may **lower** levels of clozapine and others |
| Interferon | Depression, suicidality |

### Differential diagnosis
- Delirium vs primary psychosis or mania.
- Hypoxia; hyponatraemia; hypoglycaemia (steroids, diabetes).
- Stroke.
- Encephalitis.
- Non-convulsive status.
- Withdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines).
- Medication effects.

### Management: clozapine in COVID [1]
- Infection and inflammation inhibit CYP1A2, so clozapine levels rise, causing toxicity (sedation, seizures, aspiration).
- **Halve the dose** during infection; monitor levels.
- Check neutrophils (lymphopenia in COVID vs neutropenia from clozapine).

### Critical appraisal
- Many reports are **case series**, with confounding by ICU care, hypoxia and steroids.
- **Electronic record studies** (Taquet) depend on coding and lack symptom-level data.
- **Distinguishing COVID-specific from critical-illness effects** (post-intensive care syndrome) is difficult.

### Indian context
- High caseloads in the **second (Delta) wave**; **mucormycosis** co-epidemic (steroids, diabetes), with its own psychiatric burden (disfigurement) [general knowledge].
- **IPS CPG supplement (2022)** [1].
- **Tele-consultation** for CL psychiatry in COVID wards.

### Recent advances
- Neuroinflammation imaging (TSPO PET).
- Long COVID cognitive trials.
- **Vaccination** reduces long-term neuropsychiatric sequelae [general knowledge].
