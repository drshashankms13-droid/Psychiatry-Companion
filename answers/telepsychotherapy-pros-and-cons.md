## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Telepsychotherapy** (tele-therapy, tele-counselling) is psychotherapy delivered at a distance using ICT. It can be **synchronous** (video, audio, live chat) or **asynchronous** (email or messaging-based therapy, guided internet programmes). India's **Telepsychiatry Operational Guidelines 2020 (TOG)** state that, "irrespective of the tool of communication, the core principles … remain the same", with **the same professional accountability, ethical standards and laws as in-person therapy** [1]. Group psychotherapy by video goes back to **1961** [2].

### Advantages (pros)
**Access and equity:**
- Reaches **rural and remote** patients and areas without trained therapists. India has very few clinical psychologists.
- Helps the **homebound** (disability, elderly, perinatal), people with **mobility or transport problems**, **shift workers** and students.
- **Continuity** during pandemics and disasters, and when patients relocate.
- **Reduces costs** of travel and lost wages.

**Acceptability:**
- **Reduced stigma** (no visit to a "mental hospital"); a sense of anonymity.
- Comfort of home; useful for **social anxiety and agoraphobia**, where the setting may lower avoidance initially.
- High patient satisfaction (75–100% in reviews) [2].

**Clinical effectiveness:**
- Video-delivered CBT, IPT, behavioural activation and trauma therapies (prolonged exposure, CPT) show **non-inferior outcomes** to in-person delivery for depression, anxiety and PTSD in RCTs and meta-analyses [general knowledge].
- **Guided iCBT** is effective for depression and anxiety.

**Therapeutic opportunities:**
- **In-vivo work in the real environment:** exposure at home (e.g., OCD contamination), observing family interaction, home-based behavioural activation.
- **Family and couple sessions** with members in different places.
- Easier homework review (screen-sharing worksheets).
- **Flexible scheduling**, shorter booster sessions.
- **Recording** (with explicit consent) for supervision and training.

**System benefits:**
- Task-sharing support (therapist supervision of lay counsellors).
- Scalable programmes (Tele-MANAS counsellors).
- Collaborative care with primary care.

### Disadvantages (cons)
**Clinical:**
- **Reduced non-verbal communication:** limited body language, eye contact, subtle affect; audio-only loses even more.
- **Risk management is harder:** assessing suicidality or violence remotely; responding to a crisis requires knowing the patient's location and local services. The TOG requires an **emergency check before each session** and in-person referral in any crisis [1].
- **Unsuitable groups:**
  - acute psychosis, mania, severe suicidality;
  - severe cognitive impairment;
  - active substance intoxication;
  - **POCSO survivors and perpetrators, and forensic cases** (in-person first) [1];
  - some severe personality disorders (boundary issues);
  - very young children.
- **Diagnostic uncertainty:** the TOG requires an **in-person assessment and a diagnosis or provisional diagnosis before starting** [1].

**Therapeutic relationship:**
- **Alliance** may be harder to build, though studies suggest alliance ratings are comparable.
- **Boundary problems:** informal settings (bedroom, casual dress) and contact at odd hours. The TOG insists on **professional attire, timings and privacy** [1].
- **Disinhibition** online; dropout is easier ("closing the window").

**Privacy and confidentiality:**
- **Lack of a private space at home**, especially in joint families. Women, adolescents and **people experiencing domestic violence** may be overheard or monitored by the abuser.
- **Data security** on consumer platforms; hacking; **unauthorised recording** (illegal without explicit consent) [1].

**Technical:**
- connectivity, audio lag and frozen video break the flow of therapy;
- device and digital-literacy barriers (the **digital divide**);
- **screen fatigue** for both therapist and patient.

**Legal and ethical:**
- jurisdiction (inter-state; international clients are excluded under Indian guidelines);
- consent documentation;
- regulation of **non-medical therapists** (psychologists and counsellors fall outside the TPG);
- commercial app platforms with variable quality.

**Evidence limits:**
- fewer data for **psychodynamic, group, child and family** therapies and for **severe mental illness**;
- **Indian outcome data are sparse** [2].

### Good practice (TOG 2020) [1]
1. **In-person detailed assessment** before starting.
2. Diagnosis or provisional diagnosis; **assess suitability** for an online alliance, and keep reassessing.
3. **Screen for emergencies** at each session.
4. Decide whether family may be present.
5. Professional conduct: appointment, attire, duration (**30–45 minutes**), timing, privacy.
6. **Informed consent** (Appendix 3), covering:
   - modes;
   - confidentiality;
   - no emergency service;
   - no recording without consent;
   - patient responsible for the accuracy of information;
   - homework adherence.
7. **Document** all communications. Basic medical records under **MHCA Section 25** (Rules 2018, Form B).
8. Either party may discontinue. The therapist may refer for in-person therapy at any point.
9. Fees as for in-person therapy, with receipts.

**Conclusion.** Telepsychotherapy **expands access, reduces stigma and cost, and is broadly as effective** as in-person therapy for common mental disorders. Its limits are in **risk management, non-verbal communication, privacy, technology and suitability for severe illness**. In India, a **hybrid model** works best: in-person assessment, then structured, consented tele-sessions with emergency safeguards, as the TOG 2020 lays out.

## In depth

### Background
- **Evolution:**
  - telephone therapy and crisis lines (1950s onward);
  - video therapy (1961);
  - computerised CBT (1990s);
  - internet-delivered CBT (2000s);
  - app-based and chatbot interventions (2010s–2020s).
- **COVID-19** forced a rapid shift of most outpatient therapy online in 2020.

### Epidemiology (uptake)
- During COVID, most therapists worldwide moved to remote delivery. Many continue in hybrid form [general knowledge].
- India has few clinical psychologists relative to need [general knowledge; check RCI figures].

### Classification
| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Synchronous video | Individual CBT via secure video |
| Synchronous audio | Telephone counselling (Tele-MANAS tier 1) |
| Synchronous text | Live chat counselling |
| Asynchronous | Email therapy; therapist-guided iCBT modules |
| Self-guided digital | Apps, unguided programmes |
| Blended | In-person plus digital modules |

### Mechanism
- Common therapeutic factors (alliance, expectancy, structure) survive transmission.
- **Online disinhibition effect:** may increase disclosure (an advantage) or impulsive communication (a risk).
- Structured therapies (CBT) transfer best. Highly relational or non-verbal therapies transfer less well.

### Clinical features: patient suitability checklist
- Stable risk profile.
- Private space and reliable device.
- Capacity to engage and give consent.
- Preference for remote care.
- Diagnosis suited to structured therapy.
- Emergency contact available.

### Assessment
- Baseline and session-by-session measures (PHQ-9, GAD-7) to monitor progress remotely (measurement-based care).
- **Alliance measures** (Working Alliance Inventory).

### Differential: teletherapy vs in-person therapy for specific uses
| Condition | Teletherapy suitability |
| --- | --- |
| Depression, GAD, panic | Good (CBT, BA) |
| PTSD | Good (PE, CPT by video) |
| OCD | Good (ERP at home) |
| Insomnia | Good (CBT-I, digital) |
| Psychosis | Adjunctive (family work, CBTp); risk caution |
| Eating disorders | Feasible (FBT by video) with medical monitoring in person |
| Children | Feasible with parent involvement; shorter sessions |
| Couple and family | Feasible; privacy issues |

### Management: handling disruptions
- Agree a **backup plan**: switch to phone, then reschedule.
- Note premature termination and its reason in the record [1].

### Evidence table
| Source | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| TOG 2020 | Principles, consent, documentation for teletherapy [1] |
| Indian telepsychiatry reviews | Satisfaction 75–100%; methodological weaknesses [2] |
| Backhaus 2012 (general knowledge) | Video-conference psychotherapy: comparable outcomes and alliance |
| Andersson & Cuijpers (general knowledge) | Guided iCBT comparable to face-to-face CBT for depression and anxiety |

### Critical appraisal
- Most RCTs exclude high-risk patients and come from high-income countries.
- **Alliance** studies rely on self-report.
- Long-term outcomes and dropout rates are less studied.
- Cultural adaptation (language, family presence) in India is under-researched.

### Indian context
- **TOG 2020 Chapter 7** (teletherapy) and **Appendix 3** (documentation and consent) [1].
- **Tele-MANAS** (counsellor tier 1).
- University and NGO helplines.
- **RCI** regulates clinical psychologists. Tele-practice guidance for non-medical professionals is still evolving [general knowledge].
- **Privacy in joint families** is a practical obstacle. Suggest headphones and scheduling when the house is empty, or chat-based check-ins.

### Recent advances
- **AI chatbots** (CBT-based): early RCTs; safety and regulation are major concerns [general knowledge].
- **VR exposure** delivered remotely.
- **Digital therapeutics** with regulatory approval (e.g., FDA-cleared apps for insomnia and substance use) [general knowledge].
- **Blended stepped-care** models.
