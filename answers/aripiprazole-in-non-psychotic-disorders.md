> **Source discipline:** this answer uses only *Maudsley Prescribing Guidelines* 15e [1]. The indications are collected from the relevant sections, cited below. **Receptor-pharmacology details beyond those the source states are not included.**

## Exam answer

**Introduction [1].**
- Aripiprazole is a **dopamine D2 partial agonist** antipsychotic.
- It is valued outside psychosis for its **low weight, metabolic and prolactin burden**:
  - "Aripiprazole has **no effect on prolactin**", making it the preferred option in ASD;
  - it **lowers prolactin** when added to other drugs;
  - it has **no effect on QT** (in children and young people).
- **But:** akathisia, restlessness, somnolence and weight gain can still occur, and it carries a risk of **impulse-control disorders** (gambling).

### Uses in non-psychotic disorders
**1. Treatment-resistant unipolar depression [1, Table 3.3]:**
- **Add aripiprazole 2–20 mg/day** to an antidepressant. It has a **good evidence base**, and brexpiprazole and cariprazine are also effective.
- Usually well tolerated. **Low doses (2–10 mg)** may suffice. **Akathisia and restlessness are common.**
- **Late-life TRD:** in OPTIMUM, augmenting with aripiprazole (or bupropion) beat switching to bupropion.

**2. Bipolar disorder: mania and maintenance [1]:**
- Licensed for **mania, mixed episodes and maintenance**.
- In pregnancy it **may not be associated with gestational diabetes**, though there is a neurodevelopmental safety signal to study.

**3. Obsessive–compulsive disorder [1]:**
- **Adults:** second line is **adding risperidone or aripiprazole** to an SSRI (Box 3.9: "most evidence supports aripiprazole or risperidone").
- **Children:**
  - low-dose augmentation (**2.5–5 mg**) after two failed SSRIs;
  - a **larger paediatric evidence base than risperidone**;
  - a 6–8-week trial; stop if no response.
- **Not effective alone.** **Not recommended in BDD.**

**4. Tourette syndrome and tics [1]:**
- **Effective and well tolerated** in children.
- **10-week RCT (n = 61):** tics fell.
- **8-week RCT (n = 133):** much or very much improved in **69% (low dose) and 74% (high dose) vs 38% placebo**.
- Lowers prolactin, but weight rises by 1.6 kg.
- Two meta-analyses support it.

**5. Autism spectrum disorder (irritability) [1]:**
- With risperidone, **the most effective** drug for **irritability** (moderate to large effect sizes).
- Cochrane: probably reduces **irritability and self-injury**, but **not aggression**. Possibly ineffective for self-injury in some data.
- **Dose:** start **2 mg/day**; maintenance **5–15 mg**.
- **Prolactin-neutral**, so preferred over risperidone.
- Re-evaluate after stabilisation: relapse did not differ on discontinuation.

**6. Borderline personality disorder [1]:** SGAs bring **no overall improvement** in BPD, but aripiprazole has a **beneficial effect on anger** (low-certainty evidence).

**7. Anorexia nervosa [1]:**
- **Non-RCT evidence** for weight gain.
- Limited evidence that it reduces **eating-specific anxiety**.
- Prolactin-sparing, which matters for osteoporosis risk.

**8. Behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia [1]:** modest efficacy for **overall BPSD**, and it is **less likely to cause parkinsonian effects**. Weigh dementia-related mortality warnings.

**9. Drug-induced problems [1]:**
- **Hyperprolactinaemia:** adjunctive aripiprazole **normalises prolactin** (with risperidone, benzamides or LAI risperidone). **Duloxetine-linked** hyperprolactinaemia has been treated with aripiprazole.
- **Antipsychotic weight gain:** add it to reduce weight with **clozapine**.
- **Antidepressant sexual dysfunction:** adjunctive aripiprazole **improved sexual functioning and desire, in women only**.

**10. Stimulant-induced agitation [1]:** with methamphetamine (EPSE risk ×4), aripiprazole **may be preferred for rapid tranquillisation**, as olanzapine and benzodiazepines are the alternatives.

### Cautions [1]
- **Akathisia and restlessness**, the commonest problem in depression augmentation.
- **Impulse-control disorders and gambling** (MHRA reminder), especially in people at risk of problem gambling.
- Weight gain, metabolic change and **somnolence even with aripiprazole** in children.
- **Renal impairment:** under 1% excreted unchanged; no adjustment needed. Avoid depot where possible.

**Conclusion.** Aripiprazole's partial agonism and benign metabolic and prolactin profile make it useful beyond psychosis:
- **TRD augmentation** (2–10 mg);
- OCD augmentation;
- **tics and Tourette syndrome**;
- **ASD irritability**;
- bipolar maintenance;
- correcting **hyperprolactinaemia** and clozapine weight gain.

Weigh these benefits against **akathisia** and **impulse-control** risks.

## In depth

### Dose by indication [1]
| Indication | Dose (as given in the source) |
|---|---|
| TRD augmentation | 2–20 mg/day (low doses 2–10 mg may suffice) |
| Paediatric OCD augmentation | 2.5–5 mg/day |
| ASD irritability | Start 2 mg; maintenance 5–15 mg |
| Tourette syndrome (RCT) | Low dose 5 mg (under 50 kg) or 10 mg; high dose 10 or 20 mg |
