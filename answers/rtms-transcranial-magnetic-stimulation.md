## Exam answer

**Definition.** Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) is a **non-invasive, non-convulsive** method of brain stimulation [1]:
- **History:** first described by **Anthony Barker and colleagues in 1985**; in clinical use since the 1990s.
- **How it works:** a multisession treatment in which magnetic fields from recurring TMS pulses stimulate nerve cells in a chosen brain region.
- **Effects:** neuromodulation of neuronal excitability, with implied **neuroplastic** effects.

### Forms and parameters [1]
- **Conventional rTMS:**
  - **high-frequency (>5–10 Hz)**, which is excitatory;
  - **low-frequency (≤1 Hz)**.
- **Patterned rTMS:** theta-burst stimulation (TBS) and quadri-pulse stimulation.
  - TBS sub-forms: **intermittent (iTBS)**, **continuous (cTBS)** and intermediate (imTBS).
- **Schedules:**
  - once daily;
  - twice or more daily (**accelerated or intensive**);
  - maintenance schedules, from 3–5 per week down to monthly.
- **Coils:** the **figure-of-eight** coil is the most common; H-coils (deep TMS) and double-cone coils reach deeper structures. About 50 coil designs are under study.
- **Targets:**
  - conventional: **DLPFC** and temporoparietal cortex;
  - newer: cerebellum, OFC, SMA, bilateral stimulation.
- **Motor threshold (MT):** the minimum single-pulse intensity that produces a response in the contralateral **APB or FDI** in ≥50% of stimuli (≥5 of 10). It is measured by EMG or by visible finger twitch; visual MT is higher than EMG MT.
- **When to re-check MT:**
  - weekly;
  - after medication changes;
  - after heavy alcohol or substance use in the previous 24 hours;
  - if headache occurs.
- **Target location:**
  - the **5-cm rule** or the **10–20 EEG system**, which is cost-effective;
  - **neuronavigation** with MRI, SPECT or PET, which is more accurate.

### Role in psychiatry — IPS-CPG evidence [1]
**rTMS is used as an adjunct** to treatment as usual in almost all the meta-analyses reviewed.

| Condition | Evidence | Preferred protocol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unipolar depression (acute)** | **Strong**: effect sizes 0.30–0.83; response ORs 3.26–3.64; remission ORs 2.45–4.63 | **HF left DLPFC** best; then LF right DLPFC; bilateral, iTBS, deep TMS and priming also effective |
| **Treatment-resistant depression** | Significant; **better as augmentation** than as stand-alone | HF left DLPFC, then LF right DLPFC |
| **Bipolar depression** | Moderate (ES 0.30; OR 2.72); one meta-analysis found significance only in unipolar | **Only HF left DLPFC** effective |
| Peripartum depression | Positive (ES 0.65–1.39); safe for mother and fetus | HF left DLPFC |
| Post-stroke depression | Strong (ES 1.01–4.92) | HF left DLPFC |
| Post-TBI depression | Positive (ES 1.03) but lost by 1 month | HF left DLPFC |
| Parkinson's depression | Strong, moderate effect (ES 0.62–0.86) | HF left DLPFC |
| **OCD** | Moderate (SMD 0.50–0.79); 10–30 sessions | **LF right DLPFC**, HF bilateral DLPFC, LF SMA |
| GAD | Positive (ES 1.45–1.87) | LF right DLPFC |
| Panic disorder | **Not effective** | — |
| PTSD | Positive (ES 0.68–1.16) | HF or LF right DLPFC |
| **Schizophrenia: resistant auditory hallucinations** | Moderate (SMD 0.24–0.51) | **LF left temporoparietal cortex (T3P3)** |
| **Schizophrenia: negative symptoms** | Moderate to large (SMD 0.49–0.64) | HF left DLPFC; >10 sessions |
| Clozapine-refractory schizophrenia | Not significant | — |
| Bipolar mania | Not significant | — |
| **Smoking cessation** | Positive | HF left DLPFC; deep TMS over bilateral DLPFC and insula |
| **Alzheimer's dementia** (cognition, BPSD) | Positive (AD, not MCI) | HF left or bilateral DLPFC |
| Insomnia | Positive | LF right DLPFC |
| Migraine; fibromyalgia | Positive | HF primary motor cortex |
| Chronic tinnitus | Positive at 2–6 months | LF primary auditory cortex |
| Suicidality | **Not recommended for acutely suicidal patients** | — |
| ASD; eating disorders; ADHD; BPD | Insufficient or no data | — |

### US FDA clearances [1]
- **MDD:** NeuroStar (2008); Brainsway deep TMS H1-coil (2013); several others, including **TBS (2018)**.
- **OCD:** H7 deep TMS coil (2017); MagVenture (2020).
- **Smoking cessation:** H4 coil (2020).
- **Anxiety comorbid with MDD:** H1 coil (2021).
- **Migraine:** eNeura (2013).

### Pre-rTMS evaluation and contraindications [1]
- **Consent and history:**
  - informed consent;
  - psychiatric, medical and neurological history;
  - personal or family epilepsy, TBI, loss of consciousness, stroke, brain tumour, seizure-threshold-lowering drugs.
- **Screening tools:** the **TMS Adult Safety Screen (TASS)** or the **Rossi et al. questionnaire**.
- **Contraindications:**
  1. Ferromagnetic or magnetically sensitive implants or metal in the **brain, head or neck**.
  2. DBS leads in the scalp, if the coil is within 10 cm.
  3. Other metallic devices (pacemakers, cochlear implants, pumps, dental implants, piercings, ferromagnetic tattoo ink) within **10 cm** of the coil.

  Hip and knee prostheses are safe. X-rays cannot tell whether metal is ferromagnetic.
- **Before each session:**
  - adequate sleep;
  - no acute medical problem (fever, uncontrolled BP or sugar, acute headache, vertigo, scalp injury);
  - not acutely violent or suicidal;
  - no alcohol, tobacco or other substances beforehand.

### Adverse effects [1, 3]
- **Seizures:** **62%** occur in the first session and **75%** in the first three, so take most care early [1].
- **In 53 sham-controlled trials** [1]:
  - **no increase in serious adverse events** (0.9% vs 1.5%) or in dropouts;
  - more **headache** (22.6% vs 10.9%), **discomfort** and **pain at the stimulation site** (23.8% vs 5.0%).
- **Mood switch:** not related to rTMS (25 trials) [1].
- **Hearing:** **earplugs** for patient and operator. Patients with **cochlear implants should not** have TMS [1].
- **Cognition:** no cognitive impairment, and no anaesthesia needed [3].
- **Indian data:** seizures and affective switch both <0.5% [1].

**Conclusion.** rTMS is a safe, non-invasive, adjunctive neuromodulation treatment [1, 3]:
- **Strongest evidence:** unipolar and treatment-resistant depression with HF left DLPFC stimulation.
- **Moderate evidence:** OCD, resistant hallucinations, negative symptoms, smoking cessation and dementia.
- **Compared with ECT:** less effective, but better tolerated and without cognitive effects.

## In depth

### Background [1]
- **Non-response is common:** 20–60% of patients don't respond to drugs or psychotherapy. Poor adherence ("pseudo-resistance") adds to this.
- **IPS-CPG method:**
  - an **umbrella review of 97 meta-analyses** (2018–July 2022), counting only sham-controlled pooled effect sizes;
  - informed by GRADE, other guidelines and expert consensus.
- **Training in India:** IPS with NIMHANS and AIIMS runs annual workshops; CIP Ranchi and KMC Manipal also train.

### Mechanism in detail
- **Neuromodulation and neuroplasticity** [1].
- **SAINT targeting** [2]:
  - uses the left DLPFC subregion most **anticorrelated** with the **subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sgACC)**, found on resting-state fMRI;
  - the sgACC is hyperactive in depression, so stimulating an anticorrelated DLPFC region may reduce that hyperactivity indirectly.
- **Placebo-related activation:** the left DLPFC and left sgACC/ventral striatum [1].

### Who provides rTMS [1]
- **"TMS physician":** has prescribing rights, is trained and credentialed in rTMS, and has a neurophysiology background (psychiatry, neurology or neurosurgery).
- **"TMS operator":** can be non-medical, preferably a nurse. Must recognise mental-state changes and manage seizures.

### Set-up [1]
- **Suite:** air-conditioned, with emergency seizure management and anticonvulsants available.
- **Device components:**
  - main unit;
  - coil (figure-of-eight);
  - cooling unit;
  - power supply;
  - **EMG machine**;
  - coil holder;
  - computer.
- **Accessories:** recliner chair, earplugs, skin marker, measuring tape.
- **Specification:** a stimulator of at least **50 Hz with burst mode** for TBS; two cooled figure-of-eight coils.

### Clinical features in detail — practical depression points [1]
- **Antidepressant response is durable in about half:**
  - 66.5% of responders still respond at 3 months;
  - **52.9% at 6 months**;
  - **46.3% at 12 months**.

  Female sex and maintenance treatment predict sustained response. The user's notes put this as "50% sustained response till 12 months" [2].
- **Maintenance rTMS:** promising for treatment-resistant MDD, but evidence is not enough for a recommendation.
- **Placebo response is large:** g = 0.8.
- **Devices:** NeuroStar, MagPro and Magstim did not differ. The H1 vs figure-of-eight differences were not clinically relevant.
- **Predictors of response:**
  - frequency of stimulation predicted outcome; intensity and session number did not (one meta-analysis);
  - for TBS: ≥1,800 pulses per session, subthreshold intensity and ≤2-week courses predicted response;
  - in people over 50: higher age and more sessions.
- **Suicidality:**
  - reduced in non-TRD depression (g 0.39), but **not in TRD**;
  - HF left DLPFC added to antidepressants has the best evidence for reducing suicidal behaviour in TRD (Godi 2021);
  - still **not recommended for acute suicidality**.
- **rTMS + psychosocial intervention:** no added effect.

### Accelerated protocols [1, 2]
- **SAINT** (Stanford Accelerated Intelligent Neuromodulation Therapy):
  - **fMRI-guided iTBS** with neuronavigation;
  - **10 sessions a day for 5 days**, about 90,000 pulses vs about 18,000 in a conventional course [2];
  - **86.4% remission in TRD**, but the IPS-CPG notes it "remain[s] to be tested in controlled studies" [1];
  - **limitations:** needs imaging and equipment, is logistically demanding, and its durability is unknown [2].
- **Accelerated left-DLPFC rTMS/TBS:** not significantly better than sham in one meta-analysis [1].

### Comparison with other treatments [1, 3]

| Comparison | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Bitemporal ECT vs HF left rTMS, cTBS, deep TMS | **ECT had higher response** [1] |
| High-dose right unilateral ECT vs cTBS | ECT better [1] |
| Bilateral rTMS vs bitemporal ECT | rTMS **more acceptable** [1] |
| ECT non-responders | Unlikely to respond to rTMS [3] |
| rTMS vs ECT overall | rTMS less effective but needs no anaesthesia and causes no cognitive impairment [3] |

### Safety in special situations [1]
- **Children:** single- and paired-pulse TMS are safe from age 2 with hearing protection; headache is the commonest side effect.
- **Pregnancy:**
  - the figure-of-eight coil induces about 100 mV/m at the uterus (60 cm away);
  - this is far below the 800 mV/m nerve-stimulation threshold;
  - so the risk is minimal.
- **Combination with drugs:**
  - no toxicity has emerged, so no special caution is needed;
  - document clozapine and other seizure-threshold factors (alcohol, sleep loss, infection).
- **Implanted devices:**
  - safe if the coil is >10 cm from pulse generators;
  - with **DBS**, only with strong reasons and ethics oversight.
- **Operators:** stay >40 cm from the coil; earplugs are mandatory.

| Meta-analysis | Finding |

| Mutz 2018 (56 studies) | HF-LDLPFC g 0.72; deep TMS 0.29 |

| Hyde 2022 (OCD, 26 studies) | −0.66 |

| Indian meta-analysis (52 studies) | Large pre-post effects, but sham-controlled benefit only for migraine and alcohol craving, lost on sensitivity analysis; seizures <0.5% |

### Critical appraisal [1]
- **Adjunct only:** rTMS was almost always added to treatment as usual.
- **Large placebo response** in depression (g 0.8).
- **Weak Indian sham-controlled evidence:** high risk of bias and heterogeneity.
- **Limits of the guideline's method:** an umbrella review is prone to positive reporting bias, involved no quantitative synthesis, and could not fully apply GRADE.
- **Unanswered questions:**
  - how many sessions define non-response;
  - comorbid conditions (e.g. depression with OCD);
  - alternative targets.
- **Newer forms not yet systematically reviewed:** deep TMS, prolonged iTBS, synchronised TBS, priming, accelerated TMS, and new targets such as the cerebellum and OFC.

### Indian context [1]
- **IPS-CPG 2023:** the national guideline.
- **Training:** IPS, NIMHANS, AIIMS, CIP Ranchi and KMC Manipal.
- **Indian meta-analysis (52 studies):** see the evidence table above.

### Recent advances [1, 2]
- **TBS:** iTBS is FDA-cleared and is similar in effect to HF rTMS.
- **Accelerated and fMRI-guided protocols:** SAINT.
- **Neuronavigation:** robotic coil positioning.
- **Deep TMS** for OCD and smoking.
- **New targets:** cerebellum, OFC.
