## Exam answer

**Definition.** Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) is a non-invasive, non-convulsive brain stimulation technique. A rapidly changing current in a coil held against the scalp produces a magnetic field that induces an electric current in the underlying cortex and depolarises neurons. Given repeatedly over a course of sessions (repetitive TMS, rTMS), it changes cortical excitability and network activity beyond the session itself. Barker and colleagues described it in 1985; it entered clinical psychiatry in the 1990s, and the US FDA first cleared a device for major depression in 2008 [1].

### Mechanism
Coil current → time-varying magnetic field (about 1.5–2 tesla, passes through skull without attenuation) → induced electric field in cortex (depth ~2–3 cm with a figure-of-eight coil) → neuronal depolarisation → repeated trains produce LTP-like (excitatory) or LTD-like (inhibitory) plasticity → effects spread trans-synaptically along networks (e.g., left DLPFC → subgenual anterior cingulate cortex, sgACC) [1,2].

- **High frequency (≥5 Hz, usually 10 Hz)** → increases cortical excitability.
- **Low frequency (≤1 Hz)** → decreases excitability.
- **Theta burst stimulation (TBS)**: bursts of three pulses at 50 Hz repeated at 5 Hz. **Intermittent TBS (iTBS)** is excitatory and takes about 3 minutes instead of about 37; **continuous TBS (cTBS)** is inhibitory [1].
- In depression, the best-supported model is **network rebalancing**: the left DLPFC is hypoactive and the sgACC hyperactive. Stimulating the DLPFC subregion most *anticorrelated* with the sgACC predicts better response [2,3].

### Procedure (IPS guideline) [1]

| Step | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| Screening | Psychiatric, medical, neurological and drug history; epilepsy (self/family), head injury, stroke, tumour, seizure-threshold-lowering drugs; TMS Adult Safety Screen or Rossi questionnaire |
| Consent | Explain side effects and their likelihood |
| Motor threshold (MT) | Lowest intensity giving a response in contralateral abductor pollicis brevis/first dorsal interosseous in ≥5 of 10 single pulses; re-measure weekly, and after dose changes, heavy alcohol or substance use |
| Targeting | "5-cm rule", International 10–20 EEG system (Beam F3 for left DLPFC) — cost-effective; neuronavigation (MRI/fMRI) more accurate |
| Session | Earplugs for patient and operator; operator >40 cm from coil |
| Course | Typically 20–30 sessions over 4–6 weeks for depression |

**Contraindications:** ferromagnetic or magnet-sensitive implants or metal in head/neck; cochlear implants; DBS leads, pacemakers, pumps or ferromagnetic tattoo ink within 10 cm of the coil. Implants below the neck (hip, knee) are safe [1].

| OCD | LF right DLPFC (moderate); HF bilateral DLPFC, LF SMA (low); 800–1,200 pulses, 10–30 sessions | — |

| Schizophrenia — resistant auditory hallucinations | LF (1 Hz) left temporoparietal cortex (T3P3) | Low |

For depression, ≥1,800 pulses/session, subthreshold intensity and ≤2-week duration predicted response in TBS studies; accelerated (several sessions/day) protocols were not better than once-daily treatment in meta-analysis [1].

### Adverse effects
Headache (22.6% vs 10.9% sham), scalp discomfort (23.8% vs 16.2%) and site pain (16.2% vs 5.0%) are common but mild. Serious adverse events (0.9% vs 1.5%) and drop-outs (3.3% vs 2.3%) do not differ from sham [1]. **Seizure** is rare with conventional parameters (well under 1 per 1,000 sessions [8]); 62% occur in the first session and 75% in the first three, so the first sessions need the most vigilance [1]. Others: syncope, transient hearing change (earplugs), facial twitching, and a hypomanic switch that meta-analysis did not find above sham [1].

### Key evidence
- **O'Reardon 2007** (n=301) and **George 2010 OPT-TMS** (n=190): HF left DLPFC rTMS superior to sham in medication-resistant MDD [4,5].
- **Mutz 2018 network meta-analysis** (56 trials): response OR 3.75 for HF left DLPFC; LF right, bilateral, iTBS and deep TMS also effective [1].
- **THREE-D, Blumberger 2018** (n=414): iTBS (3 min) non-inferior to 10 Hz rTMS (37.5 min) [6].
- **SAINT, Cole 2020/2022**: fMRI-targeted accelerated iTBS, 10 sessions/day for 5 days; 86.4% remission open-label and 78.6% vs 13.3% sham remission in an RCT (n=29) [3,7].
- Durability: of responders, 66.5% still respond at 3 months, 52.9% at 6 months and 46.3% at 12 months [1].

**Conclusion.** rTMS is an established, well-tolerated second-line treatment for unipolar depression after one or more failed antidepressants. Strong evidence supports high-frequency left DLPFC stimulation, and iTBS cuts session time without losing efficacy. It is less effective than ECT and should not replace ECT for psychotic, catatonic or acutely suicidal depression. Its place in OCD, PTSD, negative symptoms and dementia is real but rests on moderate evidence from small trials with large sham responses.

## In depth

### Background
Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction (1831) is the physical basis. d'Arsonval (1896) produced phosphenes with a head coil. Barker, Jalinous and Freeston at Sheffield (1985) built the first practical stimulator for the motor cortex, initially as a diagnostic tool for central motor conduction. In the 1990s, George and Pascual-Leone showed antidepressant effects of left prefrontal stimulation. FDA clearances followed: NeuroStar for MDD (2008), Brainsway H1 deep TMS for MDD (2013), H7 coil for OCD (2017), iTBS (2018), H4 coil for smoking cessation (2020), anxious depression (2021), and SAINT/neuronavigated accelerated iTBS (2022) [1,3].

### Mechanism in detail
**Physics.** A capacitor discharges through the coil in about 100–300 µs → magnetic field → induced E-field parallel to the scalp → preferentially activates horizontally oriented axons and interneurons in superficial cortex. Figure-of-eight (butterfly) coils give a focal field at the junction of the wings. H-coils (deep TMS) reach deeper but less focally. Double-cone coils reach medial areas such as the SMA and ACC [1].

**Neurophysiology.**
1. Single pulse → motor-evoked potential (MEP); used for MT and research measures of excitability.
2. Paired pulse → short-interval intracortical inhibition (GABA-A) and intracortical facilitation (glutamate).
3. Repetitive trains → NMDA-receptor-dependent synaptic plasticity, raised BDNF, changed monoamine release (dopamine release in caudate after left DLPFC rTMS), and normalised HPA axis activity.

**Network model of depression.** Left DLPFC (top-down control) is hypoactive → limbic sgACC hyperactive → negative bias and rumination. Excitatory left DLPFC stimulation → reduced sgACC activity. Fox et al. (2012) showed that clinical efficacy of different DLPFC sites correlated with their anticorrelation with sgACC; SAINT targets this individually with resting-state fMRI [3]. Placebo response in depression trials also activates left DLPFC and sgACC/ventral striatum, which partly explains the large sham response (g ≈ 0.8) [1].

**Why low frequency on the right?** Inter-hemispheric imbalance model: relative right prefrontal overactivity in depression and anxiety; 1 Hz inhibits it.

### Parameters explained

| Parameter | Typical value (depression) | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | 10 Hz (HF-L); 1 Hz (LF-R); iTBS 50 Hz bursts at 5 Hz | Excitatory vs inhibitory |
| Intensity | 110–120% resting MT (TBS often 80–120%) | Prefrontal cortex lies deeper than the motor strip |
| Train | 4 s on, 26 s off (10 Hz) | Inter-train interval limits seizure risk |
| Pulses/session | 3,000 (10 Hz); 600 (iTBS) | ≥1,800 better in TBS meta-analysis |
| Sessions | 20–30, 5 days a week | Accelerated: 2–10 a day |

### Assessment in detail
- **Before treatment:** diagnosis and severity (HAM-D, MADRS), treatment history (number of adequate trials — the Antidepressant Treatment History Form), substances, sleep, medical illness, pregnancy, metallic implants. Use the TASS or Rossi 15-item questionnaire [1].
- **Investigations:** none routinely required. EEG only if seizure history is unclear. MRI when neuronavigation is used. X-ray cannot tell whether metal is ferromagnetic [1].
- **Medications:** record seizure-threshold-lowering drugs (clozapine, bupropion, high-dose TCAs, theophylline), alcohol use, sleep loss and infection. Benzodiazepines and anticonvulsants raise MT and may reduce the effect; re-measure MT after dose changes [1].
- **Monitoring:** rating scales weekly; ask about hearing, headache and switch symptoms.

### Differential choice — rTMS compared with other treatments

| Option | When preferred over rTMS | Evidence point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECT | Psychotic depression, catatonia, high suicide risk, refusal to eat, need for rapid response | Bitemporal ECT had higher response than HF-L rTMS in network meta-analysis [1] |
| Ketamine/esketamine | Rapid relief needed, outpatient access to monitoring | Response within hours; durability limited |
| tDCS | Low cost, home use | Smaller and less consistent effects |
| Psychotherapy augmentation | Mild–moderate depression, patient preference | Combining with rTMS showed no added benefit in a small meta-analysis [1] |

### Management details
- **Augment, don't replace.** Nearly all trials used rTMS as an adjunct to treatment as usual. In TRD, response was better when rTMS was added to antidepressants than when given alone [1].
- **Suicidality:** rTMS reduced suicidal ideation (g 0.39) in non-TRD patients. IPS does **not** recommend rTMS for acutely suicidal patients; ECT is preferred [1].
- **Maintenance:** evidence is accumulating. Tapering schedules (twice weekly → weekly → monthly) are used for responders with relapse history, but no specific recommendation is made [1].
- **Pregnancy:** the E-field at the uterus from a figure-of-eight coil over the DLPFC (≈100 mV/m at 60 cm) is far below the threshold for nerve stimulation (800 mV/m), so risk is minimal. This makes rTMS an attractive option in perinatal depression [1].
- **Children:** single- and paired-pulse TMS are safe from age 2 with hearing protection. Therapeutic rTMS in adolescents is still investigational [1].
- **Elderly:** brain atrophy increases scalp-to-cortex distance. Older age and more sessions predicted greater response in a meta-analysis of patients aged over 50 [1].
- **Implants:** pacemakers, VNS and other stimulators are allowed if the coil stays more than 10 cm away. Use DBS only with clear justification and ethics committee oversight [1].
- **Staff:** a *TMS physician* (psychiatrist, neurologist or neurosurgeon trained in rTMS) prescribes; a *TMS operator* (often a nurse) delivers and must be trained in seizure management [1].

### Critical appraisal
- **Blinding is imperfect.** Active rTMS causes scalp sensations and twitching that sham coils do not fully mimic. The large placebo response (g ≈ 0.8) narrows the real-minus-sham difference [1].
- **Effect size is moderate.** Response rates in clinical trials are 30–50% and remission 15–30%, lower than ECT.
- **Heterogeneity:** protocols, targets, intensity and session number vary widely, and most non-depression indications rest on a few small trials.
- **Durability:** about half of responders lose benefit by 1 year without maintenance [1].
- **Accelerated protocols:** SAINT's dramatic remission rates come from small samples with individualised fMRI targeting that few centres can provide. Pooled accelerated studies have not beaten once-daily treatment [1].
- **Cost and access:** daily attendance for 4–6 weeks is a barrier, especially for rural patients.

### Indian context
- The **IPS Clinical Practice Guideline (Tikka et al., 2023)** is the reference standard in India. It was based on an umbrella review of 97 meta-analyses (2018–2022) [1].
- Training: IPS with NIMHANS and AIIMS New Delhi runs annual workshops; CIP Ranchi and KMC Manipal also train [1].
- NIMHANS and CIP Ranchi have contributed many Indian trials on auditory hallucinations, negative symptoms, OCD and depression.
- Under the **Mental Healthcare Act 2017**, rTMS is not a restricted procedure (unlike ECT, it needs no anaesthesia), but informed consent and documentation remain mandatory. A nominated representative's consent applies for patients lacking capacity.
- Cost per course and availability outside cities limit use. Twice-daily or iTBS schedules can shorten hospital stay.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** iTBS (3-minute sessions); deep TMS H-coils for MDD and OCD.
- **Emerging:** SAINT / accelerated neuronavigated iTBS (FDA-cleared 2022; replication ongoing) [3,7].
- **Investigational:** EEG-synchronised TMS, closed-loop systems, priming protocols, cerebellar iTBS for negative symptoms, orbitofrontal targets for OCD, and connectivity-guided targeting for other disorders [1].
- Combining rTMS with D-cycloserine (NMDA partial agonist) to enhance plasticity is being studied.
