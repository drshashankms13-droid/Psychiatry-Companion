## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)** is a lifelong **neurodevelopmental disorder** with onset in early childhood. It has two core features [1]:
- **(A) Persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction:** social-emotional reciprocity, non-verbal communication, and developing and maintaining relationships.
- **(B) Restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities (RRBIs):** stereotypies, insistence on sameness, restricted intense interests, and **sensory hyper- or hypo-reactivity**. At least 2 of 4 are required.

Symptoms appear in the early developmental period, cause impairment, and are not better explained by intellectual disability. **DSM-5 and ICD-11** merged autistic disorder, Asperger's and PDD-NOS into one spectrum. Severity is graded by **level of support needed (1–3)** [1].

### Mechanism
- **Highly heritable (about 80%)**, with polygenic common variants and rare de novo variants:
  - CNVs: 16p11.2, 15q11-13.
  - Genes: CHD8, SHANK3, SCN2A.
  - Syndromic forms (about 10–20%): **fragile X**, tuberous sclerosis, Rett (MECP2), Down syndrome.
- **Environmental risks:** advanced parental age, prematurity, maternal valproate, maternal diabetes or infection. **Vaccines do NOT cause autism.**
- These lead to **altered early brain development**: early brain overgrowth, atypical synaptic pruning, excitation/inhibition imbalance, and local over- and long-range under-connectivity → impaired social attention (joint attention, faces, voices) → cascading developmental deficits.

### Early signs (red flags) [1]
- **12 months:** no babbling, pointing or waving; no response to name.
- **18 months:** no single words; no pretend play; not following simple directions.
- **24 months:** no spontaneous two-word phrases.
- **Any age:** **any regression** in language or social skills.
- **Also:** poor joint attention, lack of social smile, preference for objects, unusual sensory interests, distress at change.

### Assessment
- **Screening:** **M-CHAT-R/F** at 16–30 months (sensitivity about 91%, specificity about 95% with follow-up). Also SCQ (4+ years), ASSQ (7–16 years), AQ (adults) [1].
- **Diagnosis:**
  - **ADOS-2** (observation; modules by language and age) + **ADI-R** (parent interview) together form the research **"gold standard"** [1].
  - **India:** **INDT-ASD** (INCLEN Diagnostic Tool for ASD) for diagnosis; **ISAA** for severity and disability certification [2].
- **Comprehensive work-up:**
  - Cognition (Leiter-3, WISC); adaptive behaviour (Vineland/VSMS); speech and language; sensory profile and occupational therapy.
  - **Hearing test**; **chromosomal microarray / fragile X**; EEG if seizures or regression; metabolic tests if indicated [1].
- **Comorbidity screen** [1]:
  - ID 30–40%; epilepsy 20–30%; **ADHD 50–70%**; anxiety 40–60%; depression 20–40%.
  - Sleep problems 50–80%; OCD 17–37%; GI problems about 50%; tics 20–35%.

### Management
**1. Early intensive behavioural and developmental intervention (core)** [1]:
- **ABA** (discrete trial training, naturalistic teaching, **pivotal response training**; Lovaas up to 40 h/week).
- **Early Start Denver Model** (12–60 months; 20–25 h/week; Dawson 2010 RCT).
- **TEACCH** (structured teaching, visual supports).
- **Parent-mediated interventions** (PACT; Indian adaptation **PASS**).
- Speech and language therapy; **AAC/PECS**; occupational and sensory therapy.
- Social skills training: Social Stories, PEERS.

**2. Education:** inclusive education with an individualised education plan; RPwD and RTE entitlements.

**3. Pharmacotherapy (for co-occurring symptoms only; no drug treats the core social deficits)** [1]:
| Target | Drug | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Irritability, aggression, self-injury** | **Risperidone** (FDA-approved age 5+; 0.5–3 mg) and **aripiprazole** (FDA-approved age 6+; 2–15 mg) | Strongest: RUPP 2002 and others. Watch weight, prolactin, EPS |
| ADHD symptoms | Methylphenidate (lower response, more side effects than in non-ASD ADHD), atomoxetine, guanfacine/clonidine | Moderate |
| Anxiety | SSRIs (low dose) | Limited |
| Repetitive behaviours | SSRIs | Mixed or negative (citalopram STAART trial negative) |
| **Sleep** | **Melatonin** (0.5–3 mg, up to 6–10 mg; prolonged-release paediatric) + behavioural measures | Good |
| Self-injury | Naltrexone (adjunct), antipsychotics | Limited |

**4. Family:** psychoeducation, parent training, respite, support groups, **disability certification** (ISAA), **National Trust** schemes (Niramaya insurance, guardianship) [2].

**5. Transition and adulthood:** vocational support; independent-living skills; mental health care.

### "Early diagnosis of autism will not make a difference as there is no treatment available": a critique
The statement is **false**:
- **Treatments exist.** They target **developmental trajectories and function**, not "cure". Early intensive behavioural and developmental interventions (ESDM, ABA-based NDBIs, parent-mediated programmes) improve **IQ, language, adaptive behaviour and social engagement**. Effects are moderate, and greatest when started **before age 3** (the neural plasticity window) [1].
  - Dawson 2010 (ESDM RCT, n=48): IQ gain about 17.6 points vs 7 with community care at 2 years.
  - PACT follow-up: reduced symptom severity about 6 years later (Pickles 2016).
- **Early diagnosis** also enables:
  - **genetic diagnosis** (fragile X, recurrence counselling);
  - **identification and treatment of comorbidities** (epilepsy, ADHD, sleep, GI, hearing);
  - **parent education** and reduced stress and blame;
  - access to **educational supports and legal entitlements**;
  - prevention of secondary problems (behaviour, anxiety, school exclusion).
- **Caveats:** effect sizes are modest and heterogeneous, with high risk of bias in many trials (Sandbank 2020 meta-analysis). Not all children respond. Intervention intensity and cost are major barriers. Over-diagnosis risks false positives with screening. **Neurodiversity perspectives** caution against "normalising" interventions.

**Conclusion.** ASD is a heterogeneous, highly heritable neurodevelopmental condition. It needs early recognition (screening at 18–24 months), multidisciplinary assessment, and **early, intensive, family-centred developmental and behavioural intervention**, with education, comorbidity management and lifelong support. Medication treats associated irritability, ADHD, anxiety and sleep, not core autism. The claim that "early diagnosis makes no difference" is wrong: early diagnosis enables proven interventions that change developmental trajectories and family outcomes, even if they do not cure.

## In depth

### Background
- **1943:** Leo Kanner described "autistic disturbances of affective contact" in 11 children.
- **1944:** Hans Asperger described "autistic psychopathy" (his Nazi-era associations have since been documented).
- **1960s:** the "refrigerator mother" theory (Bettelheim) was later discredited.
- **Rutter and others** established autism as a developmental, biological disorder (twin studies, Folstein & Rutter 1977).
- **DSM-III (1980):** infantile autism. **DSM-IV:** PDD categories. **DSM-5 (2013):** a single ASD [1]. **ICD-11 (2022):** 6A02 ASD, with specifiers for intellectual development and functional language.
- **1998:** the Wakefield MMR paper was retracted (2010) for fraud.

### Epidemiology
- **US CDC (ADDM 2020 surveillance):** about **1 in 36** 8-year-olds (2.8%); 2023 data report about 1 in 31. Male:female about 4:1 (girls are under-diagnosed).
- **Global:** about 1 in 100 (WHO).
- **India:** about 1% in some studies (INCLEN multicentre study, 2–9-year-olds: about 1 in 100). NMHS adolescents screened positive at 1.6% [3].
- Rising prevalence reflects broader criteria, awareness and diagnostic substitution, rather than a true epidemic alone.

### Classification and criteria
| Feature | DSM-5-TR | ICD-11 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | Autism spectrum disorder | Autism spectrum disorder (6A02) |
| Core | A (all 3) + B (≥2 of 4) | Social communication + RRBIs (including sensory) |
| Specifiers | With or without intellectual impairment, language impairment; known genetic/medical condition; catatonia; severity levels 1–3 | With or without disorder of intellectual development; degree of functional language impairment (none/mild, impaired, absent); loss of acquired skills |
| Asperger's | Removed (Level 1 without ID or language impairment) [1] | Removed |
| Social (pragmatic) communication disorder | Separate (no RRBIs) | Part of developmental language disorder |

### Mechanism in detail
- **Genetics:** sibling recurrence about 10–20%; twin concordance (MZ >80%).
- **Neurobiology:**
  - Early brain overgrowth (head circumference increase in infancy in a subset).
  - Minicolumn abnormalities; reduced Purkinje cells.
  - Excitation/inhibition imbalance (GABA).
  - Synaptic genes (neuroligin, neurexin, SHANK).
  - mTOR pathway (tuberous sclerosis, PTEN).
- **Cognitive theories:** theory-of-mind deficit (Baron-Cohen); weak central coherence (Frith); executive dysfunction; enhanced perceptual functioning; predictive coding ("priors"); the double empathy problem (Milton).

### Clinical features in detail
- **Social communication:** reduced eye contact and joint attention; atypical prosody; literal language; echolalia; difficulty with peer relationships.
- **RRBIs:** hand flapping, lining up objects, rigid routines, narrow intense interests, sensory seeking or avoidance.
- **Associated features:** uneven cognitive profile (splinter skills); **catatonia** in adolescence (12–17%); **regression** in about 20–30% around 18–24 months.
- **Girls and women:** camouflaging; more socially motivated; internalising problems; later diagnosis.
- **Adults:** often undiagnosed; present with anxiety, depression or social difficulties.

### Assessment in detail
| Tool | Age | Notes [1] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M-CHAT-R/F | 16–30 months | 20 items; follow-up interview for medium risk |
| SCQ | 4+ | Based on ADI-R |
| ADOS-2 | 12 months to adult | 5 modules; social affect + RRB scores |
| ADI-R | Mental age 2+ | 90–150-minute parent interview |
| CARS-2 | 2+ | Rating scale; used in India |
| ISAA (NIMH, India) | 3–22 | 40 items, 6 domains; severity and disability % [2] |
| INDT-ASD | 2–9 | Indian diagnostic tool (DSM-based) [2] |

**ISAA disability bands (2024 guidelines)** [2]:
- ISAA 70–106: mild, 40–59%.
- ISAA 107–153: moderate, 60–79%.
- Above 153: severe.
- **Under 6 years:** certificate is temporary (60–79%), with reassessment at 6–7.

### Differential diagnosis
| Condition | Distinguishing points |
| --- | --- |
| Intellectual disability | Social skills consistent with mental age; no RRBIs out of proportion |
| Language disorder / social (pragmatic) communication disorder | No RRBIs |
| Hearing impairment | Audiology |
| Reactive attachment disorder | Severe neglect history; improves with good care |
| ADHD | Social problems secondary to impulsivity (can co-occur) |
| Selective mutism | Normal communication in some settings |
| OCD | Ego-dystonic obsessions vs ego-syntonic RRBIs [1] |
| Rett syndrome | Girls; regression; hand-wringing; MECP2 |
| Childhood-onset schizophrenia | Hallucinations and delusions after a period of normal development |
| Landau-Kleffner syndrome | Acquired aphasia with epileptiform EEG |

### Management details
- **Intensity matters:** about 20–25 h/week of structured intervention is recommended in early childhood. Parent-delivered models extend the hours affordably [1].
- **Antipsychotics:**
  - Risperidone: 0.25–0.5 mg/day initially. RUPP (2002): response 69% vs 12% on placebo for irritability. Long-term weight gain and prolactin issues.
  - Aripiprazole: start 2 mg. Monitor metabolic parameters.
- **Melatonin:** slow-release melatonin improved total sleep time (Gringras 2017).
- **Investigational or discouraged:**
  - Oxytocin: large RCT (Sikich 2021) negative.
  - Bumetanide: phase 3 negative.
  - Gluten-free/casein-free diets and chelation: no evidence; chelation is dangerous.
  - Stem cells: unproven.
- **Transition planning** at 14–16 years; vocational support; adult services.

### Critical appraisal
- **Intervention evidence:** many studies are small, unblinded and outcome-biased (parent report). Long-term outcome data are limited. Effects vary widely.
- **Cost and access:** intensive therapy is unaffordable for most Indian families. Task-sharing (PASS, lay workers) addresses this.
- **Neurodiversity:** autistic advocates challenge interventions aimed at "normalisation" (e.g., suppressing stimming). Modern approaches emphasise **quality of life, communication and autonomy**.
- **Screening debates:** the USPSTF finds insufficient evidence for universal screening. AAP recommends screening at 18 and 24 months.
- **Pharmacotherapy:** only symptomatic. Antipsychotic overuse and metabolic risk are concerns.

### Indian context
- **RPwD Act 2016:** ASD is a specified disability. ISAA-based certification (2024 guidelines); 1% reservation shared; free education 6–18; UDID [2].
- **National Trust Act 1999:** Niramaya insurance; legal guardianship (local level committees); Disha early-intervention and day-care schemes.
- **RBSK:** screens the "4 Ds" (defects, diseases, deficiencies, developmental delays) at District Early Intervention Centres (DEICs).
- **Resources:** Action for Autism (Delhi); NIMHANS Child and Adolescent Psychiatry; AIIMS autism clinics; the INCLEN study; PASS (Sangath).
- **Barriers:** stigma; late diagnosis (often after 3–4 years of age); urban concentration of therapists; quackery (e.g., stem-cell clinics).

### Recent advances
- **Established:** DSM-5/ICD-11 spectrum model; NDBIs; parent-mediated models; INDT-ASD and ISAA [2].
- **Emerging:**
  - Eye-tracking-based early diagnosis (FDA-cleared EarliPoint, Canvas Dx).
  - Genetic diagnostics: exome sequencing as a first-tier test in some guidelines.
  - Digital and tele-delivered parent coaching.
  - AI-assisted video screening.
- **Investigational:** bumetanide (failed); oxytocin (failed); vasopressin antagonists (balovaptan failed); microbiome-transfer therapy; precision treatments for syndromic forms (e.g., mTOR inhibitors in TSC).
