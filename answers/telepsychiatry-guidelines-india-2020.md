## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Before 2020, telepsychiatry in India was held back by **legal and administrative ambiguity**, clinician scepticism, and uncertain cost-effectiveness [1, 2]. The **COVID-19 lockdown** forced the issue, and two documents followed:
1. **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (TPG), 25 March 2020.**
   - Issued by the **Board of Governors in supersession of the Medical Council of India**, with **NITI Aayog** and MoHFW.
   - Given statutory force as **Regulation 3.8 ("Consultation by Telemedicine") and Appendix 5** of the **Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002**.
   - Applicable **beyond the pandemic**; continued under the **National Medical Commission Act, 2019** [3, 4].
2. **Telepsychiatry Operational Guidelines (TOG), May 2020.**
   - Issued by **NIMHANS, the Indian Psychiatric Society and the Telemedicine Society of India**.
   - A psychiatry-specific operational manual aligned with the TPG and the **MHCA 2017** [4].

### TPG 2020: key provisions [1, 3]
- **Definitions:**
  - **telemedicine** (WHO): delivery of care where distance is a critical factor, using ICT;
  - **telehealth;**
  - **registered medical practitioner (RMP).**
- **Scope:**
  - RMPs may consult patients **anywhere in India**, using **any mode**: video, audio, text, apps, email, chat platforms (WhatsApp), fax.
  - **Exclusions:** hardware/software specifications; remote surgery; research and education; **consultations outside India**.
- **Training:** a **mandatory online course within 3 years** of notification.
- **Four classification axes:**
  1. Mode: video, audio, text.
  2. Timing: synchronous or asynchronous.
  3. Purpose: emergency or non-emergency.
  4. Interaction: RMP–patient, caregiver–RMP, health worker–RMP, RMP–RMP.
- **Seven elements before any teleconsultation:**
  1. Context.
  2. Identification of RMP and patient (**verify identity and age**).
  3. Mode.
  4. **Consent:** **implied** if the patient initiates; **explicit** if a caregiver, health worker or RMP initiates.
  5. Type: **first consult**, or **follow-up**, meaning the same RMP, the same condition and **within 6 months** of an in-person visit.
  6. Patient evaluation.
  7. Patient management.
- **Prescribing:**
  - only after a **diagnosis or provisional diagnosis**; prescribing without one is **professional misconduct**;
  - **drug lists:**
    - **List O:** OTC drugs;
    - **List A:** relatively safe drugs, allowed at the **first consult by video**;
    - **List B:** add-on drugs, **follow-up only**;
    - **Prohibited list:** Schedule X and NDPS drugs.
- **Emergencies:** first aid, counselling and facilitating referral only. The patient **must be advised in-person care**.
- **Ethics:**
  - IMC ethics regulations apply;
  - **IT Act 2000** and **SPDI Rules 2011** apply;
  - the RMP is not liable for a breach caused by technology failure or a third party;
  - **misconduct** includes:
    - insisting on telemedicine when the patient wants an in-person visit;
    - misusing images;
    - prescribing prohibited drugs;
    - **advertising or soliciting**;
  - fees are allowed; records must be kept (logs, messages, prescriptions).
- **Technology platforms** must verify that the RMPs on them are registered.

### TOG 2020: psychiatry-specific provisions [4]
- **Legal framework:**
  - follow **MHCA 2017 and Rules 2018**, RPwD Act 2016, NDPS Act, Drugs & Cosmetics Act, IT Act;
  - **no advertising**; social media and professional boundaries; never share patient data online.
- **Technology standards:**
  - internet of **at least 512 kbps**, router, power backup;
  - a dedicated, private room near the OPD;
  - simple, secure software.
- **Records:** **MHCA Section 25 basic medical records** (Rules 2018, Rule 6(3), Form B). **Explicit consent is needed for any recording**; covert recording is illegal.
- **Procedure before, during and after the consultation:**
  - pre-registration with **risk screening** (violence, self-harm);
  - professional attire and normal hours;
  - plan for disconnection;
  - **know the patient's location** for emergencies and inform local police or ambulance (102/108) in a suicide or homicide attempt;
  - a typical consult lasts 10–20 minutes, and **telepsychotherapy 30–45 minutes**.
- **Caregiver-initiated consultations** (common in India):
  - **with the patient present:** assess **capacity on video (MHCA Sections 4 and 81)**. If capacity is present, take the patient's consent. If absent, take the consent of the nominated representative or family member;
  - **without the patient:** the **first consult must be in person**. A **follow-up** (in-person visit within the past 6 months) may proceed if specified conditions are met;
  - **formal authorisation letter** (Appendix 4).
- **Stop telepsychiatry and refer for in-person care if:**
  - consent is refused;
  - there is risk to self or others;
  - an emergency is present;
  - the patient is **in conflict with the law** (forensic);
  - the patient is a **POCSO survivor or perpetrator** (Section 19 mandatory reporting; follow-up by tele is possible later in the child's best interest);
  - **certificates** are requested (follow MHCA).
- **Health worker–psychiatrist collaborative consults:**
  - DMHP, prisons, beggars' homes, protection homes, NGOs;
  - injectable prescriptions only via such collaboration.
- **Psychiatric drug lists:**
  - **List A** (first consult by video; any mode at follow-up):
    - imipramine, escitalopram, fluoxetine;
    - lithium, carbamazepine, valproate;
    - haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine;
    - trihexyphenidyl;
    - phenobarbitone, phenytoin;
    - **clonazepam, clobazam** (MCI amendment of 11 April 2020);
    - injectable fluphenazine, haloperidol and promethazine via a health worker;
  - **List B** (follow-up only): most other antipsychotics, antidepressants, anti-craving drugs, anti-dementia drugs, atomoxetine, lamotrigine, propranolol, pregabalin and others;
  - **List C (prohibited):**
    - Schedule X drugs;
    - **NDPS drugs: buprenorphine, methadone, benzodiazepines (other than the List A exceptions), zolpidem, tramadol, ketamine.**
    - Lorazepam and zolpidem are not to be prescribed online despite being on the DMHP drug list.
  - Investigations required before a drug (e.g., **lithium: RFT, TFT, electrolytes**) still apply.
  - **Generic names** only.
- **Telepsychotherapy:**
  - **in-person assessment first**;
  - diagnosis before therapy;
  - judge suitability for an online alliance;
  - screen for emergencies each session;
  - decide whether family may be present;
  - document per MHCA Section 25;
  - informed consent (Appendix 3).
- **Appendices:** new and follow-up proformas, teletherapy documentation, authorisation letter, prescription format.

### Critical evaluation
**Strengths [2]:**
1. **Legal clarity:** for the first time a binding framework, which removed the main barrier to adoption.
2. **Timely:** it enabled continuity of care during lockdown.
3. **Inclusive and flexible:** any mode, any part of India, caregiver and health-worker pathways suited to Indian family-centred care.
4. **Protects patients:**
   - identity verification;
   - consent;
   - right to demand in-person care;
   - advertising ban;
   - safeguards on controlled drugs.
5. **Psychiatry-specific operationalisation** (TOG): capacity assessment, nominated representatives, POCSO and forensic exclusions, documentation templates.
6. **Equity and reach:** supports MHCA Section 18 access duties with public health spending at about 1% of GDP; telepsychiatry is cost-effective [2].
7. **Basis for national programmes:** e-Sanjeevani, **Tele-MANAS (2022)**, DMHP supervision.

**Limitations [2, general knowledge]:**
1. **Jurisdiction:** an RMP registered in one state treats patients in another, and **the forum for grievances is unclear** [2]. State health regulations vary.
2. **Prescribing restrictions:**
   - **benzodiazepine** limits (except clonazepam and clobazam) complicate alcohol withdrawal and anxiety care [2];
   - **buprenorphine OST** cannot be continued remotely;
   - the requirement for video for List A drugs is impractical with **poor connectivity** [2].
3. **Privacy:** consumer platforms (WhatsApp, Zoom) have unclear data handling. Digital record-keeping is hard; aggregators could leak data [2]. India's comprehensive data law (DPDP Act 2023) came only later [general knowledge].
4. **Exclusions:** education, research and training are outside the TPG [2]. There is no guidance on **virtual physical examination** or on wearables and apps [2].
5. **Clinical gaps:**
   - **no standards for assessing capacity remotely** (a video judgement under MHCA is debatable);
   - limited guidance for **children and adolescents** (consent and assent; age verification);
   - little on **suicide risk** management protocols beyond "refer";
   - asynchronous psychotherapy (text) is permitted under the TPG but its quality is uncertain.
6. **Digital divide:** rural, elderly, poor, female and low-literacy populations have less smartphone access, so the guidelines may **widen inequity**.
7. **Quality assurance:** no accreditation of platforms; no outcome monitoring or audit; the mandatory course was slow to be rolled out.
8. **Text and audio first consults** are allowed for some drugs, raising diagnostic accuracy and misuse concerns. TOG is narrower (video preferred).
9. **Non-psychiatrist mental health professionals** (psychologists, counsellors) are not covered by the TPG, which is for RMPs only. Their tele-practice falls under other regulators (RCI) [general knowledge].
10. **Static drug lists** need regular updating. Newer drugs and long-acting injectables are inconsistently placed.

**Suggestions:**
- clarify interstate jurisdiction and grievance redress (online regulatory boards) [2];
- harmonise with the **DPDP Act** and **ABDM** (Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission) health records;
- certify platforms;
- **allow buprenorphine continuation** under DTC supervision;
- add child and adolescent, capacity, and suicide protocols;
- cover training and research;
- integrate with DMHP and Tele-MANAS;
- evaluate outcomes and user perspectives [2].

**Conclusion.** The 2020 TPG and TOG were a **landmark**: they legalised and structured telepsychiatry, protected patients and practitioners, and laid the groundwork for Tele-MANAS. They are a **necessary first step, not a substitute for human resources** [2]. Their weaknesses (jurisdiction, data protection, prescribing rigidity, the digital divide, and thin guidance on capacity, minors and emergencies) need revision as tele-mental health moves from pandemic stopgap to a permanent part of care.

## In depth

### Background
- **History** [5]:
  - telepsychiatry was first used in **1956 (University of Nebraska)**;
  - group psychotherapy by video in 1961;
  - the term "telepsychiatry" was coined by **Dwyer (1973)**;
  - it expanded in the 1990s with better technology.
- **India** [5]:
  - **2003** recommended guidelines and standards for telemedicine (Department of IT), not binding;
  - a proposed **National e-Health Authority (NeHA)**;
  - models before 2020: SCARF STEP (Pudukkottai), PGIMER "Psychiatrist on Web", Ganiyari (Jan Swasthya Sahyog), NIMHANS hub-and-spoke, Maharashtra telemedicine project (2007, asynchronous).

### Epidemiology / the need
- **NMHS:** treatment gap 75–93% [2]; about 0.3 psychiatrists per 100,000; services concentrated in cities.
- **Teleconsultation volumes rose steeply after March 2020.** e-Sanjeevani recorded millions of consultations in its first year [general knowledge].

### Classification of telepsychiatry modes [5]
- **Synchronous:** video, telephone, chat; immediate.
- **Asynchronous (store-and-forward):** email, recordings.
- **Settings:** direct-to-patient (home); clinic-to-clinic; collaborative (health worker–specialist); hub-and-spoke.

### Mechanism: how guidelines change practice
- They remove **legal uncertainty**, the most-cited barrier in Indian surveys [2, 5]. They standardise **consent, records and prescribing**, which reduces liability fear. They define **when not** to use telepsychiatry, which protects quality.

### Clinical features: scenarios in the TOG
| Scenario | Rule [4] |
| --- | --- |
| Patient-initiated, first consult | Video preferred; List A by video; provisional diagnosis needed |
| Patient-initiated, follow-up (within 6 months) | Any mode; Lists A and B |
| Family-initiated with patient | Capacity on video; consent from patient or NR |
| Family-initiated without patient | First consult in person; follow-up allowed under conditions |
| Health worker–psychiatrist | Collaborative; injectables; institutions (prisons, homes) |
| Emergency | First aid, counselling, referral; local emergency services |
| Forensic / POCSO / certificates | In person |

### Assessment: capacity on video
- MHCA Section 81 (capacity for admission) and Section 4 (capacity to make treatment decisions): understand information, appreciate consequences, communicate a decision.
- TOG allows this assessment **only by video** [4].

### Differential: TPG vs TOG
| Feature | TPG 2020 | TOG 2020 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issued by | BoG-MCI / MoHFW / NITI Aayog | NIMHANS, IPS, TSI |
| Status | Statutory (IMC regulations, Appendix 5) | Professional operational guidance |
| Audience | All RMPs | Psychiatrists |
| Modes | Video, audio, text | Focus on video |
| Mental health law | Not specific | MHCA integrated (capacity, NR, records) |
| Drug lists | Generic (O, A, B, prohibited) | Psychotropic-specific (O, A, B, C) |
| Psychotherapy | Counselling mentioned | Telepsychotherapy principles |

### Management: setting up a telepsychiatry service (TOG checklist) [4]
1. Register; complete the training course.
2. Choose secure software; arrange bandwidth and backup.
3. Set up a dedicated room near the OPD.
4. Prepare proformas, consent forms and the prescription template.
5. Define emergency protocols (patient location, local contacts).
6. Make record-keeping compliant with MHCA Section 25.
7. Set the fee structure and receipts.

### Critical appraisal (beyond the text)
- The guidelines were **drafted rapidly** for a crisis, with **little stakeholder consultation** (patients, psychologists).
- **Evidence base:** telepsychiatry outcomes are broadly equivalent to in-person care for many disorders (international RCTs), but Indian evaluations are mostly observational [5].
- **Post-2020 developments** (check current status):
  - the NMC's 2023 professional conduct regulations (held in abeyance) [general knowledge];
  - DPDP Act 2023;
  - ABDM;
  - Tele-MANAS.

### Indian context
- **MHCA 2017:** Section 18 (right to access), Section 25 (records), Sections 4 and 81 (capacity), nominated representatives, advance directives.
- **Programmes:** Tele-MANAS; e-Sanjeevani (OPD and AB-HWC); DMHP tele-supervision; prison telepsychiatry (Karnataka).
- **NIMHANS Digital Academy**; the Primary Care Psychiatry Programme's tele-on-consultation training.

### Recent advances
- **AI-supported triage and documentation** (no guideline coverage yet).
- **Hybrid care models.**
- **Digital therapeutics** (app-based CBT) need regulatory pathways (CDSCO software as a medical device) [general knowledge].
