## Exam answer

**Summary.**
- A **20-year-old woman** from semi-urban Bengaluru.
- **Delayed milestones**, below-average schooling, **cleared 10th standard on the third attempt**, then dropped out.
- Functions at home: household chores, buying groceries.
- For **1 year**: **repeated hand-washing**, **hours spent bathing** and cleaning after toileting.
- A previous psychiatrist's tablets caused **marked irritability and insomnia**, so the parents stopped them.

### Diagnosis
1. **Intellectual disability, probably mild**, to be confirmed by IQ and adaptive testing:
   - developmental delay, persistent academic difficulty, and adaptive skills enough for simple household and shopping tasks;
   - ICD-10 **mild ID = IQ 50–69** [1].
2. **Comorbid obsessive–compulsive disorder** (contamination and washing compulsions), or obsessional behaviour as part of the ID picture.
   - In ID, psychiatric illness "presents itself more diffusely and atypically" [1].
   - Beware **diagnostic overshadowing**: attributing the symptoms to ID itself rather than a comorbid disorder [2].
3. **Adverse reaction to the earlier drug:** irritability and insomnia suggest **activation by an SSRI** (SSRIs "may initially exacerbate symptoms" [2]) or **hypomania**. SSRIs in LD carry a "risk of **precipitation of hypomania**" [2]. Find out which drug and dose were used.

**Differentials to exclude:**
- ASD with repetitive behaviours (assess social communication);
- depression presenting atypically;
- psychosis (washing driven by delusions);
- a medical cause, such as **hypothyroidism** (common in ID) [1, 2].

### Assessment [1]
- **History from parents and the patient**, separately if needed:
  - onset and timeline of compulsions;
  - obsessions she can describe;
  - distress and resistance;
  - time taken and interference with function;
  - triggers; family accommodation.
- **Timeline method; behavioural observation** over a period; **functional (ABC) analysis** [1].
- **Developmental history:** milestones, school records, prenatal and perinatal history; family history (ID, OCD).
- **Physical examination:** dysmorphism (minor physical anomalies), neurological signs, **epilepsy**, sensory impairment [1].
- **Psychometry** [1, 3]:
  - **IQ:** BKT, MISIC or WISC;
  - **adaptive behaviour:** **VSMS** (social quotient). When IQ and SQ disagree, **SQ decides severity**;
  - disability percentage follows VSMS bands.
- **Investigations** as indicated: thyroid function, and genetic or metabolic testing if a syndrome is suspected [1, 2].
- **Capacity and consent:** understanding may be limited. Improve it with appropriate verbal and written communication, and **involve carers** [2].

### Management
**1. Principles in ID [1, 2]:**
- Where a mental illness can be diagnosed, drug treatment "should, in general, be **similar to that in the population at large**" [2].
- **Start low, go slow.** Allow an **adequate trial**. Change **one drug at a time**. Monitor outcomes at home [1, 2].
- People with LD may be **more sensitive to adverse effects** [2].

**2. Psychological and behavioural treatment:**
- **CBT with ERP** is first-line for OCD in young people [4]. Adapt it to her cognitive level with simple language, concrete steps and **caregiver involvement**.
- **Behavioural management** (ID CPG) [1]:
  - restructure the environment;
  - **differential reinforcement** of adaptive behaviour;
  - stop inadvertent reinforcement, such as family accommodation of washing.

**3. Pharmacotherapy:**
- **SSRIs are first line for OCD** (Box 3.9) [2]. The ID CPG lists **SSRIs for depression, obsessionality and anxiety** in ID [1]. There is "**some good evidence for fluoxetine in OCD in LD/autism**", although drop-out is high [2].
- **Given her previous reaction** [1, 2]:
  - **lower starting dose**, slower titration;
  - for example **fluoxetine 5–10 mg/day** or **sertraline 25–50 mg/day** initially (effective dose 50–100 mg) [1];
  - review closely for **irritability, insomnia and hypomania**.
- **If one SSRI is not tolerated or does not work, try an alternative SSRI.** Clomipramine is next, after at least one SSRI. Low-dose **aripiprazole or risperidone** augmentation for partial response [2].
- **OCD response is slower** (10–12 weeks). Continue for **at least 1 year** after response [2].
- **Insomnia:** sleep hygiene; melatonin is listed for sleep disturbance in ID [1].
- **Epilepsy:** caution with drugs that lower the seizure threshold (e.g. bupropion) [2].

**4. Family and rehabilitation [1]:**
- **Psychoeducation:** OCD is treatable and separate from ID.
- **Address caregiver burden.**
- **Vocational and life-skills training** (functional approach).
- **Disability certification** for benefits.

**5. Follow-up:** symptom diaries; repeat reviews for adverse effects; adherence support.

**Conclusion.** This young woman probably has **mild intellectual disability with comorbid OCD**. Her earlier medicine (probably an SSRI) caused activation or hypomanic-type adverse effects. Steps:
1. Confirm ID with IQ and VSMS testing, and screen for medical and syndromic causes.
2. Offer **adapted CBT/ERP** with behavioural family work.
3. Re-introduce an **SSRI at a low dose with slow titration** and close monitoring for activation or hypomania. Switch SSRIs or move to clomipramine or augmentation if needed.

## In depth

### Diagnostic pitfalls in ID [1, 2]
| Pitfall | Example |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic overshadowing | Compulsions dismissed as "part of ID" |
| Atypical presentation | Depression as self-injury; persecutory ideas as complaints of being "picked on" |
| Misreading normal behaviour | Self-talk mistaken for psychosis |
| Care-environment effects | Behaviour reflects problems in the setting, not illness |

### Severity bands [1, 3]
- **ICD-10 by IQ:** mild 50–69; moderate 35–49; severe 20–34; profound below 20.
- **Disability % by VSMS:** 0–20 profound (100%); 21–35 severe (90%); 36–54 moderate (75%); 55–69 mild (50%); 70–84 borderline (25%).
