## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 6-year-old boy has **difficulties in studies and hyperactivity**, with **delayed motor and speech milestones**. **A maternal cousin has intellectual disability.** His anxious parents want to know whether he has the same problem.

### Provisional diagnosis and reasoning
- **Probable intellectual developmental disorder (IDD)**, severity to be determined, with **ADHD-like hyperactivity**.
  - Global delay (motor and speech) with academic difficulty fits a disorder of **global cognitive development** [1].
  - The core feature is **IQ below 70 *and* deficits in adaptive functioning** (conceptual, social, practical), with **developmental onset** [1].
- **Aetiological hypothesis:** an affected **male** with an affected relative **on the mother's side** suggests **X-linked inheritance**. The leading candidate is **Fragile X syndrome (FXS)**: the **commonest inherited cause of ID** and the commonest single-gene cause of ASD [1; general knowledge].
  - FXS phenotype in boys:
    - **ADHD-like hyperactivity**, speech delay;
    - social anxiety, gaze aversion;
    - hand flapping and hand biting;
    - perseverative speech;
    - long face, large prominent ears, joint hyperlaxity, flat feet;
    - **macro-orchidism** (post-pubertal).
- Other differentials:
  - other X-linked ID genes (e.g., *ARX*, *MECP2* duplication);
  - **chromosomal microdeletions or duplications** (e.g., 22q11.2);
  - **ADHD with specific learning disorder and developmental coordination disorder** (normal IQ);
  - **ASD** (with or without ID);
  - **hypothyroidism**; hearing loss; **lead poisoning**; epilepsy (absence seizures);
  - environmental deprivation;
  - **perinatal causes** (HIE, prematurity) [1].

### (a) Assessment
**1. History:**
- **Developmental milestones in all domains** (gross and fine motor, language receptive and expressive, social, cognitive), compared against norms. Examples [1]:
  - sitting without support by 7 months;
  - walking at 12–15 months;
  - first words at 12–15 months;
  - social smile at 2–4 months;
  - object permanence at about 1 year.
- **Regression?** If skills were lost, consider Rett syndrome, metabolic or neurodegenerative disorders, Landau–Kleffner syndrome or epilepsy.
- **Hyperactivity:** onset; whether it occurs in more than one setting; whether it is appropriate for mental age (overactivity is common in ID itself).
- **Academic:** school type, grade, teacher reports.
- **Adaptive functioning:** self-care (feeding, dressing, toileting), communication, social skills, safety awareness.
- **Behaviour:** autistic features (social reciprocity, repetitive behaviours, sensory issues), aggression, self-injury, sleep.
- **Prenatal and perinatal history** [1]:
  - maternal infections (**STORCH**), alcohol and drugs, nutrition, iodine, teratogens, maternal age;
  - **birth asphyxia**, prematurity, low birth weight, neonatal jaundice, sepsis, hypoglycaemia.
- **Postnatal history:** CNS infections, head injury, seizures, **lead exposure**, malnutrition, under-stimulation [1].
- **Family history:**
  - **three-generation pedigree**;
  - which maternal relative (cousin through the mother's sister or brother?);
  - other affected males;
  - **maternal history of premature ovarian insufficiency** (FXPOI) or a **maternal grandfather with late-onset tremor or ataxia** (FXTAS), both of which point to premutation carriers;
  - consanguinity (autosomal recessive conditions);
  - learning difficulties; psychiatric illness.
- **Psychosocial:** parents' concerns and coping; siblings; finances.

**2. Examination:**
- **Growth:** height, weight, **head circumference** (macro- or microcephaly).
- **Dysmorphology:** facial features, ears, palate, hands, joint laxity, **testicular volume** (prepubertal: usually normal).
- **Neurocutaneous markers:** café-au-lait spots, ash-leaf macules.
- **Neurological examination:** tone, reflexes, coordination, gait.
- **Vision and hearing.**
- Cardiac (mitral valve prolapse in FXS).

**3. Psychological assessment:**
- **IQ:** Binet-Kamat Test, MISIC, WISC, Seguin Form Board; Developmental Screening Test (DST) for younger or lower-functioning children.
- **Adaptive behaviour:** **Vineland Social Maturity Scale (VSMS)**, which is **mandated for disability certification in India (2024)** (see `assessment-of-intellectual-disability`).
- **ADHD rating:** Conners or Vanderbilt (parent and teacher), interpreted against *mental age*.
- **ASD screening:** INCLEN-ASD, ISAA, M-CHAT (younger children), ADOS if available.
- **Speech and language assessment; occupational therapy assessment.**

**4. Investigations:**
- **First-tier genetic testing:**
  - **FMR1 CGG repeat analysis** (PCR with Southern blot):
    - normal: below 45 repeats;
    - intermediate: 45–54;
    - **premutation: 55–200**;
    - **full mutation: above 200** (with hypermethylation and gene silencing);
  - **chromosomal microarray** (yield about 15–20%); karyotype if CMA is unavailable [general knowledge].
- **Thyroid function**; blood lead level (if pica or exposure); CBC and ferritin.
- **Metabolic screen** if there is regression, consanguinity, organomegaly or episodic decompensation:
  - aminoacidopathies (PKU, homocystinuria);
  - MPS;
  - urea cycle disorders;
  - organic acidurias;
  - mitochondrial disorders (MELAS) [1].
- **EEG** if seizures or staring spells; **MRI brain** if there are neurological signs, microcephaly or regression.
- **Exome sequencing** if first-tier tests are negative.

### (b) Severity and expected attainments [1]
| Degree | IQ | Adult attainment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild | 50–70 | Literacy, independent self-help, good speech, semi-skilled work |
| Moderate | 35–50 | Limited literacy, self-help with support, domestic-level speech, unskilled work |
| Severe | 20–35 | Assisted self-help, few words, assisted household chores |
| Profound | below 20 | Dependent |

Mild ID affects about **1.5–2%** of the population; moderate to profound about **0.5%** [1].

### (c) Management
**1. Disclosing the diagnosis and counselling (the parents' question):**
- Answer honestly once assessment is complete. Use a **respectful, optimistic, humanistic** approach [1].
- Explain that development **improves with stimulation, training and intervention** [1].
- Address guilt and blame (especially with X-linked inheritance, where mothers may feel blamed).

**2. Genetic counselling (if FXS is confirmed):**
- The **mother is an obligate carrier** (premutation or full mutation).
- Each son has a **50% risk** of inheriting the X. Premutations **expand** to full mutations when transmitted through the mother. Daughters with a full mutation are variably affected.
- **Cascade testing** of maternal relatives (aunts, cousins).
- **Prenatal diagnosis** (CVS, amniocentesis) for future pregnancies.
- Counsel about **FXPOI** (mother: about 20% risk of early menopause) and **FXTAS** (maternal grandfather) [general knowledge].

**3. Educational and developmental interventions:**
- **Early intervention:** special education or inclusive education with an IEP;
- **speech and language therapy; occupational therapy** (sensory integration, fine motor); physiotherapy;
- **RTE Act** and the **RPwD Act 2016**: inclusive education, reservation, exam concessions;
- NIEPID resources [general knowledge].

**4. Behavioural management:**
- **parent training** (behaviour modification: reinforcement, structure, visual schedules);
- managing hyperactivity, attention and anxiety.

**5. Pharmacological treatment (targeted):**
- **ADHD symptoms:** **methylphenidate** (lower response and more side effects in ID; start low, titrate). Alternatives: atomoxetine, clonidine or guanfacine;
- **anxiety:** SSRIs (sertraline in FXS);
- **aggression or irritability:** risperidone or aripiprazole;
- **sleep:** melatonin;
- **seizures:** anticonvulsants.

**6. Medical follow-up:**
- hearing, vision, otitis media (common in FXS), cardiac review, seizures, growth;
- dental care; vaccination.

**7. Social and legal support:**
- **disability certificate** (RPwD Act 2016, 2024 guidelines; VSMS-based percentage);
- **UDID card**;
- schemes such as **Niramaya health insurance** and **guardianship under the National Trust Act 1999**;
- parent support groups; respite;
- financial planning.

**8. Family support:** address stress (emotional, social, caregiving, financial), stigma, sibling needs [1].

**9. Review:** reassess every 1–2 years; transition planning; vocational training in adolescence.

## In depth

### Background
- **Nomenclature** [1]:
  - "mental retardation" → **intellectual disability (DSM-5)** → **disorders of intellectual development (ICD-11 6A00)**;
  - IDD is best understood from a **biopsychosocial perspective**.
- **FXS** was identified by Lubs (1969; fragile site on Xq27.3). The **FMR1** gene and CGG repeat expansion were described by Verkerk (1991) [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology
- **ID:** about 1–3% of the population; mild ID about 85% of cases [1].
- **FXS:**
  - about 1 in 4,000–7,000 males and 1 in 8,000 females;
  - **premutation carriers** about 1 in 150–250 females;
  - accounts for about **2–3% of ID in males** [general knowledge].
- **India:** NIMHANS and Indian genetics centres report FXS in about 2–5% of males with ID [Guessing; check].

### Classification and criteria
- **ICD-11 6A00** (disorders of intellectual development):
  - mild, moderate, severe, profound (based on standardised tests, or behavioural indicators when tests are unavailable);
  - provisional diagnosis under 4 years.
- **DSM-5-TR:** severity by **adaptive functioning** (conceptual, social, practical), not IQ alone; **global developmental delay** under 5 years.

### Mechanism (fragile X)
- **CGG expansion above 200** leads to promoter **hypermethylation**, **FMR1 silencing** and **absence of FMRP**.
- FMRP normally **represses translation** of synaptic mRNAs. Its loss causes:
  - excess **mGluR5-dependent protein synthesis**;
  - exaggerated long-term depression;
  - **immature, long, thin dendritic spines** (the "mGluR theory").
- **Premutation (55–200)** causes **RNA toxicity** (not FMRP loss), leading to **FXTAS** (tremor, ataxia, cognitive decline in older men) and **FXPOI**.
- **Anticipation through maternal transmission:** repeat instability increases with each generation. AGG interruptions stabilise the repeat.

### Clinical features in detail (FXS)
| Domain | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Cognitive | Mild–moderate ID (males); deficits in working memory, attention, visuospatial skills; relative strength in vocabulary |
| Behavioural | **ADHD (about 80%)**, **social anxiety, gaze aversion**, hyperarousal, tactile defensiveness, hand flapping and biting; **ASD in about 30–50%** |
| Speech | Delayed; perseverative, cluttered, echolalia |
| Physical | Long narrow face, prominent jaw, large ears, high-arched palate, hyperextensible joints, flat feet, soft skin; macro-orchidism after puberty; mitral valve prolapse; strabismus |
| Neurological | Seizures in about 15% |
| Females (full mutation) | Milder: learning difficulty, anxiety, shyness; about 50% with ID |

### Assessment in detail
- **Interpretation of IQ in a 6-year-old:** scores are less stable in young children, so reassess. Interpret hyperactivity relative to **mental age**.
- **Indian norms:** VSMS (Malin); BKT; DST (Bharat Raj).
- **Genetic test choice:**
  - FMR1 testing is a **targeted** first-line test because the family history suggests it;
  - CMA does not detect repeat expansions, so **both** are needed.

### Differential diagnosis
| Condition | Clue |
| --- | --- |
| Fragile X | X-linked pedigree, physical and behavioural phenotype |
| Down syndrome | Characteristic facies; usually diagnosed at birth |
| Klinefelter syndrome (XXY) | Tall, small testes, language and learning delay; usually not X-linked familial |
| Rett (MECP2) | Girls; regression; hand-wringing |
| Duchenne muscular dystrophy | X-linked; motor delay with cognitive impairment; raised CK |
| Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder | Maternal alcohol; facial features; growth deficiency |
| ADHD + SLD + DCD | Normal IQ |
| Hypothyroidism / lead poisoning | Biochemistry |

### Management details: targeted treatments in fragile X
- **mGluR5 antagonists** (mavoglurant, basimglurant) **failed** in phase 2/3 RCTs, despite strong animal data [general knowledge].
- **Minocycline** (MMP-9 inhibition) showed modest benefit in one RCT [general knowledge].
- **Zatolmilast** (PDE4D inhibitor; BPN14770) phase 3 trials are ongoing [general knowledge; check].
- **Cannabidiol gel (Zygel)** phase 3 (CONNECT-FX) was negative overall, positive in a methylation subgroup [general knowledge].

### Critical appraisal
- **IQ-based severity** is culturally loaded. Adaptive functioning is a better guide [1].
- **Genetic testing access in India** is limited by cost (CMA about ₹15,000–25,000) [Guessing], though government genetic centres and ICMR programmes exist.
- **Translational disappointment** in FXS (mGluR5 failures) shows the gap between mouse models and humans.

### Indian context
- **Rights and entitlements:**
  - **RPwD Act 2016:** ID is a benchmark disability at 40% or more;
  - **2024 guidelines:** VSMS-based certification;
  - **National Trust Act 1999:** legal guardianship;
  - **Samagra Shiksha** inclusive education.
- **Services:**
  - **NIEPID** (Secunderabad);
  - **District Early Intervention Centres (DEIC)** under **RBSK (Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram)** for developmental delay screening (birth to 18 years) [general knowledge];
  - NIMHANS child psychiatry and **Girimaji's** work on IDD services [1].
- **Prevention:**
  - iodine supplementation;
  - folic acid;
  - newborn screening for congenital hypothyroidism (patchy);
  - antenatal care;
  - institutional delivery;
  - **consanguinity counselling** (common in some communities) [1].

### Recent advances
- **Exome and genome sequencing** as first-tier tests (diagnostic yield 30–40%) [general knowledge].
- **Newborn screening for FXS** (pilot programmes).
- **Gene-reactivation strategies** (CRISPR demethylation of FMR1) in research.
