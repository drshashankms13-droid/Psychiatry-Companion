## Exam answer

### Introduction

India is undergoing a demographic transition, with a growing proportion of older adults. Ageing is associated with increased risks of dementia, depression, delirium, substance-related problems, late-life psychosis and suicide. Geriatric psychiatric care requires integration of mental, physical, social and functional care.

### Systemic challenges

1.  **Demographic burden:** the number of older adults is increasing,
    creating greater demand for long-term and community-based care.
2.  **Workforce shortage:** there are insufficient geriatric
    psychiatrists, psychologists, nurses and trained caregivers.
3.  **Diagnostic complexity:** multimorbidity, polypharmacy, sensory
    impairment and cognitive decline complicate assessment.
4.  **Treatment gap and stigma:** mental illness may be viewed as a
    normal part of ageing, delaying help-seeking.
5.  **Fragmented services:** mental health, primary care, neurology and
    geriatric medicine often function separately.
6.  **Geographical and financial barriers:** specialist services are
    concentrated in urban centres; travel and out-of-pocket costs are
    substantial.
7.  **Caregiver burden:** families may experience psychological,
    financial and physical strain.
8.  **Inadequate long-term care:** day-care, respite, rehabilitation and
    supported living services are limited.
9.  **Social determinants:** loneliness, poverty, elder abuse,
    bereavement and loss of independence affect mental health.

### Innovative service delivery models

-   **Integration with primary care:** train primary-care teams to
    detect and manage common mental disorders, dementia and substance
    use, with clear referral pathways.
-   **Task-sharing:** train community health workers and nurses to
    provide screening, psychoeducation, adherence support and caregiver
    education under supervision.
-   **Telepsychiatry:** use teleconsultation for specialist access,
    follow-up and caregiver support; Tele-MANAS can complement crisis
    and referral pathways.
-   **Memory clinics:** multidisciplinary assessment of cognition,
    function, behaviour, caregiver needs and reversible contributors.
-   **Home-based care:** outreach, home visits and community
    rehabilitation for people with mobility or cognitive limitations.
-   **Caregiver interventions:** psychoeducation, behavioural management
    skills, support groups and respite options.
-   **Day-care and community centres:** structured activities, social
    engagement, rehabilitation and caregiver relief.
-   **Hospital-based age-friendly services:** delirium prevention,
    medication review, sensory aids, liaison psychiatry and discharge
    planning.
-   **Public–private and NGO partnerships:** expand access to
    affordable services and community support.

### Indian policy and programme context

Relevant initiatives include the National Mental Health Programme and District Mental Health Programme, National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly (NPHCE), National Health Mission, Mental Healthcare Act 2017, Tele-MANAS and the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007. Their implementation and coordination need strengthening.

### Proposed stepped-care model

1.  Community awareness and early identification.
2.  Primary-care screening and basic management.
3.  District-level specialist assessment and treatment.
4.  Tertiary multidisciplinary care for complex cases.
5.  Community rehabilitation, home care and caregiver support.
6.  Ongoing monitoring and crisis pathways across levels.

### Conclusion

Geriatric psychiatric care in India requires a shift from institution-centred specialist care to integrated, age-friendly and community-based services. Primary-care integration, task-sharing, telehealth, memory clinics and caregiver support can improve access, provided they are adequately funded, supervised and evaluated.
