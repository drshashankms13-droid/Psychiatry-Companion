DSM-5-TR criteria, epidemiology, functional models and psychotherapy trials are **general knowledge**, marked as such. Verify them before relying on this answer.

## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI)** is the deliberate, self-inflicted destruction of body tissue **without suicidal intent**, for purposes not socially sanctioned. Examples are cutting, burning, scratching, hitting, head-banging, skin-picking to injury and interfering with wound healing [general knowledge].

Terminology differs:
- **Self-harm (UK/NICE)** is broader: any intentional self-poisoning or self-injury **irrespective of motive or intent** [general knowledge].
- The key difference between **NSSI and an attempt to die by suicide is intent to end life** [1].
  - Example: a person takes "plenty of tablets because of being upset and impulsive". They "did not want to kill himself but wanted to de-escalate a stressful situation". This is NSSI [1].
  - **Both can occur in the same individual**, and NSSI can **accidentally cause death** "by ignorance, accident, delay in treatment, or miscalculation" [1].

### Classification and diagnosis [general knowledge]
- **DSM-5-TR:**
  - "**Non-suicidal self-injury**" is a *condition for further study* (Section III);
  - there is also a diagnostic code under "other conditions that may be a focus of clinical attention": **personal history of NSSI**.
- **Proposed DSM criteria:**
  - **A.** on **5 or more days in the past year**, intentional self-inflicted damage to the body surface, expecting only minor or moderate physical harm, without suicidal intent;
  - **B.** done with the expectation of: **relief from a negative feeling or cognitive state**; **resolving an interpersonal difficulty**; or **inducing a positive feeling**;
  - **C.** associated with at least one of: interpersonal difficulty or negative feelings immediately before the act; **preoccupation** that is difficult to control; frequent urges;
  - **D.** not socially sanctioned (not tattoos or piercing, nor religious or cultural rituals), and not limited to scab-picking or nail-biting;
  - **E.** clinically significant distress or impairment;
  - **F.** not exclusively during psychosis, delirium, intoxication or withdrawal. In neurodevelopmental disorders, not part of repetitive stereotypies. Not better explained by another disorder.
- **ICD-11:** no separate disorder. Recorded with **external-cause codes** (intentional self-harm, X60–X84 equivalents). NSSI is a **symptom of borderline pattern** personality disorder.
- **Differentiate from:**
  - stereotypic self-injurious behaviour in **ID/ASD** and genetic syndromes (Lesch–Nyhan: lip and finger biting; Smith–Magenis: nail-pulling, self-hugging);
  - trichotillomania and excoriation disorder (body-focused repetitive behaviours);
  - psychotic self-mutilation (command hallucinations, genital or eye enucleation);
  - factitious disorder.

### Epidemiology [general knowledge]
- **Adolescents:**
  - lifetime prevalence about **17–18%** in community samples worldwide;
  - onset typically **12–14 years**;
  - peaks mid-adolescence and declines by early adulthood.
- **Adults:** about 5.5%; university students about 13%.
- **Gender:** girls more than boys in clinical samples (cutting); boys hit or burn.
- **India:** school and college studies report about 20–30% lifetime NSSI in some urban samples. Data are heterogeneous.
- **Clinical populations:** BPD (up to 70–80%), depression, eating disorders, PTSD (young people with PTSD have "nearly 50%" self-harm [2]), substance use, autism.

### Aetiology and functions
**Biopsychosocial vulnerability [general knowledge]:**
- **childhood adversity** (abuse, neglect, bullying, family conflict, invalidating environment);
- **emotion dysregulation** (Linehan's biosocial model);
- impulsivity;
- low distress tolerance;
- self-criticism and shame;
- **social contagion** (peers, social media).

**Neurobiology:**
- **endogenous opioid** dysregulation (analgesia during the act; "addictive" quality);
- reduced pain sensitivity;
- serotonergic and HPA-axis dysfunction.

**Four-function model (Nock and Prinstein) [general knowledge]:**
| | Intrapersonal (automatic) | Interpersonal (social) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Negative reinforcement** | **Relief of unbearable emotion**, tension or dissociation (most common) | Escape from demands or conflict |
| **Positive reinforcement** | Feeling "something"; self-punishment | Eliciting care or communicating distress |

**Integrated / experiential-avoidance models:** NSSI is a learned, reinforced coping strategy. Relief is short-lived, then guilt and shame follow, and the cycle repeats.

### Why NSSI matters: link with suicide
- NSSI is one of the **strongest predictors of future suicide attempts**, stronger than previous attempts in some adolescent studies [general knowledge].
- **Joiner's interpersonal theory:** repeated self-injury builds **acquired capability for suicide** through habituation to pain and fear of death [3].
- **Recurrence after hospital-treated self-harm** is high, and **suicide risk in the year after self-harm is about 50–100 times the general population** [general knowledge].

### Assessment
1. **Engage non-judgementally.** Avoid dismissive labels ("attention-seeking", "manipulative"). They increase shame and disengagement.
2. **Medical:** wound severity, infection, tetanus. With overdose, **always** check paracetamol and salicylate levels.
3. **Characterise the NSSI:**
   - methods, frequency, onset;
   - triggers and **functions** (functional analysis: antecedents, behaviour, consequences);
   - concealment;
   - escalation;
   - urges and resistance.
4. **Suicide risk:**
   - **intent at each act**, past attempts, current ideation, hopelessness;
   - **C-SSRS**; Suicide Intent Scale; Beck Hopelessness Scale [3].
   - Risk scales **must not** be used to predict risk or allocate care. "Low positive predictive value" [3].
5. **Psychiatric comorbidity:** depression; BPD traits; PTSD and trauma; eating disorder; substance use; ADHD; ASD; psychosis.
6. **Psychosocial:**
   - family conflict; abuse (consider **POCSO** reporting for minors);
   - bullying and cyberbullying; academic stress;
   - sexuality and gender-identity issues; social media exposure;
   - protective factors.
7. **Interview adolescents alone** as well as with parents [4].
8. **Tools [general knowledge]:** Deliberate Self-Harm Inventory (DSHI); Inventory of Statements About Self-Injury (ISAS: methods and functions); SITBI.

### Management
**Principles:** compassionate, collaborative, **harm-minimising**, and treating the underlying drivers.

**Immediate:**
- medical care of wounds;
- **psychosocial assessment** for everyone presenting to hospital with self-harm [general knowledge, NICE NG225];
- a **safety plan** (warning signs, coping strategies, people and places, professional contacts, **means restriction**: blades, stockpiled medicines);
- a **parent or carer holds medications** for young people [2].

**Psychological therapy (first line) [general knowledge]:**
- **DBT-A (adolescent DBT):** the strongest evidence for reducing self-harm in adolescents (McCauley 2018; Mehlum 2014).
- **Mentalisation-based treatment for adolescents (MBT-A)** (Rossouw 2012).
- **CBT-informed psychological therapy**: NICE NG225 recommends CBT-informed therapy for adults with self-harm.
- **Emotion regulation group therapy**; **family therapy**; problem-solving therapy.
- **Harm reduction:** NICE 2022 does **not** recommend teaching "safer self-harm" routinely. It acknowledges harm-minimisation discussions in selected long-term cases [general knowledge; verify].
- **Replacement skills** (DBT distress tolerance): delay; urge-surfing; distraction; self-soothing; paced breathing; physical exercise; contacting a support person.
  - Avoid substitutes that cause pain or sensory shock, or that imitate self-injury (for example, snapping rubber bands or drawing red lines). They reinforce the pattern rather than interrupt it.

**Pharmacotherapy:**
- **No drug is licensed or recommended specifically for NSSI.**
- Treat **comorbid disorders**: SSRIs for depression (fluoxetine first line in young people [2]); ADHD treatment.
- **BPD [5]:**
  - NICE says drugs "should not be used routinely … for repeated self-harm";
  - a 2022 Cochrane review found little or no effect on self-harm;
  - **clozapine** may reduce self-harm in severe refractory BPD;
  - **lithium** is licensed for "intentional self-harm" in the UK but is little used;
  - crisis sedation for **no more than 1 week**.
- **ID/ASD self-injury [5]:**
  - **risperidone and aripiprazole** reduce irritability and self-injury in ASD (aripiprazole may be ineffective for self-injury in some reports);
  - **naltrexone** has inconsistent evidence;
  - lithium is licensed for self-injurious behaviour and aggression.
- **Avoid** drugs that are toxic in overdose (TCAs) and benzodiazepines (disinhibition).

**Systemic:**
- school liaison; anti-bullying measures; digital-safety guidance;
- treat parental psychopathology;
- **MHCA 2017:** admission only if risk is high (s89/s90; minors under **s87**). Self-harm with suicidal intent falls under **s115** [1].

**Prognosis [general knowledge]:**
- Most adolescent NSSI **resolves by early adulthood** (about 90% in a Victorian cohort).
- Persistence is predicted by early onset, high frequency, multiple methods, BPD traits and continuing adversity.

**Conclusion.** NSSI is deliberate self-injury **without suicidal intent**. Its main function is **emotion regulation**. It is common in adolescents, distinct from suicide attempts, but a **powerful predictor of later suicide**. Management rests on a non-judgemental assessment of intent, function, comorbidity and adversity; a safety plan; and **psychological therapies (especially DBT-A)**. Medication is reserved for comorbid disorders [1, 3, 5].

## In depth

### Background
- **Menninger (1938), *Man Against Himself*:** "focal suicide".
- **Favazza:** classification of self-mutilation: major, stereotypic, superficial or moderate (compulsive, episodic, repetitive).
- **Kahan and Pattison (1984)** proposed a "deliberate self-harm syndrome".
- NSSI entered DSM-5 Section III in 2013.

### Epidemiology in detail [general knowledge]
- Swannell 2014 meta-analysis: adolescents 17.2%, young adults 13.4%, adults 5.5%.
- **Contagion:** clusters in schools and hostels; online communities; "self-harm challenges".
- Rates rose in adolescent girls in the 2010s and **during COVID-19**.

### Mechanism in detail
- **Emotion regulation:** after self-injury, physiological arousal falls.
- **Pain analgesia:** higher pain thresholds in people who self-injure.
- **Endogenous opioids:** low β-endorphin and met-enkephalin levels. This is the rationale for naltrexone.
- **Dissociation:** self-injury "ends" depersonalisation.
- **Self-punishment:** linked to self-criticism and shame (trauma).
- **Theory links [3]:**
  - **Baumeister's escape theory:** escape from aversive self-awareness;
  - **Shneidman:** psychache;
  - **IMV:** NSSI can be a volitional moderator that increases capability.

### Clinical features in detail
- **Methods:** cutting (forearms, thighs; often concealed with full sleeves), burning, scratching, hitting, biting, embedding objects, ingesting objects.
- **Warning signs:** unexplained injuries, wearing long sleeves in hot weather, blood-stained tissues, possession of blades, withdrawal.
- **Medical complications:** infection, tendon or nerve injury, scarring, anaemia (frequent bloodletting).

### Assessment: distinguishing NSSI from a suicide attempt
| Feature | NSSI | Suicide attempt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent | Relief, regulation, communication | Death [1] |
| Lethality | Low; repetitive | Variable, often higher |
| Frequency | Frequent, chronic | Episodic |
| Method | Cutting, burning | Overdose, hanging, pesticide |
| After the act | Relief, then guilt | Ambivalence or regret about survival |
| Cognitions | "I need to feel better" | Hopelessness, "better off dead" |
| Overlap | Both can co-exist, and NSSI can cause death by miscalculation [1] | |

### Differential diagnosis
- BPD; depressive disorder; PTSD/CPTSD.
- Eating disorders (self-harm plus purging).
- ASD/ID stereotypies.
- Psychosis (bizarre self-mutilation).
- **Factitious disorder.**
- **Body integrity dysphoria.**
- Cultural or religious practices (excluded by definition).

### Management details: DBT-A skills modules [general knowledge]
1. **Mindfulness:** observe, describe, participate.
2. **Distress tolerance:** crisis-survival skills, distraction (ACCEPTS), self-soothing, radical acceptance.
3. **Emotion regulation:** identifying emotions, opposite action, PLEASE skills.
4. **Interpersonal effectiveness:** DEAR MAN, GIVE, FAST.
5. **Walking the middle path** (adolescent-specific; for families).
- Delivered with **individual therapy, a multifamily skills group, phone coaching and therapist consultation**.
- Uses diary cards and **chain analysis** of each NSSI episode.

### Evidence table [general knowledge unless cited]
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Mehlum 2014 (Norway) | DBT-A better than enhanced usual care for self-harm in adolescents |
| McCauley 2018 (*JAMA Psychiatry*) | DBT better than individual and group supportive therapy for self-harm and suicide attempts at 6 months |
| Rossouw 2012 | MBT-A reduced self-harm and depression |
| Hawton 2015 Cochrane | Limited evidence for most interventions in adolescents; DBT-A promising |
| Witt 2021 Cochrane (adults) | CBT-based psychotherapy modestly reduces repetition |
| Cochrane 2022 BPD medicines [5] | Little or no effect on self-harm |
| Maudsley ASD table [5] | Risperidone and aripiprazole reduce irritability and self-injury in ASD |

### Critical appraisal
- **Definitions differ** (UK "self-harm" includes overdoses irrespective of intent; US "NSSI" excludes them). This complicates comparisons.
- **Intent is fluid** and often mixed or ambivalent.
- **Small trials**; most come from high-income countries.
- Labelling self-harm as "attention-seeking" in emergency departments worsens outcomes.
- Concerns about **iatrogenic contagion** in group treatments are not borne out by DBT data.

### Indian context
- High academic pressure; family conflict; restrictions on relationships; **under-recognition** because of stigma.
- **MHCA s115** covers attempts (with intent). NSSI presentations in emergency departments often still lead to MLC procedures [1].
- **Treatment gap:** few DBT-trained therapists. Adapt with brief DBT-skills groups and school-based programmes.
- **NSPS 2022:** school mental-wellbeing curricula; gatekeeper training (teachers); responsible media reporting [general knowledge for the NSSI link].
- **Tele-MANAS (14416)** [general knowledge].

### Recent advances
- **Ecological momentary assessment** shows that urges fluctuate over hours, which supports **just-in-time digital interventions**.
- Brief **DBT skills** apps.
- **Safety planning plus follow-up calls** (Stanley–Brown) for youth.
- **ICD-11 personality disorder dimensional model** (borderline pattern specifier) [general knowledge].
- Investigation of **ketamine** and **opioid antagonists**: experimental.
