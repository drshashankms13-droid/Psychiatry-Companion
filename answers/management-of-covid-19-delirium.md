## Exam answer

**Why it matters [1].**
- Delirium is a **common manifestation of severe COVID-19**, especially in **COVID ICUs**.
- It is **occasionally the presenting complaint**.
- So **all** patients should be **screened for delirium**.

### Risk factors named in the source [1]
- **Severe infection and ICU admission.**
- **Older age with physical disability or sensory deprivation**, which "may predispose to delirium" (Table 4).
- **Substance withdrawal**, especially alcohol, when detoxification is unavailable in COVID wards.
- **Anti-COVID medications**, through a temporal association with their start.
- Past history of delirium (to be recorded at baseline).

### Assessment [1]
1. **Baseline at diagnosis:** cognitive functioning, **past delirium**, substance use (type, quantity, **last intake**), current medications.
2. **Screening in the ward:** **CAM** or **CAM-ICU**. Rate **sedation and motor activity** on the **Richmond Agitation–Sedation Scale (RASS)**.
3. **Cognition:** attention, concentration, recent memory; **MMSE, HMSE or MoCA**.
4. **Course:** onset and **fluctuating course**; **sleep–wake cycle disturbance**.
5. **Aetiology:**
   - evaluate the underlying cause and communicate it to the treating team;
   - check the **temporal association with COVID drugs** (HCQ, remdesivir, favipiravir, dexamethasone or prednisolone, tocilizumab, convalescent plasma);
   - look for **withdrawal signs and seizure history**.
6. **Contextual factors:** pain, sleep, inability to contact family, and the ward environment.

### Management (Table 7) [1]
**1. Treat the cause.**
- Identify and address the underlying cause with the physicians.
- **Discuss reducing or changing the offending drug.**

**2. Non-pharmacological measures.**
- **Re-orientation cues.**
- Monitor cognition and the sleep–wake cycle.
- Keep **family contact** (telephone or video) and involve family in day-to-day monitoring.

**3. Pharmacological measures.**
- **Melatonin.**
- **Haloperidol or quetiapine**, chosen and monitored by the **QTc interval**. COVID drugs and antipsychotics have a **synergistic QTc effect**.
- **Dexmedetomidine** (printed "dexmetodimine").
- **Lorazepam for alcohol-withdrawal delirium**, while **monitoring for respiratory depression**.
- **Prescribing principles:**
  - start low and go slow;
  - take account of oxygen requirement, renal and hepatic function, coagulopathy and bleeding, and sodium.

**4. Safety.**
- A **high-risk management protocol**: 24×7 observation and safety measures.
- In agitation, use psychotropics to calm the patient **while watching oxygen saturation**.
- Obtain consent from the patient or family according to **capacity**.

**5. Liaison.**
- Regular updates to the family.
- Communicate the mental state and risk to nursing and medical staff.

**6. After recovery.**
- Having gone through delirium is listed as a **distressing experience** that can contribute to post-COVID **PTSD**.
- **Cognitive deficits** may persist and need follow-up assessment.

**Conclusion.** [Certain, per the source] COVID delirium is common in severe and ICU cases and can be the presenting feature, so **screen everyone with CAM or CAM-ICU and RASS**. Management means:
- finding and correcting the cause, **including COVID drugs and withdrawal**;
- re-orientation and family contact;
- low-dose **melatonin, haloperidol or quetiapine (with QTc monitoring) or dexmedetomidine**;
- **lorazepam only for alcohol withdrawal**, watching respiration;
- follow-up for post-delirium PTSD and cognitive deficits.
