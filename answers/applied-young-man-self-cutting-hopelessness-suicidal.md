## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 22-year-old unmarried college student has had **2 years** of low mood, **difficulty maintaining friendships** and **self-cutting with a blade**. He has **conflict with his parents and frequent anger outbursts**, is **irregular with treatment and psychotherapy**, and says "everything will end soon". He appears **euthymic** but expresses **hopelessness, worthlessness** and **active suicidal ideation**.

**Immediate priority:** this is a **high-risk** presentation. Active suicidal ideation, hopelessness, a statement of finality ("everything will end soon"), repeated self-harm, male sex, poor engagement and family conflict all increase risk. Safety assessment comes before diagnostic refinement.

### 1. Differential diagnoses

| Diagnosis | Supporting features | Points to clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Borderline personality disorder** (ICD-11: personality disorder, moderate/severe, borderline pattern) | Chronic self-harm, unstable relationships, anger outbursts, emptiness/worthlessness, euthymic appearance despite distress, poor treatment engagement [1] | Abandonment fears, identity disturbance, impulsivity, affective instability over hours, stress-related dissociation; onset by adolescence |
| **Major depressive disorder** (single or recurrent) or **dysthymic disorder** | 2 years of low mood, hopelessness, worthlessness, suicidal ideation | Pervasive low mood most days, anhedonia, biological symptoms; "euthymic" appearance argues against a current severe episode |
| **Bipolar disorder** (II or mixed features) | Anger outbursts, irritability | Hypomanic episodes, reduced need for sleep, episodic course, family history |
| **Substance use disorder** | Common in young men with self-harm; worsens impulsivity | Alcohol, cannabis, other drugs |
| **Complex PTSD** | Negative self-concept, relationship difficulty, affect dysregulation | History of prolonged trauma or abuse |
| **ADHD** (adult) | Impulsivity, anger, academic and relationship problems | Childhood onset of inattention and hyperactivity |
| **Other personality disorders** (antisocial, narcissistic) | Anger, relationship problems | Disregard for others, grandiosity, conduct problems before 15 |
| **Adjustment disorder** | Stressor-related | Not adequate for a 2-year pervasive pattern |
| **Emerging psychotic disorder** | Social withdrawal | Unusual beliefs, perceptual disturbances |
| **Non-suicidal self-injury** as a behaviour | Cutting for emotion regulation | Function of cutting: relief, punishment, communication |

Most likely formulation: **borderline personality disorder with comorbid depressive symptoms (possibly persistent depressive disorder), at high suicide risk**.

### 2. Factors determining prognosis

| Favourable | Unfavourable |
| --- | --- |
| Young age (BPD symptoms tend to improve with time; most patients remit over a decade) [1, 4] | **Active suicidal ideation, hopelessness, repeated self-harm**: risk of completed suicide (lifetime suicide mortality in BPD about 50 times general population) [2] |
| Still in college; some social roles maintained | Male sex (more lethal methods) |
| Has engaged with treatment at times | **Irregular treatment and poor adherence** |
| Absence of substance use (if confirmed) | Comorbid substance use, depression, PTSD |
| Supportive relationships if repaired | Ongoing family conflict and invalidation |
| Good intelligence, insight, motivation | Severe personality pathology, antisocial traits |
| Access to structured psychotherapy (DBT, MBT) [2, 3] | Childhood abuse, early onset, chronicity |
| | Poor social and vocational functioning (lags behind symptom remission) [3, 4] |

### 3. Detailed psychosocial intervention plan

**Phase 1: Crisis and safety (days)**
- Detailed **suicide risk assessment**: intent, plan, access to means, preparatory acts, previous attempts, lethality of past self-harm, protective factors.
- **Admission** if imminent risk or inability to keep safe; brief and goal-directed. Under **MHCA 2017**, independent admission if he has capacity; supported admission (Section 89) if he lacks capacity and is at serious risk.
- **Collaborative safety plan:** warning signs, personal coping strategies, people and places for distraction, family members to contact, professional and crisis contacts (including **Tele-MANAS 14416**), **means restriction** (blades, medications held by family).
- Wound care without punitive attitudes.
- Treat comorbid depression; if medication is used, prescribe small quantities and involve family in safekeeping.

**Phase 2: Engagement and psychoeducation (weeks 1–4)**
- Explain the diagnosis non-judgementally: BPD as a treatable problem of emotion regulation, with a hopeful course [1].
- Explore reasons for irregular treatment (stigma, feeling misunderstood, cost, timing with classes) and agree concrete goals with him [1].
- **Treatment contract:** attendance, crisis plan, single coordinating clinician.
- Motivational interviewing for ambivalence about treatment.

**Phase 3: Structured psychotherapy (6–12 months)**
- **DBT** (first choice if available) [2]:
  - Individual therapy targeting, in order: suicidal and self-harm behaviour → therapy-interfering behaviour (missed sessions) → quality-of-life problems (family conflict, studies).
  - Skills group: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness.
  - Phone coaching for skills use before self-harm; diary cards; chain analysis of each cutting episode.
- **Alternative: MBT** (Bateman and Fonagy) focusing on understanding his own and others' mental states during interpersonal stress [3].
- If specialist therapy is unavailable: **Good Psychiatric Management / structured clinical management**, with regular appointments, psychoeducation and crisis planning.
- CBT for depressive cognitions (worthlessness, hopelessness).

**Phase 4: Family intervention**
- Family psychoeducation about BPD and suicide risk; teach validation and reduce criticism and hostility (expressed emotion).
- **Family skills training** (e.g. DBT family skills, "Family Connections").
- Agree how parents respond to crises without reinforcing escalation.

**Phase 5: Social and academic rehabilitation**
- Liaison with the college counsellor (with consent); academic adjustments during crises.
- Build peer relationships through structured activities; interpersonal effectiveness skills.
- Anger management within DBT emotion regulation.
- Address substance use if present.
- Vocational planning toward independence.

**Phase 6: Maintenance and relapse prevention**
- Ongoing outpatient follow-up; review of crisis plan; booster sessions.
- Monitor for depressive relapse and emerging bipolar features.

**Conclusion.** This young man most likely has borderline personality disorder with depressive symptoms and is at high suicide risk. Management begins with a thorough risk assessment, safety plan and means restriction, followed by engagement, structured psychotherapy (DBT or MBT, or generalist structured care), family work and academic rehabilitation. Prognosis is reasonably hopeful if he engages, but irregular treatment, hopelessness and male sex keep short-term risk high.

## In depth

### Background

- Self-harm in young people is common and the strongest predictor of eventual suicide.
- BPD has historically been viewed as untreatable; K&S emphasises that this belief is a preventable error and a self-fulfilling prophecy [1].

### Clinical features to elicit in detail

| Domain | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Self-harm | Onset, frequency, method, site, depth, function (relief, punishment, communication), escalation |
| Suicidality | Intent, plans, rehearsal, access to means, past attempts, final acts |
| Mood | Duration and reactivity of low mood; anhedonia; biological symptoms |
| Personality | Abandonment fears, identity, emptiness, impulsivity, relationships |
| Anger | Triggers, violence to others or property |
| Substance use | Type, amount, relation to self-harm |
| Trauma | Abuse, bullying, neglect |
| Treatment history | What helped, reasons for dropout |
| Family | Conflict, mental illness, suicide in family, expressed emotion |

### Assessment tools

- Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale; Beck Hopelessness Scale.
- SCID-5-PD or ZAN-BPD.
- PHQ-9 or HAM-D; Mood Disorder Questionnaire.
- AUDIT/ASSIST for substance use.

### Mechanism in detail

The biosocial model fits the case: emotional vulnerability combined with an invalidating family environment produces emotion dysregulation; cutting gives short-term relief (negative reinforcement), while conflict and anger outbursts maintain invalidation, creating a self-perpetuating cycle [2]. Mentalizing failure during relationship stress explains anger and self-harm after interpersonal conflict [3].

### Management details

**Pharmacotherapy (adjunctive):**
- SSRIs for comorbid depression; low-dose SGA or mood stabiliser for severe affective instability or anger, time-limited [1].
- Avoid TCAs (overdose lethality) and benzodiazepines (disinhibition, dependence) [1].
- Lithium has anti-suicidal effects in mood disorders but toxicity in overdose limits use in impulsive patients.

**DBT 24-hour rule:** no skills coaching from the therapist for 24 hours after self-harm, to avoid reinforcing self-harm with warmth; a contingency plan is used instead [2].

**Hospitalisation:** may be needed briefly; long admissions risk regression.

### Evidence

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| DBT RCTs (Linehan 1991, 2006) | Reduced suicide attempts and self-harm [2] |
| MBT partial hospital RCT | NNT about 2; benefits sustained at 8 years [3] |
| McLean Study | Most patients remit over 10 years [4] |
| Safety planning intervention (Stanley 2018) | Reduced suicidal behaviour after emergency visits |

### Critical appraisal

- Diagnosing BPD in a 22-year-old is appropriate when features are pervasive and long-standing, but must not stop the search for treatable depression or bipolar disorder.
- Evidence for DBT comes mainly from women; data for young men are limited [2].
- Euthymic appearance with stated hopelessness may lead clinicians to underestimate risk.

### Indian context

- Male help-seeking is low; stigma around both personality disorder and suicide is high.
- Families may respond to self-harm with anger or secrecy; psychoeducation is essential.
- **BNS 2023 (from 1 July 2024)** has no general offence of attempted suicide, and MHCA 2017 Section 115 presumes severe stress and obliges the state to provide care.
- College mental health services and Tele-MANAS (14416) offer access; trained DBT therapists remain scarce.

### Recent advances

- Brief contact interventions and caring contacts after self-harm.
- Digital safety planning apps and DBT skills apps.
- MBT and DBT adaptations for adolescents and young adults [2, 3].
