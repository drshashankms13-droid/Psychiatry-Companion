## Exam answer

**Definition.** **Tele-MANAS** (Tele Mental Health Assistance and Networking Across States) is the **digital arm of the National Mental Health Programme**. It is a free, 24 × 7, toll-free tele-mental health service of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
- **Announced:** Union Budget 2022–23.
- **Launched:** 10 October 2022 (World Mental Health Day).
- **Numbers:** **14416** or **1-800-891-4416**.
- **Aim:** universal access to **equitable, accessible, affordable and quality** mental health care [1, 2].

### Rationale (why it was needed)
- NMHS 2015–16: about **15% of adults** need intervention; **treatment gap 70–92%** [1].
- Shortage and maldistribution of specialists; stigma; travel costs for rural patients.
- COVID-19 pandemic: increased distress and normalised teleconsultation. The Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (2020) gave legal cover [1].
- **MHCA 2017** makes access to mental health care a statutory right, including through primary care [1].

### Objectives
1. A 24 × 7 tele-mental health cell in every state/UT.
2. A full service network giving counselling plus **integrated medical and psychosocial care** with referral to in-person services.
3. Reach **vulnerable and hard-to-reach** groups [1].

### Organisation

| Level | Body | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy | MoHFW + **National Technical Advisory Group (NTAG)** with 3 sub-committees | Strategy, oversight, indicators |
| Service delivery (nodal) | **NIMHANS, Bengaluru** (national apex centre) | SOPs, training curriculum for Tier-1 counsellors, IEC content, research, ethics/legal guidance |
| IT architecture | **IIIT-Bangalore** | IVRS platform, app, data security, ABDM integration |
| Health systems | **NHSRC, New Delhi** | Resource mapping, integration with NHM, monitoring |
| Regional | **Regional Coordinating Centres** (NIMHANS; LGBRIMH Tezpur; CIP Ranchi; IHBAS Delhi; PGIMER Chandigarh) and **mentoring institutes** (23 tele-mental health centres of excellence announced) | Mentor states, train, accept complex referrals |
| State | **State/UT Tele-MANAS cells** (53 cells in 36 states/UTs by February 2026) | 24 × 7 counselling |

Sources: [1, 2].

### Service flow: two-tier model
Caller dials 14416 → **IVRS** language selection (**20 languages**) and auto-callback → **Tier 1**: state cell, where trained counsellors (clinical psychologists, PSWs, psychiatric nurses) give 15–30 minutes of support and triage (each handles about 30–50 calls per shift) → **escalation** to a mental health specialist (psychiatrist) by audio or video → **Tier 2**: specialist care via **DMHP**, medical colleges, or **e-Sanjeevani** video consultation → in-person referral to AB-HWCs, PHCs, DMHP or tertiary centres for medication and follow-up → **callback and continuity of care** [1, 2].

### Community linkages
- ASHAs, ANMs and CHOs at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (HWCs) promote the service and refer callers.
- **CBAC including PHQ-2** is used for screening.
- School health and wellness ambassadors refer students.
- VHSNCs, SHGs and Mahila Arogya Samitis help with outreach [1].
- **Inter-programme linkage:** RBSK, RKSK, NPHCE, NPCDCS, NTEP and NACP [1].

### Monitoring and ethics
- **Monitoring:** cost-effectiveness and out-of-pocket savings are tracked.
- **Consent and confidentiality:** informed consent; minimal data collection; caller numbers **masked**; calls **not audio-recorded**; data on Indian government-empanelled cloud servers.
- **Legal and ethical framework:** MHCA 2017, NMHP 2014, Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020, telepsychiatry operational guidelines, IT Act 2000 [1].

### Performance
More than **32.8 lakh calls** handled by February 2026 [2].

**Conclusion.** Tele-MANAS is India's most ambitious attempt to close the treatment gap. It uses a two-tier, technology-enabled, task-shared model that links helpline counselling to the DMHP and primary care. Its success will depend not on call volume but on whether calls lead to **closed-loop care**: medication access, follow-up and outcomes for severe mental illness. That in turn needs quality assurance, counsellor retention and digital inclusion.

## In depth

### Background
- **NMHP 1982** → **DMHP 1996** (Bellary model; district as the implementation unit) → NMHP re-strategised in 2003 → manpower development schemes in 2009 [1].
- **2014:** National Mental Health Policy (universal access). **2017:** National Health Policy and **MHCA 2017** [1].
- **2020:** Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (Board of Governors, MCI/NITI Aayog) and the Telepsychiatry Operational Guidelines (NIMHANS, IPS, Telemedicine Society of India). During the pandemic, the NIMHANS psychosocial helpline (080-46110007) and **KIRAN** (1800-599-0019, launched 2020 by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment) showed that helplines were feasible at scale [1].
- **Union Budget, 1 February 2022:** announced the National Tele Mental Health Programme with 23 centres of excellence, NIMHANS as nodal centre and IIIT-B as technology partner [1].
- **Operational guidelines** released 8 December 2022; training manuals followed [1].
- **October 2024:** the Tele-MANAS mobile app was launched, with self-help modules and the option to call or video; the app supports 10 regional languages [2].

### Epidemiology underpinning the programme
- India has about 18% of the world's population. Mental disorders were the 2nd leading cause of YLDs in 2019 [1].
- Suicide deaths rose by about 40% between 1990 and 2016 [1].
- NMHS 2015–16: lifetime prevalence 13.7%, current 10.6%; treatment gap about **83%** overall (70–92% across disorders) [1].
- Treating depression and anxiety yields about a **four-fold return on investment** [1].

### Programme design in detail
- **Cell staffing and size:** cell size scales with population. One "unit" is planned per roughly 5 crore population. Category 1 cells serve states/UTs with more than 20 lakh population; Category 2 cells serve smaller ones [1].
- **Tier-1 counsellor training:**
  - Delivered by NIMHANS via a Learning Management System.
  - Content: communication, active listening, psychological first aid, crisis and suicide protocols, triage rules, professional boundaries, documentation and self-care.
  - Delivered both online and onsite, with periodic retraining by mentoring institutes [1].
- **IT architecture:**
  - A federated cloud system. Components: IVRS voice teleconsultation with auto-callback, video consultation via e-Sanjeevani, web and mobile app, the **e-MANAS** EHR platform, and governance dashboards.
  - Integration with the **Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)**: ABHA ID and consent-based data sharing [1].
- **Data protection:**
  - Data collected: only optional demographics (name, age, gender, district, state).
  - Phone numbers are encrypted and can be unmasked only by designated staff in emergencies; counsellors see history only during a call or assigned callback.
  - Servers are hosted in India by MeitY-empanelled providers; dashboards contain no personally identifiable information.
  - Planned: CERT-In audits and separation of demographic from health data [1].
- **Financing:** a 100% grant to states for HR, equipment, infrastructure and contingencies for 3 years. States then budget through NHM Programme Implementation Plans (PIPs) [1].

### Illustrative case types (from state cells)
- **Exam anxiety** in an adolescent: repeated supportive calls, study-skill advice, follow-up after results [1].
- **Active suicidal ideation:** risk clarified, location obtained, DMHP team contacted and patient linked to treatment [1].
- **Post-midnight overdose:** caller persuaded to involve family; reluctant family convinced with the on-call psychiatrist's help to take the patient to hospital [1].
- **Self-harm urges with a blade:** de-escalation and moving to a safe place [1].
- **Caller on a highway intending suicide at 2 am:** crisis intervention, psychiatrist escalation, consented callback, attended the OPD next day [1].

These illustrate the core functions: **crisis intervention, escalation, and linkage to in-person care.**

### Comparison with other Indian helplines and models

| Service | Owner | Model | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tele-MANAS (14416) | MoHFW | Two-tier, 24 × 7, linked to DMHP/HWC/e-Sanjeevani, state cells | Scale vs quality |
| KIRAN (1800-599-0019) | MoSJE | Helpline, volunteer psychologists | No integrated referral chain; overlap and confusion [1] |
| NGO helplines (e.g., iCall, Vandrevala, AASRA) | Private/NGO | Counselling, crisis | Limited hours or languages; no prescriptions |
| e-Sanjeevani OPD / HWC | MoHFW | General teleconsultation | Not mental-health specific |
| Kashmir model (Tele-MANAS cell, 2022) | State | Local Kashmiri/Urdu-speaking counsellors | Shows value of linguistic and cultural fit (about 4,000 calls in early months) [1] |

### Critical appraisal
- **Strengths:**
  - 24 × 7, free, multilingual (20 languages) and anonymous, which reduces the stigma barrier.
  - Two tiers allow task-sharing.
  - Integration with NMHP/DMHP, HWCs and ABDM aims at **closing the loop**.
  - A uniform training curriculum [1].
- **Weaknesses and challenges:**
  - The range of services was initially unclear (counselling vs prescribing). Prescribing psychotropics remotely is limited by the Telemedicine Guidelines (List O/A/B/prohibited) [1].
  - **Severe mental illness** needs in-person assessment, medication and follow-up; a helpline cannot replace community teams [1].
  - **Digital divide:** poor digital literacy, phone access and connectivity, especially for women and the elderly [1].
  - **Workforce:** counsellor attrition, burnout and boundary issues; handling emergencies, prank and frequent callers [1].
  - **Duplication:** KIRAN and NGO helplines confuse the public [1].
  - **Quality assurance:** there are few published outcome data. Process indicators (calls) are reported rather than clinical outcomes, linkage rates or cost-effectiveness [1].
  - **Legal issues:** confidentiality vs duty to protect in suicidal emergencies; consent with minors; documentation across state lines [1].
- **What is needed:** regular monitoring and outcome evaluation, standard and culturally adapted protocols, state-specific resource mapping, strong DMHP linkage, and independent evaluation [1].
- **Wider lesson:** helplines expand **access** but can deepen **inequity** if the poorest lack phones. Sustainability depends on how states fund it after the initial 3-year grant.

### Indian context: legal framework
- **MHCA 2017:**
  - Section 18: right to access mental health care, including services at district level, with free care for BPL persons.
  - Section 21: equality and non-discrimination.
  - Section 23: confidentiality.
  - Section 115: presumption of severe stress in attempted suicide, with a duty to provide care and rehabilitation.
- **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020:** consent, identification, documentation and prescription lists.
- **IT Act 2000** and the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023** (health data as personal data).
- **National Suicide Prevention Strategy 2022:** Tele-MANAS acts as a crisis line.

### Recent advances
- **Established:**
  - 53 state/UT cells and more than 32.8 lakh calls (February 2026) [2].
  - Tele-MANAS app with self-help and video consultation (2024).
  - Integration with e-Sanjeevani and HWCs [1, 2].
- **Planned or under development:** ABHA-linked records (consent-based); chatbot and digital self-help modules; video consultations from cells; evaluation research by NIMHANS; link with the WHO Special Initiative for Mental Health experience in other LMICs [1].
