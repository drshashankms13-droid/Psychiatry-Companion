## Exam answer

**Definition.** Psychedelics are substances that profoundly alter perception, mood and sense of self without causing delirium. The term covers three groups:
- **Classic psychedelics:** 5-HT2A receptor agonists such as psilocybin, LSD, DMT/ayahuasca and mescaline.
- **Entactogens:** MDMA.
- **Dissociatives (sometimes grouped with them):** ketamine.

**Psychedelic-assisted therapy (PAT)** is a structured treatment with three parts: one or a few supervised doses of a psychedelic, preparation before dosing, and psychological integration afterwards. Drug and psychotherapy are treated as inseparable [1, 2].

### Mechanism
Psilocybin is converted to psilocin, a **5-HT2A agonist** acting on cortical layer V pyramidal neurons. The proposed chain:
- 5-HT2A activation → glutamate surge (AMPA) → BDNF/TrkB and mTOR signalling → **rapid neuroplasticity**: dendritogenesis and synaptogenesis in medial PFC, hippocampal neurogenesis, plasticity-gene expression [2].
- Network level: **default mode network disintegration** and increased global connectivity. The "REBUS" model proposes that psychedelics relax rigid high-level beliefs, giving a window of cognitive and psychological flexibility [2].
- Psychological level: a **"mystical"/ego-dissolution experience**, emotional breakthrough and insight. Their intensity predicts outcome [1, 2].

**MDMA** releases serotonin, noradrenaline, dopamine and oxytocin. This reduces amygdala fear response and increases trust, so traumatic memories can be processed within a tolerable window [1, 3].

### Agents

| Agent | Class / target | Duration | Main indication studied | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Psilocybin | 5-HT2A agonist (prodrug of psilocin) | 4–6 h | TRD, MDD, cancer-related distress, AUD, tobacco, OCD, anorexia | Phase 3 (TRD); approved in Australia (2023) |
| LSD | 5-HT2A (also D2) agonist | 8–12 h | Anxiety (GAD), cancer distress, AUD (1950s–60s) | Phase 2; FDA breakthrough designation (anxiety) |
| MDMA | Monoamine releaser (entactogen) | 4–6 h | PTSD | Phase 3 positive; FDA declined approval (2024); approved in Australia (2023) |
| DMT / 5-MeO-DMT, ayahuasca | 5-HT2A / 5-HT1A | 15 min–4 h | TRD | Phase 1–2 |
| Ketamine / esketamine | NMDA antagonist | 1–2 h | TRD, suicidality | Licensed (esketamine) |

Sources: [1, 2, 3].

### Assessment before treatment (selection)
- **Include:** moderate–severe TRD, chronic PTSD, AUD, cancer-related depression/anxiety (in trials).
- **Exclude:** personal or first-degree family history of **psychosis or bipolar I** (risk of psychosis or manic switch); active suicidality (trial practice); uncontrolled hypertension, **valvular or ischaemic heart disease**; pregnancy; seizures [2].
- **Drug interactions:** MAOIs (serotonin toxicity with MDMA); **lithium** (seizures reported with classic psychedelics); SSRIs blunt the psilocybin response, which raises the question of tapering [2].
- Baseline: MADRS/HAM-D or CAPS-5, C-SSRS, ECG, BP, expectancy rating [2].

### Management: how PAT is delivered
**Preparation** (2–3 sessions: alliance, expectations, "set" and "setting") → **dosing session** (e.g., psilocybin 25 mg; MDMA 80–120 mg + optional half-dose booster), 6–8 hours in a calm room with eyeshades and music, two trained monitors, non-directive support → **integration** (2–3 sessions to make meaning of the experience and translate it into behaviour change) → 1–3 dosing sessions in total → follow-up for durability and adverse events [1, 2].

Psilocybin trials give support **before and after** dosing (historically "psychedelic therapy"). MDMA protocols provide therapy **during** dosing (historically "psycholytic therapy") [2].

### Key evidence
- **Psilocybin, cancer distress** (Griffiths 2016, n=51; Ross 2016, n=29): rapid, large and sustained reductions in depression and anxiety, maintained up to 4.5 years [2].
- **Psilocybin, TRD** (Goodwin 2022, NEJM, n=233): 25 mg beat 1 mg on MADRS at 3 weeks (difference −6.6); 10 mg was no better than 1 mg. Suicidal behaviour occurred in all arms but more in the higher-dose arms [2, 4].
- **COMP005 phase 3** (2025, n=258): single 25 mg dose vs placebo, MADRS difference −3.6 at week 6 (p<0.001). No suicidality imbalance [5].
- **Psilocybin vs escitalopram** (Carhart-Harris 2021, NEJM, n=59): no significant difference on the primary QIDS outcome; secondary outcomes favoured psilocybin [2].
- **MDMA for PTSD** (MAPP2, 2023, n=104): **71%** no longer met PTSD criteria vs **48%** with placebo + therapy [1, 3].
- **Psilocybin for AUD** (Bogenschutz 2022, n=95): fewer heavy-drinking days over 32 weeks [2].

**Conclusion.** Psychedelics are the first mechanistically new treatments in psychiatry in decades. They give rapid, durable benefit after one or two supervised doses with psychotherapy, most convincingly for TRD (psilocybin) and PTSD (MDMA). However, the evidence comes from small, highly selected samples, functional unblinding inflates expectancy effects, and long-term safety (suicidality, mania, psychosis, HPPD) is incompletely known. They should be regarded as **promising but investigational**, to be delivered only in regulated, trained settings. In India they remain illegal under the NDPS Act outside approved research.

## In depth

### Background
- **1938–43:** Albert Hofmann synthesised LSD and discovered its effects. He believed psychedelics could help people reach their potential [1].
- **1950s–60s:** LSD was studied for alcohol dependence (Humphry Osmond, Saskatchewan), anxiety and psycholytic psychotherapy; more than 1,000 papers. Osmond coined the word "psychedelic" in 1957.
- **1970:** The US Controlled Substances Act placed LSD and psilocybin in **Schedule I** (no accepted medical use), as did the UN 1971 Convention. Research stopped, opening the "dark ages" of the 1970s–80s [1].
- **Revival:** In 2000 the FDA approved Johns Hopkins to study psilocybin in healthy volunteers. Griffiths et al. (2006, n=36) showed mystical-type experiences with lasting personal meaning. The Johns Hopkins Center for Psychedelic and Consciousness Research opened in 2019 [1, 2].
- **2017–19:** FDA breakthrough-therapy designation for MDMA (PTSD) and psilocybin (TRD, MDD).
- **2023:** Australia became the first country to let authorised psychiatrists prescribe MDMA (PTSD) and psilocybin (TRD) [1].
- **August 2024:** The FDA declined to approve MDMA-assisted therapy and asked for a further phase 3 trial, citing functional unblinding, expectancy, missing safety data and trial-conduct concerns [1, 6].

### Epidemiology and burden that drives interest
- About 1 billion people worldwide live with a mental disorder. Conventional antidepressants have modest efficacy and frequent relapse, and around a third of patients with depression do not respond to two adequate trials [1].
- More than 200 psychedelic trials were under way by 2025, and the industry was projected to exceed US$8 billion by 2028 [1]. This commercial pressure is itself a source of bias.

### Classification and criteria
- **ICD-11:** recreational use falls under "disorders due to use of hallucinogens" (including intoxication and hallucinogen-induced psychotic disorder). MDMA falls under "disorders due to use of MDMA or related drugs". There is no diagnostic category for therapeutic use.
- **DSM-5-TR:** hallucinogen-related disorders, including **hallucinogen persisting perception disorder (HPPD)**.
- **Regulatory scheduling:** LSD, psilocybin and MDMA are Schedule I in the US and Schedule 1 in the UK. In India they are listed psychotropic substances under the NDPS Act, 1985, permitted only for licensed research.

### Mechanism in detail

| Level | Finding | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receptor | 5-HT2A agonism. Psychedelic effects are blocked by ketanserin or risperidone pre-treatment | Trials testing psilocybin + 5-HT2A blocker ask whether the "trip" is needed for benefit [2] |
| Cellular | ↑ BDNF, TrkB (some psychedelics bind TrkB directly), mTOR → spinogenesis in PFC within 24 h | "Psychoplastogen" concept; window for psychotherapy [2] |
| EEG / imaging | ↓ alpha power, DMN disintegration, ↑ global functional connectivity; EEG plasticity markers after psilocybin in MDD | Breaks rigid ruminative network patterns [2] |
| Psychological | ↑ psychological and cognitive flexibility; mystical experience, ego dissolution, emotional breakthrough | Effect size relates to experience intensity [1, 2] |
| MDMA | 5-HT release → ↓ amygdala, ↑ PFC activity; oxytocin → trust and openness | Memory reconsolidation of trauma within the "window of tolerance" [3] |

### Clinical features in detail: the acute experience
Onset 20–40 minutes after oral psilocybin, peak at 1–2 hours, resolution by about 6 hours. Effects include visual distortions, synaesthesia, altered time sense, emotional intensification, autobiographical memories, feelings of unity and transcendence, and sometimes intense fear ("challenging experience"). Physiological effects: mild rise in BP and heart rate, nausea, headache the next day [2].

### Assessment in detail
- **Screening:** structured interview (MINI/SCID) to exclude psychosis and bipolar disorder; family history; C-SSRS; medical history including cardiac disease; medication review.
- **Outcome scales:** MADRS, GRID-HAMD, QIDS-SR (depression); CAPS-5 (PTSD); timeline follow-back (AUD).
- **Experience scales:** Mystical Experience Questionnaire (MEQ-30), 5-Dimensional Altered States of Consciousness (5D-ASC), Emotional Breakthrough Inventory, Challenging Experience Questionnaire.
- **Expectancy and blinding checks:** ask participants and raters to guess allocation [2].

### Differential diagnosis: complications during or after treatment

| Problem | Distinguishing feature | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Challenging experience ("bad trip") | Time-limited fear and panic during dosing | Reassurance, "trip-sitting"; benzodiazepine rarely |
| Emergent psychosis | Persists beyond drug action; often a predisposed person | Antipsychotic, admission |
| Manic switch | Elated mood, reduced sleep in days after dosing | Screen out bipolar; mood stabiliser |
| HPPD | Recurrent perceptual changes (trails, halos) when sober; reported by about 30% at week 4 in one trial, distressing in about 1% | Reassurance; avoid cannabis; clonazepam/lamotrigine reported |
| Suicidality | New or worsened ideation in days to weeks after dosing (5–9% in higher-dose arms) | Close follow-up in the integration phase |

Sources: [2].

### Management details
- **Dose:** psilocybin 25 mg for depression; up to 40 mg/70 kg used in AUD [2]. MDMA 80–120 mg with an optional booster of 40–60 mg after about 1.5–2 h [3].
- **Antidepressants:** most trials taper SSRIs for at least 2 weeks before dosing, because SSRIs blunt the experience. Whether this is necessary is unresolved, and the taper itself carries relapse risk [2].
- **Physical comorbidity:** avoid in uncontrolled hypertension, ischaemic or valvular heart disease (5-HT2B agonism is linked to valvulopathy with chronic dosing), on antithrombotics where risk is uncertain, and with severe headache disorders. MDMA risks include hyperthermia, hyponatraemia, rhabdomyolysis and hepatotoxicity [2, 3].
- **Lithium:** avoid combining with classic psychedelics (seizure reports) [2].
- **Abuse potential:** low for psilocybin and LSD (no dependence syndrome); MDMA has moderate abuse potential [2].
- **Ethics and safeguards:**
  - Consent should be obtained before dosing, as capacity is altered during the session.
  - Two monitors, video recording and touch policies are needed because suggestibility is high. A boundary-violation case in a MAPS trial has been widely cited.
  - Therapists need standardised training and supervision.
  - Avoid public hype and personal bias [2].

### Critical appraisal
- **Internal validity:**
  - **Functional unblinding:** over 90% of participants guess their allocation, so expectancy (positive and nocebo) inflates effects.
  - Small samples and selection bias (enthusiastic, often prior-user volunteers) [1].
- **External validity:** strict exclusions (psychosis, bipolar, suicidality, cardiac disease) and a very intensive therapy model mean trial patients look little like routine clinic patients [1].
- **Construct validity:** it is unclear how much benefit comes from the drug, how much from the therapy, and how much from the experience. Therapy components (number, length and type of sessions; therapist qualifications; adherence) are poorly reported and not standardised [1].
- **Comparators:** placebo is inadequate. Newer trials use active controls (low-dose psilocybin, niacin, methylphenidate), expectancy measures and blinded remote raters [1].
- **Durability and harm:** follow-up is short. Rare harms (persistent psychosis, suicidality, HPPD) and re-dosing schedules are unknown [1, 2].
- **"Grandiosity effect" and commercial pressure:** advocacy among researchers, and investment, can bias design and reporting [1].
- **Resource cost:** two therapists for 6–8 hours per session makes scaling difficult, especially in low- and middle-income countries [1].
- **Counterpoint:** effect sizes after a single dose are large compared with daily antidepressants, and patients often describe benefit as qualitatively different (meaning, acceptance).

### Indian context
- **Law:** LSD, psilocybin and MDMA are psychotropic substances under the **NDPS Act, 1985**. Possession is a criminal offence, and clinical use is not permitted outside licensed research. Ketamine is also controlled; esketamine nasal spray availability and approval in India should be checked against current CDSCO status.
- **Culture:** naturally occurring psilocybin mushrooms are used recreationally in some hill areas (for example, around Kodaikanal). Cannabis (bhang) has long-standing religious use, which shapes attitudes to "plant medicines".
- **Services:** India has no regulated psychedelic therapy service. Its resource constraints (2 monitors × 8 hours) and the risk of unregulated "retreat" tourism are practical concerns.
- **Research priorities:** culturally adapted set and setting, and ketamine/esketamine as the near-term "rapid-acting" option.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** esketamine for TRD; Australia's authorised-prescriber scheme for MDMA and psilocybin (2023) [1].
- **Investigational:**
  - COMP360 psilocybin phase 3 programme (COMP005 positive in 2025; COMP006 two-dose trial) [5].
  - MDMA requires a new phase 3 after the 2024 FDA rejection [6].
  - LSD (MM120) for GAD.
  - **Non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogens** (designed to keep plasticity without the "trip").
  - Psilocybin with 5-HT2A blockade.
  - **Group-format** PAT to reduce cost [2].
