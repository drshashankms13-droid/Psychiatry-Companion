## Exam answer

**Concept [1].**
- **Gender dysphoria** replaced "gender identity disorder" in **DSM-5**. It means a **marked sense of unease** at the incongruence between a person's **biological sex and gender identity**.
- The distress may be severe enough to **impair social and occupational functioning** and cause **anxiety or depression**.
- **Gender nonconformity is not a mental disorder by itself.** It needs evaluation only if there is distress.
- It is seen in **children and adolescents**, and includes **disorders of sex development** where a gender was assigned by parents or physicians.
- ⚠️ The source also says that "many patients … want to change their biological sex to conform to their **sexual orientation**". This wording conflates sexual orientation with **gender identity**, which the source's own definition uses. It is reproduced here, flagged, and not relied on.

### Why psychiatry is involved [1]
- **Social stigma** and cultural attitudes towards gender nonconformity cause **many mental-health problems** in people with gender dysphoria.
- Medical education on the subject is lacking, so professional bodies rely on the **WPATH Standards of Care** and **Endocrine Society** guidelines.
- **The goals of treatment** are to **resolve distress and affirm gender identity**.
- **A multidisciplinary team** is needed:
  - mental health professional (psychiatrist, psychologist or counsellor);
  - plastic surgeon, endocrinologist, urologist, gynaecologist;
  - for children: paediatrician and paediatric endocrinologist.

### Treatment pathway (Figure 9) [1]
1. Diagnosis.
2. Screening tools.
3. Treatment of underlying depression or anxiety.
4. Psychotherapy and counselling.
5. **Social gender transition.**
6. **Gender-affirming surgery.**

### Role of the mental health professional before surgery (WPATH SOC 7 and Endocrine Society; Table 15) [1]
**Diagnosis:**
- **Only a qualified, trained MHP** should diagnose gender dysphoria. Others may diagnose only with training in gender and mental health.
- Use **DSM-5 or ICD-11** criteria and examine **psychosocial functioning**.
- A **detailed psychological evaluation**, with screening tools or tests.

**Treatment planning:**
- **Discuss treatment options** for gender dysphoria **and for concomitant mental disorders**.
- **Psychoeducate** about gender identity and expression. Assess comfort with gender expression and **social support**.

**Social transition ("real-life experience"):**
- Living **full time in the preferred gender for at least a year**, in all areas of life.
- It is **reversible**, so it comes before surgery. It shows the person what to expect from family, work and community.
- **Document** the timeline, coping and commitment. Counsel the patient and family, and strengthen support.

**Hormone therapy:**
- **Refer** when the WPATH readiness and eligibility criteria are met. Liaise with the endocrinologist about expectations.
- **Cross-sex hormones for at least 12 months:**
  - they induce the desired secondary sex characteristics and reduce those of the birth sex;
  - they **improve quality of life and sexual functioning, reduce psychopathology**, ease social transition and relieve dysphoria;
  - **continue psychotherapy for those 12 months**;
  - a **record of hormone therapy** is required for some surgeries.

**Referral for surgery:**
- **Surgery (general rule):** **two referral letters** from MHPs. The source says "for surgery" without naming the type; it lists chest surgery and facial procedures separately below. The letters must document:
  - **persistent gender dysphoria**;
  - **capacity for a fully informed decision and consent**;
  - **legal age of majority**;
  - **adequately controlled** medical and psychiatric comorbidity.
- **Breast or chest surgery:** **one letter**.
- **No letter** is needed for **facial feminisation or masculinisation** or **thyroid laryngoplasty**.
- **Individualised care:** surgeon and patient discuss **goals**, **realistic expectations**, cost, aesthetics, post-operative care, recovery and **complications**.

**After surgery and beyond:**
- **Legal documents:** educate the patient and family about **changing gender on legal documents** under national law.
- **Peer support:** give information and referrals.

### Indian legal context [1]
- The **Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019** **allows gender-affirming surgeries and change of name** after the procedure laid down in the Act.
- **Needs** identified by the source:
  - **multidisciplinary gender teams**;
  - **curriculum reform**;
  - **awareness among MHPs and doctors** of the transgender community's needs.

**Conclusion.** [Certain, per the source; partial] The psychiatrist's role in gender-affirming surgery is to:
- **diagnose gender dysphoria** (not gender nonconformity) and treat any comorbid anxiety or depression;
- **psychoeducate and support** the person and family through social transition and hormone therapy;
- **certify readiness**: persistent dysphoria, **informed consent and capacity**, legal age, and controlled comorbidity (two letters for surgery in general, one for chest surgery);
- help manage **expectations** within a multidisciplinary team.

India's 2019 Act permits these surgeries. The source does not give post-operative psychiatric outcomes, and its WPATH standard (SOC 7) has since been revised.
