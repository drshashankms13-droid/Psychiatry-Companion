## Exam answer

**Concept as given in the source [1].**
- After recovery, people may have **new-onset mental health problems** or a **continuation** of those in the acute phase.
- Some of these are regarded as symptoms of **"Long COVID"**, which needs **periodic monitoring and early intervention** at follow-up.
- No formal definition or duration is given (see the gaps above).

### Symptoms of long COVID listed in the source [1]
- **Mental and cognitive:**
  - **depression**, **anxiety**, **PTSD**: "sufficient evidence" exists for these;
  - **mild cognitive impairment** and **dementia**: also "sufficient evidence";
  - **prolonged fatigue** and **cognitive deficits** up to 6 months: "new emerging issues";
  - **confusion**, **sleep problems**;
  - **psychosis** and **substance use disorders**: higher prevalence reported.
- **Physical** (listed for "due focus"): **persistent fatigue**, **loss of smell and taste**, **shortness of breath**, **chest pain**, **headache**.

### Why survivors are distressed [1]
- The whole experience from diagnosis to discharge: **ICU admission**, prolonged **oxygen** and **tubes**, **seeing others die**, **delirium**, **near-death experiences**.
- **Bereavement**, often without the usual death rituals.
- **Stigma** and apprehension about facing society after recovery.
- Family members also carry distress.

### Assessment (Table 5) [1]
- **Clinical review:**
  - the experience of going through COVID-19; **loss of family members**;
  - **post-COVID symptoms in other organs** and the patient's reaction to them;
  - monitoring for symptoms suggestive of long COVID.
- **Screening tools:**
  - **depression** (PHQ-9/4/2); **anxiety** (GAD-7);
  - **PTSD** (primary-care PTSD screen for DSM-5; IES-R for severity);
  - **cognition** (MMSE, HMSE, MoCA, IQCODE);
  - **sleep** (Pittsburgh scale); **fatigue** (Fatigue Assessment Scale);
  - **suicidality** (C-SSRS, Beck scales).
- **Function and context:** functioning and disability; coping; relationship and financial problems.
- **Repeat** the assessment periodically.
- Every patient with a **new psychiatric disorder** should be asked about **recent COVID in self or family, and COVID deaths**.

### Management (Table 8) [1]
| Problem | Management |
| --- | --- |
| Anxiety, depression | Tailor to severity. **Supportive therapy or CBT** for mild to moderate symptoms, alone or with antidepressants. **SSRIs/SNRIs**. Short-term low-dose BZDs. **Relaxation and yoga**; **activity scheduling**; thought diary and mood chart; family support |
| Sleep | Reassurance; **sleep hygiene**; low-dose melatonin, zolpidem or BZD |
| PTSD | **SSRIs**; short-term BZDs; relaxation; **trauma-focused psychotherapy**; supportive sessions |
| Cognitive deficits/dementia | Check for **stroke or TIA**, premorbid function and ADLs; dementia laboratory work-up, **MRI**, neuropsychology. **Cognitive rehabilitation**; **donepezil**; **memantine** if vascular risk factors; behavioural management of BPSD |
| New psychosis | Exclude organic causes (fever, inflammatory markers, imaging); then **acute psychosis** may be diagnosed; **SGA**; BZD for agitation |
| Grief | Facilitate adaptation; allow ventilation and crying; address **missed rituals** and help create new ones |

**Service model:**
- **A mental health professional in every post-COVID clinic.**
- If none is available, train physicians to ask about mental health and refer.
- **Psychological support for everyone**, with or without a diagnosable disorder.
- **Address stigma** so that survivors do not feel discriminated against.

**Conclusion.** [Likely, given the definitional gap] In psychiatric terms, long COVID is a cluster of persisting or new problems after recovery:
- **depression, anxiety, PTSD**;
- **cognitive impairment and fatigue**;
- sleep disturbance;
- sometimes psychosis or substance use;
- all of it on a background of **grief and stigma**.

The IPS guideline recommends **routine, repeated screening in post-COVID clinics**, standard guideline-based treatment, cognitive work-up with rehabilitation, and psychological support for all survivors.
