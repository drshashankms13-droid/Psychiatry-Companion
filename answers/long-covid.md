## Exam answer

**Definition.** Long COVID is a **multisystem, often fluctuating syndrome** of symptoms persisting after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection. **Definitions vary** [1]:
- **WHO "post-COVID-19 condition"**: usually **3 months from onset**, with symptoms lasting **at least 2 months** and not explained by an alternative diagnosis;
- **UK NICE**: **ongoing symptomatic COVID-19** at 4–12 weeks; **post-COVID-19 syndrome** after 12 weeks;
- **US (CDC)**: symptoms at **4 weeks or more**.

This lack of consensus produces heterogeneous prevalence estimates and hinders comparison [1].

### Epidemiology [1]
- **Incidence varies by sample:**
  - **50–85%** in hospitalised, unvaccinated cohorts;
  - **10–35%** in community unvaccinated cohorts;
  - **about 8–12% in vaccinated** people.
- Meta-analytic pooled estimates (45–57%) are probably inflated by bias and heterogeneity.
- **Risk factors:**
  - **female sex (especially 35–50 years)**;
  - socioeconomic deprivation;
  - comorbidities (diabetes, asthma);
  - **severe acute illness**; high BMI;
  - **lack of vaccination**.
- **It can follow mild or asymptomatic infection** [1].

### Clinical features [1, 2]
- **Fatigue** (often **post-exertional malaise**).
- **Cognitive dysfunction ("brain fog")**: attention, memory, processing speed, executive function.
- Breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations.
- **Autonomic dysfunction:** **POTS**, orthostatic intolerance.
- **Anosmia/ageusia** and other sensory changes.
- GI symptoms, pain, headache, rashes.
- **Sleep disturbance.**
- **Mood and anxiety symptoms; PTSD** (especially after ICU).
- **Functional impairment** (loss of work capacity) is central [1].
- **Clusters:**
  - fatigue-dominant;
  - breathlessness-dominant;
  - **neurocognitive**;
  - multisystem;
  - dysautonomic.

  Some align with biomarker patterns (inflammatory vs non-inflammatory) [1].

### Psychiatric aspects (IPS CPG 2022) [2]
- Post-COVID mental morbidity:
  - **depression, anxiety, PTSD**;
  - **mild cognitive impairment and dementia**;
  - **fatigue and cognitive deficits up to 6 months**;
  - psychosis;
  - substance use disorders.
- **6-month neurological or psychiatric diagnosis** in about 34% overall, and about 46% after ICU (Taquet 2021) [2].
- **Distressing experiences** contribute:
  - ICU stay, prolonged oxygen and tubes;
  - seeing others die;
  - delirium;
  - near-death experiences;
  - bereavement;
  - stigma on returning to society [2].

### Pathophysiology [1]
**Three primary mechanisms:**
1. **Persistent viral components** (RNA, spike or nucleocapsid protein) in tissues; possible **reactivation of latent viruses (EBV)**.
2. **Immune dysregulation and autoimmunity:** aberrant immune-cell subsets, sustained cytokine signalling, autoantibodies.
3. **Endothelial inflammation and immunothrombosis:** microvascular injury, microclots.

**Additional hypotheses:**
- mitochondrial dysfunction;
- protein misfolding and amyloid;
- **gut dysbiosis**;
- **autonomic** and endocrine dysregulation;
- **blood–brain barrier disruption**;
- genetic and epigenetic susceptibility.

These may coexist in an individual [1].

### Assessment [1, 2]
- **Chronological history:** infections, **vaccination status**, symptom trajectory. **Validate the patient's narrative.**
- **PROMs:** **C19-YRS** (COVID-19 Yorkshire Rehabilitation Scale).
- **Basic investigations:** FBC, renal and liver function, **B12**, CRP (plus TSH, glucose, ferritin, vitamin D as indicated).
- **Targeted investigations:**
  - spirometry, chest imaging;
  - ECG, echocardiography, **cardiac MRI** if indicated;
  - **autonomic tests: NASA lean test, tilt-table**;
  - **cognitive screening** (MoCA) with formal **neuropsychology** if needed.
- **Mental health screening** [2]:
  - PHQ-9, GAD-7, PTSD screen;
  - MMSE/HMSE/MoCA/IQCODE;
  - Pittsburgh Insomnia Scale;
  - **Fatigue Assessment Scale**;
  - C-SSRS;
  - functioning, coping, relationships and finances.
- **No validated diagnostic biomarker** exists [1].

### Differential diagnosis
- Depression (fatigue and cognition overlap).
- Anxiety and panic (breathlessness, palpitations).
- PTSD.
- **ME/CFS** (overlapping phenotype).
- Hypothyroidism, anaemia, B12 deficiency.
- Post-ICU syndrome.
- Myocarditis, pulmonary embolism, interstitial lung disease.
- Dementia (new or unmasked).
- Medication effects.

### Management [1, 2]
**Principles:** **multidisciplinary, personalised, rehabilitation-centred**. Delivered through a **tiered model** [1]:
1. self-management and primary care;
2. community rehabilitation;
3. specialist multidisciplinary clinics;
4. highly specialised centres.

**Rehabilitation (the cornerstone) [1]:**
- **pacing** (energy management; avoid post-exertional crashes);
- symptom-guided graded activity, **with caution**: graded exercise can worsen post-exertional malaise;
- breathing retraining;
- **cognitive rehabilitation**;
- **olfactory training**;
- occupational support (phased return to work).

**Psychiatric care [2]:**
- **Anxiety and depression:**
  - supportive therapy, **CBT**;
  - **SSRIs/SNRIs**;
  - short-term low-dose benzodiazepines;
  - relaxation, yoga, activity scheduling.
- **PTSD:** trauma-focused psychotherapy, SSRIs.
- **Cognitive deficits:** cognitive rehabilitation. Donepezil can be considered; memantine if vascular risk factors are present [2].
- **Sleep:** hygiene; melatonin; short-term zolpidem.
- **New-onset psychosis:** exclude organic causes; SGAs.
- **Grief work** for COVID bereavement.
- **Address stigma.**

**Pharmacological and mechanism-targeted treatments** remain **experimental**:
- antivirals for established long COVID (e.g., extended nirmatrelvir: negative or inconclusive RCTs);
- anticoagulants; immunomodulators.

More than 400 trials are ongoing [1].

**Prevention [1]:**
- reduce transmission;
- **vaccination**: two doses reduce long COVID risk by about a third, three doses by **more than two-thirds** (pooled data);
- **early antivirals** (nirmatrelvir, molnupiravir) during acute infection may reduce risk (awaiting RCT confirmation).

**Conclusion.** Long COVID is a heterogeneous multisystem syndrome. Fatigue, **cognitive dysfunction**, dysautonomia and **psychiatric morbidity** are prominent, and it is driven by viral persistence, immune dysregulation and endothelial injury. Diagnosis is clinical, with no biomarker. Care is **multidisciplinary rehabilitation with pacing**, plus treatment of depression, anxiety, PTSD and cognitive problems. **Vaccination** is the best prevention.

## In depth

### Background
- The term **"long COVID"** was **patient-coined** (2020), an example of patient-led recognition [1].
- **Major cohorts:** **PHOSP-COVID** (UK, post-hospitalisation) and **RECOVER** (US NIH) [1].

### Epidemiology (expanded)
- Long COVID affected tens of millions globally, with major workforce consequences [1].
- **Risk is lower with Omicron and with vaccination** than with early variants [general knowledge].

### Classification
| Definition | Timing |
| --- | --- |
| Acute COVID | Up to 4 weeks |
| Ongoing symptomatic (NICE) | 4–12 weeks |
| Post-COVID syndrome (NICE) | More than 12 weeks |
| WHO post-COVID condition | 3 months from onset, lasting at least 2 months |

### Mechanism: neuropsychiatric focus
- **Neuroinflammation:** microglial activation; cytokines crossing a disrupted BBB.
- **Hypoxic injury** after severe illness.
- **Microvascular** changes.
- **Structural MRI:** UK Biobank before-and-after study showed **grey matter reduction in orbitofrontal and parahippocampal regions** and cognitive decline (Douaud 2022) [general knowledge].
- **Autonomic dysfunction** contributes to brain fog and fatigue.

### Clinical features: brain fog
- Slowed thinking, poor concentration, word-finding difficulty, memory lapses.
- **Worse after exertion.**
- Must be distinguished from depression-related cognitive impairment.

### Assessment: red flags requiring urgent work-up [1]
- Chest pain with ECG changes.
- Syncope.
- Severe breathlessness or desaturation.
- New neurological deficits.
- Suicidality.

### Differential: ME/CFS overlap
- **Post-exertional malaise** is the hallmark of both.
- Long COVID may be a post-viral fatigue syndrome in a subset.
- Lessons from ME/CFS: **avoid push-through graded exercise therapy**; use pacing.

### Management details: service tiers [1]
- Continuity of care, clear referral pathways, equity of access.
- **Global service availability is patchy** [1].
- **Mental health professionals** should be part of post-COVID clinics [2].

### Critical appraisal [1]
- **Methodological limits:**
  - inconsistent definitions;
  - **sampling bias** (help-seeking, hospital cohorts);
  - lack of controls (symptoms are common in uninfected people);
  - short follow-up.
- **Biomarker studies** are preliminary and need replication.
- **Psychologisation risk:** labelling symptoms as "functional" or anxiety without investigation alienates patients. Conversely, ignoring psychiatric comorbidity under-treats patients.
- **LMIC data** are sparse.

### Indian context
- Post-COVID clinics were set up in many tertiary centres (AIIMS, PGIMER) during 2021. The MoHFW issued a **post-COVID management protocol** (2020) [general knowledge].
- India's mostly vaccinated, previously infected population makes the current incidence of new long COVID likely lower [Guessing].
- **The IPS CPG (2022)** recommends mental health professionals in post-COVID clinics and physician training [2].

### Recent advances [1]
- Deep phenotyping (proteomics, metabolomics) to identify **treatable traits**.
- Trials of antivirals, immunomodulators, anticoagulants, metformin (metformin during acute infection reduced long COVID in COVID-OUT) [general knowledge].
- **Updated vaccines.**
- Research priorities:
  - standard definitions;
  - follow-up beyond 2 years;
  - genomics;
  - mechanism-informed RCTs;
  - LMIC studies.
