## Exam answer

### Case formulation

An 18-year-old assigned female at birth reports a male gender identity for two years, is in a relationship with a long-term friend who is a woman, wishes to marry her and requests an immediate letter supporting transition. The case requires respectful assessment, diagnostic clarification, evaluation of capacity and needs, and an explanation of the mental health professional's role.

### Possible diagnoses

-   **Gender incongruence (ICD-11):** marked and persistent incongruence
    between experienced gender and assigned sex. It is classified under
    conditions related to sexual health, not mental disorders.
-   **Gender dysphoria (DSM-5-TR):** gender incongruence accompanied by
    clinically significant distress or impairment. Not every transgender
    person experiences dysphoria.
-   Assess for co-occurring depression, anxiety, trauma-related
    disorders, substance use, eating disorders, self-harm and
    suicidality when clinically indicated.
-   Consider differential explanations only where suggested by history;
    do not presume that relationship orientation or non-conformity
    invalidates gender identity.

### Important clinical principle

Being attracted to or wishing to marry a woman does not contradict a male gender identity. Gender identity and sexual orientation are distinct dimensions.

### Assessment

1.  **Gender history:** onset, persistence, self-understanding,
    preferred name/pronouns, social transition and sources of distress
    or affirmation.
2.  **Goals:** clarify whether the requested letter concerns social
    transition, hormone treatment, surgery, legal documentation or
    marriage.
3.  **Psychiatric history:** symptoms, past treatment, self-harm,
    suicide risk, trauma and substance use.
4.  **Capacity:** assess understanding, appreciation, reasoning and
    ability to communicate a choice regarding the specific decision.
5.  **Medical and reproductive context:** discuss fertility,
    contraception, sexual health and relevant medical evaluation.
6.  **Psychosocial context:** family support, safety, discrimination,
    education, housing, finances and relationship dynamics.
7.  **Expectations:** explore benefits, limitations, reversibility and
    risks of each intervention.

### Response to request for an immediate letter

Do not issue an unsubstantiated certificate solely on demand, but do not create unnecessary barriers. Clarify the purpose and legal/clinical requirements of the letter. Provide accurate documentation of the assessment completed, explain any further information needed and arrange timely follow-up. A person's gender identity should not be subjected to arbitrary proof or stereotypes.

### Role of mental health professional

-   Affirm identity and use respectful language.
-   Assess mental health and decision-specific capacity.
-   Provide psychoeducation and supportive counselling.
-   Facilitate informed consent and referral to appropriate
    multidisciplinary services.
-   Address distress, discrimination and family conflict.
-   Avoid coercive or conversion practices.
-   Maintain confidentiality, explaining its limits where safety or law
    requires disclosure.

### Comorbidities and risks

Transgender people may experience elevated rates of depression, anxiety, trauma, self-harm and suicidality, often related to minority stress, discrimination and barriers to care. These are not inevitable features of being transgender and should be assessed individually.

### Indian legal and ethical context

Consider the NALSA judgment (2014), Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019 and Rules 2020, applicable medical standards and current legal requirements for the specific requested documentation. Avoid giving legal guarantees; refer for legal advice when needed.

### Conclusion

The appropriate response is a timely, affirming and comprehensive assessment. Gender identity, sexual orientation and relationship goals must be understood separately. The mental health professional's role is to support autonomy, assess relevant clinical needs and facilitate safe access to care–-not to act as an arbitrary gatekeeper.
