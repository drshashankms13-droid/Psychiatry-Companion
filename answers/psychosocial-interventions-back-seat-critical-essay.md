## Exam answer

### Introduction

Psychiatry has witnessed major advances in biological treatments, including psychopharmacology, ECT, rTMS, deep brain stimulation and rapid-acting antidepressant approaches. However, these advances have not eliminated the need for psychosocial interventions. A biopsychosocial approach remains essential because mental disorders affect cognition, relationships, occupation and social functioning.

### Advances in biological treatment

-   Antidepressants, mood stabilisers and antipsychotics have improved
    symptom control.
-   ECT remains important for selected severe and urgent presentations.
-   rTMS and other neuromodulation approaches offer options for some
    treatment-resistant conditions.
-   Ketamine/esketamine have expanded rapid-acting treatment research
    and practice in selected settings.
-   Precision psychiatry and biomarker research may eventually improve
    treatment matching, although routine clinical utility remains
    limited.

### Psychosocial interventions

-   CBT and behavioural activation.
-   Interpersonal psychotherapy.
-   DBT and other structured therapies.
-   Family-focused interventions.
-   Psychoeducation.
-   Social skills training and cognitive remediation.
-   Supported employment and educational interventions.
-   Community rehabilitation and peer support.
-   Relapse-prevention and recovery-oriented services.

### Why psychosocial interventions may be neglected

1.  Biological treatments are often easier to prescribe and scale within
    brief consultations.
2.  Shortage of trained therapists and uneven geographical distribution.
3.  Funding and reimbursement systems may favour medication-based care.
4.  Acute symptom reduction can take priority over long-term recovery.
5.  Stigma and limited public understanding of psychotherapy.
6.  Fragmentation between medical and social care.
7.  Overemphasis on symptom scores rather than functioning and quality
    of life.

### Evidence across disorders

-   **Schizophrenia:** family intervention, CBT for psychosis, supported
    employment and early intervention complement antipsychotics.
-   **Depression:** CBT, interpersonal therapy and behavioural
    activation are effective treatments, alone or combined with
    medication depending on severity and context.
-   **Bipolar disorder:** psychoeducation, FFT, CBT and IPSRT improve
    adherence and relapse prevention.
-   **OCD:** CBT with ERP is a first-line evidence-based treatment,
    often combined with medication.
-   **Substance use disorders:** motivational interviewing, CBT,
    contingency management and family interventions are central.
-   **Personality disorders:** structured psychotherapies such as DBT
    and mentalisation-based treatment are important.

### Why biological treatment alone is insufficient

Medication may reduce symptoms but does not automatically restore relationships, employment, independent living or social participation. Adverse effects, partial response and relapse also remain concerns. Psychosocial interventions address coping, behaviour, family environment, stigma and functional recovery.

### Limitations of psychosocial treatment

Psychotherapy requires trained personnel, time, patient engagement and cultural adaptation. Access may be inequitable, and not every intervention is appropriate for every clinical state. Severe mania, psychosis or acute suicidality may require urgent biological and safety interventions before intensive psychotherapy is feasible.

### Indian context

India needs integration of psychosocial care into the National Mental Health Programme and District Mental Health Programme, primary care, community rehabilitation and Tele-MANAS pathways. Task-sharing, culturally adapted interventions, family involvement and collaboration with NGOs can expand access. The Mental Healthcare Act 2017 supports rights-based, community-oriented care.

### Way forward

-   Adopt integrated, person-centred treatment plans.
-   Use shared decision-making.
-   Expand therapist training and task-sharing.
-   Measure symptoms, functioning and recovery.
-   Strengthen family and community interventions.
-   Improve funding and implementation research.
-   Use digital delivery where appropriate while protecting privacy and
    safety.

### Conclusion

Biological advances have transformed psychiatry but have not displaced psychosocial interventions. The best outcomes generally require appropriately selected combinations of biological, psychological and social treatments. Modern psychiatry should move beyond a false dichotomy and provide integrated care.
