## Exam answer

### Part A. Coronary heart disease (CHD) and depression

**Size of the problem** [1]:
- **Up to 50%** of patients with coronary artery disease have depressive symptoms; **about 20% have major depressive disorder**.
- **Heart failure:** depression in up to 50%.
- **ACS:** doubles the risk of MDD.
- **WHO projections:** ischaemic heart disease is the leading cause of death, and by 2030 depression will carry the greatest disability burden.

**A bidirectional relationship** [1, 2]:
- **Depression → heart disease:**
  - depressed people are more likely to have an MI, even after controlling for conventional risk factors;
  - cardiovascular mortality is higher;
  - depression raises the risk of CVD and **sudden cardiac death**, possibly through platelet activation, reduced heart-rate variability, inactivity and diabetes [2].
- **Heart disease → depression:** ACS doubles MDD risk; atrial fibrillation increases depression and anxiety [2].
- **Prognosis:** depression worsens long-term prognosis in all cardiovascular disorders. After ACS it reduces adherence to rehabilitation and medication, and increases mortality and readmission [1]. **Untreated depression worsens prognosis in CVD** [2].

**Mechanisms** [1]:
- **Monoamines:** serotonin, noradrenaline and dopamine abnormalities underlie depression and also contribute to **arterial stiffening, narrowing and endothelial reactivity**.
- **Immune and neuroendocrine dysfunction** destabilises plaques, leading to rupture, thrombosis and ACS.
- **Heart-rate variability (HRV)** is reduced in depression and falls further with severity. Low HRV mediates the effect of depression on post-MI survival.

**Diagnostic difficulty** [1]:
- **Symptom overlap:** fatigue and low energy are common in CVD and can be side effects of cardiac drugs (e.g., **beta-blockers**).
- **Reactions to illness:** denial, avoidance, withdrawal and anxiety complicate assessment.

**Treatment of depression in CHD** [1, 2]:
- **SSRIs are first line.**
  - **Sertraline** (SADHART) and **citalopram** (CREATE) are suggested first choices in CAD.
  - **Fluoxetine** is recommended for post-MI depression.
  - SSRIs after acute MI may **reduce cardiovascular morbidity and mortality** (ENRICHD) [1].
- **Maudsley:**
  - **sertraline is the drug of choice post-MI**; other SSRIs and **mirtazapine** are likely safe;
  - post-MI, SSRIs and mirtazapine are neutral or beneficial for mortality, so **SSRIs should not be withheld after MI**;
  - SSRIs may protect against MI;
  - the benefit seems to come from the drug (antiplatelet effect or fewer arrhythmias); **CBT may be ineffective in this respect** [2].
- **TCAs are contraindicated in ACS** [1] and in recent MI [2].
- **Heart failure:**
  - SSRIs are first choice;
  - sertraline was **safe but not more effective than placebo** (SADHART-CHF) [1];
  - escitalopram did not affect mortality, and a systematic review found no adverse mortality effect for any SSRI [2];
  - some evidence links antidepressant treatment with **worse** heart-failure outcomes [1];
  - responders in the first year have lower morbidity and mortality [1].

### Part B. Cardiovascular effects of antidepressants

| Class | Cardiovascular effects | Use in cardiac disease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **TCAs** | Fast sodium-channel block slows conduction: **↑PR, QRS, QT**; AV block (especially with pre-existing bundle-branch block); Brugada pattern; VF and re-entry arrhythmias; **reduced HRV**; α1-block causes **orthostatic hypotension**; anticholinergic tachycardia; reduced contractility. Complete heart block and death in overdose [1, 2] | **Contraindicated after recent MI and in ACS; avoid in IHD.** If unavoidable, ECG at baseline, 1 week after each dose rise and periodically; cardiology advice [2]. **Lofepramine** is less cardiotoxic [2] |
| **SSRIs** | Usually mild at therapeutic doses: orthostatic hypotension, mild bradycardia, occasional QT prolongation. TdP mainly with risk factors (congenital long QT, recent MI, low K⁺/Mg²⁺, overdose) [1]. **Citalopram** (and escitalopram) cause dose-related QTc increase and are **the most cardiotoxic SSRIs, though modestly so** [2]. **Antiplatelet effect:** cardioprotective, but **bleeding risk** with anticoagulants [1, 2]. Rare vasospasm (Prinzmetal angina) [1] | **First line.** Sertraline safest (QT +5–10 ms only at 400 mg/day); fluoxetine and paroxetine have no QTc effect [2] |
| **SNRIs** | Raised sympathetic activity: **↑HR and BP**; monitor BP, especially with venlafaxine [1]. **Venlafaxine:** QTc prolongation only in overdose; arrhythmogenicity slim; avoid in IHD [2]. **Duloxetine:** important BP effect; **not recommended in cardiac disease** [2] | Caution; monitor BP |
| **Mirtazapine** | Minimal HR and BP effect; no QTc effect [2]. Moderate hypotension in overdose [1]. Bleeding risk [2] | **Safe post-MI; good alternative to SSRIs** [2] |
| **Trazodone** | Postural hypotension; QT prolongation and AV block in overdose [1]. Case reports of arrhythmia [2] | **Contraindicated in acute MI** [2] |
| **Bupropion** | Slight ↑BP, sometimes significant [2] | Well tolerated for smoking cessation post-MI; caution in arrhythmia risk [2] |
| **MAOIs** | Postural hypotension; **hypertensive crisis with tyramine**; effects appear 12–24 hours after toxic levels [1]. Moclobemide QTc ↑ in overdose [1, 2] | **Not recommended in CVD** [2] |
| Reboxetine | Significant ↑HR, ectopic beats [2] | Probably best avoided in IHD [2] |
| Agomelatine, vortioxetine, vilazodone | Little or no cardiac effect [2] | Probably safe [2] |

**Arrhythmia risk overall** [1, 2]:
- **Incidence:** sudden cardiac death or ventricular arrhythmia about **3.3 per 1,000 person-years** after antidepressant exposure [1].
- **Meta-analysis:** antidepressants are associated with **atrial fibrillation** but not with ventricular arrhythmias or sudden death [2].
- **TCAs** have a very low risk outside overdose or high doses [2].
- **Fatal Toxicity Index:**
  - high for TCAs (except lofepramine);
  - medium for venlafaxine and moclobemide;
  - low for SSRIs, mirtazapine and reboxetine [2].

**QT and TdP** [1]:
- **QTc thresholds:** normal is about 400 ms; **above 500 ms** risks R-on-T and TdP, which presents with syncope, seizures or VF.
- **QT effect by drug:**
  - **considerable QTc prolongation:** imipramine, amitriptyline, nortriptyline, desipramine, maprotiline, doxepin;
  - **mild:** clomipramine, mirtazapine, trazodone;
  - **no QTc abnormality at therapeutic doses:** SSRIs and SNRIs.
- **Risk factors for TdP:** female sex, **age over 65**, bradycardia, myocardial hypertrophy, congenital long QT, low K⁺/Mg²⁺, hepatic or renal failure.
- **HRV** predicts arrhythmia better than the ECG in CAD, CHF and diabetes. TCAs markedly reduce HRV; sertraline has no effect or a mild increase.

**Maudsley recommendations for antidepressants in cardiac disease** [2]:
1. Non-tricyclics generally have **very low** arrhythmia risk.
2. **Sertraline is recommended post-MI**; other SSRIs and mirtazapine are also likely safe.
3. **Bupropion, citalopram, escitalopram, moclobemide and venlafaxine:**
   - use with caution, or avoid, in people at risk of serious arrhythmia (heart failure, LVH, previous arrhythmia or MI);
   - **ECG at baseline and 1 week after every dose increase**.
4. **Avoid TCAs** (except lofepramine) in those at risk. If unavoidable, ECG monitoring and cardiology advice.
5. **Arrhythmogenicity is dose-related.** Consider ECGs at high doses and with interacting drugs (e.g., fluoxetine pharmacokinetically, diuretics pharmacodynamically).
6. **Beware antiplatelet activity and CYP interactions** with cardiac drugs.

### Part C. Other psychotropics in cardiovascular disease [2]

**Antipsychotics and QT:**

| QTc effect | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| None | Brexpiprazole, cariprazine, lurasidone, lumateperone |
| Low | Aripiprazole, asenapine, clozapine, flupentixol, fluphenazine, olanzapine, paliperidone, risperidone, sulpiride, zuclopenthixol |
| Moderate | **Amisulpride** (TdP in overdose), chlorpromazine, **haloperidol**, iloperidone, levomepromazine, quetiapine, ziprasidone, pimavanserin |
| High | **Pimozide, sertindole** |

- **General points:**
  - most antipsychotics are associated with **sudden cardiac death**;
  - the risk is dose-related, and the overall dose determines QT prolongation;
  - **assume all antipsychotics are linked to sudden cardiac death**.
- **Risk factors**:
  - **cardiac:** long QT syndrome, bradycardia, IHD, myocarditis, MI, LVH;
  - **metabolic:** low K⁺, Mg²⁺ or Ca²⁺;
  - **other:** exertion, stress, anorexia nervosa, extremes of age, female sex.
- **QT-prolonging non-psychotropics:** macrolides, quinolones, antimalarials, antiarrhythmics, methadone, tamoxifen and others.
- **ECG monitoring:**
  - on admission;
  - yearly if there is a previous abnormality or risk factor;
  - **within a week of reaching a therapeutic dose** of a moderate- or high-effect drug or a combination.
- **Management of QTc:**
  - **over 440 ms (men) or 470 ms (women) but under 500 ms:** consider reducing the dose or switching;
  - **over 500 ms or abnormal T waves:** stop or switch the drug and **refer to cardiology immediately**.
- **Bigger cardiovascular risks:** smoking, obesity and impaired glucose tolerance carry more cardiovascular risk than QT changes.

**Clozapine:**
- **Myocarditis:**
  - incidence about 7 per 1,000 (up to 3% in Australian studies); case fatality 12.7%;
  - **median onset 3 weeks** (first 6–8 weeks);
  - features: tachycardia, fever, hypotension, dyspnoea, chest pain, ST changes, eosinophilia;
  - monitor **troponin and CRP**;
  - risk factors: rapid titration, valproate, older age.
- **Cardiomyopathy:** about 6 per 1,000, median onset 9 months.
- **Thromboembolism:** 1 in 2,000 to 1 in 6,000.
- **Signs of heart failure → stop clozapine and refer.**

**Atrial fibrillation:**
- **Psychosis:**
  - consider **cariprazine, brexpiprazole or lurasidone** in paroxysmal or persistent AF;
  - AF has been reported with clozapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole and paliperidone;
  - all antipsychotics increase bleeding with DOACs.
- **Bipolar disorder:** valproate, lithium and carbamazepine do not appear to affect AF risk (valproate may cause AV block).
- **Depression:**
  - SSRIs, but beware warfarin and DOAC bleeding;
  - mirtazapine and trazodone may increase bleeding with DOACs;
  - avoid TCAs in coronary disease.
- **Anxiety:** benzodiazepines or SSRIs; reports of AF with pregabalin and gabapentin.
- **Dementia:** cholinesterase inhibitors, but **avoid in "vagal" paroxysmal AF** (bradycardia); rivastigmine has fewest interactions.

### General principles [1, 2]
1. **Treat depression:** it worsens cardiac prognosis.
2. **Choose an SSRI** (sertraline first) or mirtazapine; avoid TCAs, MAOIs and duloxetine in significant cardiac disease.
3. **ECG before and after starting** in pre-existing cardiac disease or risk factors, with periodic QTc monitoring [1]; for higher-risk drugs, ECG after each dose increase [2].
4. **Correct electrolytes** (K⁺, Mg²⁺) and avoid QT-prolonging combinations and metabolic inhibitors (fluvoxamine, fluoxetine, paroxetine, valproate).
5. **Monitor BP** with SNRIs and bupropion.
6. **Watch for bleeding** with SSRIs plus anticoagulants or antiplatelets.
7. **Individualise:** there is no robust guideline, so weigh each patient's risks and benefits [1].

**Conclusion.** Depression and CHD are bidirectionally linked, and depression roughly doubles adverse cardiac outcomes. SSRIs (especially sertraline, then citalopram, fluoxetine) and mirtazapine are safe and should not be withheld after MI. TCAs are contraindicated in recent MI and ACS. Psychotropic prescribing in CVD requires attention to QTc, blood pressure, heart rate, bleeding and drug interactions, with baseline and follow-up ECGs in at-risk patients.

## In depth

### Why SSRIs may be cardioprotective [1, 2]
- **Platelets:** they inhibit serotonin uptake into platelets, reducing activation and aggregation, and normalise platelet indices in IHD.
- **MI risk:** SSRI users have lower MI rates than TCA users.
- **Caveat:** the same antiplatelet effect raises bleeding risk with anticoagulation.

### Unresolved issues (from the sources)
- **Heart failure:** some evidence suggests worse outcomes with antidepressants. In one study, beta-blockers plus TCAs had lower mortality than beta-blockers plus SSRIs [1].
- **Pharmacovigilance signals:** these link antidepressants to arrhythmia and cardiovascular death, but are confounded [2].
- **Citalopram:** causes QT prolongation in about a third of overdoses, yet overdose toxicity is low. Data on cardiac arrest at standard doses conflict [2].
- **Choice of QTc formula:** Bazett's overestimates QT at high heart rates [2].

### Not covered by the sources (deliberately left blank)
- Psychological therapies (CBT, IPT) for depression in CHD, beyond Maudsley's note that CBT may not reduce cardiac events.
- Cardiac rehabilitation programmes.
- Lithium and stimulants in cardiac disease, beyond the AF table.
