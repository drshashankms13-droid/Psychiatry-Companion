## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 7-year-old boy has school complaints:
- he **appears to be day-dreaming**;
- his **classwork is frequently incomplete**;
- he **keeps going out of class**.

At home he **cannot sit for long** for homework or even board games. The parents have read about ADHD and ask whether he has it, and (in the 2024 version) about its **long-term implications**.

### Provisional diagnosis
**Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), combined presentation, is suspected:**
- **inattention:** day-dreaming, incomplete work;
- **hyperactivity:** leaving his seat or class, unable to sit even for preferred activities;
- **across two settings** (school and home).

This must be confirmed by a **multi-informant, multi-setting assessment** [1]. Note that symptoms "may be minimal or absent" in the clinic: one-to-one, novel setting, interesting tasks. Their absence there "should not be taken as evidence of absence of ADHD" [1].

**Differentials and comorbidities to consider [1]:**
| Condition | Distinguishing points |
| --- | --- |
| **Specific learning disorder** | Persistent difficulties in specific academic skills; in ADHD, performance is "strikingly variable". Treat ADHD first, then reassess for SLD [1] |
| **Intellectual disability** | Inattention explained by poor comprehension; symptoms must be inappropriate **for mental age** [1] |
| **Absence seizures** | Brief staring spells with unresponsiveness (the "day-dreaming"); EEG if suspected [1] |
| **ASD** | Overactivity due to stereotypies, sensory issues, social-communication deficits [1] |
| **ODD/CD** | Defiance without core ADHD symptoms does not justify an ADHD diagnosis [1] |
| **Anxiety / depression / adjustment** | Recent onset; a definite change from the premorbid state; worry [1] |
| **Hearing or visual impairment; sleep disorder (OSA)** | Examination; sleep history [general knowledge] |
| **Medical / drug** | Thyroid disease, head injury, **lead**, bronchodilators, antihistamines, akathisia [1] |
| **Environmental** | Inconsistent parenting, chaotic home, inappropriate school demands, abuse or neglect |
| **Normal variation** | A young-for-grade child; temperament [1] |

### (a) Evaluation [1]
**1. History from multiple informants:**
- parents, **teachers** (report or rating scale, obtained **with parents' consent** and the child's assent), and grandparents in joint families [1].
- **Core symptoms** (DSM-5-TR: 6 or more of 9 inattentive and/or 6 or more of 9 hyperactive-impulsive symptoms for at least 6 months; onset **before 12 years**; in 2 or more settings; impairment).
- **ICD-11 6A05** has the same core concept.
- **Developmental history:** milestones, language, motor coordination.
- **Antenatal and perinatal history:** prematurity, low birth weight, maternal smoking or alcohol.
- **Academic:** report cards and workbooks (variable performance vs specific deficits).
- **Social:** peers, rejection, bullying.
- **Family:** ADHD, learning or psychiatric disorders, **parental psychopathology**, discord, parenting style.
- **Medical history before medication [1]:**
  - **syncope, exercise intolerance, congenital heart disease, cardiac surgery**;
  - **family history of sudden cardiac death in the young**;
  - seizures, tics;
  - **sleep and appetite baseline**.
- **Strengths:** sports, art, obedience [1].
- **Impact:** self-esteem, burden on the family.

**2. Observation and mental state examination:**
- age-appropriate play materials;
- estimate intellectual level and language;
- observe parent–child interaction (critical, hostile or overprotective);
- screen for anxiety and depressive cognitions [1].

**3. Physical examination:**
- height, weight, **BP, pulse** (baseline);
- dysmorphism (fragile X, FASD);
- neurocutaneous markers;
- vision and hearing;
- soft neurological signs;
- thyroid.

**4. Rating scales** (supportive, not diagnostic) [1]:
- **Conners' parent and teacher**, **Vanderbilt**, **ADHD-RS-IV**;
- **SDQ** and **CBCL** (accessible; generic);
- **CGI** and **CGAS** for baseline and monitoring.

**5. Investigations: only if indicated [1]:**
- **IQ testing** if ID is suspected. Repeat after symptom control if the score is low.
- **SLD testing after ADHD is controlled** (NIMHANS Index for SLD).
- **EEG** for absence seizures; **thyroid** tests if clinical features suggest thyroid disease; **lead levels** if exposed.
- **No routine neuroimaging, EEG or continuous performance tests** [1].

### (b) Management [1, 2]
**Build the alliance and communicate the diagnosis:**
- present it as a "**neurodevelopmental / brain disorder**" with genetic and environmental influences;
- address **guilt and blame** ("not the child's or parents' fault");
- **do not inform the school without consent** [1].

**Psychoeducation (2–3 sessions)** [1]:
- prevalence about **5–7%**;
- core symptoms and their variability;
- comorbidities;
- treatment options;
- **long-term course**.
- Involve **extended family** (grandparents), because inconsistency undermines behavioural plans [1].

**Stepwise plan for a school-aged child (6 years and over) [1]:**
1. **Environmental modifications first:**
   - *Home:* structure and routine; "a place for everything"; planners and visual cues; a distraction-free study area facing away from the door; an adult sitting alongside; **5-minute breaks after 15–20 minutes** on task; physical activity (sports, cycling, swimming); warning 3–5 minutes before transitions.
   - *School:*
     - seat in front, away from the door and window;
     - brief instructions; visual and verbal reminders; praise;
     - "helper" tasks to channel energy;
     - **extra time**, smaller assignments, reduced workload.
     - Exam accommodations are allowed by some boards, but never as the sole measure [1].
2. **Mild–moderate ADHD: parent training plus school interventions:**
   - behavioural parent training: positive attention; praise; token economy; clear commands; planned ignoring; time-out; consistent consequences;
   - teacher training; daily report card.
3. **Medication** if the response is inadequate or parent training is unavailable and severity is at least moderate. **Medication must be offered if ADHD is severe** [1].
   - **Sequence: stimulant, then atomoxetine, then clonidine** [1].

**Medication details [1, 2]:**
| Drug | Start | Maximum | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Methylphenidate IR** (1–4 h) | 5 mg once or twice daily | 60 mg/day | The only stimulant available in India; Schedule X [1] |
| Methylphenidate intermediate release (6–8 h) | 10 mg in the morning | 60 mg | |
| **Methylphenidate OROS** (12 h) | 18 mg in the morning | 72 mg | Once daily; avoids school dosing and stigma |
| **Atomoxetine** (NRI) | About 0.5 mg/kg/day | Lesser of 1.2 mg/kg/day or 80 mg | Adequate trial about **12 weeks**; CYP2D6; LFTs at baseline; suicidality warning [1] |
| **Clonidine** (α2) | 0.1 mg at night, titrated in 0.1 mg steps (2–4 divided doses) | 0.2 mg (27–40.5 kg) to 0.4 mg (heavier children) [1; table partly illegible in the PDF, so verify] | Sedation, hypotension; **do not stop abruptly** (rebound hypertension) [1] |
- An **adequate methylphenidate trial is 6 weeks** at an adequate dose with good adherence [1].
- **Monitoring [1]:**
  - weight every visit during titration, then 3-monthly; **height every 3–6 months** (growth chart);
  - **pulse and BP** every visit during titration, then 3-monthly;
  - sleep, appetite, tics, mood.
- **Side-effect management [1]:**
  - give with or after meals; a large breakfast and late-evening meal;
  - **weekend and vacation drug holidays** for catch-up growth;
  - **melatonin 3 mg** for sleep-onset delay;
  - reduce the dose if there is **sustained tachycardia above 120/min** or BP above the 95th percentile on 2 occasions.
- **Lisdexamfetamine** (not available in India) is a NICE option after a 6-week methylphenidate trial. **Guanfacine** is licensed in the UK [2].
- **SGAs are not recommended** for ADHD [2].

**Comorbidity:**
- treat comorbidities (SLD remediation, ODD parent training, anxiety);
- social-skills training if peer problems persist [1];
- address **parental stress or depression**.

**Diet:** no routine elimination diets. Use a food diary if specific triggers are suspected [1].

### (c) Long-term implications (counselling the parents)
1. **Course:**
   - ADHD "may be a life-long disorder in a significant proportion" [1];
   - **hyperactivity declines** with age, becoming "inner restlessness" in adolescence;
   - **inattention and disorganisation persist** [1];
   - about **50–65% have impairing symptoms in adulthood** [general knowledge].
2. **Academic:** cumulative learning gaps; lower attainment; grade retention; school drop-out [1].
3. **Social and emotional:**
   - **peer rejection**, social immaturity;
   - **low self-esteem** from constant criticism [1];
   - bullying (as victim or perpetrator).
4. **Psychiatric:**
   - **ODD (about 40%) and CD**; depression and anxiety;
   - **substance use**: "risk-taking and substance use" in adolescence [1];
   - later antisocial personality disorder, especially with conduct problems [general knowledge].
5. **Accidents and injuries**; **traffic accidents** when driving; teenage pregnancy; criminality [general knowledge].
6. **Occupational:** under-employment; job instability [general knowledge].
7. **Good news:**
   - "long-term outcomes **including risk of substance use** are known to **improve with treatment**" [1];
   - comorbidity, family substance use and psychosocial adversity worsen outcomes [1];
   - most children with support do well.
   - Registry studies (Swedish and Danish) show reduced injuries, crime and substance misuse during medicated periods [general knowledge].
   - Methylphenidate has **little long-term effect on adult height** (about 1–2 cm on average with continuous use) [general knowledge].

**Conclusion.** The presentation strongly suggests **ADHD (combined presentation)**. It must be confirmed through multi-informant, multi-setting assessment and exclusion of SLD, ID, absence seizures, sensory deficits and environmental causes. Management follows the IPS CPG sequence: **psychoeducation and environmental modification, then parent training and school intervention, then methylphenidate (atomoxetine, then clonidine, as alternatives)**, with growth and cardiovascular monitoring. With treatment, long-term outcomes improve substantially [1, 2].

## In depth

### Background
- **Still (1902)** described "defects of moral control".
- The concept evolved through minimal brain dysfunction, hyperkinetic reaction of childhood (DSM-II), ADD (DSM-III) and ADHD (DSM-III-R).
- **ICD-10** "hyperkinetic disorder" was stricter (all three domains needed; no comorbid anxiety or depression). **ICD-11** aligned with DSM.

### Epidemiology
- **5–7% of school-aged children** [1]. Indian school studies range from about 1.3% to 11% [general knowledge].
- Male:female about 3:1 in community samples. Girls are more often inattentive and under-diagnosed [general knowledge].
- **Heritability about 70–80%** [general knowledge].

### Classification and criteria
- **DSM-5-TR presentations:** combined, predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive.
- Severity specifiers; "in partial remission".
- **ICD-11 6A05:** same presentations; specifiers.

### Mechanism [general knowledge]
- **Dopamine and noradrenaline** dysfunction in fronto-striatal and fronto-cerebellar circuits.
- **Executive dysfunction** (Barkley's response-inhibition model).
- **Delay aversion** (Sonuga-Barke's dual pathway).
- **Delayed cortical maturation** by about 3 years (Shaw 2007).
- **Default mode network** interference.
- **Genes:** DRD4, DAT1 (SLC6A3); polygenic.
- **Environment:** prematurity, low birth weight, prenatal tobacco and alcohol, lead, early deprivation.

### Clinical features by age [1]
| Age | Manifestation |
| --- | --- |
| Preschool | "Whirlwind" overactivity; does not listen; no sense of danger; play sequences under 3 minutes |
| Primary school | Cannot remain still in class or assembly; activities under 10 minutes; forgetful; disorganised; interrupts; accidents |
| Adolescence | Inner restlessness, fidgeting; procrastination, missed deadlines; risk-taking, substance use |

### Assessment: factors affecting symptoms [1]
- **Symptoms are minimised** in one-to-one interaction, novel settings, close supervision, frequent reward, or activities with interesting or constant stimulation (TV, video games).
- **Symptoms are worse** with lengthy, boring, monotonous tasks.
- **Diagnostic overshadowing** (for example, ADHD missed in ID) and **overdiagnosis** (for example, ASD sensory issues mislabelled as ADHD) [1].

### Differential diagnosis: details [1]
- **Mania:** recent onset; goal-directed activity; grandiosity.
- **Psychosis:** delusions and hallucinations.
- **Tics:** prolonged observation distinguishes them.
- **OCD:** goal-directed, ritualistic compulsions.

### Management details: treatment of preschoolers vs school-age children [1, 2]
- **Under 6 years:** **parent training first**. Medication is not first line; there are tolerability concerns. NICE requires a second specialist opinion for medication under 5 years [2].
- **6 years and over:** as above (stepwise, severity-based).
- **Responders on medication but with a residual domain impairment** (for example, peers): add a focused psychosocial intervention [1].
- **Non-response:** check adherence (side effects, cost, stigma), then optimise, then switch [1].

### Evidence table
| Source | Key point |
| --- | --- |
| IPS CPG (Shah, Grover, Avasthi 2019) [1] | Multi-informant assessment; stepwise treatment; stimulant, then atomoxetine, then clonidine; 6-week stimulant trial; 12-week atomoxetine trial |
| Maudsley 15e [2] | Methylphenidate first line; NICE Box 5.1 (ECG only if there are cardiac indicators); lisdexamfetamine more effective than methylphenidate in network meta-analyses; SGAs not recommended |
| MTA study (1999) [general knowledge] | Medication management better than behavioural treatment for core symptoms at 14 months; the combination helped comorbidity and functioning; the advantage faded by 3 years |
| Cortese 2018 NMA [general knowledge] | Methylphenidate first choice for children; amfetamines for adults |

### Critical appraisal
- **Long-term efficacy evidence is inadequate**; the evidence is mostly short-term [1].
- Rating scales **lack Indian norms** and are expensive [1].
- Concerns about **overdiagnosis** (relative-age effect).
- The **cardiovascular risk of stimulants** is poorly quantified [2].

### Indian context
- **Methylphenidate is the only stimulant available.** It is a Schedule X drug requiring licensed retail [1].
- **Clonidine ER and guanfacine are not available** [1].
- **Board accommodations:** CBSE, ICSE and state boards provide provisions mainly for SLD. ADHD gets limited accommodation [1; general knowledge].
- **RPwD Act 2016:** ADHD is not a listed benchmark disability (SLD is) [general knowledge].
- Stigma; teachers' attitudes; the role of grandparents [1].

### Recent advances
- **Viloxazine ER** (FDA 2021) [2].
- **Centanafadine**, dasotraline (investigational) [2].
- **Digital therapeutics:** EndeavorRx (FDA-cleared video game, 2020) [general knowledge].
- **Trigeminal nerve stimulation** (FDA-cleared device, 2019) [general knowledge].
- Long-term registry data on medication benefits.
