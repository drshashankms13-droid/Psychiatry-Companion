## Exam answer

### Definition/types

Group psychotherapy uses planned interaction among members and therapist(s) to improve symptoms, interpersonal learning, coping, and recovery. Formats include supportive, psychoeducational, CBT, interpersonal, psychodynamic, skills-based, family-oriented, open/closed, homogeneous/heterogeneous, and time-limited groups.

### Therapeutic factors (Yalom)

Instillation of hope, universality, imparting information, altruism, corrective recapitulation of family experience, socializing techniques, imitative behaviour, interpersonal learning, cohesiveness, catharsis, and existential factors.

### Selection/preparation

Assess goals, readiness, communication, cognition, symptoms, risk, substance use, interpersonal functioning, and ability to respect boundaries. Acute disorganization or uncontrolled risk may require stabilization or another format. Explain aims, rules, attendance, confidentiality limits, and expectations. Consider accessibility, language, culture, and transport.

### Conduct

Establish a safe structure; facilitate balanced participation; manage conflict and exclusion; link interactions to goals. Techniques may include skills practice, role-play, feedback, behavioural experiments, problem-solving, psychoeducation, or exploration of interpersonal patterns. Monitor group climate and individual deterioration.

### Indications/effectiveness

Groups can help in depression/anxiety, SUD, psychosis-related recovery, social-skills work, chronic illness coping, and caregiver support. Benefits include peer support, universality, modelling, and efficient clinician time. Outcomes depend on group structure, facilitator competence, attendance, cohesion, and fit.

### Limitations/ethics

Confidentiality cannot be guaranteed because members may breach it; explain this clearly. Other risks include scheduling barriers, social anxiety, conflict, uneven participation, and distress from disclosures. Clarify boundaries, records, crises, dual relationships, and communication outside sessions. Culturally responsive groups should consider language, stigma, gender, and disability.

### Conclusion

Group therapy is a versatile modality, not merely a cheaper substitute for individual care. Selection, preparation, competent facilitation, safety, and a coherent model are essential.
