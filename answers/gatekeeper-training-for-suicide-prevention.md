## Exam answer

**Definition.** A **gatekeeper** is anyone in regular contact with people who may be at risk of suicide, and who is in a position to **recognise warning signs, ask directly about suicide, respond supportively and refer** to help. Gatekeepers are often not health professionals.

**Gatekeeper training (GKT)** teaches these skills. It is a **selective/indicated, community-level** prevention strategy, and one of the most widely implemented suicide-prevention interventions worldwide [general knowledge].

**Rationale:**
- Most people who die by suicide are **not in contact with mental health services** in the preceding months, but **are** in contact with family, teachers, employers, police, religious leaders or primary-care staff [general knowledge].
- In India, about **65% of suicides occur at 18–45 years**, and about **60% of people who died had not completed school** [1]. Community "first responders" therefore matter more than specialists.
- WHO's **LIVE LIFE** approach includes "early identification, assessment, management and follow-up". It names training of non-specialised health workers and community gatekeepers as a cross-cutting pillar [1; general knowledge].

### Who are gatekeepers?
| Setting | Gatekeepers |
| --- | --- |
| Primary-care health | ASHA, ANM, MPW, CHO at Health and Wellness Centres, general practitioners, pharmacists |
| Education | **Teachers**, school counsellors, wardens, college faculty; the **School Health Ambassador Initiative** (2 teachers per government school) [1] |
| Law and order | **Police**, prison staff, railway staff |
| Community | **Faith leaders**, **political leaders**, **Panchayati Raj Institution members**, **Nehru Yuva Kendra and youth volunteers** [1] |
| Other health systems | **AYUSH / alternate-medicine practitioners** [1] |
| Workplace and agriculture | HR staff, supervisors, agricultural extension workers, **pesticide shop owners** |
| Special groups | Armed forces peers, hostel peers (student suicide), elderly-care workers |

**The NSPS (2022)** explicitly lists training of "**teachers, police personnel, political and faith leaders, PRI members, NYK and other youth volunteers, practitioners of alternate system of medicine**". It sits under the priority area of developing **community resilience** [1].

### Content of a gatekeeper training programme
1. **Knowledge and myths:**
   - "asking about suicide puts the idea in the head" (false);
   - "people who talk about it don't do it" (false);
   - suicide is preventable [1, 2].
2. **Risk factors and warning signs [2]:**
   - talk of death or hopelessness; giving away belongings;
   - withdrawal; sudden calm after depression;
   - substance use;
   - recent loss, failure or humiliation;
   - previous attempt (the strongest predictor);
   - access to means (pesticide, rope, medicines).
3. **Asking directly and non-judgementally:** "Are you thinking of killing yourself?"
4. **Listening and persuading:** empathy, avoiding lectures, instilling hope; **reasons for living** [2].
5. **Immediate safety:** do not leave a person at high risk alone; **remove or restrict means**.
6. **Referral pathway:** local PHC/DMHP, district hospital, **Tele-MANAS 14416**, NGOs (SNEHA etc.) [general knowledge]; follow-up.
7. **Self-care** of the gatekeeper and handling of postvention.

**Method:** brief workshops (1–2 hours to 2 days), role-play, and booster sessions. Training is **adapted to local language and culture**.

### Established programmes [general knowledge]
| Programme | Format | Feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **QPR** (Question, Persuade, Refer) | 1–2 hours | Most widely used; analogy with CPR |
| **ASIST** (Applied Suicide Intervention Skills Training, LivingWorks) | 2 days | Intensive skills; the "Pathway for Assisting Life" model |
| **safeTALK** | 3–4 hours | Alertness: "Tell, Ask, Listen, KeepSafe" |
| **Mental Health First Aid (MHFA)** | 12 hours | Broader mental health; ALGEE action plan |
| **Youth Aware of Mental health (YAM)** | Student-delivered to peers (not classic GKT) | Effective in the SEYLE trial |
| **WHO mhGAP** "self-harm/suicide" module | For non-specialist health workers | Used in DMHP training |
| **NIMHANS / NMHP "Living Life Positively"** manual | Life skills, stress management, suicide prevention workshops [1] | Indian programme |

### Evidence [general knowledge unless cited]
- **Knowledge, attitudes and self-efficacy** improve consistently after training.
- **Behaviour change** (actual asking and referring) is **less consistent** and **decays without boosters**.
- **Effects on suicide rates** are shown mainly within **multi-component programmes**:
  - **US Air Force** programme: about a 33% reduction;
  - **Nuremberg Alliance Against Depression** (4-level: GP training, public campaign, gatekeepers, support for high-risk groups): reduced suicidal acts.
- **SEYLE (Wasserman 2015, *Lancet*):** in 11,110 European adolescents, **QPR** did **not** significantly reduce new suicide attempts or severe ideation; **YAM** did (about 50% reduction).
- **Systematic reviews (Mann 2005; Zalsman 2016, *Lancet Psychiatry*):** evidence for gatekeeper training alone is **insufficient**. The best-evidenced interventions are **means restriction**, **school-based awareness programmes**, **physician education in depression recognition** and **follow-up after attempts**.
- **Indian studies:** small pre-post studies in teachers, ASHAs, police and medical students show knowledge gains. There are no outcome trials.

### Strengths and limitations
**Strengths:**
- low cost; scalable; uses existing community structures;
- reduces **stigma**;
- builds a referral culture;
- fits India's specialist shortage (task shifting).

**Limitations:**
- **referral needs services**: training without accessible care is futile;
- **skill decay**;
- gatekeepers may feel **burdened** or face medico-legal fears;
- little evidence of **mortality** effect alone;
- **cultural fit** (stigma, family honour);
- impulsive suicides with short crisis windows give little time for gatekeepers to act.

### Implementing gatekeeper training in India (proposed)
1. Map local high-risk groups, such as farmers, students in coaching hubs, migrant workers and the elderly.
2. Choose cadres (NSPS list) and **integrate training into existing programmes**: DMHP, RKSK, School Health Ambassadors, Ayushman Arogya Mandir, police training academies.
3. Use a **standardised, vernacular curriculum** (QPR-like) with role-play. Use **Tele-MANAS** as a common referral endpoint.
4. Run **booster sessions every 6–12 months**. Use supervision and peer networks.
5. Link with **means restriction**: pesticide shop staff as gatekeepers, safe storage. Link with **responsible media reporting**.
6. **Evaluate** pre-post knowledge and self-efficacy, referrals made, and district suicide surveillance (NSPS surveillance objective within 3 years) [1].

**Conclusion.** Gatekeeper training equips the community's natural first contacts to **notice, ask, listen and link**. It is a core element of India's NSPS (2022). It reliably improves knowledge and confidence, but on its own has weak evidence for reducing suicides. It works best as one component of a **multi-level strategy** that guarantees accessible care, follow-up, means restriction and surveillance [1].

## In depth

### Background
- The concept was developed in the US in the 1990s and is a core element of US, UK and WHO national strategies.
- The term borrows from "gatekeeping" in referral systems: the gatekeeper is the person who opens the gate to care.

### Epidemiology underpinning target selection (India) [1, 2]
- More than 1 lakh suicides a year (about 1.53 lakh in 2020, NCRB [general knowledge for the exact number]). The rate rose from **10.2 to 11.3 per 100,000** [1].
- Family problems account for about 34% and illness for about 18%.
- **Hanging and poisoning** account for more than 80% of methods [1].
- **Daily-wage earners, housewives, students, farmers** and the unemployed are prominent occupational groups [1; general knowledge].
- **Females:** India accounts for **36.6%** of the world's female suicides [1].
- NMHS 2015–16: about 0.9% of adults were at high current suicidal risk [general knowledge; verify]. The seminar slides give NMHS rates per 100,000 for suicidality and attempts [2].

### Classification: where gatekeeper training sits
- **Universal:** media guidelines, school mental health curricula, alcohol policy.
- **Selective:** **gatekeeper training**, community programmes for farmers and students.
- **Indicated:** follow-up of attempters, crisis lines, safety planning, treatment of depression.

### Mechanism: theory of change
1. **Increase detection:** gatekeepers ask, so hidden ideation becomes known.
2. **Reduce barriers:** stigma and lack of knowledge of services.
3. **Connect to care:** referral and accompaniment.
4. **Temporary safety:** means removal and not leaving the person alone. This matters because suicidal crises are often brief, and 80% of Indian attempts were impulsive (Vadlamani 2019) [general knowledge for the citation link].
5. Links to suicide theories [2]:
   - **Interpersonal theory (Joiner):** gatekeeper contact counters **thwarted belongingness**.
   - **IMV model (O'Connor):** reduces entrapment and opens alternatives at the motivational stage; **means restriction** acts at the volitional stage.
   - **3ST (Klonsky):** **connectedness** is the key protective factor.

### Clinical features: warning signs taught (mnemonic "IS PATH WARM", AAS) [general knowledge]
- **I**deation; **S**ubstance use;
- **P**urposelessness; **A**nxiety; **T**rapped; **H**opelessness;
- **W**ithdrawal; **A**nger; **R**ecklessness; **M**ood change.

### Assessment skills taught (simplified)
- Ask: thoughts → plan → means → timing → previous attempts → protective factors.
- Gatekeepers **do not formally risk-assess**. They escalate any "yes" to a professional.
- Professionals use scales such as **C-SSRS**, SIS, BHS and SAD PERSONS. These have **low predictive value** for individual suicide [2].

### Differential: gatekeeper training vs related interventions
| Intervention | Who | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gatekeeper training | Lay or non-specialist | Identify and refer |
| Mental Health First Aid | Lay | Broader first-response to mental health crises |
| Physician education | GPs | Diagnose and treat depression (Gotland study: suicide reduction) |
| Crisis helplines | Trained volunteers or counsellors | Immediate support |
| Postvention | Bereaved | Support survivors, prevent clusters |

### Management details: post-referral pathway (district level)
- Gatekeeper refers to an ASHA or CHO, then to the **DMHP** psychiatric OPD. NSPS: suicide-prevention services in all districts within 5 years [1].
- Alternatively, **Tele-MANAS** for counselling, with escalation to a district team.
- Attempt survivors: liaison psychiatry and follow-up under **MHCA s115(2)** (State duty of care).

### Evidence table [general knowledge unless cited]
| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Isaac 2009 systematic review | Gatekeeper training improves knowledge and skills; limited outcome data |
| Knox 2003 (US Air Force) | Multi-component programme: about 33% suicide reduction |
| Hegerl 2006 (Nuremberg) | 4-level programme: about 24% reduction in suicidal acts |
| Wasserman 2015, SEYLE | QPR not effective; YAM effective in adolescents |
| Zalsman 2016 | Insufficient evidence for gatekeeper training alone |
| NSPS 2022 [1] | Recommends gatekeeper training as part of community resilience |

### Critical appraisal
- **Outcome measurement** is weak: most studies use surrogate outcomes.
- **Contamination and confounding** in community trials.
- Risk of **"training without services"**. India has about 0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000 [general knowledge].
- **Gatekeeper wellbeing**, and liability after a suicide following contact, are under-studied.
- Programmes developed in high-income countries may not fit the Indian context. Examples: family-centred decisions, gender (women's suicides linked to marriage and domestic violence), agrarian distress.

### Indian context
- **NSPS 2022:**
  - goal: **10% reduction in suicide mortality by 2030** (vs 2020);
  - REDS priority areas;
  - timelines: immediate 1–3 years, intermediate 4–7 years, long-term 8–10 years;
  - gatekeeper training of multiple community stakeholders [1].
- **Kota (Rajasthan)** coaching-centre measures, such as "gatekeeper" hostel wardens and spring-loaded fans [general knowledge].
- **SNEHA (Chennai)** befriending services [general knowledge].
- **Supreme Court (2025)** guidelines on student suicides direct institutions to train staff [general knowledge; verify].

### Recent advances
- **Digital and online GKT** (e-learning QPR; app-based refreshers).
- **Peer-gatekeeper** models in universities.
- Combining GKT with **safety planning** and **caring contacts**.
- **Machine-learning risk flags** in electronic records to support clinicians (experimental).
