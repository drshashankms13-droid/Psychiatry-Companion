## Exam answer

### Introduction

Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) is a research framework developed by the US National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) to investigate mental disorders using dimensions of functioning across multiple units of analysis. It was proposed to complement, not immediately replace, traditional diagnostic classifications such as DSM and ICD.

### Framework

RDoC organises research around broad functional domains:
1. **Negative valence systems:** responses to aversive situations, including fear, anxiety, loss and frustration.
2. **Positive valence systems:** reward responsiveness, reward learning, approach motivation and habit.
3. **Cognitive systems:** attention, perception, memory, language and cognitive control.
4. **Systems for social processes:** affiliation, attachment, social communication, perception and understanding of self and others.
5. **Arousal and regulatory systems:** arousal, circadian rhythms, sleep and homeostatic regulation.
6. **Sensorimotor systems:** increasingly incorporated into the framework to examine motor and action-related processes.

Each domain is studied across units of analysis: genes, molecules, cells, circuits, physiology, behaviour and self-report. The RDoC matrix is intended to be updated as scientific knowledge evolves.

### Applications and significance

RDoC encourages investigation of mechanisms that cut across diagnostic categories. For example, altered reward processing may be relevant to depression, substance use disorders and schizophrenia. It supports translational neuroscience, biomarker research and development of mechanistically informed interventions.

### Advantages

-   Moves beyond rigid categorical diagnoses.
-   Encourages dimensional assessment of psychopathology.
-   Integrates genetic, neural, behavioural and experiential data.
-   Facilitates research into shared mechanisms across disorders.
-   May support development of biologically informed treatment targets.

### Limitations

-   Primarily a research framework, not a clinical diagnostic system.
-   Risk of excessive neurobiological reductionism.
-   Complex and costly methods may limit applicability.
-   Social, cultural and developmental contexts may be underrepresented.
-   No validated clinical algorithm currently replaces DSM/ICD
    diagnosis.

### Conclusion

RDoC is an important framework for studying the dimensions and mechanisms underlying mental disorders. Its major contribution is to connect multiple levels of analysis rather than assume that current diagnostic categories map neatly onto discrete biological diseases.
