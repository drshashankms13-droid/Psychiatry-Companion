## Exam answer

### Definition

Pilowsky described abnormal illness behaviour as the persistence of an inappropriate or maladaptive mode of perceiving, evaluating or responding to one's health status, despite adequate medical reassurance and explanation.

It concerns the person's response to symptoms and illness, not simply whether a medical disease is present.

### Forms

-   Hypochondriacal disease conviction.
-   Disease phobia.
-   Thanatophobia or fear of death from illness.
-   Symptom amplification.
-   Abnormal illness attribution.
-   Denial or minimisation of illness.
-   Excessive help-seeking or repeated reassurance-seeking.
-   Inappropriate adoption or rejection of the sick role.

### Aetiology

A biopsychosocial model is useful. Factors include physical symptoms, anxiety sensitivity, prior illness experiences, family modelling, cultural beliefs, reinforcement, stress and difficulties regulating emotion. Leventhal's common-sense model explains how illness representations influence coping and help-seeking.

### Assessment

Obtain medical and psychiatric history, review investigations, assess functional impairment, illness beliefs, health-related anxiety, reassurance-seeking and healthcare utilisation. Exclude genuine medical illness and avoid dismissing symptoms as psychogenic without appropriate evaluation.

### Differential diagnosis

Consider illness anxiety disorder, somatic symptom disorder, functional neurological symptom disorder, factitious disorder, malingering, obsessive-compulsive disorder, depression and genuine medical conditions.

### Management

-   Establish a consistent therapeutic relationship.
-   Validate distress without reinforcing catastrophic interpretations.
-   Provide planned, regular follow-up.
-   Coordinate care through one principal clinician.
-   Avoid unnecessary investigations and repeated reassurance cycles.
-   Use CBT to modify catastrophic beliefs and checking/reassurance
    behaviours.
-   Treat comorbid anxiety or depression.
-   Involve family when appropriate.

### Conclusion

Abnormal illness behaviour is a maladaptive pattern of responding to perceived illness. A non-judgemental biopsychosocial approach, continuity of care and targeted psychological treatment are central to management.
