## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- **Definition:** frontotemporal dementia (FTD; frontotemporal NCD) is a group of clinical syndromes caused by **degeneration of the frontal and anterior temporal lobes**, with heterogeneous pathology (frontotemporal lobar degeneration) [1, 2].
- **Presentation:** progressive change in **personality, behaviour and/or language**, with relative sparing of memory and visuospatial function.
- **Pick's disease** is one *pathological* variant, not a clinical diagnosis [1].
- **Importance to psychiatry:** it presents with behavioural disorder and is **often mistaken for a primary psychiatric illness** [1, 2].

### Epidemiology [1, 2]
- **Prevalence:**
  - 3–15 per 100,000 at age 45–65, close to AD in this age band [1];
  - 2–31 per 100,000 [2];
  - **about 5% of dementias** at autopsy;
  - 20–25% of cases start after 65 [2].
- **Onset:** usually **45–70 years** (commonly the 50s; range 20s–80s).
- **Sex:** M ≥ F in some series; equal in others.
- **Course:** survival **6–11 years from onset** (3–4 years from diagnosis); **faster than AD**; shorter still with motor neurone disease (MND) [2].

### Clinical features [1, 2]

**1. Behavioural variant (bvFTD).** The commonest form: about 60% [2]; 40% in the Cambridge series [1].
- **DSM-5-TR:** three or more of:
  - **behavioural disinhibition**;
  - **apathy or inertia**;
  - **loss of sympathy or empathy**;
  - **perseverative, stereotyped or compulsive/ritualistic behaviour**;
  - **hyperorality and dietary change**;
  - **plus** prominent decline in social cognition and/or executive abilities.
- **Orbitobasal features:**
  - disinhibition, poor impulse control, **antisocial acts**, tactlessness;
  - jocularity or "**facile hilarity**".
- **Dorsolateral features:** apathy, inertia, loss of planning, distractibility, poor judgement.
- **Striatal features:** **compulsive/repetitive behaviour**, rituals (checking, counting), hoarding.
- **Klüver–Bucy features:** **hyperorality**, gluttony (food from others' plates).
- **Insight** is lost early; self-care declines; **early incontinence**.
- **Memory and orientation relatively preserved** (especially visual and spatial memory); executive tests impaired (Wisconsin Card Sorting, Stroop, verbal fluency).

**2. Semantic dementia (temporal variant):**
- loss of **word meaning** ("memory for words"); fluent but empty speech; impaired comprehension, often unrecognised by the patient;
- **category fluency** severely impaired;
- **right-sided atrophy: prosopagnosia**;
- remote more than recent memory affected; orientation, executive function and non-verbal reasoning preserved early;
- **bilateral but asymmetric (left > right) anterior and inferior temporal atrophy** [2].

**3. Progressive non-fluent (agrammatic) aphasia:**
- **effortful, dysfluent speech**, word-finding difficulty, agrammatism, spelling errors; comprehension relatively preserved;
- may remain a pure language disorder for **10–15 years**;
- **left posterior frontal–insular atrophy** [2];
- pathology variable (sometimes AD).

**Also:** the **logopenic** variant (left temporoparietal) is usually **AD pathology** [2].

**Neurological signs:**
- often none early;
- **primitive reflexes** (grasp, pout, suck); extensor plantars;
- late akinesia and rigidity.
- **Overlaps:**
  - **FTD–MND**: fasciculations, wasting, dysarthria, dysphagia; faster course;
  - **PSP/CBD** (tauopathies): parkinsonism, gaze palsy, apraxia;
  - visual hallucinations in a subset [2].

**Presentation to psychiatry (misdiagnosis)** [1, 2]:
- **personality disorder** (especially after a life event);
- alcohol misuse;
- **hypomania/bipolar** (disinhibition, elevated mood);
- depression or anxiety (apathy, hypochondria);
- **OCD** (compulsions, lacking insight);
- **schizophrenia**.

Lishman's cases: a 42-year-old woman with counting rituals; a 40-year-old accountant labelled "obsessional neurosis"; a 44-year-old professional suspended from work.

### Aetiopathogenesis and genetics [1, 2]

**Pathology:**
- **Gross:**
  - circumscribed **frontal and temporal "knife-blade" atrophy** (orbital frontal; temporal pole, sparing the posterior superior temporal gyrus);
  - parietal and occipital lobes spared;
  - dilated horns; caudate atrophy.
- **Histological subtypes:**
  1. **Microvacuolar type** (commonest): superficial laminar spongiosis, little gliosis, no inclusions.
  2. **Pick type:** marked gliosis; swollen (ballooned) neurones; **tau- and ubiquitin-positive Pick bodies**.
  3. **Microvacuolar with MND.**
- **Molecular classification:**
  - **Tauopathies (FTLD-tau):**
    - **3R tau in Pick's disease**; **4R tau in PSP and CBD**;
    - **FTDP-17 (MAPT mutations)**;
    - **mechanism**: mutations in or near the microtubule-binding domains impair function, while **exon 10 splice mutations alter the 3R:4R ratio**. Tau then detaches, becomes hyperphosphorylated and aggregates, disrupting **axonal transport**.
  - **Tau-negative, ubiquitin-positive inclusions:** now known to involve **TDP-43** [2].

**Genetics:**
- **Family history** in about 40%; **autosomal dominant inheritance in about 10%** [2]. Lishman: 20–40% familial [1].
- **Genes** [2]:

| Gene | Protein / notes |
| --- | --- |
| ***MAPT*** (chromosome 17) | Microtubule-associated protein tau: FTDP-17; tau pathology [1, 2] |
| ***GRN*** | Progranulin [2] |
| ***C9orf72*** | [2]. Lishman (2009) notes only linkage of familial MND with dementia to chromosome 9, gene then unidentified [1] |
| ***TARDBP*** | TDP-43 [2] |
| ***VCP*** | Valosin-containing protein [2] |
| ***CHMP2B*** | Chromatin-modifying protein 2B [2] |
| ***FUS*** | Fused in sarcoma [2] |

- Many familial cases have **no known mutation** [2].
- **Probable FTD (DSM-5-TR)** requires a **causative mutation** or **disproportionate frontotemporal involvement on imaging** [2].

### Investigations [1, 2]
- **EEG:** **normal even late** (a useful marker vs AD and CJD).
- **CT/MRI:**
  - frontal and/or anterior temporal atrophy (medial frontal plus anterior temporal in bvFTD);
  - asymmetric temporal (semantic variant);
  - left frontoinsular (non-fluent variant).
- **SPECT/FDG-PET:** **anterior hypoperfusion**, even when the structural scan is near normal.
- **AD biomarkers** (CSF Aβ/tau, amyloid PET) to exclude AD: 10–30% of clinical FTD has AD pathology at autopsy [2].
- **Genetic testing** in familial cases.

### Management [3, 1]
- **No disease-modifying or cognitive treatment.**
  - **NICE: do not offer AChE-Is or memantine.**
  - BAP: no first or second choice.
  - **AChE-Is may worsen agitation** [3].
- **Behavioural symptoms:** **SSRIs and trazodone** may reduce behavioural symptoms, though not cognition [3]. Molecular therapies are in early research.
- **Non-pharmacological:**
  - structured routines;
  - **supervision** for disinhibition and hyperorality (food access, finances, sexual behaviour);
  - environmental management; carer education.
- **Social and legal:**
  - early-onset effects on **work, family, finances**;
  - **legal and forensic problems** (antisocial acts);
  - **driving**;
  - lasting power of attorney and capacity while possible;
  - benefits;
  - **young-onset dementia services**;
  - carer support and respite (high burden; may need residential care despite physical health) [1, 2].
- **Genetic testing** in familial cases helps confirm the diagnosis [2]. Counselling protocols for FTD are not described in the sources.

### FTD vs Alzheimer's disease [1, 2]

| Feature | FTD | AD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age at onset | Usually **presenile (45–65)** | Mostly elderly |
| Presenting feature | **Personality, behaviour or language change** | **Memory loss** |
| Insight | Lost early | Partly retained early |
| Memory and orientation | **Relatively preserved early** (spatial memory preserved) | **Early amnesia**, disorientation |
| Parietal signs (dysphasia, apraxia, agnosia, acalculia, visuospatial) | Uncommon | Common (second stage) |
| Executive and social cognition | **Early, prominent** | Later |
| Klüver–Bucy / hyperorality | **Early** | Late |
| Mood | **Facile hilarity, aspontaneity**, disinhibition | Depressed, anxious, overactive |
| Compulsions and stereotypies | Common | Uncommon |
| Circadian disturbance | Relatively lacking | Common |
| Early incontinence | Suggestive | Late |
| Gait and EPS | Uncommon early (except in overlap syndromes) | Late |
| **EEG** | **Normal** even late | Early alpha loss, then slowing |
| Imaging | **Frontal and anterior temporal atrophy; anterior hypoperfusion** | Hippocampal/medial temporal atrophy; **posterior temporoparietal** hypoperfusion |
| Genetics | MAPT, GRN, C9orf72; about 40% family history | APP, PSEN1/2 (rare); APOE ε4 |
| Course | Faster (median 6–11 years) | 2–8+ years |
| AChE-Is | **Not effective; may worsen agitation** | Effective (modest) |

The distinction is **easiest early**; later, differences blur. Some AD presents frontally (anterior hypoperfusion) [1].

**Conclusion.** FTD is an important early-onset dementia:
- **behavioural variant**: disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy, compulsions, hyperorality, executive failure with preserved memory;
- **language variants**: semantic and non-fluent aphasia.

It arises from frontotemporal lobar degeneration (tau, TDP-43 or FUS pathologies), with a strong genetic contribution (*MAPT*, *GRN*, *C9orf72*). It is often misdiagnosed as primary psychiatric disorder. Diagnosis rests on the clinical picture, normal EEG and frontotemporal atrophy or hypoperfusion. There is no disease-modifying or cognitive treatment (AChE-Is are ineffective). Management is SSRIs or trazodone for behaviour, supervision, legal and financial planning, and carer support.

## In depth

### Background
- **Arnold Pick (1892)** described circumscribed lobar atrophy with aphasia. **Alzheimer (1911)** described the argyrophilic inclusions (**Pick bodies**) and ballooned cells [1; general knowledge for dates].
- Swedish (Lund) and Manchester groups described "frontal lobe dementia of non-Alzheimer type". This led to the **Lund–Manchester criteria (1994)** and **Neary criteria (1998)** [1; general knowledge for criteria names].
- **Hodges (Cambridge)** delineated three forms: **frontal variant, semantic dementia, progressive non-fluent aphasia** [1].
- **1998:** **MAPT** mutations in FTDP-17. **2006:** **GRN** mutations and **TDP-43** as the ubiquitinated protein. **2011:** **C9orf72** hexanucleotide repeat expansion, the commonest genetic cause of FTD and ALS [1; general knowledge for 2006 and 2011].
- **2011:** international consensus criteria for **bvFTD (Rascovsky)** and **PPA (Gorno-Tempini)** [general knowledge].

### Epidemiology
- **3–15 per 100,000 at age 45–65**, comparable to AD in this age band [1]. Prevalence estimates range from 2 to 31 per 100,000 [2].
- **The second commonest young-onset dementia** after AD [general knowledge].
- **Familial in 20–40%** [1]; autosomal dominant in about 10% [2].
- **Survival:** 6–11 years from onset; shorter with MND (about 2–3 years) [2; general knowledge for MND].
- **India:** in young-onset dementia clinic series (NIMHANS, Kerala), FTD makes up about **15–25%** of cases, with **bvFTD predominant** [general knowledge].

### Classification
**Clinical syndromes** [1, 2; general knowledge for the PPA naming]:
| Syndrome | Current name | Atrophy | Usual pathology |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frontal variant FTD | **bvFTD** | Orbitofrontal, anterior cingulate, insula, anterior temporal | Tau or TDP-43 (about half each); FUS rare |
| Semantic dementia | **Semantic variant PPA (svPPA)** | **Left > right anterior temporal** | **TDP-43 type C** (about 80%) |
| Progressive non-fluent aphasia | **Non-fluent/agrammatic variant PPA (nfvPPA)** | Left posterior frontoinsular | **Tau** (CBD, PSP, Pick) |
| (Logopenic PPA) | lvPPA | Left temporoparietal | **AD pathology** [2] |
| Right temporal variant | Right svPPA / rtvFTD | Right anterior temporal | TDP-43 C: prosopagnosia, behavioural rigidity, loss of empathy [1] |
| FTD-MND | FTD-ALS | Frontotemporal + motor neurone | TDP-43 type B; C9orf72 |
| Overlap | CBS, PSP-S | | 4R tau [1] |

**Neuropathology (FTLD)** [1; general knowledge for subtypes]:
- **FTLD-tau (about 40%):**
  - **3R**: Pick's disease;
  - **4R**: CBD, PSP, argyrophilic grain disease, globular glial tauopathy;
  - **3R + 4R**: some MAPT mutations.
- **FTLD-TDP (about 50%):**
  - **type A** (GRN; bvFTD and nfvPPA);
  - **type B** (C9orf72; FTD-MND);
  - **type C** (svPPA);
  - **type D** (VCP; inclusion body myopathy with Paget's disease);
  - **type E** (rapid course).
- **FTLD-FUS (about 5–10%):** young onset, severe behavioural change, **caudate atrophy**, no family history.
- **FTLD-UPS (CHMP2B):** rare (Danish family).

Lishman's older histological scheme (microvacuolar, Pick type, microvacuolar with MND) maps onto these molecular classes [1].

### International bvFTD criteria (Rascovsky 2011) [general knowledge]
- **Possible bvFTD:** at least 3 of 6:
  - **A.** early **disinhibition** (socially inappropriate behaviour; loss of manners; impulsive, rash acts);
  - **B.** early **apathy or inertia**;
  - **C.** early **loss of sympathy or empathy**;
  - **D.** early **perseverative, stereotyped or compulsive/ritualistic** behaviour;
  - **E.** **hyperorality** and dietary change (sweet preference, binge eating, oral exploration);
  - **F.** **neuropsychological profile**: executive deficits with **relative sparing of episodic memory and visuospatial function**.
- **Probable bvFTD:** possible bvFTD **plus** significant functional decline **plus** imaging (frontal and/or anterior temporal atrophy, hypoperfusion or hypometabolism).
- **Definite bvFTD:** histopathology or a known pathogenic mutation.
- **Exclusions:** better explained by another disorder, including a **psychiatric diagnosis**.
- DSM-5-TR criteria are closely aligned (3 or more behavioural features, or social cognition/executive decline) [2].

### Mechanism in detail
**Tau [1]:**
- six isoforms from alternative splicing (3R or 4R; 0, 1 or 2 N-terminal inserts);
- **MAPT** mutations either reduce microtubule binding (near the binding domains) or alter **exon 10 splicing** (the 3R:4R ratio);
- tau then detaches, is hyperphosphorylated and aggregates, so **axonal transport fails**.

**TDP-43 and the genes [1, 2; general knowledge for details]:**
- **TDP-43** (TARDBP) is a nuclear RNA-binding protein. In disease it **mislocalises to the cytoplasm**, causing loss of nuclear function (cryptic exon splicing, e.g., **STMN2, UNC13A**) and toxic gain.
- **GRN** loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency) reduces **progranulin**, causing **lysosomal dysfunction** and neuroinflammation. Plasma progranulin is low in carriers.
- **C9orf72** GGGGCC repeat expansion acts through:
  - RNA foci;
  - **dipeptide repeat proteins** (repeat-associated non-AUG translation);
  - loss of C9orf72 function.
- **Network degeneration:** bvFTD targets the **salience network** (anterior insula, anterior cingulate, frontoinsular von Economo neurones). This explains loss of empathy, social-emotional processing failure and interoceptive deficits (Seeley) [general knowledge].

**Behavioural–anatomical correlations [1]:**
- **orbitobasal**: disinhibition, antisocial acts;
- **dorsolateral**: executive failure and apathy;
- **striatal-predominant** pathology: compulsions (Snowden).

### Clinical features in detail
**Genetic phenotypes** [general knowledge]:
| Gene | Approximate share of familial FTD | Distinctive features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ***C9orf72*** | 25–40% familial; 5–10% sporadic | **Psychosis (delusions, hallucinations: about 20–40%)**, somatic delusions, **bizarre behaviour**; FTD-ALS; slow-progressor "phenocopy-like" cases; thalamic and cerebellar atrophy; often mistaken for **schizophrenia or bipolar disorder** |
| ***GRN*** | 5–20% | **Asymmetric** atrophy; nfvPPA or bvFTD; CBS; parkinsonism; apathy; white-matter lesions; later onset (about 60) |
| ***MAPT*** | 5–20% | Earlier onset (40s–50s); **semantic and behavioural** features; symmetric anteromedial temporal atrophy; parkinsonism |
| *VCP* | Rare | Inclusion body myopathy, Paget's disease |
| *TBK1*, *CHMP2B*, *TARDBP*, *FUS* | Rare | FTD-ALS (TBK1); Danish family (CHMP2B) |

**Psychiatric phenomenology** [1, 2; general knowledge]:
- **Compulsions** differ from OCD: they are **ego-syntonic**, simple and stereotyped (clapping, humming, collecting, rigid routines, "clock-watching"). Insight is absent. Lishman's accountant was diagnosed with obsessional neurosis though he lacked insight [1].
- **Emotional changes:** loss of empathy; reduced embarrassment; **emotional blunting**; jocularity ("Witzelsucht").
- **Eating:** carbohydrate craving; food cramming; **eating non-food objects** late.
- **Hypersexuality** and disinhibited sexual behaviour; **hoarding**.
- **Antisocial acts:** shoplifting, traffic offences, inappropriate sexual comments. These are a **forensic risk** in previously law-abiding middle-aged people.
- **Depression** is less frequent than in AD, but apathy mimics it.
- **Somatic complaints** and **hypochondriasis** (Lishman's 42-year-old woman with stomach pains) [1].
- **Psychosis** is common in C9orf72 carriers; uncommon otherwise.

**Neuropsychological profile [1]:**
- executive deficits (WCST, Stroop, verbal fluency). These tests are **dorsolateral-sensitive** and may miss **orbitofrontal** disease [1].
- **Social cognition tests** detect orbitofrontal disease better [general knowledge]:
  - **Mini-SEA** (Social cognition and Emotional Assessment);
  - faux-pas test;
  - emotion recognition;
  - Iowa Gambling Task.

### Assessment
- **Informant history is essential.** The patient lacks insight [1].
- **Scales** [general knowledge]:
  - **Frontal Behavioural Inventory (FBI)**;
  - Cambridge Behavioural Inventory (CBI-R);
  - NPI;
  - **FAB** (Frontal Assessment Battery);
  - **ACE-III** (fluency and naming subscores; ACE-III/M-ACE better than MMSE);
  - Mini-SEA;
  - the **FTLD-CDR** (CDR plus behaviour and language).
- **Imaging [1, 2]:**
  - MRI: frontal or anterior temporal atrophy (knife-blade);
  - **FDG-PET or HMPAO-SPECT**: anterior hypometabolism even with normal MRI [1].
- **EEG:** normal [1].
- **AD biomarkers** (CSF Aβ42/p-tau; amyloid PET; plasma p-tau217) to exclude **frontal-variant AD** [2; general knowledge for plasma].
- **Genetic testing:** C9orf72, GRN, MAPT if there is a family history (dementia, ALS, psychiatric illness, suicide) or **early onset** [general knowledge].
- **Plasma progranulin** (low in GRN carriers); **neurofilament light** (raised in FTD vs psychiatric disorders) [general knowledge].
- **Exclude mimics:** depression, bipolar disorder, late-onset schizophrenia, autism (adult), **brain tumour (frontal meningioma)**, neurosyphilis, HD, **thiamine deficiency** [1; general knowledge].

### Differential diagnosis: bvFTD vs primary psychiatric disorders [1; general knowledge]
| Feature | bvFTD | Primary psychiatric disorder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Usually after 45, no prior history | Earlier; prior episodes |
| Course | **Progressive**, relentless | Episodic; fluctuating |
| Insight | **Absent** | Variable |
| Compulsions | Simple, ego-syntonic | Ego-dystonic (OCD) |
| Hyperorality | Common | Rare |
| Social cognition | Severely impaired | Relatively preserved (except autism, schizophrenia) |
| Imaging | Frontal/anterior temporal atrophy; hypometabolism | Normal |
| NfL | Raised | Normal |
| Treatment response | Poor | Responds to antidepressants, antipsychotics, lithium |

**bvFTD "phenocopy" syndrome** [general knowledge]:
- meets clinical criteria but has **normal imaging and no progression** over years;
- some are slow-progressing **C9orf72** carriers; others have psychiatric or personality causes;
- needs longitudinal follow-up (the **Ducharme 2020 Neuropsychiatric International Consortium** recommendations).

**Other differentials [1]:** personality disorder (after a life event), alcoholism, hypomania, depression or anxiety, OCD, HD; AD with frontal presentation.

### Management details
**Pharmacological** [3; general knowledge for trials]:
- **SSRIs** (citalopram, sertraline, paroxetine, fluvoxamine) for **disinhibition, compulsions and hyperorality**: small trials and open studies [3].
- **Trazodone 150–300 mg/day** reduced NPI scores in an RCT (Lebert 2004) [3; general knowledge for the trial].
- **Antipsychotics:** only for severe agitation or psychosis; low dose (quetiapine). FTD patients have **heightened EPS sensitivity**; mortality warning applies.
- **Avoid cholinesterase inhibitors** (they may worsen behaviour). **Memantine is ineffective** (Boxer 2013 RCT) [3].
- **Oxytocin** (intranasal) for empathy and apathy: small trials, mixed results [general knowledge].
- **FTD-ALS:** riluzole and multidisciplinary ALS care.

**Non-pharmacological [1, 3; general knowledge]:**
- **structured routines**; use preserved habits and routines;
- **environmental modification**: lock food cupboards, remove car keys, supervise finances;
- **caregiver education and support** (higher burden than in AD; young families, financial loss);
- **speech and language therapy** for PPA (lexical retrieval training in svPPA; communication aids);
- physiotherapy for FTD-ALS;
- **social care**: young-onset dementia services, respite, residential care.

**Legal and ethical:**
- **capacity** (financial and testamentary);
- driving cessation;
- employment (early retirement, disability benefits);
- **forensic** issues (diversion from the criminal justice system);
- **genetic counselling** and predictive testing (HD-like protocols);
- **MHCA 2017** advance directives while capacity remains;
- **RPwD Act 2016** certification [general knowledge].

### Critical appraisal
- **Clinical–pathological correlation is imperfect**: bvFTD can be tau, TDP-43, FUS or even AD [1, 2].
- **Executive tests miss orbitofrontal dysfunction**; early cases may test "normal" [1].
- **Phenocopy and psychiatric mimic** problems mean criteria specificity in psychiatric settings is lower than in neurology clinics [general knowledge].
- **Treatment evidence** is from small, short trials [3].
- **Lishman (2009)** predates C9orf72, TDP-43 subtyping and the 2011 criteria [1].

### Indian context [general knowledge]
- FTD is **under-recognised**. Many patients spend years in psychiatric care with diagnoses of mania, OCD or personality disorder.
- **Genetic testing** (C9orf72, GRN, MAPT) is available at a few centres (NIMHANS, CMC Vellore, commercial laboratories). Cost limits access.
- **Families bear the care burden.** Young-onset dementia services are almost absent.
- **Cultural interpretation** of behavioural change (spirit possession, moral failing) delays help-seeking.
- **Disability certification** (RPwD Act 2016) and pension access are important.

### Recent advances [general knowledge]
- **Plasma NfL** distinguishes bvFTD from primary psychiatric disorders (high specificity).
- **GRN-targeted therapies:**
  - **latozinemab** (anti-sortilin antibody, AL001; phase 3 INFRONT-3 did not meet its clinical endpoint in 2025, verify);
  - **AAV-progranulin** gene therapy (PR006, AVB-101).
- **C9orf72 antisense oligonucleotides** (BIIB078 discontinued); new approaches targeting dipeptide repeats.
- **4R tau PET** ligands (PI-2620) for PSP and CBD spectrum.
- **ALLFTD** and **GENFI** cohorts define pre-symptomatic biomarker trajectories.
