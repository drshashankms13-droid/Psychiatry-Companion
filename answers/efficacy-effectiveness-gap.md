## Exam answer

### Definitions

Efficacy asks whether an intervention works under ideal, controlled conditions. Effectiveness asks whether it works in routine practice among diverse patients, clinicians, and services. Efficiency concerns outcomes relative to resources. The efficacy–effectiveness gap is the difference between trial outcomes and real-world results.

### Reasons for the gap

1. **Patient selection:** Trials may exclude
comorbidity, substance use, suicidality, medical illness, or complex
social needs.
2. **Delivery:** Research settings may provide expert
clinicians, close supervision, protocol fidelity, and frequent follow-up
unavailable in routine care.
3. **Adherence/acceptability:** Side
effects, cost, stigma, travel, and competing demands reduce adherence.
4. **Outcome choice:** Trials may focus on symptom scales and short
follow-up; practice must also consider functioning, quality of life,
relapse, and patient priorities.
5. **Service constraints:** Waiting
lists, staff shortages, limited psychotherapy, medicine stock-outs, and
fragmented referrals affect outcomes.
6. **Culture/context:** Language,
family structure, explanatory models, and socioeconomic conditions
influence engagement.
7. **Publication bias:** Positive findings may be
more visible than null findings.

### Examples

An antidepressant may show benefit in selected trial participants but lower remission in routine care because of comorbidity, non-adherence, and inadequate follow-up. A psychotherapy may be efficacious with trained therapists but unavailable or difficult to complete in underserved areas.

### Bridging the gap

Use pragmatic trials; include representative patients; apply implementation science; adapt interventions culturally while preserving core ingredients; strengthen shared decision-making, measurement-based care, adherence support, training, supervision, and task-sharing; measure patient-centred outcomes and harms. PRECIS-2 helps characterize trial pragmatism.

### Indian context

Treatment gaps, uneven specialist distribution, affordability, travel, stigma, and limited psychotherapy access may widen the gap. Primary-care integration, community health workers, tele-mental health, and supervised task-sharing can improve reach if quality and referral pathways are maintained.

### Conclusion

Efficacy shows an intervention can work; effectiveness shows whether it works in real-world care. Both are necessary.
