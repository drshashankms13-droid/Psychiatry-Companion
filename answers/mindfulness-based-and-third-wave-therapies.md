## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Behaviour therapy has developed in three "waves" [1]:
1. **First wave** (1950s–60s) — behaviourism: classical and operant conditioning; only observable behaviour (Wolpe, Skinner).
2. **Second wave** (1960s–70s) — cognitive therapy: changing the **content** of thoughts (Beck's CBT, Ellis' rational emotive therapy); today the dominant psychotherapy worldwide.
3. **Third wave** (1990s onwards) — changes the **relationship** to thoughts and emotions rather than their content, using **acceptance, mindfulness, metacognition, values, dialectics and the therapeutic relationship** [1].

The third wave grew partly from **Jacobson's component analysis**, in which behavioural activation alone was as effective as full CBT for depression; **Dimidjian (2006)** then showed behavioural activation was as effective as antidepressants and more effective than cognitive therapy in severe depression [1].

**Mindfulness** is paying attention in a particular way — **on purpose, in the present moment and non-judgementally** (Kabat-Zinn's definition, cited in DBT) [2].

### The main third-wave therapies [1]

| Therapy | Developer | Core idea | Main indications |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Behavioural activation (BA)** | Jacobson & Martell; Hopko & Lejuez | Depression persists through loss of positive reinforcement and avoidance; schedule valued, rewarding activities | Depression (including severe), grief, anxiety; deliverable by nurses |
| **Acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT)** | Steven Hayes, 1990s | Suffering comes from **experiential avoidance**; build **psychological flexibility**; act on values | Depression, anxiety, chronic pain, psychosis |
| **Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR)** | Jon Kabat-Zinn (1979) | Meditation, body scan, yoga for stress and chronic illness | Stress, chronic pain, anxiety |
| **Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT)** | Segal, Williams & Teasdale (mid-1990s) | MBSR + CBT; "thoughts are not facts"; **decentring** from ruminative thinking | **Relapse prevention in recurrent depression** |
| **Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT)** | Marsha Linehan | Acceptance + change; skills (mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness) | Borderline personality disorder, suicidality |
| **CBASP** | James McCullough, late 1990s | Situational analysis; interpersonal discrimination; uses therapeutic relationship | Chronic depression |
| **Functional analytic psychotherapy (FAP)** | Kohlenberg & Tsai | Shape interpersonal behaviour within the therapy relationship | Interpersonal problems |
| **Metacognitive therapy, compassion-focused therapy** | Wells; Gilbert | Target worry about thinking; self-criticism and shame | GAD, depression, shame (author's knowledge) |

### Mindfulness-based therapies — format and components [1]

- **MBSR / MBCT:** usually **8 weekly sessions of about 2–3 hours** plus a **day-long retreat**; abbreviated formats also show benefit.
- Practices: **raisin exercise** (mindful eating), sitting meditation, **body scan**, mindful walking, **urge surfing**, yoga, 3-minute breathing space, daily home practice.
- Principles: attention regulation; openness, curiosity and acceptance of the here and now; non-judgemental awareness of thoughts, emotions and sensations.

### Mechanism

Mindfulness → attention regulation + **decentring** ("I am not my thoughts") + acceptance → reduced **rumination** and experiential avoidance → less reactivity to negative mood → reduced relapse into depression and lower anxiety [1].

ACT's **hexaflex** — acceptance, cognitive defusion, present-moment contact, self-as-context, values, committed action (author's knowledge) — summarised by **FEAR** (fusion, evaluation, avoidance, reason-giving) vs **ACT** (accept, choose a valued direction, take action) [1].

### Evidence

- **MBCT halves relapse** risk in people with **three or more previous depressive episodes** (Teasdale 2000; Ma & Teasdale 2004) and is comparable to maintenance antidepressants (Kuyken 2015, PREVENT) (author's knowledge).
- MBSR and MBCT show promise for anxiety, depression and other problems [1].
- **BA** as effective as antidepressants and more effective than cognitive therapy in severe depression [1].
- **DBT** has the strongest evidence for BPD and self-harm [2].
- **ACT** has RCT support in chronic pain, depression and anxiety (author's knowledge).

### Indications and cautions

- **Indications:** recurrent depression (MBCT), stress and chronic illness (MBSR), BPD (DBT), chronic depression (CBASP), anxiety, chronic pain, substance use (urge surfing), psychosis-related distress (ACT).
- **Cautions:** acute severe depression, active psychosis, recent trauma — intensive meditation can increase distress, dissociation or re-experiencing; adapt practice and screen participants (author's knowledge).

**Conclusion.** Third-wave therapies shift from challenging thought content to changing one's relationship with thoughts and feelings through mindfulness, acceptance and values-based action. Mindfulness-based therapies — especially MBCT for preventing depressive relapse — and DBT and BA have good evidence, while others are promising. They broaden the CBT family rather than replace it.

## In depth

### Background

- First-wave behaviour therapy treated only observable behaviour; second-wave CBT added cognition; the third wave reintroduced topics previously left to other traditions — emotion, acceptance, spirituality, the therapeutic relationship [1].
- Hayes described third-wave therapies as "grounded in empiricism, principle-focused, sensitive to the context and functions of psychological phenomena", building broad, flexible repertoires rather than eliminating narrow problems [1].
- Mindfulness derives from Buddhist meditation (*sati*, *vipassana*) and Christian contemplative traditions [2].

### Behavioural activation in detail [1]

- Model: low response-contingent reinforcement + reinforcement of depressed behaviour + aversive experiences → withdrawal → further loss of reinforcement.
- Steps: functional assessment of depressed behaviour → motivation (pros and cons) → psychoeducation → activity monitoring (reward, difficulty, importance) → values and goals across life areas → **activity hierarchy of 15 activities** from easiest to hardest → weekly graded assignments.
- 8–20 sessions of about 1 hour; effective even when delivered by previously untrained mental health nurses.

### ACT in detail [1]

- Originally "comprehensive distancing"; uses metaphor, paradox and experiential exercises.
- Goal: **psychological flexibility** — contacting the present moment fully and changing or persisting in behaviour in the service of chosen values.

### MBCT in detail [1]

- Combines MBSR with CBT elements that encourage decentred views ("thoughts are not facts").
- Unlike CBT, does **not** try to change thought content.
- Designed for people **in remission** to prevent relapse, not for acute depression (author's knowledge).

### CBASP in detail [1]

Three techniques: **situational analysis** (consequences of one's behaviour on others), **interpersonal discrimination exercises** (distinguish past traumatic relationships from the therapist relationship), **behavioural skill training/rehearsal**.

### Comparison of waves

| Feature | First wave | Second wave | Third wave |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target | Behaviour | Thought content | Relationship to thoughts and emotions |
| Key techniques | Exposure, reinforcement | Cognitive restructuring | Mindfulness, acceptance, defusion, values |
| Stance on symptoms | Eliminate | Correct distortions | Accept and act despite them |
| Examples | Systematic desensitisation, token economy | Beck's CBT, REBT | ACT, MBCT, DBT, BA, CBASP |

### Critical appraisal

- Third-wave therapies are heterogeneous; whether they are a true "wave" or extensions of CBT is debated (author's knowledge).
- Many trials are small, with waiting-list controls and developer allegiance; effects versus active treatments are often modest.
- Mechanism research (does mindfulness mediate change?) is still developing.
- Commercial "mindfulness" programmes may dilute evidence-based protocols.

### Indian context

- Mindfulness and yoga have deep roots in Indian contemplative traditions (*dhyana*, *vipassana*), which can increase acceptability.
- NIMHANS and other centres have studied yoga as adjunctive treatment in depression and schizophrenia (author's knowledge).
- Group MBCT/MBSR and BA delivered by trained non-specialists are scalable in Indian settings.
- Religious meaning of meditation should be discussed sensitively; secular framing helps across faiths.

### Recent advances

- Digital and app-based mindfulness programmes; online MBCT (author's knowledge).
- MBCT for current depression and for anxiety, bipolar disorder and suicidality under study.
- Process-based therapy approaches focusing on mechanisms across therapies (author's knowledge).
