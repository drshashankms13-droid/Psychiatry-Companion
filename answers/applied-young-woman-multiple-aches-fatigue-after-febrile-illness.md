## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 26-year-old homemaker has had **multiple aches and pains for 3 years** and **tires with routine work**.
- **Onset:** after a **febrile illness**.
- **Mood:** she has felt **sad since her father died a year ago**.
- **Alcohol:** she drinks, mostly at **weekends with her husband**.

### 1. Differential diagnosis (from the sources)
1. **Fibromyalgia** [1]:
   - widespread musculoskeletal pain with tenderness and stiffness, reflecting **central sensitisation**;
   - with **fatigue, poor sleep, cognitive difficulty and depression**;
   - should be suspected in **multifocal pain without a definable cause**;
   - diagnosed in 2–8% of the population, though criteria are debated.
2. **Depressive disorder with somatic presentation** [1, 2]:
   - about **60% of depressed patients complain of pain**, and depression carries about a **fourfold** higher prevalence of chronic pain;
   - "**presentation bias**": only **22%** of patients presenting with purely somatic complaints were correctly diagnosed with depression or anxiety, vs 77% of those presenting psychosocially;
   - her **sadness since her father's death** (1 year) needs evaluation for MDD or prolonged grief versus normal grief.
3. **Somatic symptom disorder / multisomatoform presentation** [1]:
   - DSM-5 SSD no longer requires symptoms to be medically unexplained;
   - subsyndromal **multisomatoform** presentations are commoner than the full disorder in multiple unexplained pain.
4. **Chronic fatigue presentation after infection** [3]:
   - K&S notes that fibromyalgia and **chronic fatigue syndrome** show CNS dysfunction and possibly genetic contributions, and are now **established medical diagnoses**;
   - historically, such complaints were grouped under "neurasthenia".
5. **Alcohol-related contribution** [1]:
   - substance use disorder is a recognised comorbidity of chronic pain (the order of frequency is depression, then anxiety, somatoform disorders and SUD);
   - **quantify her drinking**, and check whether it is used to cope with pain or grief.
6. **Medical causes to exclude:** pain is often a manifestation of systemic disease, so **examine all systems** [1].

### 2. Assessment [1, 2]
- **Pain history:**
  - quality, site and number of sites;
  - temporal pattern; aggravating and relieving factors;
  - **interference with daily function**;
  - response to previous treatment.
- Rate on a **0–10 scale or VAS**, and repeat the same scale over time.
- **Psychiatric history:**
  - depression: core symptoms, hopelessness, guilt, **suicidality** (risk is raised 2–3-fold in chronic pain);
  - anxiety and PTSD;
  - grief;
  - **alcohol and other substances**;
  - personality vulnerabilities;
  - work and legal history.
- **Cognitions:**
  - **catastrophising**;
  - **fear-avoidance** (avoiding activity for fear of pain or injury);
  - beliefs about the illness ("doctors think it is imaginary"; "a mysterious disease");
  - self-efficacy and locus of control.
- **Physical examination:** all systems, plus a focused neurological and musculoskeletal examination.
- **Investigations:** be selective. **Imaging correlates poorly with pain**, and repeated examinations can increase fear [1, 2].
- **Symptom diary** linking symptoms with stressors, sleep and activity [2].

### 3. Management [1, 2]
**Engagement:**
- Build a **therapeutic relationship**: elicit beliefs and expectations, empathise, educate, negotiate a plan.
- **Validate** that the pain is real. "Psychogenic pain with no real cause" is a misconception, and psychological and physical factors interrelate.
- Give **reassurance** and focus on **function rather than cure**.
- **Limit investigations** [2].

**Treat comorbidity concurrently:**
- Treat depression **aggressively**: treating it reduces pain and improves function independently [1].
- Address grief and alcohol use.

**Pharmacotherapy [1]:**
- **SNRIs:** duloxetine and milnacipran are effective in fibromyalgia.
- **Gabapentin / pregabalin:** effective in fibromyalgia.
- Muscle relaxants and tramadol (the latter carries an abuse risk).
- **TCAs:** low dose, titrated.
- **Avoid benzodiazepines.** They are linked to lower activity, more disability and depression, and are dangerous with opioids. Alcohol use adds risk.

**Psychological [1]:**
- **CBT:** relaxation, activity pacing and graded increase, cognitive restructuring of catastrophising, coping skills. Effects last more than 6 months.
- **Operant approach:** reinforce healthy behaviour rather than pain behaviour, and involve her husband.
- **Acceptance-based (third-wave) approaches.** Acceptance predicts better adjustment.
- **Multidisciplinary rehabilitation** if function stays poor.

**Alcohol:** brief assessment and advice. Address joint drinking with her husband, and avoid sedative combinations [1].

**Follow-up:** reassess mood, function and suicidality. Use the same rating scale over time.

## In depth

### Why the psychiatric angle matters here [1, 2]
- Up to **two-thirds** of chronic pain patients have psychiatric comorbidity, which worsens prognosis.
- Baseline depression predicts future pain and disability better than initial pain ratings do.
- The more physical symptoms a patient reports, the higher the rate of mood or anxiety disorder: 4% with 0–1 symptoms, 78% with 9 or more (Kroenke) [2].
