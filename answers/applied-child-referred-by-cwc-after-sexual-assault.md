## Exam answer

**Case summary.** An **8-year-old girl** has been referred to the child psychiatry OPD by the **Child Welfare Committee (CWC)**. A **neighbour** allegedly sexually assaulted her **a few days ago**. Her **parents and a counsellor** (probably the CWC-appointed **support person**) accompany her.

### Legal framing
- **POCSO Act 2012** applies (child under 18).
  - Because she is **below 12**, any assault is "**aggravated**" (Sections 5/9).
- **The referral is via the CWC**, so the case has already entered the child protection system:
  - a sexually abused child is a "**child in need of care and protection**" under the **JJ Act 2015**;
  - the police must inform the CWC **within 24 hours** [1, 3].
- **Check before you start:**
  - Has an **FIR** been registered? (Section 19 mandatory reporting.)
  - Has the **medical examination** under **Section 27 / CrPC 164A** been done?
  - Has the statement been recorded (Sections 24–26)?
  - Has a **support person** been assigned (Rule 4)?
  - What exactly does the CWC want: assessment, treatment, a report, an opinion on fitness to testify, or rehabilitation advice?
- If you learn of **any** other unreported offence, **you must report it** (Section 19). Failure is punishable (Section 21) [1, 2].
- **Telepsychiatry is not appropriate** for the initial assessment (TOG 2020) [4].

### (a) Assessment
**Principles:**
- trauma-informed and child-friendly;
- **avoid re-traumatisation** and **repeated interviewing** (secondary victimisation) [1];
- **do not conduct a forensic interrogation**: the clinician's role is mental health assessment, not establishing guilt;
- use non-leading questions;
- record verbatim any spontaneous statements;
- get **consent from parents and assent from the child**, explaining the limits of confidentiality.

**1. With the parents and counsellor (child not present initially):**
- what is known about the incident (from them, not by questioning the child);
- **the child's reactions since**;
- **pre-incident functioning:** development, temperament, school, behaviour;
- **developmental history:** ID, ASD and speech delays increase vulnerability and affect communication. Under Section 26(2) a special educator or expert may be needed;
- previous abuse or trauma; **ongoing safety**: the neighbour's access to the child; threats; community pressure to "compromise";
- **family functioning:**
  - parental distress, guilt, blame;
  - parents' own trauma histories;
  - support systems;
  - finances;
  - stigma;
- legal status and expectations; medical findings (injuries, prophylaxis given, STI or pregnancy testing as appropriate).

**2. With the child (with a trusted adult present if she wishes):**
- **build rapport through play, drawing and neutral topics**;
- assess developmental level, language and understanding;
- **mental state:** appearance, interaction, affect (fearful, numb, tearful), anxiety, play themes (re-enactment), sleep, appetite;
- symptoms:
  - **re-experiencing** (nightmares, post-traumatic play);
  - **avoidance**;
  - hyperarousal (startle, irritability);
  - **regression** (enuresis, thumb-sucking, clinging);
  - fears (of the neighbour, of going out);
  - **sexualised behaviour**;
  - somatic complaints;
  - school refusal;
  - guilt and self-blame;
  - **self-harm** (rare at 8 but ask carers);
- **do not** probe for details of the abuse. If the child spontaneously discloses, listen, reassure ("you did the right thing by telling"), do not promise secrecy, and **document verbatim**.

**3. Collateral information:** school (with consent and confidentiality); CWC records.

**4. Rating scales (age-appropriate):**
- Child PTSD Symptom Scale (CPSS-5), UCLA PTSD-RI, TSCC (8 years and older), CBCL;
- **TSCYC** (caregiver report, young children).

**5. Differential and comorbid diagnoses:**
- acute stress reaction / **PTSD** (ICD-11 requires symptoms for several weeks, so this is early);
- **adjustment disorder**;
- separation anxiety;
- depression;
- enuresis;
- pre-existing neurodevelopmental disorders (ADHD, ID, ASD).

**6. Risk assessment:**
- **safety** (perpetrator proximity: the neighbour);
- threats;
- family coping;
- the need for a shelter or relocation (CWC decision).

### (b) Management
**1. Safety and coordination:**
- liaise with the **CWC, DCPU, police, support person and Special Court** as needed;
- ensure the **perpetrator has no access** (CWC and police);
- ensure medical follow-up (HIV/STI prophylaxis schedule).

**2. Psychological first aid and psychoeducation:**
- for the child: safety, normalising reactions, routine;
- for the parents:
  - common reactions to expect;
  - **believe and support the child** (the most powerful protective factor);
  - avoid blaming or repeated questioning;
  - maintain routine and school;
  - protect privacy (the child's identity must not be disclosed; Section 23).

**3. Trauma-focused treatment:**
- **TF-CBT (PRACTICE components)**:
  - Psychoeducation and parenting skills;
  - Relaxation;
  - Affect regulation;
  - Cognitive coping;
  - Trauma narrative;
  - In-vivo exposure;
  - Conjoint child–parent sessions;
  - Enhancing safety;
- if symptoms persist, typically **8–16 sessions**;
- **play therapy** and child–parent psychotherapy as alternatives for younger or less verbal children.

**4. Symptom-specific treatment:**
- sleep hygiene;
- enuresis management (behavioural);
- **medication only for severe comorbidity**: SSRIs are not first-line for paediatric PTSD; avoid benzodiazepines.

**5. Support the parents:** treat parental distress or depression; encourage support groups.

**6. Legal process support (Section 39 expert role):**
- prepare the child for court (court familiarisation);
- recommend accommodations: **in-camera hearing**, screen or video-link, **breaks**, a trusted adult present, **no aggressive cross-examination**, child not to see the accused (Sections 33, 36, 37);
- provide objective reports to the CWC and court about her mental state and needs;
- **do not opine on whether abuse occurred** unless qualified as a forensic expert and asked;
- **compensation** application (Section 33(8)) and **free legal aid** (Section 40).

**7. School and community reintegration:** confidential liaison; prevent stigma and gossip in the neighbourhood.

**8. Follow-up:**
- monitor for delayed PTSD, depression, sexualised behaviour;
- note developmental transitions (puberty) that may reactivate symptoms;
- long-term risk of revictimisation, so teach **body-safety skills**.

**9. Documentation:**
- consent;
- who referred and why;
- findings;
- verbatim statements;
- reports sent;
- communication with authorities.

**Prognosis:** better with **parental support and belief**, early TF-CBT, removal from danger, and less intrusive legal processes. Worse with intrafamilial abuse, repeated abuse, disability and a hostile environment.

## In depth

### Background
- **POCSO 2012** (amended 2019) and the **JJ Act 2015** (amended 2021) form the child protection framework [1, 3].
- **CWC:** a bench of a chairperson and four members per district. It has magistrate powers for children in need of care and protection [3].

### Epidemiology
- CSA prevalence in India: 4–41% (girls) [1].
- **Perpetrators are mostly known to the child**; neighbours and relatives are common [general knowledge].
- PTSD develops in about a third of sexually abused children [general knowledge].

### Classification and criteria
- **ICD-11:**
  - acute stress reaction (QE84, not a disorder);
  - **PTSD (6B40)**: re-experiencing in the present, avoidance, sense of current threat, lasting several weeks;
  - **complex PTSD (6B41)** with repeated or prolonged trauma;
  - adjustment disorder.
- **DSM-5-TR:** PTSD criteria for children 6 years and younger are separate. At 8 she uses adult criteria with developmental notes (post-traumatic play).

### Mechanism
- Traumagenic dynamics: sexualisation, betrayal, powerlessness, stigmatisation (Finkelhor).
- **Parental response** shapes outcome.

### Clinical features in detail [1]
- Physical: genital injury, vulvovaginitis, UTI, dysuria, anal symptoms.
- Behavioural: regression, enuresis, clinging, sleep problems, school decline, sexualised behaviour.

### Assessment in detail: interviewing a child who discloses
- Phrases to use: "Thank you for telling me." "It is not your fault." "I need to tell people whose job is to keep you safe."
- Avoid: "Did he touch you here?" (leading), "Why didn't you tell earlier?", and promises of secrecy.

### Differential diagnosis
- Accidental genital injury; dermatological conditions.
- Normal childhood sexual curiosity.
- Allegations arising from custody disputes (rare).
- Pre-existing psychiatric disorder.

### Management details: TF-CBT evidence
- **Cohen, Deblinger & Mannarino** RCTs show TF-CBT is superior to child-centred therapy for PTSD, depression and shame in sexually abused children [general knowledge].
- **NICE NG116 (2018):** trauma-focused CBT for children with PTSD; drugs not routinely recommended [general knowledge].

### Critical appraisal
- **Multiple interviews** (police, magistrate, doctor, CWC, court) re-traumatise children. Integrated child advocacy centre models (one interview, recorded) are better but rare in India [general knowledge].
- **Shortage of child mental health professionals** and trained support persons.
- Delays in trial despite the 1-year rule.

### Indian context
- **Institutions:**
  - **Childline 1098 / 112**;
  - **One Stop Centres**;
  - **DCPU**;
  - SJPU;
  - **Special Courts**;
  - **NIMHANS** child and adolescent psychiatry: protocols for POCSO assessment and the JJ Act preliminary assessment form [3].
- **Community pressure** to "compromise" or settle cases with neighbours is common. POCSO offences are **non-compoundable** [general knowledge].

### Recent advances
- Vulnerable-witness deposition centres.
- Trauma-informed policing training.
- **Online CSA** surveillance.
- **Child advocacy centre** pilots.
