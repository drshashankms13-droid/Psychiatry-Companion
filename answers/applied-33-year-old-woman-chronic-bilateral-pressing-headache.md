> **Gap:** the sources do **not** give the ICHD criteria for chronic tension-type headache. The diagnosis below rests on (a) the headache failing the IHS migraine criteria and (b) K&S listing chronic tension-type headache as a chronic daily headache. Check against ICHD before the exam.

## Exam answer

**Summary.** A 33-year-old woman has had **bilateral, pressing (non-pulsating), mild** scalp headache on **20–25 days a month for 2 years**. She has **no photophobia, phonophobia or vomiting** and only occasional mild nausea. Eye and ENT examinations are normal.

### Diagnosis
**Most likely: chronic tension-type headache**, within the **chronic daily headache** group [2].

**Reasons:**
1. **Does not meet the IHS criteria for migraine without aura** [1]:
   - **Criterion C** needs 2 of: unilateral, pulsating, moderate or severe, aggravated by activity. She is **bilateral, non-pulsating and mild**: 0 of the first 3.
   - **Criterion D** needs nausea or vomiting, **or** photophobia **and** phonophobia. She has only occasional **mild** nausea, no photophobia or phonophobia.
   - **POUNDing score:** not pulsating, not unilateral, not disabling. At most 1–2 of 5, so migraine is **unlikely** [1].
2. **Frequency:** at least 15 days a month places it among **chronic daily headaches**. This group includes **chronic tension-type headache**, transformed migraine, medication-overuse headache and hemicrania continua [2].
3. **No sinus or eye cause:** normal ophthalmological and ENT examination argues against glaucoma and chronic sinus disease [1].

### Differential diagnosis [1, 2]
| Condition | Points to check |
|---|---|
| **Transformed (chronic) migraine** | Past episodic migraine attacks? Lishman defines it as headache on at least 15 days a month, often with depression and over-medication |
| **Medication-overuse headache** | Analgesic use frequency; overuse leads to rebound headache [2] |
| **Hemicrania continua** | Listed by K&S among chronic daily headaches [2] |
| **Depression or anxiety presenting with headache** | Depression and anxiety are commoner in chronic daily headache [2] and in all headache types [1] |
| **Raised intracranial pressure, hypertension** | Must be excluded [1]: check BP, fundi, neurological signs |
| **Subdural haematoma** | Any head injury, fluctuating consciousness or cognition [1] |
| **Post-traumatic headache** | Any history of injury or whiplash; diffuse, unremitting, analgesic-resistant headache that fluctuates with tension or depression [1] |

### Evaluation
1. **History** [1, 2]:
   - headache diary: frequency, duration, timing, triggers;
   - **analgesic use: type, days a month** (medication overuse);
   - past typical migraine attacks;
   - stressors: **sustained emotional tension**, work and relationship conflicts, the "let-down" period after effort [1];
   - head injury;
   - sleep and meal pattern;
   - family history.
2. **Psychiatric assessment:** screen for **depression, anxiety, phobias** and suicidality. Headache sufferers have 2–3 times the risk, with a dose–response with headache frequency [1].
3. **Examination:** blood pressure; fundi for papilloedema; full neurological examination [1].
4. **Investigation:** simple investigations are usually enough. Image if there are focal signs, raised-pressure features, or atypical onset [1].

### Management
**1. Education and reassurance:** explain the benign nature and the role of tension and stress [1].

**2. Stop analgesic overuse** if present, to prevent **rebound headache** [2].

**3. Lifestyle:** regular sleep and meals; manage stress where possible [1].

**4. Psychological treatment:**
- **CBT** [2];
- **relaxation training** and tension-reducing techniques. The evidence for these in the source is for migraine [1];
- address work or interpersonal conflicts [1].

**5. Pharmacotherapy:**
- **Mirtazapine** decreases the **duration and intensity** of chronic tension-type headache [2];
- **TCAs** (low dose, titrated) help headache; nortriptyline is better tolerated than amitriptyline [2];
- **treat comorbid depression or anxiety** if present [1, 2].

**6. Combine medication and CBT:** for chronic daily headache this is **better than either alone** [2].

**7. Follow-up:** headache diary, functional outcomes, analgesic use, mood.

**Conclusion.** Bilateral, pressing, mild, frequent headache without photophobia, phonophobia or vomiting does not meet migraine criteria and fits chronic tension-type headache. Exclude medication overuse, secondary causes and mood or anxiety disorders, then treat with **CBT and relaxation plus mirtazapine or a low-dose TCA**, and avoid analgesic overuse.

## In depth

### Personality and stress in tension-type headache [1]
- Anxiety, neuroticism and harm-avoidance scores are **equally raised** in tension-type headache and migraine (Di Piero 2001; Cao 2002; Mongini 2005).
- In one study **only tension-type headache** (not migraine) was linked to high EPI neuroticism (Rasmussen 1992).
- After a mental-arithmetic stressor, fatigue took longer to recover in **both** headache groups than in controls (Stronks 1999).
- **Clinic attenders** have more psychopathology than non-attenders with the same headache severity (Ziegler and Paolo 1995). Interpret clinic findings with this selection bias in mind.
