## Exam answer

Psychiatric research may involve participants vulnerable through illness, fluctuating capacity, stigma, disadvantage, or dependence on clinical services. Ethical research protects rights and welfare while producing valid, socially valuable knowledge.

### Core principles

1. **Respect/autonomy:** Explain purpose,
procedures, risks, benefits, alternatives, confidentiality, and
withdrawal in understandable language. Consent must be voluntary.
2. **Capacity:** Capacity is decision-specific and may fluctuate. Diagnosis
alone does not establish incapacity. Use plain language, teach-back,
interpreters, and time. Follow applicable safeguards for participants
lacking capacity; proxy consent does not make every study permissible.
3. **Beneficence/non-maleficence:** Minimize risks and ensure
proportionality. Plan for distress, suicidality, symptom worsening, and
adverse events.
4. **Justice:** Recruit fairly; avoid exploiting
institutionalized or economically disadvantaged people; justify
exclusions.
5. **Privacy/confidentiality:** Collect only necessary data,
secure it, restrict access, de-identify where possible, and explain
limits. Genetic and digital data may be re-identifiable.
6. **Scientific
validity:** Poorly designed research is unethical because it exposes
participants without a reasonable prospect of useful knowledge.
7. **Independent review:** An Institutional Ethics Committee reviews
protocol, consent, recruitment, risk management, compensation, data
protection, and conflicts before recruitment.
8. **Integrity/transparency:** Register trials where required; report
accurately; disclose conflicts; avoid fabrication, falsification,
plagiarism, and selective reporting.

### Special issues

Suicide-risk, trauma, psychosis, involuntary care, placebo use, and digital surveillance require tailored safeguards. Refusal must not affect clinical care. Compensation should not be undue inducement. Genetic, imaging, and app-derived data need careful governance.

### Indian framework

Key sources include ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants (2017), applicable New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules and other regulations, IEC requirements, Declaration of Helsinki, and CIOMS guidance. Check current amendments and institutional requirements.

### Conclusion

Ethical psychiatric research requires valid science, voluntary consent, independent review, proportionate risk, fair selection, privacy, and transparent reporting. Safeguards should support rather than unnecessarily exclude participation.
