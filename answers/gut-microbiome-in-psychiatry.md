## Exam answer

### Introduction

The gut microbiome consists of the microorganisms inhabiting the gastrointestinal tract, including bacteria, viruses, fungi and archaea. Increasing evidence suggests bidirectional communication between gut microbes and the central nervous system through the gut–brain axis.

### Mechanisms

1.  **Neural pathway:** vagal afferents and enteric nervous system
    signalling communicate gut conditions to the brain.
2.  **Endocrine pathway:** gut signals can influence the
    hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis and stress responses.
3.  **Immune pathway:** microbial products influence cytokines, immune
    activation and intestinal barrier function.
4.  **Metabolic pathway:** microbes produce short-chain fatty acids
    (SCFAs), including acetate, propionate and butyrate, and modify bile
    acids and tryptophan metabolism.
5.  **Neuroactive compounds:** gut microbes can influence pathways
    involving GABA, serotonin precursors and other neuroactive
    metabolites.

### Psychiatric associations

-   **Depression and anxiety:** studies report differences in microbial
    composition and possible links with inflammation, stress and
    tryptophan metabolism.
-   **Schizophrenia:** microbiome differences and altered
    inflammatory/metabolic pathways have been described, but causality
    is uncertain.
-   **Autism spectrum disorder:** gastrointestinal symptoms and
    microbial differences have been reported in some cohorts.
-   **Bipolar disorder:** preliminary studies suggest altered microbiome
    composition, potentially influenced by diet and medication.

### Therapeutic implications

Potential interventions include dietary modification, prebiotics, probiotics, synbiotics and faecal microbiota transplantation (FMT). Evidence is emerging, but no microbiome-based intervention is currently an established replacement for standard psychiatric treatment. FMT requires particular caution and specialist oversight.

### Limitations

Studies are affected by diet, geography, medication, age, illness severity and sampling methods. Findings are heterogeneous; association does not establish causation. Commercial microbiome testing has limited established clinical utility in psychiatry.

### Conclusion

The gut microbiome is a promising area of psychobiological research. Its role is likely modulatory and interactive rather than a single causal explanation for psychiatric disorders.
