## Exam answer

**Introduction.**
- Psychosomatic medicine aims to diagnose and treat **psychiatric disorders in patients with complex medical conditions** [1].
- The **severity of the medical illness** is the greatest risk factor for needing psychiatric intervention [1].
- As more illnesses become chronic (cancer, HIV), the work has moved into outpatient settings too [1].
- Comorbid psychiatric disorders **lengthen hospital stay, increase readmission and raise costs** [1].
- Depression and anxiety are associated with a **threefold higher rate of non-adherence** to medical regimens (meta-analysis of more than 20 studies in chronic medical illness) [5].

### Ways psychiatric problems arise in chronic illness (Table 27.1–2) [1]
| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| Psychiatric symptoms **secondary to** the medical condition | Delirium, dementia |
| **Reaction** to the illness or its treatment | Anxiety about chemotherapy; depression after amputation |
| **Complication of treatment** | Depression with interferon; steroid mood disorder |
| **Psychological factors** contributing to medical symptoms | Somatic symptom disorders |
| **Medical complications of psychiatric treatment** | NMS; alcohol withdrawal |
| **Co-occurring** independent conditions | Recurrence of depression during cancer treatment; schizophrenia with ESRD |
| **Psychosocial assessment** | Capacity; pre-transplant evaluation |

### Major psychiatric complications, by syndrome
**1. Depression: the commonest, and it changes medical outcome.**
- **Cardiac [2]:**
  - major depression in **15–20% after MI** and about **20%** with ACS, stable CAD or CHF;
  - depression carries a relative risk of **1.5–2.0** for incident MI and cardiac death, and **doubles** adverse outcomes in established CAD;
  - dose–response relationship with 5-year mortality after MI;
  - in CHF, major depression more than **doubles** (in class III–IV, more than **triples**) 1-year mortality;
  - it **does not remit spontaneously** in many patients, contrary to the belief that it is "normal".
- **Diabetes [5]:**
  - major depression **12%** (meta-analysis of 39 studies), a **twofold** excess;
  - the relationship is **bidirectional**: depression raises the risk of type 2 diabetes by about 60%;
  - depression is chronic or recurrent (**79% relapse** over 5 years);
  - it is linked to higher HbA1c, all six diabetic complications, foot ulcers (×2), **10-fold** functional impairment, and about **50% higher mortality**.
- **COPD [4]:** depression in **25–50%**. It predicts functional impairment more strongly than lung severity, and reduces adherence to rehabilitation.
- **Cancer [6]:** about 50% have a psychiatric disorder (adjustment disorder in more than two-thirds of them, major depression 10–15%). Pancreatic, head and neck, breast and lung cancers carry the highest risk.
- **Chronic pain [8]:** up to 50% of pain-clinic patients have MDD. The relationship is bidirectional, and depression predicts future pain better than pain ratings do.
- **Functional GI disorders (IBS) [3]:** more than half have depression, anxiety or hypochondriasis.
- **Transplant [7]:** mood disorders in up to **20% (kidney), 30% (liver) and 63% (heart)**. Depression raises post-transplant mortality by **65%**.
- **Vascular depression [2]:** late-onset depression with white-matter infarcts. It is not linked to female sex or family history, and responds less well to antidepressants.

**2. Anxiety disorders:**
- **Cardiac:** anxiety disorders (panic, phobia) in **5–10%** of cardiology outpatients; PTSD increasingly recognised [2].
- **Asthma:** associated with panic, GAD, PTSD, social phobia, depression, alcohol use, schizophrenia and **suicide**. **20–40%** of patients get airway obstruction from emotional triggers [4].
- **COPD:** panic disorder is much commoner than in the general population. Benzodiazepines depress respiratory drive, so **buspirone or SSRIs** are alternatives [4].
- **Transplant:** anxiety in up to one-third; PTSD after critical events and delirium [7].

**3. Delirium and cognitive disorders:**
- Delirium in cancer: **25–85%** depending on stage [6]; after cardiac surgery [2]; in most transplant patients at some point [7].
- **Organ-failure encephalopathies:** hepatic (minimal HE in up to 60% of cirrhotics) and uraemic [7].
- **Cognitive decline:**
  - diabetes: risk ×1.2–1.5; depression plus diabetes **doubles** dementia risk [5];
  - COPD: working memory and attention, worsening with progression [4];
  - asthma: MCI and dementia [4].

**4. Drug-induced syndromes:**
- **Corticosteroids [4, 6]:**
  - acute high doses lean towards mania; chronic low doses towards depression;
  - psychosis and memory impairment (hippocampal effects) also occur;
  - **dose** is the main risk factor.
- **Theophylline and β2-agonists:** anxiety and depression; theophylline is also linked to suicidal ideation [4].
- **Interferon and IL-2, tamoxifen, vinca alkaloids:** depression [6].
- **Calcineurin inhibitors:** tremor, delirium, PRES [7].

**5. Substance use:**
- **Tobacco** in COPD; smoking explains much of the mood and anxiety excess there [4].
- **Opioid misuse** in chronic pain: severe SUD in 7–20%; distinguish **pseudoaddiction** [8].
- Alcohol relapse after liver transplant [7].

**6. Eating disorders:** in type 1 diabetes, bulimia and combined eating disorders are more frequent, and **insulin withholding** is used to lose weight [5].

**7. Somatic symptom presentations:** these accompany IBS, fibromyalgia and chronic pain. "**Presentation bias**" means somatic presenters are often not diagnosed (22% vs 77%) [3, 8].

**8. Suicide:**
- Raised in cancer (relative risk **12.6 in the first week** after diagnosis) [6].
- Raised **2–3-fold** in migraine, abdominal pain and orthopaedic pain [8].
- Raised in asthma [4].

**9. Illness behaviour and adherence:**
- Non-adherence (threefold with depression or anxiety) [5].
- **Sick-role dependency** in long-standing illness [7].
- Denial [2].

### Why it matters: bidirectional pathways [2, 5]
- **Behavioural:** smoking, poor diet, inactivity, poor adherence.
- **Biological:** sympathoadrenal activation, high cortisol, platelet adhesiveness, inflammation, endothelial dysfunction.
- Depression roughly **doubles the odds of having 3 or more Framingham risk factors** in diabetes [5].
- **Severe mental illness** is the reverse case:
  - life expectancy is **10–15 years shorter**;
  - CHD death rate ×3 under age 50;
  - medical care is poor (in CATIE, only 38% of patients with hypertension and 12% with dyslipidaemia were treated) [5].

### Evaluation [1]
- **Speak to the referring clinician:** why was the referral made, and is there team frustration?
- Collateral history; an interview adapted to the patient's medical state.
- **Full MSE with cognition**: MMSE or MoCA as a baseline.
- Physical and neurological examination.
- **Investigations:** chemistry, haematology, thyroid, LFT, B12, folate, toxicology, syphilis, HIV; imaging, EEG and LP when indicated.
- **The patient's experience of illness:** stress, personality, coping and defences.
- A written report with recommendations, discussed with the referrer.

### Management principles
- **Pharmacological [1, 2, 7]:**
  - drug interactions and hepatic and renal function;
  - metabolic and cardiac side-effects;
  - antipsychotic risks in dementia.
  - **Cardiac:**
    - TCAs are no longer first-line (type 1A antiarrhythmic effect);
    - **sertraline (SADHART)** and **citalopram (CREATE)** are safe and effective after MI or in CAD; citalopram has a dose-related QT effect;
    - mirtazapine was no better than placebo after MI [2].
  - **Diabetic neuropathy:** venlafaxine, duloxetine, gabapentin and pregabalin work whether or not the patient is depressed [5].
  - **Transplant:** avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors [7].
- **Psychosocial [1]:**
  - every major modality has been used;
  - goals are tailored: role change, death and dying in cancer, pain and function in arthritis, **adherence in diabetes**;
  - benefits: QoL, psychiatric outcomes, and some effect on adherence and medical outcomes.
- **Collaborative care [1, 5]:**
  - a care manager plus a mental health specialist plus primary care education;
  - effective for depression in diabetes (IMPACT, Pathways; effect sizes 0.47–0.68) and cancer;
  - **cost-offset** from savings in medical costs;
  - short-term trials did not change HbA1c, but **maintenance** antidepressant treatment improved glycaemic control.
- **Severe mental illness:** integrate medical care into community mental health centres [5].
- **Specific measures:**
  - pulmonary rehabilitation with stress reduction or psychotherapy for COPD depression [4];
  - interdisciplinary pain rehabilitation [8];
  - meaning-centred therapy in advanced cancer [6];
  - behavioural contracting and adherence interventions in transplant [7].

**Conclusion.**
- Chronic medical illness raises the risk of depression, anxiety, delirium, cognitive decline, substance use and suicide.
- The relationship is **bidirectional**: psychiatric comorbidity worsens adherence, disability, cost and **mortality** (cardiac, diabetic and transplant data).
- Psychiatrists should screen actively, treat with medically appropriate drugs and adapted psychotherapies, and work through **collaborative and integrated care**.

## In depth

### Historical models of mind–body relationship (Table 27.1–1) [1]
- **Psychoanalytic:**
  - Freud: conversion;
  - Ferenczi and Groddeck: organic symptoms with psychic meaning;
  - **Alexander**: specific conflicts acting through autonomic organs (for example, peptic ulcer, colitis, asthma, migraine, hypertension, arthritis).
- **Psychophysiological:**
  - **Selye**: the general adaptation syndrome;
  - Wolff and Wolf: mucosal hyper- and hypofunction;
  - **Holmes and Rahe**: life events precede illness (death of a spouse is the most intense);
  - the giving-up–given-up complex.
- **Personality:** Dunbar; **Friedman and Rosenman type A**, in which **hostility** is the risk component.
- **Lipowski's (1967) common consultation questions:**
  - Is the complaint psychogenic?
  - Is the mental change caused by, or a reaction to, organic disease?

### Common reasons for consultation (Lipowski list) [1]
1. Suicide attempt or threat.
2. Disturbed behaviour (delirium, psychosis).
3. Excessive emotion (depression, anger).
4. Refusal to cooperate.
5. Delayed convalescence.
6. Conflict between patient and staff.
7. Psychiatric history.
8. Psychiatric side-effects of drugs.
9. Selection or preparation for procedures.
10. Disposition.
