## Exam answer

### Introduction

Gender Identity Disorder (GID) is a historical diagnostic term used in earlier editions of psychiatric classifications. Contemporary terminology has moved away from pathologising gender identity itself.

### Evolution of classification

-   DSM-III and DSM-IV used Gender Identity Disorder.
-   DSM-5 replaced it with **Gender Dysphoria**, focusing on clinically
    significant distress or impairment rather than identity alone.
-   ICD-11 uses **Gender Incongruence**, classified under conditions
    related to sexual health rather than mental disorders.

### Gender dysphoria

DSM-5-TR describes marked incongruence between experienced/expressed gender and assigned gender, associated with clinically significant distress or impairment. Criteria differ for children and adolescents/adults.

### Aetiology

Gender identity is complex and likely reflects interacting biological, developmental, psychological and social influences. No single causal theory explains all gender identities. Gender diversity is not itself psychopathology.

### Assessment

Explore gender history, distress, goals, social context, mental health, capacity, medical needs and support systems. Assess depression, anxiety, trauma and suicidality when appropriate.

### Differential diagnosis

Distinguish gender incongruence from gender nonconformity without incongruence, body dysmorphic disorder, psychotic beliefs, culturally specific gender roles and transient uncertainty. Avoid assuming that sexual orientation determines gender identity.

### Management

Provide affirming, individualised care. Options may include psychosocial support, social transition, hormone therapy and surgical procedures according to goals, eligibility, informed consent and applicable standards. Treat coexisting psychiatric conditions without making them a reason for automatic exclusion.

### Conclusion

GID is an outdated term. Contemporary practice distinguishes gender identity from clinically significant distress and emphasises autonomy, dignity and access to appropriate gender-affirming care.
