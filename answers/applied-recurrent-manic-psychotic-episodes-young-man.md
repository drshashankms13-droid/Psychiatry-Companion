## Exam answer

**Case summary.** A 25-year-old man has a current episode (2 weeks to 2 months) of marked irritability, irrelevant talk, aggression, **decreased sleep** and suspiciousness. He has had **three similar episodes in 5 years**, each lasting about a month and **recovering with treatment**. One episode included depression, worthlessness and death wishes. His father had an **episodic illness**, and an elder sibling had **depression and died by suicide**. The episodic course with full recovery, manic-type features, a depressive episode and a strong family history make **bipolar I disorder, current episode manic with psychotic features** the leading diagnosis. Schizoaffective disorder, ATPD, substance-induced and organic psychosis are the main alternatives [1,2,3].

### (a) What further clarifications are needed in the history?

| Area | What to ask | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current mood | Elation vs irritability, grandiosity, pressured speech, flight of ideas, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity, risky spending, sexual disinhibition, decreased **need** for sleep (not insomnia) | Manic syndrome |
| Psychotic symptoms | Content of suspiciousness (mood-congruent, e.g., persecution because of special powers?), hallucinations; whether psychosis occurs **only during mood episodes** or also in their absence (≥2 weeks) | Bipolar with psychotic features vs schizoaffective vs schizophrenia |
| Past episodes | Detailed life chart: onset, duration, polarity, mixed features, precipitants (sleep loss, stress, substances, antidepressants), treatments, response, interepisode functioning (full recovery?) | Course pattern; rapid cycling (≥4/year) |
| Depressive episode | Duration ≥2 weeks, severity, suicidal attempts, psychotic depression, treatment (antidepressant? switch?) | Bipolarity; suicide risk |
| Hypomania between episodes | Brief elevated periods | Bipolar II/spectrum [3] |
| Substance use | Cannabis, alcohol, stimulants, steroids, timing relative to episodes; urine drug screen | Substance-induced mania/psychosis |
| Medical | Head injury, seizures, thyroid symptoms, HIV/VDRL risk, steroid use, neurological signs | Secondary mania |
| Treatment history | Drugs, doses, duration, adherence, side effects; lithium levels; why stopped | Choose maintenance |
| Family history | Father's illness: nature (mania? psychosis?), treatment and lithium response; sibling's suicide (diagnosis, method); other relatives | Heritability; lithium response tends to run in families [4] |
| Premorbid personality and function | Hyperthymic or cyclothymic temperament, education, work, relationships | Prognosis; soft bipolarity [3] |
| Risk | Aggression, violence, suicide, vulnerability, driving, finances | Admission decision |
| Insight and capacity | — | MHCA 2017 |
| Psychosocial | Stressors, marital status, family expressed emotion | Relapse prevention |

**Examination and investigations:** mental status examination; physical and neurological examination; CBC, RFT (before lithium), LFT, TSH, glucose and lipids, urine drug screen, HIV and VDRL, ECG; pregnancy test is not applicable. Neuroimaging or EEG only if atypical (late onset, neurological signs, confusion).

### (b) Differential diagnosis

| Diagnosis | For | Against / to clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bipolar I disorder, current episode manic, severe with psychotic features** (ICD-11 6A60.2) | Episodic course, full recovery, decreased sleep, irritability, one depressive episode, family history of episodic illness and suicide | Confirm manic syndrome |
| Bipolar I, current episode mixed | Irritability with death wishes in the past | Check concurrent depressive symptoms now |
| Schizoaffective disorder, bipolar type | Psychosis + mood | Requires psychosis for ≥2 weeks without mood symptoms (DSM) or concurrent full criteria (ICD-11) |
| Schizophrenia with episodic course | Suspiciousness, irrelevant talk | Full interepisode recovery and family history favour bipolar disorder |
| ATPD, recurrent | Recurrent brief psychoses with recovery | Prominent manic features and a depressive episode argue against; ATPD excludes mood episodes [5] |
| Substance-induced psychotic/mood disorder | Young male | Ask about cannabis or stimulants; toxicology |
| Secondary mania (organic) | — | Thyroid, steroids, head injury, epilepsy, HIV, neurosyphilis |
| Recurrent depressive disorder with psychotic features | One depressive episode | Current manic features rule this out |
| Personality disorder (borderline/antisocial) | Irritability, aggression | Episodic course with clear episodes and decreased sleep argue against |

### (c) Approach to management
**Setting:** with aggression and psychosis, admission is likely. Use independent admission if possible, or supported admission (MHCA 2017 Sec 89) if he lacks capacity and is at risk. Keep a safe environment and observe for violence and suicide.

**Acute mania**
1. **Rapid tranquillisation** if needed: oral lorazepam ± oral olanzapine or haloperidol; IM lorazepam or haloperidol + promethazine for severe agitation [6].
2. **Antimanic drug:** an SGA (olanzapine 10–20 mg, risperidone 2–6 mg, quetiapine 400–800 mg, aripiprazole 15–30 mg) for rapid control **plus lithium** (target 0.8–1.0 mmol/L), or valproate (men; 20–30 mg/kg loading) [6].
3. **Stop any antidepressant** [7].
4. **ECT** if dangerously excited, catatonic, or not responding [6].
5. **Sleep restoration.**

**Maintenance (after 3 manic and 1 depressive episode = long-term prophylaxis indicated)**
- **Lithium is first choice:** classic episodic course with full recovery, family history of episodic illness (possible lithium-responsive lineage), sibling suicide (lithium cuts suicide by ~80%) [6].
- Maintenance level 0.6–0.8 mmol/L; continue the SGA for 3–6 months, then consider tapering, or continue combination if breakthrough occurs [6].
- Alternatives: quetiapine, aripiprazole (oral or LAI if adherence is poor), olanzapine, valproate [6].
- If depression recurs: quetiapine, lurasidone, lamotrigine; antidepressant only as an adjunct [7].

**Psychosocial**
- Psychoeducation for patient and family: early warning signs (reduced sleep, irritability), adherence, avoiding substances, regular sleep.
- Family-focused therapy; interpersonal and social rhythm therapy (IPSRT); CBT; crisis plan; vocational support.
- Genetic counselling: risk to children about 10–15% with one affected parent [4].

**Monitoring:** lithium level, renal function, TFTs and calcium every 6 months; metabolic monitoring for SGA; mood charting [6].

**Conclusion.** This is most likely **bipolar I disorder, current episode manic with psychotic features**, recurrent, with a high familial loading and suicide risk. Acute management with an SGA plus lithium in a safe setting should be followed by long-term lithium-based prophylaxis, psychoeducation, and close attention to depressive relapse and suicide prevention.

## In depth

### Background
Kraepelin's manic-depressive insanity unified episodic mood psychoses with good interepisode recovery. Leonhard (1957) separated bipolar from unipolar forms [3]. The **episodic course with full recovery** remains the strongest clinical discriminator from schizophrenia, alongside family history of affective illness. The "third psychosis" (ATPD/cycloid) sits between them [5].

### Epidemiology
- Bipolar I lifetime prevalence about 1%; mean age of onset about 20–25; men and women equally affected; first episode in men is more often manic [3].
- Heritability 70–85%; MZ concordance 40–70%; first-degree relatives have a 7–10-fold risk; risk with one affected parent 10–15%, both 25–30% [4].
- Suicide in about 15%; family history of suicide raises individual risk [1,6].
- NMHS 2016 (India): bipolar affective disorder lifetime prevalence 0.5%.

### Classification and criteria
- **ICD-11 manic episode:** ≥1 week (or any duration if treatment is needed) of extreme mood state (euphoria, irritability, expansiveness) **and** increased activity or subjective energy, plus several symptoms: rapid speech, flight of ideas, grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, distractibility, impulsive or reckless behaviour, increased sexual drive.
- **DSM-5-TR:** ≥1 week of elevated, expansive or irritable mood and increased activity/energy, plus 3 symptoms (4 if mood is only irritable), with marked impairment, hospitalisation or psychosis.
- **Specifiers:** with psychotic features (mood-congruent/incongruent), with mixed features, rapid cycling, seasonal, peripartum.
- **Schizoaffective disorder:** DSM requires psychosis for ≥2 weeks without a major mood episode; ICD-11 requires concurrent schizophrenia and mood criteria in the same episode.

### Mechanism in detail
- **Genetics:** polygenic. GWAS loci include CACNA1C (calcium channel), ANK3, ODZ4, TRANK1, SYNE1, NCAN. CNVs: 1q21.1 duplication, 3q29 deletion, 22q11.2. Strongest genetic overlap is with schizophrenia, then MDD [4].
- **Epigenetics and environment:** early-life stress, trauma, substance use, sleep disruption, postpartum period [4].
- **Neurobiology:** circadian dysregulation (CLOCK genes), dopamine hyperactivity in mania, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, kindling.
- **Endophenotypes:** circadian abnormalities, reward hypersensitivity, amygdala reactivity [4].

### Clinical features in detail
- **Irritable mania** is common in men and in later episodes; may be misdiagnosed as schizophrenia or personality disorder.
- **Psychotic features** occur in about 50–60% of manic episodes; mood-incongruent delusions predict a poorer course.
- **Decreased need for sleep** (feeling rested despite little sleep) is a key discriminating symptom.
- **Mixed features:** dysphoria + activation; high suicide risk.
- **Course:** cycle length often shortens with successive episodes (kindling); interepisode recovery may become incomplete.

### Management details
- **Lithium initiation:** baseline eGFR, U&Es, TFTs, calcium, ECG. Start 400–600 mg at night and titrate to level; recheck at 7 days [6].
- **Valproate:** effective in mixed and rapid-cycling presentations; avoid in women of childbearing potential [6].
- **Antipsychotic LAIs:** aripiprazole and risperidone LAIs prevent manic relapse, useful with poor adherence [6].
- **Clozapine:** for treatment-resistant bipolar disorder [6].
- **Substances:** integrated treatment.
- **Sleep:** sleep hygiene; avoid shift work if possible.
- **Psychotherapy evidence:** family-focused therapy (Miklowitz) and group psychoeducation (Colom) reduce relapse.

### Critical appraisal
- **Diagnostic stability:** first-episode mania can later prove schizoaffective or schizophrenia. Follow-up and life charting matter.
- **Irritable, psychotic mania is under-recognised** in Indian emergency settings and is often labelled schizophrenia; this leads to missing lithium and prophylaxis.
- **Family history** is informative but not deterministic; genetic testing is not clinically useful [4].
- **Lithium vs SGA** maintenance: see the lithium vs atypicals answer.
- **Adherence** is the key real-world determinant of outcome.

### Indian context
- MHCA 2017: supported admission, nominated representative, advance directives (useful in recurrent bipolar disorder to state preferences for future episodes).
- Families often bring patients during manic episodes; use family psychoeducation.
- Lithium monitoring access; heat and dehydration risk.
- Marital and occupational consequences of manic behaviour; stigma.
- IPS CPG for bipolar disorder (2017) supports a similar approach [8].

### Recent advances
- **Emerging:** aripiprazole 2-monthly LAI; lithium response prediction using polygenic scores [4]; digital phenotyping (smartphone activity and sleep) for early relapse detection.
- **Investigational:** chronotherapy (sleep deprivation and light therapy for bipolar depression); anti-inflammatory adjuncts.
