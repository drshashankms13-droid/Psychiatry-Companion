## Exam answer

### Definition and burden [1, 2]
- **Definition:** BPSD are the non-cognitive symptoms of dementia:
  - **psychotic, mood and activity-related** [1];
  - aggression, agitation, vocalisation, distress during care, disinhibition, hallucinations, delusions, apathy, low mood, anxiety [2].
- **Frequency:**
  - **core, not secondary or rare, features**;
  - **over 90%** have some BPSD during the illness [2].
- **Impact:**
  - the **main source of carer burden** (sleep disturbance especially);
  - a major determinant of **institutionalisation**;
  - psychosis is associated with faster decline and with aggression [1].

### Phenomenology (AD; Table 9.1) [1]

| Cluster | Symptom | Prevalence | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Psychotic** | Delusions | 20–30% | **Theft** (secondary to amnesia: mislaid purse becomes "stolen"); persecutory; intruders |
| | Hallucinations | 20–30% | **Visual > auditory**; small animals or people, silent, often non-distressing; some insight. Early, prominent visual hallucinations suggest **DLB** |
| | Misidentification | 10–20% | Impostors (Capgras-type); relatives confused with each other; **mirror sign**. Distinguish from prosopagnosia and word-finding difficulty |
| **Mood** | Depression | Major 10% or less; minor up to 30% (30–50% comorbidity [2]) | Treatable but under-treated; may be prodromal |
| | Mania | Under 5% | — |
| | Anxiety | Over 50% | — |
| **Activity** | Apathy | 15–40% | — |
| | Agitation | 30–70% | — |
| | Wandering | 15–40% | — |
| | Aggression | 20–40% | — |
| | Circadian/sleep disturbance | 30–80% | Sundowning |
| **Other** | Disinhibition (including sexual), vocalisation, hyperorality, Klüver–Bucy features | — | [1, 2] |

**Clusters:**
- Hope et al.: overactivity, aggression, psychosis;
- Cache County: psychotic and affective groups.

**Mechanisms:**
- **psychosis**: dorsolateral frontal and parietal hypoperfusion; 5-HT receptor variants (hallucinations) and dopamine receptor variants (delusions); shared among siblings;
- **depression**: locus coeruleus and raphe loss.

### Evaluation [1, 2]
1. **Describe the behaviour precisely:** what, when, where, with whom, frequency, risk. Use **ABC charts**, sleep and pain charts, **direct observation of care** [2].
2. **Exclude delirium and physical causes:**
   - **pain**, **infection** (chest, urine), **constipation**;
   - urinary retention;
   - dehydration;
   - **medication adverse effects**, especially anticholinergic burden [1, 2].
3. **Sensory deficits:** glasses, hearing aids [2].
4. **Psychiatric syndromes:** depression, psychosis, anxiety; clarify true delusions vs amnesia-driven beliefs; misidentification vs prosopagnosia [1].
5. **Environment and care:**
   - unmet needs;
   - carer approach and burden;
   - **life history** [2].
6. **Dementia subtype:**
   - **DLB** (visual hallucinations, fluctuation): **severe neuroleptic sensitivity**;
   - **FTD** (disinhibition, hyperorality);
   - **vascular** (emotionalism) [1].
7. **Rating scales:** e.g., Cornell Scale for Depression in Dementia; scales used in research [1].
8. **Formulation meeting:** a model of contributing factors, reframing "problem behaviour" as **distress or unmet need** [2].

### Management [1, 2]

**Principles:**
- **non-drug first** (most effective and safest) [1, 2];
- **"start low, go slow"**;
- clear documented targets; stop if not met.

**A. Non-pharmacological (first line)** [2]:
- **individualised, multicomponent care plans** with carers; review effectiveness;
- treat physical causes first;
- **behavioural management and carer psychoeducation**: effects last months;
- **music therapy** (the only convincingly effective sensory intervention for agitation);
- **multicomponent multidisciplinary nursing-home interventions**;
- animal-assisted therapy (depression); doll therapy;
- **light therapy** (sundowning); **aerobic exercise**;
- reminiscence and cognitive stimulation (depression);
- aromatherapy: no evidence (Cochrane 2020);
- Lishman also cites validation, reality orientation and cognitive stimulation therapy (variable evidence) [1].

**B. Pharmacological, by target** [2]:

| Target / drug | Evidence and recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| **Pain → analgesics** | Stepwise analgesia (mostly **paracetamol**) reduced agitation; **a trial of paracetamol is worthwhile** even without overt pain. Opioids: insufficient evidence |
| **Cognitive enhancers** | AChE-Is: modest benefit, more for **depression, apathy, anxiety** than agitation. **Memantine**: agitation, aggression, delusions. Worth considering if not already prescribed |
| **Antipsychotics** (psychosis or aggression with **severe distress or risk of harm only**) | Small effect, poor tolerability, **increased mortality and cerebrovascular events** (warnings for all antipsychotics). **Risperidone**: licensed for up to **6 weeks** for persistent aggression in moderate–severe AD; **0.25–1 mg daily** (optimal 0.5 mg bd). Alternatives: **olanzapine** (aggression), **aripiprazole** (overall BPSD). **Quetiapine has limited efficacy** except at 100–200 mg. Typical antipsychotics ineffective except haloperidol (which carries higher mortality). **Brexpiprazole** is FDA-approved for AD agitation (not in UK). **Avoid in DLB** (fatal sensitivity) [1] |
| **Antidepressants** | **Agitation**: **citalopram** (CitAD; 30 mg effective, but QT; maximum 20 mg in the elderly). Escitalopram possible. **Sertraline**: mixed efficacy, cardiac-safe. **Depression**: justified for moderate–severe depression; SSRIs preferred; **avoid TCAs** (falls, anticholinergic) [1, 2]. **Trazodone**: insufficient for agitation; **50 mg nocte helps sleep**; 150–300 mg reduced BPSD in FTD. **Mirtazapine**: no benefit for agitation (possible higher mortality) or sleep |
| **Benzodiazepines** | **Avoid** (faster cognitive decline, falls, fractures, pneumonia, mortality), except single emergency use |
| **Mood stabilisers** | Carbamazepine: most robust evidence but adverse effects and interactions. **Valproate ineffective and harmful: avoid.** Gabapentin or pregabalin: low-grade evidence (avoid gabapentin in DLB) |
| **Sleep** | Sleep hygiene first. **Melatonin** (2–10 mg MR) is safe and may help. Trazodone; orexin antagonists. Avoid **promethazine** (anticholinergic) |
| **Others** | Ginkgo (BPSD and carer distress). **Pimavanserin** (psychosis in AD; one RCT). Dextromethorphan/quinidine; **prazosin** (agitation; limited); **methylphenidate for apathy**; cannabinoids: little effect. **ECT**: insufficient evidence, cognitive adverse effects |
| **Sexual disinhibition** | Treat medical causes; reduce **benzodiazepines, dopamine agonists and high-dose levodopa**; distraction and fulfilling intimacy needs in other ways; discussion with carers [2] |

**C. Antipsychotic safety and review** [2]:
- **Before:**
  - treat illness, pain, constipation; address sensory deficits; try non-drug options;
  - use only for psychosis or aggression;
  - assess falls and stroke risk;
  - discuss with carers or the patient; document.
- **Monitoring** (if used longer than a few weeks):
  - BP and pulse; weight;
  - glucose or HbA1c, U&E, FBC, lipids, LFT, prolactin;
  - ECG, at baseline, 3 months and annually.
- **Review** at 4–6 weeks, 3 months, then 6-monthly. **Consider stopping at each review.**
- **Deprescribing (Table 6.6):** taper over about 4 weeks (e.g., **risperidone by 0.25–0.5 mg every 1–2 weeks**). Discontinuation studies show **no worsening of BPSD** and improved ADL.

**D. Carer and system support** [1]:
- carer support groups, respite, day care;
- Alzheimer's Society;
- residential care when needed;
- address carer stress (a quarter of carers have significant stress).

### Update from Continuum 2024 [3]
**Course and prognosis:**
- **New late-life neuropsychiatric symptoms** usually signal an emerging neurodegenerative disease.
- **Mild behavioural impairment (MBI)**: a prodrome like MCI, assessed with the **MBI Checklist** across 5 domains:
  1. apathy;
  2. mood and anxiety;
  3. agitation and irritability;
  4. disinhibition and loss of empathy;
  5. delusions and hallucinations.
  - MBI predicts cognitive decline and dementia and is linked to AD biomarkers. **Mainly a research construct.**
- **Depression, apathy, anxiety and agitation** predict progression from normal cognition to MCI and from MCI to dementia. Symptom burden predicts **ADL loss, carer depression and burden, and institutional placement**.

**Profiles by dementia type:**

| Dementia | Early | Later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AD | Depression, anxiety, apathy, irritability | Agitation, sleep disturbance, delusions; then hallucinations, disinhibition, aberrant motor behaviour |
| Vascular | Irritability, depression, sleep disturbance | Anxiety, apathy |
| Lewy body | **Well-formed colourful visual hallucinations**, apathy, depression, anxiety, **RBD**, daytime sleepiness | Delusions, agitation |
| bvFTD | Apathy, **loss of empathy, disinhibition**, aberrant motor behaviour, **sweet/carbohydrate craving**; often **misdiagnosed as primary psychiatric illness** (e.g., "late-onset bipolar", Case 7-1) | Agitation, depression, anxiety |
| PPA variants | Semantic: disinhibition, compulsions; logopenic: anxiety, depression, apathy; non-fluent: anxiety, depression, then disinhibition | — |

**Assessment tools:**
- **Neuropsychiatric Inventory** (12 items, rated for frequency, severity and carer distress; informant-based) or the **NPI-Q** brief form as a first screen;
- depression: GDS, BDI, PHQ-9; **Cornell Scale (CSDD)** in advanced dementia;
- anxiety: GAI, BAI, GAD-7, **RAID**;
- apathy: AES, Apathy Inventory, LARS;
- agitation: **Cohen-Mansfield Agitation Inventory**.

**Neurobiology:**
- frontal–subcortical networks;
- apathy is linked to **anterior cingulate** atrophy;
- hallucinations are linked to **Lewy body pathology**; depression and agitation to **AD tau pathology**.

**IPA "Investigate, Plan, Act" agitation algorithm (2023)** [3]:
- **Investigate** the behaviour (timing, precipitants, harm potential), **plan** with the carer, **act** through a multidisciplinary plan. Repeat until the behaviour is acceptable.
- **Psychosocial care first and throughout.** Curtail drugs that may cause agitation; optimise AChE-Is in AD and DLB.
- **Drug panels by type of agitation:**
  1. **nocturnal or circadian (sundowning)**: **trazodone**, then a **dual orexin receptor antagonist**;
  2. **mild to moderate, or with mood features**: **citalopram**;
  3. **moderate to severe**: **antipsychotics**, then **carbamazepine or gabapentin**, then **prazosin, dextromethorphan/quinidine or nabilone**, then combinations;
  4. **danger of harm**: **IM antipsychotics**, **ECT**.

**Drug notes** (Table 7-1; start low, go slow):
- **First check the response to AChE-Is and/or memantine**, which reduce overall NPS. **Rivastigmine** is approved for PD dementia.
- **SSRIs** (escitalopram 5–20 mg, **citalopram 20–30 mg**, sertraline 25–200 mg):
  - for depression, anxiety, irritability;
  - **citalopram and escitalopram reduce agitation**, and are **preferred over antipsychotics for non-psychotic agitation**;
  - avoid paroxetine;
  - **SSRIs can worsen apathy**.
- **Bupropion** for depression with apathy.
- **Mirtazapine** 7.5–15 mg for sleep, 30–45 mg for depression.
- **Trazodone** from 50 mg at night for insomnia; sometimes for agitation (works within 30–60 minutes).
- **Apathy:** **methylphenidate** (ADMET 2 trial: improved apathy and global function; Case 7-2), amphetamine.
- **Mood stabilisers** for lability, agitation or mania: **lamotrigine** (best tolerated; slow titration), valproate (fast-acting but more side effects), gabapentin.
- **Anxiolytics:**
  - **buspirone** for GAD;
  - if a benzodiazepine is needed, short-term **lorazepam or clonazepam** (less dependence than diazepam or alprazolam); risk of delirium and paradoxical agitation.
- **Antipsychotics:**
  - quetiapine (sedating), **risperidone** (low dose; EPS at higher doses), olanzapine, aripiprazole;
  - **brexpiprazole: FDA 2023 for AD agitation** (not psychosis); boxed warning;
  - **pimavanserin: FDA 2016 for PD psychosis only**;
  - **avoid in Lewy body disease** if possible;
  - **boxed warning** (cerebrovascular events, mortality); **taper after stability**;
  - discuss risk vs quality of life with the family.
- **Dextromethorphan/quinidine:** FDA 2010 for **pseudobulbar affect**.
- **Severe risk:** inpatient **geriatric psychiatry** admission.
- **Education alone** can be enough, e.g., non-distressing DLB hallucinations need no drug.

⚠️ **Discrepancies, not reconciled:**
- **Citalopram dose:** Continuum lists 20–30 mg/day; Maudsley caps it at **20 mg in the elderly** (QT).
- **Mirtazapine:** Maudsley reports no benefit for agitation or sleep (possible higher mortality); Continuum uses it for insomnia and depression.
- **Pimavanserin:** Maudsley cites one RCT in AD psychosis; Continuum notes FDA approval only for PD psychosis.

**Conclusion.** BPSD affect over 90% of people with dementia and drive carer burden and institutionalisation. Evaluation is:
- precise description;
- **exclusion of pain, infection, constipation, drugs and delirium**;
- sensory and environmental review;
- subtype diagnosis (DLB);
- a formulation of unmet needs.

Management is **person-centred non-drug intervention first**. Drugs are targeted:
- analgesia;
- cognitive enhancers;
- SSRIs (citalopram for agitation; for depression);
- melatonin or trazodone for sleep;
- **short-term risperidone only for severe aggression or psychosis**, with mortality and stroke warnings, regular review and planned deprescribing;
- avoid benzodiazepines, valproate and promethazine.
