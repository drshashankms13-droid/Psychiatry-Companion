## Exam answer

**Introduction.** Hormonal strategies in depression act on four axes:
1. **thyroid**: T3 augmentation;
2. **gonadal**: oestrogen, HRT, testosterone, COCP, GnRH analogues;
3. **neurosteroid**: allopregnanolone (brexanolone);
4. **HPA axis**: glucocorticoid-receptor antagonism (mifepristone) [1, 2].

### 1. Thyroid: tri-iodothyronine (T3) augmentation [1]
- **Dose:** **20–50 mcg/day** added to the antidepressant. Higher doses have been used safely.
- **Status:** a well-supported **TRD** option (Table 3.4), with **good literature support**. It may also be effective in **bipolar depression**.
- **Monitoring:** clinical and **biochemical thyroid function**. Usually well tolerated. Needs specialist referral.
- **Limits:** some negative studies, and **no advantage over antidepressant alone in non-refractory illness** (T3 plus sertraline was not superior).
- T3 was compared with thyroxine in TCA potentiation (Joffe 1990).

### 2. Oestrogen and menopause-related depression [1]
- **Perimenopause** carries a **2–5-fold** higher risk of depression. About **6%** of women with menopause-related psychological symptoms have suicidal ideation.
- **Psychological symptoms** (Table 10.20): anxiety, low mood, loss of motivation, mood swings, tearfulness, irritability, brain fog, poor memory.
- **Diagnosis:**
  - clinical, using the Greene Climacteric Scale, over age 40;
  - under 45, two FSH levels over 30 mIU/mL, 6 weeks apart, on days 2–5 of the cycle.
- **HRT is first-line treatment for menopausal insomnia, anxiety and depression.**
- **Regimens:**
  - **perimenopause:** **sequential combined HRT** (oestrogen plus progesterone for 12–14 days of the cycle), with or without vaginal oestrogen and testosterone;
  - **post-menopause:** **continuous combined HRT**;
  - **oestrogen-only** after total hysterectomy;
  - **transdermal oestrogen** where clot risk is a concern (oral oestrogen slightly raises thrombosis risk).
- **Contraindications** (Table 10.22): oestrogen-dependent cancers, undiagnosed vaginal bleeding, pregnancy, active liver disease, recent thromboembolism or MI, untreated VTE or endometrial hyperplasia.
- **Precautions:** migraine with aura, untreated hypertension, **epilepsy (lamotrigine interaction)**, fibroids, endometriosis.
- **Risks:** small breast-cancer risk over age 50; endometrial risk; clotting depending on preparation.
- **In TRD generally:** oestrogens ("various regimens") have **limited data** (Table 3.5).

### 3. Testosterone [1]
- **Testosterone gel** is **effective in those with low testosterone levels** (TRD, Table 3.5; Pope et al., refractory depression in men).
- It is part of HRT regimens in menopause.

### 4. Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (now in ICD-11) [1]
- **Non-hormonal:** **SSRIs first line**, continuous or **luteal-phase** dosing. Intermittent SSRIs may promote **progesterone-to-allopregnanolone** conversion.
- **Hormonal:**
  - **COCP** is also first line, cyclical or back-to-back. It improves overall symptoms but not depressive symptoms (2021 meta-analysis);
  - **transdermal oestradiol** second line, with **progestogen** if she has a uterus;
  - **GnRH analogues** produce a reversible medical menopause, used diagnostically and before considering oophorectomy, with **add-back** oestradiol with or without a progestogen.
- Psychiatric and hormonal treatments can be combined.

### 5. Neurosteroid: brexanolone (allopregnanolone) [1, 2]
- An endogenous **progesterone metabolite**; a **positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A**.
- **Indication:** **moderate to severe postpartum depression**.
- **Dosing:** a stepped **IV infusion over 60 hours** (30, then 60, then 90, then 60, then 30 µg/kg/h), with in-patient monitoring for **sedation and syncope**.
- FDA-approved but **not launched in India** [2].
- **ACOG** suggests considering it for moderate to severe perinatal depression with **third-trimester onset** [1].
- Metabolised by non-CYP pathways [1].

### 6. HPA axis: glucocorticoid-receptor antagonism [1]
- **Rationale:** **HPA hyperactivity is commoner in psychotic depression**.
- **Mifepristone:** pooled data from 5 double-blind trials (n = 833 vs 627 placebo) over 7 days showed it **reduced psychotic symptoms**. Response was greater with plasma levels of at least 1637 ng/mL.

**Conclusion.**
- **T3 augmentation** is the best-established hormonal strategy in TRD.
- **HRT** is first line for perimenopausal depression, anxiety and insomnia.
- **COCP, oestradiol and GnRH analogues** treat PMDD.
- **Testosterone** helps hypogonadal men.
- **Brexanolone** (neurosteroid) is for severe postpartum depression.
- **Mifepristone** targets the HPA axis in psychotic depression.
- Oestrogen in TRD outside these contexts has limited data.

## In depth

### Summary table [1, 2]
| Hormone strategy | Target population | Evidence (from sources) |
|---|---|---|
| T3 20–50 mcg | TRD; bipolar depression | Good support; not useful in non-refractory cases |
| HRT (oestrogen ± progestogen ± testosterone) | Peri- and post-menopausal depression | First line for menopausal mood, anxiety, insomnia |
| Oestrogens in general TRD | TRD | Limited data |
| Testosterone gel | Men with low testosterone | Effective |
| COCP; transdermal oestradiol; GnRH analogue | PMDD | First and second line |
| Brexanolone | Moderate to severe PPD | FDA-approved; IV in-patient |
| Mifepristone | Psychotic depression | Reduces psychotic symptoms (pooled RCTs) |
