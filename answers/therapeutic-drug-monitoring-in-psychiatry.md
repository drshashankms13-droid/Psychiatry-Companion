## Exam answer

**Definition.** Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) is the measurement of drug (and active metabolite) concentrations in blood (plasma or serum), interpreted in the light of pharmacokinetics and clinical response. It is used to individualise dosing, check adherence, detect toxicity and interactions, and explain non-response. It is useful only when there is a defined relationship between concentration and effect or toxicity, marked inter-individual pharmacokinetic variability, and a reliable assay. The governing rule is **"treat the patient, not the level"** [1].

### Mechanism (pharmacokinetic basis)
- **First-order kinetics:** a constant fraction is cleared per unit time. Steady state is reached after about 4–5 half-lives; 3 half-lives give about 87.5%. Loading doses reach therapeutic levels faster but do not hasten steady state [1].
- **Variability** comes from CYP genotype (CYP2D6, CYP2C19, CYP1A2), age, sex, smoking (CYP1A2 induction), inflammation and infection, drug interactions, renal and hepatic function, and adherence [1].
- **Concentration → receptor occupancy → effect:** e.g., risperidone active moiety 20 µg/L ≈ 65–70% D2 occupancy (therapeutic threshold) [1].

### Indications (AGNP consensus; Maudsley)
1. Drugs with a **narrow therapeutic index**: lithium, clozapine, TCAs, carbamazepine, valproate, phenytoin.
2. Suspected **non-adherence** or partial adherence.
3. **Non-response** at adequate dose (exclude rapid metaboliser or low level).
4. **Adverse effects** at usual doses (slow metaboliser, interaction).
5. **Drug interactions**: starting or stopping inducers or inhibitors; **smoking cessation** (clozapine and olanzapine levels rise).
6. **Special populations**: elderly, children, pregnancy, hepatic or renal impairment, obesity, bariatric surgery.
7. **Toxicity or overdose.**
8. Relapse prevention; switching formulations (oral ↔ LAI); medicolegal.

### Target ranges (Maudsley Table 11.1)

| Drug | Target range | Sample timing | Time to steady state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lithium** | 0.6–1.0 mmol/L (0.6–0.8 maintenance; up to 1.0 in mania; 0.4 may suffice for some) | 12 hours post-dose | 5 days |
| **Clozapine** | 350–600 µg/L (responders 250–1,000) | Trough | 2–3 days |
| Olanzapine | 20–40 µg/L | 12 hours | 1 week |
| Aripiprazole | 100–210 (to 300) µg/L | Trough | 15–16 days |
| Risperidone / paliperidone | 20–60 µg/L active moiety | Trough | 2–3 days oral |
| Amisulpride | 200–320 µg/L (20–60 in elderly) | Trough | 3 days |
| Quetiapine | ~50–100 µg/L (poorly defined) | Trough | 2–3 days |
| Valproate | 50–100 mg/L (up to 125 in mania) | Trough | 2–3 days |
| Carbamazepine | >7 mg/L (6–8 in bipolar disorder) | Trough | 2 weeks (auto-induction) |
| Lamotrigine | ~2.5–15 mg/L (>3.3 mg/L for response in TRD) | Trough | 5 days |
| Nortriptyline / amitriptyline | 50–150 / 100–200 µg/L | Trough | 2–3 days |
| Phenytoin | 10–20 mg/L (zero-order kinetics) | Trough | Variable |

Source: [1].

### Practical steps
Decide the clinical question → ensure steady state → **sample at the right time** (trough = just before the next dose; never withhold a dose for more than 1–2 hours) → **record the time of the last dose and the sample** → request the drug plus active metabolite (e.g., norclozapine; 9-OH-risperidone) → interpret with clinical status, co-medication, smoking, inflammation → act (adjust dose, address adherence, check interactions) → repeat if the result is unexpected [1].

### Key evidence
- **AGNP Consensus Guidelines (Hiemke 2018):** levels of recommendation 1–4 (strongly recommended for lithium, clozapine, TCAs, valproate, carbamazepine, haloperidol, olanzapine, perphenazine).
- **Clozapine:** response more likely at ≥350 µg/L; about 20% of responders need levels above range; levels ≥1,000 µg/L raise seizure risk [1].
- **Lithium:** 0.6 mmol/L is the minimum for prophylaxis [1].
- Responses in clinical practice cluster around published ranges: about 25% respond below and up to 25% tolerate levels above [1].

**Conclusion.** TDM is a valuable but frequently misused tool. It is essential for lithium and clozapine, and helpful for valproate, carbamazepine, TCAs, adherence questions, interactions and special populations. Correct sampling time, steady state and clinical interpretation determine its usefulness. Levels guide clinical decisions; they do not make them.

## In depth

### Background
TDM grew from antiepileptic and lithium monitoring in the 1960s–70s. Schou established lithium levels. TCA levels came in the 1970s (nortriptyline's therapeutic window, Åsberg). Clozapine levels followed in the 1990s (Perry; VanderZwaag). The AGNP-TDM consensus (2004, 2011, 2018) systematised recommendations. Pharmacogenomics (CPIC guidelines) now complements TDM.

### Epidemiology of use
- About 1 in 10 patients on long-term lithium have sub-therapeutic levels [1].
- Non-adherence in schizophrenia: 50% at 1 year; zero or very low levels identify it [1].
- Clozapine levels vary up to 50% even with ±1–2 hours of sampling time variation in some individuals [1].
- Asians, including Indians, need lower clozapine doses (higher levels per mg; more poor metabolisers) [1].

### Classification — AGNP levels of recommendation
1. **Strongly recommended:** lithium, clozapine, amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine, haloperidol, olanzapine, perphenazine, valproate, carbamazepine.
2. **Recommended:** most SSRIs (citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline), venlafaxine, aripiprazole, risperidone, quetiapine, amisulpride, lamotrigine.
3. **Useful:** fluoxetine, paroxetine, mirtazapine, others.
4. **Potentially useful:** drugs with weak data.

### Mechanism in detail
- **Steady state:** 2, 3, 4 and 5 half-lives give 75%, 87.5%, 94% and 97% of steady state [1].
- **Zero-order kinetics:** phenytoin, alcohol. Small dose changes produce large level changes.
- **Auto-induction:** carbamazepine (and lamotrigine possibly) induce their own metabolism; steady state is delayed [1].
- **Therapeutic threshold vs range:** risperidone/paliperidone have a threshold (about 20 µg/L) above which extra D2 occupancy adds only side effects. Clozapine has a broad, population-variable range with a "point of futility" [1].
- **Inflammation and infection:** suppress CYP1A2 and CYP3A4, so clozapine levels can double or triple during infection, causing toxicity (sedation, seizures, myoclonus) [1].
- **Smoking:** polycyclic hydrocarbons induce CYP1A2. Stopping smoking raises clozapine and olanzapine levels by 30–50% within 1–2 weeks; adjust doses.

### Clinical features in detail — scenarios

| Scenario | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Very low or undetectable level | Non-adherence (only indicates no doses over the past several days), ultra-rapid metaboliser, inducer co-medication, malabsorption [1] |
| Within range but no response | True non-response; consider switch or augmentation (e.g., clozapine) |
| Within range, but adverse effects | Reduce dose ("treat the patient") [1] |
| Below range, but responding | Do not increase [1] |
| High level | Poor metaboliser, inhibitor (fluvoxamine with clozapine), inflammation, stopped smoking, dehydration (lithium) |
| Clozapine:norclozapine ratio >2 | Recent dose, inhibition, or non-trough sample; <1 suggests induction or non-adherence |

### Assessment in detail — sampling rules
- **Trough:** immediately before the next dose [1].
- **Lithium:** 12 hours post-dose (10–14 hours acceptable) [1].
- **After a dose change:** wait for steady state (lithium 5 days; clozapine 2–3 days; aripiprazole about 2 weeks) [1].
- **LAIs:** trough before the next injection; steady state takes months [1].
- **Overdose:** sample immediately regardless of timing [1].
- **Request form:** dose, time of last dose, time of sample, co-medication, smoking status, clinical question [1].

### Differential — causes of unexpected levels

| High level | Low level |
| --- | --- |
| CYP inhibitors (fluvoxamine, ciprofloxacin, valproate–lamotrigine) | Inducers (carbamazepine, rifampicin, phenytoin, smoking, St John's wort) |
| Stopping smoking (clozapine, olanzapine) | Starting or restarting smoking |
| Infection or inflammation (clozapine) | Non-adherence |
| Renal impairment, dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors (lithium) | Ultra-rapid metaboliser (CYP2D6) |
| Poor metaboliser genotype | Malabsorption, bariatric surgery, vomiting |
| Sample taken too soon after dose | Sample taken late or dose withheld |

### Management details
- **Lithium:** maintenance 0.6–0.8; mania 0.8–1.0. Check every 6 months, or every 3 months in the elderly, CKD or with interacting drugs [1].
- **Clozapine:** target ≥350 µg/L before declaring non-response. If the level is >1,000 µg/L, consider anticonvulsant cover (valproate) or dose reduction. Check levels with infection, smoking change, or toxicity symptoms [1].
- **TCAs:** in overdose, the ECG (QRS) is more informative than levels [1].
- **Valproate:** in mania, levels up to 125 mg/L are tolerated and may be more effective. Ammonia rises linearly with level [1].
- **Pharmacogenomic complement:** CYP2D6 and CYP2C19 genotyping for SSRIs, TCAs and aripiprazole (CPIC). Combine with TDM in unexplained levels.
- **Special populations:** pregnancy (lithium clearance increases; check monthly, then weekly near term), the elderly (lower targets), children (higher clearance).

### Critical appraisal
- **Target ranges are approximations:** noise from placebo responders and non-responders; laboratories differ; about 25% respond below range and up to 25% tolerate above it [1].
- **Mis-sampling** is the commonest cause of misleading results [1].
- **Limited availability** of assays (amisulpride, aripiprazole, risperidone) and cost [1].
- **Adherence assessment** by levels is crude [1].
- **Evidence that routine TDM improves outcomes** (RCT-level) exists mainly for lithium and clozapine.

### Indian context
- Serum lithium is widely available in cities, but less so in district settings. Valproate and carbamazepine levels are moderately available. Clozapine levels are available only in a few labs (NIMHANS, CMC Vellore, AIIMS, private labs).
- Indians need lower clozapine doses (higher levels per mg; poor metabolisers) [1]. Monitor carefully when starting.
- Smoking (including bidi) and abrupt cessation during admission is an important cause of clozapine toxicity.
- Hot climate and dehydration raise the risk of lithium toxicity.
- Pharmacogenomic testing is commercially available but not routine.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** AGNP 2018 consensus; clozapine dose-prediction algorithms [1].
- **Emerging:** dried blood spot and capillary sampling (home TDM for clozapine and lithium); point-of-care lithium meters; genotype-guided dosing of clozapine [1].
- **Investigational:** saliva TDM; model-informed precision dosing software.
