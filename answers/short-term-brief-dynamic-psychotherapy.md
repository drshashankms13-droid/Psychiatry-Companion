## Exam answer

**Definition.** Brief (short-term) psychodynamic psychotherapies (STPP; brief dynamic therapy) are **time-limited** (typically 12–40 sessions, often with a pre-set end date) adaptations of psychoanalytic therapy. They use psychodynamic principles: unconscious conflict, defences, transference, the therapeutic relationship and insight. Compared with open-ended psychoanalysis they add a **focus** (a core conflict or relational theme), an **active therapist**, careful **patient selection**, and deliberate use of **termination** [1].

### Mechanism (theory of change)
Symptoms arise from **unresolved unconscious conflicts and maladaptive relational patterns** (Core Conflictual Relationship Theme: wish → expected response of other → response of self), maintained by defences against painful affects (anxiety, guilt, grief, anger). Therapy uses clarification, confrontation and interpretation of defences and transference, linked to past relationships and current life (Menninger's **triangle of insight**: therapist–current–past), within the **triangle of conflict** (impulse/feeling ↔ anxiety ↔ defence). This produces **insight**, affect experiencing and a **corrective emotional experience**, leading to structural and symptomatic change. Insight gains precede symptom improvement, specifically in PDT (r ≈ 0.31) [1].

### Major models

| Model (originator) | Selection | Key technique | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Brief focal psychotherapy** (Malan, Tavistock) | Motivation, focal conflict, responds to trial interpretation | Triangles of conflict and person; focus | 20–40 sessions |
| **Short-term anxiety-provoking psychotherapy, STAPP** (Sifneos) | Circumscribed complaint, oedipal focus, motivation, psychological-mindedness, at least one meaningful relationship | Anxiety-provoking confrontation and clarification | 12–20 sessions |
| **Time-limited psychotherapy** (Mann) | Mature defences | **Fixed 12 sessions**, central issue around separation/individuation; working through termination | 12 sessions |
| **Intensive short-term dynamic psychotherapy, ISTDP** (Davanloo) | Wide range; graded format | **Pressure and challenge to defences**, "unlocking the unconscious", experiencing of feelings; video review | 1–40 sessions |
| **Supportive–expressive therapy** (Luborsky) | Depression, substance use | CCRT, supportive + expressive | 16–24 sessions |
| **Panic-focused psychodynamic psychotherapy** (Milrod) | Panic disorder | Meaning of panic; separation and anger | 24 sessions |
| **Dynamic interpersonal therapy (DIT)** (Lemma, Target, Fonagy) | Depression (UK IAPT) | Interpersonal affective focus | 16 sessions |
| **Mentalisation-based / transference-focused therapy** | Borderline personality disorder | Longer-term derivatives | 12–18 months |

### Assessment and selection
Psychological-mindedness; motivation for change (not only symptom relief); ability to form a rapid alliance; a circumscribed focal problem; response to a **trial interpretation**; adequate ego strength; at least one meaningful past relationship. **Relative contraindications:** active psychosis, severe suicidality, active substance dependence, severe antisocial or borderline pathology (needs longer, structured therapy), organic disorders [1].

### Phases and techniques
Assessment and formulation (focus agreed) → early phase (alliance, identify defences and the CCRT) → middle phase (clarification, confrontation, interpretation of defences, feelings and transference; linking past–present–therapist) → termination (working through loss and separation, consolidating gains, anticipating relapse).

### Evidence (Leichsenring et al., World Psychiatry 2023, umbrella review with GRADE) [1]

| Disorder | PDT vs controls | PDT vs other active therapies | GRADE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Depression (27 RCTs, N=3,163) | g = −0.58 (medium); vs waitlist −1.14; vs active controls −0.51 | No clinically meaningful difference, short and long term | High |
| Anxiety (17 RCTs, N=1,798; panic, social anxiety, GAD, PTSD) | g = −0.94 (−0.72 without outlier) | Equivalent | Moderate |
| Personality disorders (16 RCTs; borderline, Cluster C) | g = −0.63 for core PD symptoms | Equivalent | Moderate |
| Somatic symptom disorders (17 RCTs, N=2,106) | Superior to controls | Equivalent | High |
| PDT + antidepressants vs antidepressants | g = −0.26 post-therapy; −0.50 at follow-up | — | — |

Benefits outweigh harms; there is evidence of long-term effects, functional improvement, effectiveness in routine care and cost-effectiveness. **Strong recommendation** as an empirically supported treatment [1].

**Conclusion.** Brief dynamic psychotherapies are structured, focused, time-limited and evidence-based. They are as effective as other active psychotherapies (including CBT) for depression, anxiety, somatic symptom and personality disorders, with durable effects. Because no single therapy suits all patients, STPP is a valuable option for psychologically-minded patients and those with relational problems.

## In depth

### Background
- **Freud's** early cases were brief (Bruno Walter in 6 sessions). **Ferenczi** (active technique), **Rank** (separation and will) and **Alexander & French (1946)**, with "corrective emotional experience" and flexibility in frequency, laid the groundwork.
- 1960s–70s: Malan's Tavistock studies of brief focal psychotherapy; Sifneos's STAPP (Boston); Mann's time-limited psychotherapy (1973); Davanloo's ISTDP (Montreal; video research).
- 1980s–90s: manualisation (Luborsky's supportive–expressive therapy; Strupp's time-limited dynamic psychotherapy, TLDP).
- 2000s–2020s: RCTs and meta-analyses (Leichsenring, Abbass, Driessen); DIT within UK IAPT; umbrella review 2023 [1].

### Epidemiology of use
STPP is widely practised in Europe (Germany: insurance-covered "Tiefenpsychologisch fundierte Psychotherapie") and in South America. In India, psychodynamic training is limited and CBT dominates; NIMHANS, PGIMER and some private psychotherapy institutes offer psychodynamic training.

### Classification
Psychodynamic therapies span a spectrum (Gabbard) from **expressive** (interpretation, insight) to **supportive** (strengthen defences, advice, alliance). Brief therapies are classified by:
- **drive/structural focus** (Malan, Sifneos, Davanloo: oedipal conflicts, anxiety-provoking);
- **relational focus** (Strupp's TLDP, Luborsky's CCRT, DIT);
- **integrative or eclectic** (Mann's existential time focus; Cognitive Analytic Therapy (Ryle) blends dynamic and cognitive).

### Mechanism in detail
- **Insight:** Kallestad (Cluster C), Johansson (social anxiety) and Gibbons (depression) found insight gains preceded symptom change specifically in PDT [1].
- **Affect experiencing and integration:** ISTDP emphasises bodily experience of previously avoided feelings.
- **Alliance:** alliance–outcome correlation r = 0.28 across therapies; in PDT the effect strengthens with time [1].
- **Transference work:** benefits patients with more severe interpersonal difficulties, mediated by insight and affect awareness [1].
- **Defence maturation:** shift from immature to mature defences (Defense Mechanism Rating Scale).
- **Reflective functioning / mentalising** improvement (personality disorders).

### Clinical features in detail — the core techniques
- **Clarification:** "you said you felt nothing when your father left".
- **Confrontation:** pointing out defences, e.g., "you smile when talking about this loss".
- **Interpretation:** linking feeling, anxiety and defence across current, past and transference relationships.
- **Triangle of conflict (Malan):** hidden feeling (anger, grief, longing) → anxiety → defence (avoidance, intellectualisation, somatisation).
- **Triangle of person:** therapist (transference) – current others – past (parents).
- **CCRT:** "I wish to be close (W) → others will reject me (RO) → I withdraw and feel depressed (RS)".
- **Termination:** a crucial working-through of loss (Mann).

### Assessment in detail
- Psychodynamic formulation: presenting problem, developmental history, relational patterns, defences, strengths.
- **Operationalised Psychodynamic Diagnosis (OPD-2):** axes for illness experience, relationships, conflicts, structure.
- **Trial therapy:** a 2–3-hour trial session (Davanloo) to assess response to interpretation.
- **Outcome measures:** symptom scales, Inventory of Interpersonal Problems, Defence Style Questionnaire, Reflective Functioning.

### Differential — choosing among psychotherapies

| Patient features | Better-suited therapy |
| --- | --- |
| Psychologically minded, relational themes, recurrent relationship patterns, wish for understanding | STPP |
| Specific symptom focus (panic, OCD, phobia), prefers structure | CBT/exposure |
| Interpersonal role transitions, grief, disputes (depression) | IPT |
| Chronic suicidality, emotional dysregulation | DBT, MBT, TFP |
| Low motivation, severe impairment | Supportive therapy, case management |
| Somatic symptom disorder | CBT or PDT (both effective) [1] |

### Management details — practical delivery
Sessions are weekly, 45–50 minutes, with the number fixed in advance. Agree a focus collaboratively. Supervision and adherence monitoring are needed (94% of trials used manuals) [1]. Combine with antidepressants in moderate–severe depression (better than antidepressants alone, especially at follow-up) [1]. Internet-delivered PDT also has evidence [1].

**Special groups:**
- **Elderly:** life review.
- **Adolescents:** short-term psychoanalytic psychotherapy (IMPACT trial).
- **Ethnic minorities:** in some RCTs, PDT was more effective than medication in minority men [1].

### Critical appraisal
- **Strengths:** a growing RCT base; equivalence with CBT; durable effects; relevance to personality and relational problems [1].
- **Limitations:**
  - Fewer RCTs than CBT; risk of bias and imprecision in some areas; possible publication bias in somatic symptom disorder.
  - Researcher allegiance effects.
  - Heterogeneity of models.
  - Selection criteria may limit generalisability [1].
- **Critiques:** abstract mechanisms; therapist training is long; ISTDP's confrontational style can be distressing.
- **Equivalence debate (the "Dodo bird verdict"):** common factors (alliance) may explain much of the effect across therapies [1].

### Indian context
- Culture: family-centred selves, hierarchical relationships and somatic idioms of distress. Dynamic formulations need cultural adaptation (Neki's **guru–chela** relationship model; Kakar's work on Indian psyche and families; Surya's contributions).
- Limited availability; long training; cost. Brief, focal and structured formats are more feasible than long-term analysis.
- Psychiatrists can use psychodynamic understanding in routine practice (formulation, transference awareness, supportive–expressive techniques) even without formal STPP.

### Recent advances
- **Established:** DIT (16 sessions) in UK IAPT; internet-delivered PDT [1].
- **Emerging:** unified psychodynamic protocols for anxiety disorders (transdiagnostic); research on mechanisms (insight, mentalising, affect).
- **Investigational:** neuroscience of psychodynamic change (fMRI changes in limbic–prefrontal circuits after STPP); combined psychedelic-assisted psychodynamic approaches.
