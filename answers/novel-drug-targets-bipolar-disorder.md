## Exam answer

### Introduction

Conventional treatments include lithium, valproate, carbamazepine, antipsychotics, and selected antidepressants used cautiously. Limitations in speed, tolerability, relapse prevention, and treatment resistance have prompted investigation of targets beyond monoamines.

### Emerging targets

1. **Glutamate:** NMDA/AMPA signalling affects synaptic plasticity. Ketamine and related approaches have been studied for rapid effects in bipolar depression. Duration of benefit, switch risk, misuse potential, and need for concurrent mood stabilization remain concerns.
2. **Intracellular signalling/PKC:** Lithium and valproate influence intracellular pathways. Tamoxifen, a PKC-inhibiting agent, has been studied in acute mania; endocrine and other adverse effects limit routine use. Endoxifen is also under investigation.
3. **Inflammation:** A subgroup may show altered inflammatory markers, but findings are heterogeneous and not diagnostic. Anti-inflammatory treatments remain investigational.
4. **Mitochondria/oxidative stress:** Energetic dysfunction, calcium signalling, and oxidative stress are implicated. Mitochondria-directed and antioxidant agents need stronger clinical evidence.
5. **Neurotrophic pathways:** BDNF and related neuroplasticity pathways may vary with mood state, but peripheral BDNF is not a validated individual treatment-selection marker.
6. **Circadian systems:** Sleep and rhythm disruption are relapse-relevant. Interpersonal and social rhythm therapy, carefully supervised light therapy, and sleep interventions can complement medication; monitor for activation or switching.
7. **Hormonal/metabolic pathways:** Thyroid and glucocorticoid systems are being studied, but biomarker-guided treatment is not yet established.

### Critical appraisal

Mechanistic plausibility is not proof of clinical benefit. Many findings come from small trials, heterogeneous samples, or short follow-up. New treatments require replication, adequately powered trials, long-term safety data, and comparison with standard care.

### Conclusion

Novel targets may improve future treatment, but most remain investigational. Current care should prioritize established agents, adherence, sleep/rhythm stabilization, psychoeducation, and individualized risk–benefit assessment.
