## Exam answer

### Definition [1]
- DSM-5-TR has **no separate disorder**. Postpartum depression is a **major depressive episode "with peripartum onset"**: onset of mood symptoms **during pregnancy or within 4 weeks after delivery**.
- The specifier also applies to manic, hypomanic and depressive episodes in **bipolar I and II**.
- About **50% of "postpartum" depressive episodes begin before delivery**, hence the term "**peripartum**". Maudsley gives **about a third** [2] ⚠️ (the figures differ; not reconciled).

### Epidemiology [1, 2]
- **Major depressive episode:** about **9%** between conception and birth, and **just under 7%** from birth to 12 months (DSM-5-TR) [1].
- About **10%** of pregnant women have a new or pre-existing depressive illness [2].
- **New psychiatric episodes rise markedly in the first 3 months after delivery**; **at least 80% are mood disorders**, especially severe depression [2].
- **Higher risk:** previous depression (postpartum or not); **bipolar disorder** (also risk of mania or mixed states) [2].

### Aetiology (as given in the sources) [3, 1]
- **Hormonal:** fluctuations in ovarian hormones and the progesterone-derived **neurosteroid allopregnanolone** modulate GABAergic control of the **HPA axis** via GABA-A receptors. Allopregnanolone rises and falls with progesterone across pregnancy and delivery [3].
- **Thyroid abnormalities** and other perinatal medical conditions can cause depression. If the depression is judged due to one, diagnose **depressive disorder due to another medical condition** instead [1].

### Clinical features [1]
- Meets **full MDE criteria** (2 weeks; functional impairment).
- May occur **with or without psychotic features**. Infanticide is rare and mostly linked to postpartum **psychotic** episodes (command hallucinations, delusions that the infant is possessed), though it can occur in severe non-psychotic episodes.

### Postpartum depression vs "baby blues" [1, 2]

| Feature | Maternity ("baby") blues | Peripartum-onset major depression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Status** | **Not a mental disorder** [1] | Major depressive disorder (or bipolar) with a specifier |
| **Frequency** | "**Much more common**" [1] | About 7% in the first postnatal year; about 9% antenatally [1] |
| **Onset** | Shortly after delivery; **sudden** mood changes [1] | During pregnancy or within 4 weeks postpartum (by definition); half start before delivery [1] |
| **Symptoms** | Sudden tearfulness **without depression**, mood lability, sleep disturbance, even brief confusion [1] | Persistent low mood or anhedonia plus the full MDE syndrome; may include guilt, suicidality, psychotic features |
| **Duration** | **Temporary, self-limited; improves quickly, within a week** [1] | At least 2 weeks; persists without treatment |
| **Function** | **No functional impairment** [1] | **Clinically significant impairment** |
| **Cause** | Probably the **physiological changes after delivery** [1] | Multifactorial: hormonal/neurosteroid, prior mood disorder, bipolar diathesis [1, 2, 3] |
| **Risk to mother/infant** | None specific | Self-neglect, missed obstetric care, **self-harm**; infant harm from **neglect to infanticide** [2] |
| **Management** | **No treatment needed** [1]; reassurance and observation (⚠️ specific advice not in sources) | Psychological and/or drug treatment, ECT if severe (below) |

**Also distinguish:**
- **postpartum psychosis** (mania or mixed state with psychosis; bipolar I);
- **postpartum delirium** (fluctuating awareness or attention) [1];
- **depression due to a medical condition** (thyroid) [1].

### Management [2, 3]
**1. Psychological:**
- **CBT**:
  - **individual CBT is better than group CBT for preventing** perinatal depression (OR 0.3 vs 0.73);
  - **10–13 weeks of group CBT** is effective for **treating** postpartum depression vs waitlist (d = 0.94) [3];
- CBT for **moderate bipolar depression** in pregnancy [2].

**2. Antidepressants (Box 7.3)** [2]:
- **moderate to severe** depression in pregnancy should be treated with antidepressants;
- **already on an effective antidepressant and at high risk:** **continue the same drug** during and after pregnancy. Stopping gives a relapse rate of **68% vs 26%** with continuation;
- **new treatment:** use what worked before. If drug-naive, **sertraline** (ACOG: an SSRI first line, **sertraline or escitalopram**; SNRIs an alternative; COPE: SSRIs first line);
- **severe bipolar depression:** an SSRI or **lamotrigine** [2].

**3. Neurosteroid** [2, 3]:
- **Brexanolone**: an intravenous allopregnanolone, a **positive allosteric modulator at synaptic and extrasynaptic GABA-A receptors**. FDA-approved for postpartum depression;
- **90 µg/kg/hour over 60 hours**, with effects at 60 hours lasting to 30 days [3];
- needs **continuous observation** (sedation, **loss of consciousness**) [3];
- **ACOG:** consider brexanolone for moderate to severe perinatal depression **with onset in the third trimester** [2].

**4. ECT** [2]:
- no evidence of harm to mother or fetus (beyond anaesthetic risk);
- **NICE:** for severe depression, mixed states, mania or **catatonia** where the physical health of mother or fetus is at serious risk.

**5. Breastfeeding** [2]:
- continue the pregnancy antidepressant; when starting, **sertraline** (RID 0.5–3%) may be considered;
- **fluoxetine** gives the highest SSRI infant levels;
- breastfeeding **reduced poor neonatal adaptation** in mirtazapine-exposed infants.

**6. Safety in pregnancy and neonatal monitoring** [2]:
- SSRIs and SNRIs in late pregnancy: small risks of **postpartum haemorrhage** and **PPHN**, and **poor neonatal adaptation**;
- NICE advises **extra newborn monitoring**;
- screen for **alcohol use**; watch for **hypertension/pre-eclampsia**.

**7. Prognosis and prevention** [2]:
- prior depression predicts recurrence in later pregnancies and postpartum;
- plan **pre-conception care** and continuation of effective treatment.

### Update from IPS CPG 2022 [4]
**Prevalence and course (Table 2):**

| Condition | Prevalence | Presentation | Intervention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Postpartum blues** | **40–50%** | Mild, self-limiting emotional changes that may or may not progress to depression | Reassurance; adequate social support |
| **Postpartum depression** | **15–20%** | May be **insidious** in the first months. In bipolar disorder, an **abrupt psychotic depression** may occur | Psychotherapy alone if mild; antidepressants if moderate to severe; **risk assessment for suicide and infanticide**; in-patient care if needed |

- In pregnancy: **depression 15–20%**, **anxiety 10–15%** (Table 1).
- **PPD typically begins within the first 4 weeks** after delivery, but the **risk period extends to 2 years**.

**Screening:**
- The **Marcé International Society** position paper favours a basic inquiry (Whooley's questions, PHQ-9 or EPDS) plus past and family history. Universal screening has a **false-positive rate of about 35–40%**.
- **Whooley's two questions** (from PRIME-MD): in the past month, have you felt **down, depressed or hopeless**? Have you been bothered by **little interest or pleasure**?
- Screening must be **backed by services and referral pathways**.
- **Severity:** EPDS, HDRS.
- **Bonding:** Postpartum Bonding Questionnaire (PBQ). **Maternal behaviour:** **NIMHANS Maternal Behaviour Scale (NIMBUS)**.

**Management:**
- **Mild depression:** psychotherapy (**CBT, IPT**); follow-up.
- **Moderate to severe:** **SSRIs**.
  - **Sertraline** is preferred in lactation.
  - **Fluoxetine** (RID 1.6–14.6%) may need infant monitoring.
  - **Paroxetine** is avoided in pregnancy.
- **Psychotic depression:** add an **antipsychotic**.
- **Rule out bipolarity** (milder highs, hypomania, strong family history) before long-term antidepressants.
- **High suicide risk:** in-patient care.
- **Prophylaxis:** a previous episode of PPD may justify **prophylactic antidepressants postpartum**.
- **Deciding on prophylaxis in pregnancy:**
  - a **single mild past episode** may be tapered after **6–12 months** well;
  - **continue** prophylaxis with **4 or more lifetime episodes**, psychotic depression, past suicide attempt, past admission or ECT.
- **Domestic violence and trauma raise risk**; social support protects.

**Brexanolone (IPS dosing):**
- an endogenous **progesterone metabolite**, a GABA-A positive allosteric modulator, for **moderate to severe PPD**;
- **infusion schedule** (in-patient, closely monitored):
  - **30 µg/kg/h for 4 hours**;
  - then **60 µg/kg/h for 20 hours**;
  - then **90 µg/kg/h for 28 hours**;
  - then **60 µg/kg/h for 4 hours**;
  - then **30 µg/kg/h for 4 hours**;
- **adverse effects:** headache, dizziness, **syncope**;
- **not yet launched in India**.

**Suicide:**
- the **first postpartum year** carries women's **highest lifetime suicide risk**;
- **predictors:** younger age, middle socioeconomic status, poor support, domestic violence, depression, past suicidality;
- **more lethal methods** are used.

⚠️ **Discrepancies (not resolved):**
- **PPD prevalence:** IPS gives **15–20%**. DSM-5-TR and Maudsley figures (above) are about **7%** in the first postnatal year, or 7–9% for peripartum depression.
- **Onset:** IPS says PPD "typically begins within the first 4 weeks" with risk **up to 2 years**. The DSM-5-TR specifier counts onset **in pregnancy or within 4 weeks** and notes that about **half begin antenatally**.

**Conclusion.** Postpartum depression is a major depressive episode with peripartum onset (in pregnancy or within 4 weeks of delivery, half beginning antenatally), affecting about 7% of women in the first postnatal year. It causes impairment, risks self-harm and child neglect, and can become psychotic. The "baby blues" are far more common, begin suddenly after delivery, consist of tearfulness, lability and sleep disturbance without depressive syndrome or impairment, are physiological, and resolve within a week without treatment. Management of PPD:
- CBT (individual for prevention; group effective for treatment);
- an SSRI (sertraline first if drug-naive; continue an effective pre-pregnancy antidepressant);
- brexanolone (a GABA-A neurosteroid) for severe cases;
- ECT when life is at risk;
- with breastfeeding generally continued.
