## Exam answer

### Postpartum depression (PPD)
**Epidemiology [1, 2]:**
- PPD affects **15–20%** of mothers [1].
- About **10%** of pregnant women develop, or already have, a depressive illness [2].
- About **a third of PPD cases begin before birth** [2].
- New psychiatric episodes rise significantly in the **first 3 months** after delivery, and at least 80% of these are mood disorders [2].

**Onset and course [1]:**
- typically **within 4 weeks** of delivery, but the risk period extends to **2 years**;
- may be insidious over the first few months;
- **in bipolar disorder, an abrupt psychotic depression** may occur.

**Risk factors [1, 2]:**
- previous depression (postpartum or not) [2];
- **bipolar disorder** (also at risk of mania or mixed episodes) [2];
- **domestic violence** and previous trauma [1];
- poor family support; substance use in the spouse; marital discord [1];
- a **previous female child** and family expectations of a son [1];
- infertility or ART; obstetric trauma; NICU admission or infant illness [1].
- Adequate social support is **protective** [1].

**Consequences of untreated depression [1, 2]:**
- poor antenatal care, self-neglect and **neglect of the infant**;
- **suicide and infanticide** risk;
- poor nutrition;
- preterm labour, low birth weight, stillbirth (small effects);
- **bonding disorders**, and later internalising or externalising problems in the child.

**Assessment [1]:**
- **Screening:** Whooley's two questions, PHQ-9, **EPDS**. **Severity:** EPDS, HDRS.
- The **Marcé Society** recommends a basic symptom enquiry plus past and family history. Universal screening has a false-positive rate of about 35–40%.
- **Always screen for bipolarity** (milder highs, hypomania, strong family history) before starting an antidepressant.
- **Risk assessment** for suicide and infanticide.
- **Bonding** (PBQ).
- **Organic work-up**: thyroid, anaemia, B12 and others (Table 3).

**Management [1, 2]:**
- **Mild:** psychotherapy (CBT, IPT), psychoeducation and follow-up [1].
- **Moderate to severe:** antidepressants, with psychotherapy [1]:
  - choose drugs compatible with lactation (RID under 10%);
  - **sertraline is preferred**; avoid paroxetine and doxepin;
  - fluoxetine (RID 1.6–14.6%) needs infant monitoring.
- **Psychotic depression:** add an antipsychotic [1].
- **High suicide risk:** in-patient care for the safety of mother and infant [1].
- **ECT** if suicidality or psychosis does not respond [1].
- **Prophylaxis:** women with a previous PPD may receive **prophylactic antidepressants postpartum** [1].
- **Brexanolone** (allopregnanolone; positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A) [1]:
  - for **moderate to severe PPD**;
  - stepped **IV infusion over 60 hours** (30, then 60, then 90, then 60, then 30 µg/kg/h);
  - **in-patient monitoring**: sedation and **syncope**;
  - approved by the FDA but not yet launched in India.
  - ACOG suggests it for moderate to severe perinatal depression with **third-trimester onset** [2].
- **Relapse:** 68% of women who stopped antidepressants in pregnancy relapsed vs 26% who continued [2].
- **Bonding interventions** and an **additional caregiver** [1].

### Postpartum depression vs "baby blues"
| Feature | Baby blues | Postpartum depression |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence | **40–50%** [1] | **15–20%** [1] |
| Nature | **Mild, self-limiting** emotional changes [1] | A depressive disorder [1] |
| Onset | Early postpartum (not specified further in the sources) | Usually within **4 weeks**; risk up to **2 years** [1] |
| Course | May or may not progress to depression [1] | Persists; can be severe, with psychotic features or suicidality [1] |
| Risk assessment | Not needed routinely | **Suicide and infanticide** risk assessment [1] |
| Management | **Reassurance** and adequate **social support** [1] | Psychotherapy for mild cases; antidepressants for moderate to severe; possible admission [1] |

**Key point:** the blues need no medication, but they must be followed up, because they **may progress to depression** [1].

## In depth

### Antidepressants in pregnancy and lactation (Table 8) [1, 2]
- **No increase in major malformations** (most data are for SSRIs).
- **Cardiac septal defects:** first-trimester exposure, mostly paroxetine.
- **PPHN:** third-trimester exposure; low absolute risk (3 per 1000).
- **Postpartum haemorrhage:** slight increase with SSRIs and SNRIs late in pregnancy.
- **Neonatal adaptation and withdrawal** syndrome with late exposure. Minimal, and confounded by depression.
- **Sertraline** has the least placental exposure and is preferred in lactation.
- **TCAs:** fetal exposure is high. Amitriptyline, nortriptyline, desipramine and clomipramine are considered safe in lactation.
