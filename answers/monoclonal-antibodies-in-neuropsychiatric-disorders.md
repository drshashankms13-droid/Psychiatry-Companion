## Exam answer

### Introduction
- In the held sources, monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) appear in neuropsychiatry in four roles:
  - to **clear pathological proteins** (amyloid, tau) in dementia;
  - to **block a signalling molecule or receptor**: **CGRP** in migraine;
  - to **modulate or deplete immune cells** in neuroinflammatory disorders (MS, NMOSD, SLE);
  - experimentally against **inflammation in depression**.
- *(The sources give no general definition, mechanism overview or nomenclature of mAbs.)*

### 1. Alzheimer's disease: anti-amyloid and anti-tau antibodies [1]
**Rationale:**
- **β-amyloid (Aβ)** is cleaved from amyloid precursor protein (APP) by **β- and γ-secretase**;
- its accumulation is **neurotoxic**;
- the three anti-amyloid strategies are **secretase inhibitors, aggregation inhibitors and Aβ immunotherapy**.

**Aducanumab:**
- binds **aggregated Aβ**, reducing plaques;
- **failed futility analyses** in two phase III trials (2019); a **reanalysis** claimed one positive trial;
- **FDA conditional accelerated approval (June 2021)**; **not approved in Europe**;
- concern: **amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA)**.

**Lecanemab (Clarity-AD):**
- binds **soluble Aβ protofibrils** (and to a variable extent other forms);
- **1795** patients with **MCI or early AD** and **amyloid confirmed by PET or CSF**;
- **10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks**;
- after 18 months, **27% slower decline on CDR-SB** (absolute difference **0.45 points**; 1.21 vs 1.66); all key secondary endpoints met;
- **ARIA in 21%** (oedema or microhaemorrhage), mostly asymptomatic;
- **deaths in the open-label extension**, possibly linked to **alteplase**, raised concern with thrombolytics;
- **FDA-approved**; under EMA evaluation at the time of writing.

**Donanemab (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2):**
- IV **every 4 weeks**; **1736** patients with early symptomatic AD and **amyloid plus tau on PET**;
- **about 35% slowing** of decline over 18 months (primary population);
- **52% became amyloid-PET negative** by 12 months;
- **ARIA-E (oedema) 24%; ARIA-H (microhaemorrhage/haemosiderosis) 31.4%**;
- under FDA and NICE evaluation at the time of writing.

**Failed antibodies:**
- **solanezumab, crenezumab, gantenerumab** (anti-amyloid): negative trials;
- **anti-tau** antibodies **gosuranemab, tilavonemab, zagotenemab**: development stopped;
- **semorinemab**: improved ADAS-Cog11 but not function or global outcome;
- **anti-tau vaccines** are in trials.

### 1b. Anti-amyloid antibodies in practice: Continuum 2024 [4]
- **Eligibility:**
  - **MCI due to AD or mild AD dementia** with amyloid proven by **amyloid PET or CSF**;
  - amyloid-positive **cognitively normal** people are **not eligible**;
  - **APOE genotyping** and **MRI within 12 months** are required.
  - Lecanemab MRI exclusions include more than 4 microhaemorrhages, any macrohaemorrhage, superficial siderosis, 2 or more lacunes, Fazekas grade 3 or above, and CAA-related inflammation.
- **Lecanemab vs donanemab** (Table 11-2):

| | Lecanemab | Donanemab |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dosing | 10 mg/kg IV **every 2 weeks** | IV **monthly** |
| CDR-SB slowing | **27%** (0.45 points) | **29%** (0.67 points); less with high tau |
| Amyloid negative at 18 months | 68% | 76% |
| Net ARIA-E / ARIA-H | 11% / 8% | 22% / 12% |
| ARIA-E in **APOE ε4/ε4** (raw) | **33%** | **36%** |
| Other | **Infusion reactions 26%** | — |

- **ARIA:**
  - **ARIA-E** (oedema or effusion on FLAIR) and **ARIA-H** (microhaemorrhage or siderosis on T2\*/SWI);
  - usually **asymptomatic** (headache, confusion, gait disturbance if symptomatic);
  - early in treatment; most resolve within **4 months** of suspension;
  - highest risk with **APOE ε4 homozygosity**, CAA or cerebrovascular disease.
- **Lecanemab safety MRIs:** before the **5th, 7th and 14th infusions and at about 1 year**.
- **Aducanumab** was **discontinued (not available after 2024)**.
- ⚠️ ARIA figures differ between Maudsley and Continuum (raw vs net reporting). See the AD answer.

### 2. Migraine: anti-CGRP antibodies (Continuum 2018) [5]
- **CGRP** is central to migraine pathophysiology. Antibodies target **the CGRP receptor or the CGRP ligand**.
- They are the **first drug class designed specifically for migraine prevention**: effective in **episodic and chronic migraine** in phase 2/3 trials.
- **Erenumab** (CGRP-**receptor** antibody):
  - **FDA-approved May 2018**; **70 or 140 mg monthly**;
  - **no contraindications** on the label;
  - adverse effects: **injection-site reactions, constipation**.
- **Non-vasoconstrictive**, so potentially usable with **cardiovascular risk** (stated for CGRP antagonism).
- Ligand-targeting antibodies were in the pipeline in 2018.

### 3. Multiple sclerosis and NMOSD [2, 6]
**Natalizumab** (anti-α4β1 integrin) [2, 6]:
- very effective in relapsing MS: **96%** free of new enhancing lesions vs 68% on placebo [2];
- **PML risk about 4 per 1000 overall**, stratified by **treatment duration**, **prior immunosuppression** and **JC virus antibody status** [6];
- **PML** presents with **progressive dementia** and neurological signs, a neuropsychiatric presentation of a drug complication [2];
- the safety of immunosuppressants after natalizumab is not established [6].

**Alemtuzumab** [6]:
- an IV agent (efficacy favoured over safety);
- **infusion reactions**, **about 30% secondary autoimmunity (mostly thyroid)** and possible **long-term malignancy**; a formal risk-management programme is required.

**Anti-CD20 antibodies** [6]:
- **Rituximab** (partially humanised, **anti-CD20**, depletes B cells):
  - widely used **off-label** for refractory MS; small studies show a strong MRI effect;
  - **failed in primary progressive MS overall** (a possible benefit in younger patients with enhancing lesions).
- **Ocrelizumab** (2016: under regulatory review):
  - beat interferon β-1a on relapses, progression and MRI in relapsing MS;
  - **delayed progression in primary progressive MS**.

**NMOSD** [6]:
- rituximab is used (alone or combined);
- 2016 trials: **MEDI-551** (anti-**CD19**, also depletes plasmablasts), **eculizumab** (complement inhibitor), **SA237** (anti-**IL-6 receptor**, "similar to **tocilizumab**", which also had promising pilot data).

### 4. SLE and neuropsychiatric lupus: rituximab [2]
- An anti-B-cell antibody (anti-CD20 per [6]).
- **Potential to produce remissions** in SLE, perhaps **when other treatments fail**.

### 5. Depression: anti-inflammatory biologics [3]
- **Tocilizumab** (an IL-6-receptor antibody, per [6]), alongside minocycline and zinc, has anti-inflammatory properties and **may help**. It is at **various stages of clinical trials**, not approved.
- Rationale: anti-inflammatory effects may enhance **neuroplasticity** and help maintain remission.

### Practical issues for psychiatrists [1, 2, 4, 6]
- **Anti-amyloid therapy** needs biomarker confirmation, APOE testing and serial MRI. ARIA may present with **confusion**. Avoid **thrombolytics** (lecanemab deaths) [1, 4].
- **Benefit is modest** in absolute terms: **0.45 (lecanemab) and 0.67 (donanemab) CDR-SB points** over 18 months [4].
- **PML** (natalizumab) can mimic a **new dementia** [2].
- **Biologics in depression** remain experimental [3].

**Conclusion.** Monoclonal antibodies now span:
- **dementia**: lecanemab and donanemab slow early AD by about 27–29% on CDR-SB, with ARIA risk graded by APOE ε4, requiring biomarker-confirmed eligibility and MRI surveillance; aducanumab is withdrawn and anti-tau antibodies have failed;
- **migraine**: erenumab, the first designed-for-purpose preventive;
- **MS and NMOSD**: natalizumab (PML), alemtuzumab (autoimmunity), anti-CD20 rituximab and ocrelizumab, complement and IL-6-receptor blockade;
- **SLE**: rituximab;
- **depression**: experimental tocilizumab.

Psychiatrists need to know these drugs' eligibility rules and their neuropsychiatric complications (ARIA confusion, PML dementia).
