## Exam answer

### Introduction
- In the held sources, monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) appear in neuropsychiatry in three roles:
  - to **clear pathological proteins** (amyloid, tau) in dementia;
  - to **modulate the immune system** in neuroinflammatory disorders (MS, SLE);
  - experimentally against **inflammation in depression**.
- *(The sources give no general definition, mechanism overview or nomenclature of mAbs.)*

### 1. Alzheimer's disease: anti-amyloid and anti-tau antibodies [1]
**Rationale:**
- **β-amyloid (Aβ)** is cleaved from amyloid precursor protein (APP) by **β- and γ-secretase**;
- its accumulation is **neurotoxic**;
- the three anti-amyloid strategies are **secretase inhibitors, aggregation inhibitors and Aβ immunotherapy**.

**Aducanumab:**
- binds **aggregated Aβ**, reducing plaques;
- **failed futility analyses** in two phase III trials (2019); a **reanalysis** claimed one positive trial;
- **FDA conditional accelerated approval (June 2021)**; **not approved in Europe**;
- concern: **amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA)**.

**Lecanemab (Clarity-AD):**
- binds **soluble Aβ protofibrils** (and to a variable extent other forms);
- **1795** patients with **MCI or early AD** and **amyloid confirmed by PET or CSF**;
- **10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks**;
- after 18 months, **27% slower decline on CDR-SB** (absolute difference **0.45 points**; 1.21 vs 1.66); all key secondary endpoints met;
- **ARIA in 21%** (oedema or microhaemorrhage), mostly asymptomatic;
- **deaths in the open-label extension**, possibly linked to **alteplase**, raised concern with thrombolytics;
- **FDA-approved**; under EMA evaluation at the time of writing.

**Donanemab (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2):**
- IV **every 4 weeks**; **1736** patients with early symptomatic AD and **amyloid plus tau on PET**;
- **about 35% slowing** of decline over 18 months (primary population);
- **52% became amyloid-PET negative** by 12 months;
- **ARIA-E (oedema) 24%; ARIA-H (microhaemorrhage/haemosiderosis) 31.4%**;
- under FDA and NICE evaluation at the time of writing.

**Failed antibodies:**
- **solanezumab, crenezumab, gantenerumab** (anti-amyloid): negative trials;
- **anti-tau** antibodies **gosuranemab, tilavonemab, zagotenemab**: development stopped;
- **semorinemab**: improved ADAS-Cog11 but not function or global outcome;
- **anti-tau vaccines** are in trials.

### 2. Multiple sclerosis: natalizumab [2]
- **Target:** **α4β1 integrin** (no further mechanism given in the source).
- **Approved** in the USA and Europe for **active relapsing MS**; supported by NICE.
- **Efficacy:**
  - **96%** had no new gadolinium-enhancing lesions vs **68%** on placebo;
  - adding it to **interferon-β** reduced relapses and MRI activity.
- **Safety:** **progressive multifocal leucoencephalopathy (PML)** (JC polyomavirus) occurred with natalizumab (with interferon-β, or in Crohn's disease, especially with other immunosuppressants). PML presents with **progressive dementia** and neurological signs.
- Natalizumab and interferon-β were the **first molecularly targeted MS therapies**.

### 3. SLE and neuropsychiatric lupus: rituximab [2]
- An **anti-B-cell** mAb (used in lymphoma).
- **Potential to produce remissions** in SLE, perhaps **when other treatments fail**.

### 4. Depression: anti-inflammatory biologics [3]
- **Tocilizumab** (with minocycline and zinc) has anti-inflammatory properties and **may help**. It is at **various stages of clinical trials**, not approved.
- Rationale: anti-inflammatory effects may enhance **neuroplasticity** and help maintain remission.

### Practical issues for psychiatrists [1, 2]
- **Patient selection** requires **biomarker confirmation of amyloid** (PET or CSF) and early-stage disease (MCI or mild dementia) [1].
- **ARIA** (oedema, microhaemorrhage) is common. Caution with **thrombolytics** (lecanemab deaths) [1].
- The absolute benefit for lecanemab was **0.45 CDR-SB points** over 18 months [1].
- **PML** with natalizumab may present as **new cognitive decline or dementia**, a neuropsychiatric presentation of a drug complication [2].

**Conclusion.** Monoclonal antibodies have entered neuropsychiatry mainly as disease-modifying treatments:
- **anti-amyloid antibodies** (lecanemab, donanemab) slow decline in amyloid-positive early AD by about 27–35%, at the cost of ARIA;
- **natalizumab** suppresses relapsing MS but risks PML;
- **rituximab** is an option in refractory SLE;
- anti-inflammatory antibodies such as **tocilizumab** are under trial in depression;
- anti-tau antibodies have so far failed.

Newer uses (anti-CGRP antibodies in migraine, rituximab in autoimmune encephalitis) are not covered by the held sources.

## In depth

### Not covered by the sources (deliberately left blank)
- Anti-CGRP mAbs in migraine prophylaxis.
- Rituximab, tocilizumab and other second-line immunotherapy in autoimmune encephalitis.
- Ocrelizumab, ofatumumab, alemtuzumab in MS; their psychiatric adverse effects.
- Infliximab trials in depression (benefit only with high CRP).
- ARIA MRI monitoring schedule; APOE ε4 homozygote risk; appropriate-use recommendations.
- Vaccines and mAbs against drugs of abuse.
