## Exam answer

**Definition.** Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) is a multimodal cognitive-behavioural treatment developed by **Marsha Linehan** (University of Washington, late 1970s) for chronically suicidal women with borderline personality disorder (BPD) [1]. It combines behavioural change strategies with **acceptance and validation**, drawing on Eastern mindfulness and Western contemplative practice, held together by a **dialectical** philosophy and the **biosocial theory** of BPD [1].

### Theoretical foundations

**1. Dialectics** [1]
- A world view of interrelatedness and change: for every thesis an antithesis exists, and therapy seeks a **synthesis**.
- The **core dialectic is acceptance versus change**: accept the patient and their pain as they are, while pushing relentlessly for change.
- Moves the patient from "either–or" to "both–and" thinking.

**2. Biosocial theory** [1]
Emotional vulnerability (high sensitivity, high reactivity, slow return to baseline) × **invalidating environment** (criticising, trivialising, punishing or erratically reinforcing emotional expression) → **emotion dysregulation** → self-harm, impulsivity, anger, dissociation as attempts to regulate unbearable emotion.

### Structure: four required modes [1]

| Mode | Function | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Group skills training** | Acquire new skills | Weekly, about 2 hours, 4–10 patients, two co-leaders; a class, not a process group |
| **Individual therapy** | Motivation, apply skills, chain analysis | Weekly, 50–60 minutes; diary card reviewed each session |
| **Telephone coaching** | Generalise skills to real life | Brief calls (under 10 minutes); **24-hour rule** after self-harm |
| **Therapist consultation team** | Support therapists, maintain fidelity, prevent burnout | Weekly 1–2 hours |

A programme is not DBT unless **all four modes** are delivered [1]. Standard duration is about **1 year**.

### Skills modules [1]

| Module | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Core mindfulness** | Attention on purpose, in the present, without judgement; **wise mind** (synthesis of emotion mind and reasonable mind); observe, describe, participate; non-judgemental, one-mindful, effective |
| **Distress tolerance** | Get through crises without making them worse: distraction, self-soothing, improving the moment, breathing exercises, **radical acceptance** |
| **Emotion regulation** | Label emotions, understand their function, reduce vulnerability, increase positive events, **opposite action** |
| **Interpersonal effectiveness** | Ask for what one needs and say no while preserving the relationship and self-respect |

### Treatment stages and target hierarchy [1]

- **Pre-treatment:** orientation, assumptions, agreements, commitment.
- **Stage I** (behavioural dyscontrol → control), in order of priority:
  1. **Life-threatening behaviours** (suicidal acts, self-harm)
  2. **Therapy-interfering behaviours** (missed sessions, lateness, not doing diary cards)
  3. **Quality-of-life-interfering behaviours** (substance use, eating problems, depression)
  4. Increasing behavioural skills
- **Stage II:** process trauma (e.g. exposure for PTSD) and emotional experiencing.
- **Stage III:** ordinary problems of living, self-respect.
- **Stage IV:** capacity for joy, completeness.

### Core strategies [1]

- **Behavioural (chain) analysis:** detailed sequence of vulnerability, prompting event, links, problem behaviour and consequences → **solution analysis**.
- **Validation** at six levels, from attentive listening to radical genuineness.
- **Dialectical strategies:** metaphor, devil's advocate, extending, "making lemonade out of lemons", balancing nurturing with demanding.
- **Contingency management:** warmth for skilful behaviour, matter-of-fact response to threats.
- **Commitment strategies**; exposure and cognitive restructuring.

### Evidence

- **First RCT (Linehan 1991):** DBT superior to treatment as usual in women with BPD [1].
- **2006 RCT:** DBT superior to therapy by community experts for suicide attempts [1].
- More than **10 RCTs** across independent teams in several countries; well-established by APA Division 12 criteria [1].
- **Cochrane review (2012)** identified DBT as the best-supported psychological treatment for BPD [1].
- Meta-analyses show the largest effects on **suicidal and self-injurious behaviour** [1].

### Adaptations [1]

| Population | Adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| Adolescents (DBT-A) | Family in skills groups, 6 months, "walking the middle path" module; RCT reduced self-harm (Mehlum 2014) |
| Children 8–11 years | Skills group reduced depression and suicidal ideation |
| Substance use | DBT-SUD |
| Eating disorders | DBT for bulimia and binge eating; **Radically Open DBT** for restrictive anorexia |
| PTSD | DBT-PE, DBT-PTSD |
| Older adults | Chronic depression with personality disorder |
| Transdiagnostic | DBT skills alone for emotion dysregulation |

**Conclusion.** DBT is the most extensively validated psychotherapy for BPD. Its effectiveness comes from combining behavioural change with validation, teaching concrete skills for emotion regulation, prioritising life-threatening behaviour, and supporting therapists through a consultation team. Its main limitations are resource intensity and limited evidence in men, minorities and low-resource settings.

## In depth

### Background

- Linehan found that standard CBT's relentless focus on change made suicidal patients feel invalidated, leading to anger, dropout and therapist burnout. She added acceptance and validation, skills groups, phone coaching and a consultation team [1].
- Manuals: *Cognitive-Behavioral Treatment of Borderline Personality Disorder* and the skills training manual (1993); revised skills manual (2014) [1].
- Behavioral Tech (2004) and the DBT-Linehan Board of Certification support training and fidelity [1].

### Rationale for each mode

| Function | Mode |
| --- | --- |
| Enhance capabilities | Skills group |
| Improve motivation | Individual therapy |
| Generalise skills | Phone coaching |
| Enhance therapist capability and motivation | Consultation team |
| Structure the environment | Case management, family sessions |

### Assumptions and agreements [1]

**About patients:** they are doing the best they can; they want to improve; they need to do better and try harder; they must solve their own problems even if they did not cause them; their lives are currently unbearable; they must learn new behaviour in all contexts.

**About therapy:** patients cannot fail DBT, but therapy can fail the patient; therapists need support; the relationship is between equals.

**Patient agreements:** stay in therapy for a set time (usually 1 year), attend sessions, work on reducing self-harm, attend skills training. The **four-miss rule**: missing four consecutive sessions ends treatment for that contract [1].

### Levels of validation [1]

1. Listening and observing
2. Accurate reflection
3. Articulating the unverbalised
4. Validating in terms of past learning or biology
5. Validating in terms of present context or normality
6. Radical genuineness

### Chain analysis in practice

Vulnerability factors (poor sleep, conflict) → prompting event (criticism at work) → links (thoughts "I'm a loser", shame, urges) → problem behaviour (self-harm) → consequences (short-term relief, later shame, avoidance). The therapist and patient identify where a skill could have broken the chain and rehearse it [1].

### Mechanisms of change

- Improved **emotion regulation** and reduced experiential avoidance.
- Skills use mediates reductions in suicide attempts and depression in trials.
- Validation lowers arousal and strengthens the alliance [1].

### Comparison with other BPD therapies

| Feature | DBT | MBT | TFP | Schema therapy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | Behavioural | Psychodynamic + attachment | Psychodynamic (Kernberg) | Cognitive + experiential |
| Target | Emotion dysregulation, behaviour | Mentalizing failures | Split object relations | Maladaptive schemas and modes |
| Format | Individual + group + phone + team | Individual + group | Individual, twice weekly | Individual or group |
| Best evidence | Self-harm, suicide attempts | Long-term functioning | Personality organisation | Overall BPD severity |

### Ethical issues [1]

- Assessing risk disclosed in group: two leaders allow one to step out with the patient.
- The four-miss rule creates a gap in care until referral is completed.
- Phone coaching raises confidentiality and risk-assessment problems.
- Balancing communication within the treatment team with the aim of teaching patients to advocate for themselves.

### Critical appraisal

- **Resource intensity:** a full programme needs trained staff, weekly team meetings and phone availability, which is hard to provide in public services.
- **Components:** dismantling studies are few; whether skills training alone is enough is unclear [1].
- **Generalisability:** most trials included women; evidence in men, minorities and LMICs is limited [1].
- **Comparisons:** against well-structured generalist care (GPM), DBT shows similar outcomes in some trials.
- **Outcomes:** large effects on self-harm, smaller effects on depression, quality of life and social functioning.

### Indian context

- Few certified DBT therapists; group skills programmes in tertiary centres (including NIMHANS) are adapted to local language and family systems.
- Family involvement (as in DBT-A) is culturally congruent and can address invalidation at home.
- Telephone coaching and confidentiality need adaptation in shared households; Tele-MANAS can support crisis access.
- Self-harm presentations are common in young Indian women; DBT-informed brief interventions in emergency settings are feasible.

### Recent advances

- Transdiagnostic DBT skills groups for emotion dysregulation [1].
- **Radically Open DBT** for over-controlled disorders (restrictive anorexia, chronic depression) [1].
- DBT-PTSD and combined DBT with prolonged exposure for trauma.
- Digital and internet-delivered DBT skills training.
