## Exam answer

### Concept [1, 2]
- **What it is:** an **intermediate state** between normal ageing and dementia: impaired, but not demented. **In some people, not all, it is a prodrome of dementia** [1].
- **Earlier terms:**
  - benign senescent forgetfulness;
  - age-associated memory impairment.
- **Petersen (2001)** formulation became standard.

**Core criteria (common to all definitions)** [1]:
1. **does not meet criteria for dementia**;
2. **subjective cognitive complaint** (by the patient or an informant);
3. **objective impairment or decline**, often **more than 1.5 SD below** age-adjusted norms;
4. **no substantial functional impairment**.

**Subtypes (Winblad 2004)** [1]:
- **amnestic** (memory only) vs non-amnestic;
- **single-domain** vs **multi-domain**.

**DSM-5-TR mild neurocognitive disorder** [2]:
- **modest decline** in one or more domains: concern **plus** modest impairment on standardised testing;
- **independence preserved** (complex IADLs intact, though with greater effort or compensation);
- not only during delirium; not better explained by another mental disorder;
- specify aetiological subtype (e.g., due to AD; "possible" or "probable" by gene status and clinical criteria).

### Problems with the concept [1]
- **The MCI/dementia boundary is a clinical judgement**, depending on how diligently **functional impairment** is sought.
- Function is **individual, culture- and gender-bound**. Lishman's washing-machine example: limited use of its functions vs inability to rewire it.
- **Conversion to dementia** varies from **2% to 30% per year**:
  - **community samples about 7.5%/year**;
  - **clinic samples about 15%/year** (the care pathway selects incipient dementia);
  - Bruscoli & Lovestone 2004.
- **Predictors of conversion:**
  - APOE ε4 (some studies);
  - CSF AD proteins;
  - imaging (hippocampal atrophy, metabolism).
  - None was yet replicated for clinical use (2009) [1].

### Approach to a patient with MCI (p3-9d744f64) [1, 2, 3]
**1. History (patient and informant):**
- nature and onset of complaints (memory vs other domains);
- tempo;
- **functional change**: "Is there anything you used to do that you now find difficult?";
- mood (depression, anxiety);
- sleep; alcohol; medications (**anticholinergic burden**);
- vascular risk factors;
- head injury;
- family history (early-onset dementia).

**2. Rule out mimics:**
- **depression ("pseudodementia")**: even when it reverses, most develop dementia within 5 years [1];
- **delirium** or physical illness (treating systemic illness can improve cognition) [1];
- normal ageing (slowed processing, retrieval and strategy deficits, preserved crystallised abilities) [1];
- drugs and alcohol.

**3. Examination:** physical and neurological (focal signs, parkinsonism, gait), sensory.

**4. Cognitive testing:**
- bedside test across domains (e.g., MMSE, which was developed as a screening scale) [1];
- **neuropsychology** to quantify impairment against age norms (more than 1.5 SD) and provide a **baseline** [1, 2].

**5. Investigations (as for dementia)** [1]:
- FBC, U&E, LFT, TFT, B12/folate, ± syphilis serology;
- **MRI** (hippocampal atrophy, vascular lesions);
- ± FDG-PET or SPECT;
- research biomarkers (CSF Aβ/tau; amyloid PET) [1, 3].

**6. Classify:**
- amnestic or non-amnestic; single- or multi-domain;
- likely aetiology (AD, vascular, Lewy body, FTD, other) [2].

**7. Communicate:**
- explain uncertainty: **not all progress**;
- conversion risk about 7.5–15% per year.

**8. Management:**
- **Monitor** for conversion: guidelines recommend screening and **monitoring for conversion to dementia** [1].
- **Treat modifiable factors:** vascular risk, depression, medication review (anticholinergic drugs).
- **Activity:** physical and mental activity is protective against AD (epidemiology) [1].
- **Drugs:**
  - **cholinesterase inhibitors are *not* recommended in MCI**: very little effect on progression or cognition, with more GI adverse effects (Cochrane) [3];
  - **galantamine was linked to increased mortality** in MCI trials (FDA warning) [3];
  - **neither AChE-Is nor memantine are effective in MCI** (BAP: no first or second choice) [3];
  - rofecoxib was ineffective [3].
- **Anti-amyloid antibodies:**
  - lecanemab and donanemab trials included **MCI due to AD with amyloid confirmation** (PET or CSF);
  - they slow decline (27–35%) with a **risk of ARIA**;
  - lecanemab is FDA-approved [3].
- **Legal and future planning** while capacity is intact; driving review if relevant [1].

**Conclusion.** MCI (mild NCD) describes objective cognitive decline with preserved independence. It is heterogeneous: some patients stay stable and others convert to dementia (about 7.5% per year in the community, 15% in clinics). The approach is:
- careful history with an informant, focused on function;
- exclusion of depression, delirium and drugs;
- neuropsychological quantification;
- dementia-screen bloods and MRI;
- subtype and aetiology classification;
- honest communication;
- **monitoring** and treatment of modifiable factors.

Cholinesterase inhibitors are not recommended; anti-amyloid antibodies are an emerging option only for biomarker-confirmed MCI due to AD.
