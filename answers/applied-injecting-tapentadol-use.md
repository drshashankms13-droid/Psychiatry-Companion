## Exam answer

**Two related cases.** Both describe **pharmaceutical opioid injecting**. This is an increasingly common Indian presentation (Punjab, the northeast, metro cities).
- **Case A:** 22-year-old scrap dealer.
  - Tobacco and cannabis since 16.
  - **Intravenous tapentadol from 18**, multiple times a day for 4 years, alone and with friends. His cannabis use fell after he started tapentadol.
  - His mother has brought him to quit.
- **Case B:** 20-year-old in the emergency department.
  - Injecting tapentadol for 3 years; **800–1000 mg/day** for the past year.
  - Now **severe body ache, dilated pupils, pulse 98/min and rhinorrhoea**, i.e. **opioid withdrawal**.

**Diagnosis (both):** **opioid dependence (ICD-11 6C43.2)**, current use, **injecting route**. Comorbid **nicotine dependence** and **cannabis use** (harmful use or dependence; Case A in remission or reduced). Case B is in **opioid withdrawal (6C43.4)**, which looks moderate on the Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS).

### Why tapentadol matters
- **Dual mechanism:** **μ-opioid agonist + noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor**. A weaker serotonergic effect than tramadol, but still present.
- **Short half-life** (about 4–5 hours), so it is used repeatedly through the day.
- **Tablets crushed and injected** (sometimes mixed with other drugs such as pheniramine and benzodiazepines), causing **abscesses, thrombophlebitis, embolism and blood-borne viruses**.
- **High doses** risk:
  - **seizures** (lowered threshold);
  - **serotonin syndrome** with SSRIs or other serotonergic drugs;
  - respiratory depression with alcohol or benzodiazepines.
- Pharmaceutical opioids are diverted from pharmacies. Tapentadol is a prescription (Schedule H) drug and not a scheduled narcotic under NDPS in India [general knowledge; check current status].

### (a) Further evaluation
1. **Substance history for each drug:**
   - age at onset, pattern, dose, route, injecting frequency;
   - **needle and syringe sharing**;
   - use of other drugs (heroin/smack, buprenorphine injection, pheniramine, benzodiazepines, alcohol);
   - last dose; previous withdrawal or overdose;
   - previous treatment attempts (detox, rehab, OST); longest abstinence;
   - motivation (stage of change). Case A was brought by his mother: is it his own wish?
2. **Withdrawal assessment:**
   - **COWS** score (pulse, sweating, restlessness, pupils, bone and joint aches, rhinorrhoea and lacrimation, GI upset, tremor, yawning, anxiety, gooseflesh);
   - time since last dose.
3. **Complications:**
   - **injection-site abscess, cellulitis, DVT, endocarditis**; track marks;
   - **HIV, HCV, HBV**; TB;
   - seizures; overdose; trauma;
   - sexually transmitted infections; risky sex.
4. **Psychiatric comorbidity:**
   - depression, anxiety, suicidality;
   - ADHD; conduct disorder history; antisocial traits;
   - psychosis (cannabis).
5. **Psychosocial context:**
   - occupation (scrap dealing involves easy access to cash and peer drug networks);
   - family structure and conflict;
   - peer use;
   - legal problems (theft);
   - finances; marital status.
6. **Examination:**
   - vitals; pupils; injection sites; veins; nutrition; jaundice; murmurs; lymphadenopathy;
   - neurological examination;
   - MSE.
7. **Investigations:**
   - **urine drug screen** (standard opiate panels may **miss tapentadol**; specific assays or LC-MS are needed);
   - CBC, LFT, RFT;
   - **HIV, HCV antibody → HCV RNA, HBsAg**; VDRL;
   - ECG; chest X-ray (TB, septic emboli);
   - echocardiography if a murmur or fever is present.

### (b) Management of Case B: emergency withdrawal
1. **Confirm withdrawal and exclude complications:** sepsis, abscess, endocarditis, seizure risk, co-intoxication.
2. **Buprenorphine induction** (treatment of choice; NACO 2014) [2]:
   - wait until **objective moderate withdrawal** (COWS ≥ 12–13); here pupils, tachycardia and rhinorrhoea are already present;
   - make sure the last tapentadol dose was at least **6–8 hours** ago, to avoid **precipitated withdrawal**;
   - **day 1:** **2–4 mg sublingual**, reassess at 1–2 hours, add 2–4 mg; **maximum 8 mg on day 1**;
   - **day 2:** increase in 2 mg steps to a maximum of about **12 mg**;
   - **stabilisation:** **8–12 mg/day is usually optimal in India** (maximum 20–24 mg) [2];
   - the targets are to stop withdrawal (2–4 mg), control craving (4–8 mg) and **block opioid effects (8–12 mg)** [2];
   - **buprenorphine-naloxone** where available, to reduce diversion.
3. **Alternative if OST is declined (detoxification):**
   - buprenorphine taper over 7–14 days; **or**
   - **clonidine** 0.1–0.15 mg three times a day (watch BP), plus symptomatic treatment: NSAIDs or paracetamol (aches), loperamide (diarrhoea), ondansetron (nausea), short-term benzodiazepine or zolpidem (insomnia; with caution).

   Detox alone has **very high relapse** and a **post-detox overdose risk**, so link to ongoing care.
4. **Treat complications:**
   - antibiotics for abscesses or cellulitis;
   - tetanus immunisation;
   - hepatitis B vaccination.
5. **Harm reduction:**
   - **naloxone** education;
   - **needle–syringe programme** referral (NACO targeted intervention);
   - safer injecting education;
   - condoms.
6. **Move to maintenance** (as for Case A).

### (c) Management of Case A: long-term treatment
**Goal-setting:**
- engage him through **MI**, not just his mother's wish;
- explain options: **opioid substitution therapy (maintenance)** vs **detoxification followed by naltrexone and rehabilitation**.

**Opioid substitution therapy is the treatment of choice** (NACO criteria) [2]:
- **long duration of use or injecting (more than 3 years)**;
- injecting route;
- **previous failed detoxification**;
- high risk (HIV/HCV, overdose).

**Options:**
1. **Buprenorphine (or buprenorphine-naloxone) maintenance:**
   - 8–12 mg/day;
   - supervised daily dosing initially, take-home doses once stable;
   - **long duration** (months to years); duration is set by attainment of goals, not a fixed formula [2].
2. **Methadone maintenance** (60–120 mg/day): for heavy dependence, or poor response to buprenorphine. Monitor QTc.
3. **Oral naltrexone** (50 mg/day; after 7–10 days opioid-free and a naloxone challenge):
   - for highly motivated people with **family supervision** (his mother could supervise);
   - poor adherence otherwise;
   - **overdose risk if he relapses** (loss of tolerance).
4. **Extended-release injectable naltrexone or buprenorphine depots:** limited availability in India.

**Psychosocial treatment:**
- **MI and relapse prevention** (identify high-risk situations: friends, scrap-yard cash, boredom);
- CBT; contingency management;
- **family therapy** (mother as ally; reduce criticism; supervision of medication);
- vocational support;
- **Narcotics Anonymous**;
- treat tobacco (NRT, varenicline) and cannabis (MET/CBT).

**Medical:**
- **HCV:** if RNA positive, **direct-acting antivirals: sofosbuvir + velpatasvir for 12 weeks** (free under the **National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme**) [3]. **Active drug use is not a contraindication**; integrated OST plus HCV care improves cure (SVR12 above 95%) [3];
- HIV: ART through NACO ART centres;
- HBV vaccination.

**Monitoring:**
- urine tests;
- attendance; craving;
- injection sites;
- **retention in treatment** (the strongest predictor of outcome);
- psychiatric symptoms;
- annual HCV re-screening if he keeps injecting (reinfection) [3].

**Prognosis.** Poor prognostic factors: early onset, injecting, polysubstance use, peer network, occupation. Good prognostic factors: family support, help-seeking, young age, OST retention.

## In depth

### Background
- **India's opioid epidemic** shifted from opium and doda (poppy husk) in rural areas to heroin (smack/brown sugar) and **pharmaceutical opioids** (buprenorphine injections in the 1990s; **tramadol, tapentadol, codeine syrups** more recently) [general knowledge].
- **Tapentadol** was approved in India around 2011 (general knowledge). Easy pharmacy availability and low cost led to diversion. Reports from Punjab and Manipur describe injecting [Likely; check].

### Epidemiology
- **Magnitude of Substance Use in India (2019):**
  - **opioid use 2.1%** (about 2.3 crore people);
  - opioid use disorder about 0.7% (about 77 lakh);
  - about **8.5 lakh people inject drugs**;
  - heroin is the most common opioid, followed by pharmaceutical opioids [general knowledge; check].
- **HCV prevalence among people who inject drugs** in India: about 30–40% (higher in the northeast and Punjab) [general knowledge; check].

### Classification and criteria
- **ICD-11 6C43:** disorders due to use of opioids (harmful pattern, **dependence**, intoxication, withdrawal, opioid-induced disorders).
- **DSM-5-TR opioid use disorder:** 11 criteria; severity by count. Tolerance and withdrawal are not counted when the opioid is taken as prescribed.

### Mechanism in detail
- **μ-opioid receptor agonism** in the VTA disinhibits dopamine neurons (via GABA interneurons), causing reward.
- **Locus coeruleus:**
  - opioids suppress LC firing;
  - chronic use upregulates cAMP;
  - in withdrawal, **noradrenergic hyperactivity** causes tachycardia, sweating, mydriasis and anxiety.

  This is the basis for **clonidine** (α2 agonist) treatment.
- **Tapentadol's NRI action** may add noradrenergic withdrawal features.
- **Buprenorphine:**
  - **partial μ agonist, high affinity, slow dissociation**;
  - ceiling effect on respiratory depression (safer);
  - displaces full agonists, which can **precipitate withdrawal** if given too early [2];
  - 0.3 mg IV buprenorphine is equivalent to 10 mg morphine [2].

### Clinical features in detail
- **Opioid withdrawal** (onset 8–12 hours for short-acting opioids; peak 36–72 hours; acute phase 5–10 days; **protracted symptoms** for weeks to months) [2]:
  - lacrimation, rhinorrhoea, yawning;
  - mydriasis, piloerection;
  - myalgia, abdominal cramps, diarrhoea;
  - insomnia, restlessness, craving, dysphoria.
- **Intoxication:** miosis, sedation, respiratory depression.
  - **Overdose triad:** coma + pinpoint pupils + respiratory depression. Treat with **naloxone**.
  - With tapentadol, **seizures** may also occur, and the pupils may be less constricted.

### Assessment in detail
- **COWS bands:** 5–12 mild; 13–24 moderate; 25–36 moderately severe; above 36 severe. Alternatives: SOWS, Objective Opioid Withdrawal Scale.
- **ASSIST** for polysubstance screening.
- **Readiness to change:** URICA, or a simple ruler.

### Management details
| Phase | Key points [2] |
| --- | --- |
| Assessment | Diagnosis, severity, comorbidity, consent, treatment plan, treatment goals |
| Induction (days 1–3) | Day 1: 2–4 mg, up to 8 mg. Day 2: up to 12 mg. Daily review |
| Stabilisation (weeks 1–4) | Adjust dose to control withdrawal, craving and the blocking effect |
| Maintenance | Months to years; supervised then take-home doses; psychosocial interventions |
| Tapering / exit | Gradual, client-led reduction only once goals are achieved; re-entry allowed |

- **Drug interactions:**
  - benzodiazepines and alcohol increase respiratory depression;
  - **DAA interactions with OST are minimal** (sofosbuvir/velpatasvir is safe with buprenorphine and methadone) [3];
  - rifampicin lowers methadone and buprenorphine levels.

### Critical appraisal
- **The tapentadol-specific evidence is sparse.** Management is extrapolated from heroin and other pharmaceutical opioid OUD.
- Urine screens miss tapentadol, so clinicians must ask directly.
- **The family's wish for "complete stopping" (detox only)** conflicts with the evidence for maintenance. Psychoeducation is needed.
- **Naltrexone** works only with strong supervision. The X:BOT trial (2018) found that induction failure was the main barrier [general knowledge].

### Indian context
- **Services:**
  - **NACO targeted interventions** for people who inject drugs (NSP, OST centres);
  - **Drug Treatment Clinics** (MoHFW/AIIMS) in district hospitals provide buprenorphine;
  - **Punjab's OOAT (Outpatient Opioid Assisted Treatment) clinics** (2017–18) dispense buprenorphine-naloxone at scale [Likely].
- **Policy:**
  - **NVHCP (2018)** provides free HCV testing and DAAs [3];
  - **NDPS Section 64A** gives immunity for addicts who volunteer for treatment;
  - **MHCA 2017** applies.
- **Pharmaceutical regulation:** there are calls to place tapentadol and tramadol under stricter control (e.g., Schedule X or NDPS listing) [general knowledge; check].

### Recent advances
- Long-acting injectable buprenorphine (weekly or monthly).
- **Low-dose buprenorphine induction ("micro-induction")** for people using fentanyl or long-acting opioids, avoiding the wait for withdrawal.
- Telemedicine prescribing of buprenorphine (US).
- **HCV elimination by 2030** (WHO) through test-and-treat at OST sites.
